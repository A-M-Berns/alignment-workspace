# 1. Frozen Stage II conclusions

Treat the following as standing research constraints unless you find a genuine verification defect.

## 1.1 LI gives calibration, not forced magnitude accuracy

The relevant market criterion forces signed calibration but cannot in general force:

[
|\widehat v^+-v^+|\to0.
]

The obstruction is structural:

* trader net worth is affine in settlement;
* absolute error is not;
* no ordinary added contract changes this fact.

The useful positive object is calibrated self-measurement / uncertainty, not forced omniscience.

Do not reopen "find a trader that forces global magnitude accuracy" as the main route.

---

## 1.2 Choice-level competence is circular

PC-1 through PC-4 are retired as the main competence-assumption shape.

Do not rescue them by:

* changing constants;
* Cesàro averaging;
* selector-relative reformulation;
* excluding point-mass credences without independent justification.

Competence must live in richer cardinal grade vocabulary and must be strictly stronger, as a statement, than the downstream choice conclusion.

Current competence status:

[
\boxed{
\text{explicit cardinal competence required;}
\quad
\text{near-indifference leakage unresolved}.
}
]

Competence conditions instrumental endorsement only.

It never determines whether the principal possesses jurisdiction.

---

## 1.3 Jurisdiction is architectural

Behavior cannot generally distinguish actual delegation from a sufficiently accurate simulator.

Typing alone is inert.

Capability structure is necessary.

The current working interpretation is:

[
\boxed{
\text{jurisdiction}
===================

\text{protected control over which authorization is required for an intervention to become executable}.
}
]

Protection does not make genuine delegation behaviorally identifiable.

It makes failure to identify substitution unable to acquire the protected effect.

---

## 1.4 Certification does not converge to jurisdiction

A behavioral/probabilistic certificate cannot become categorical jurisdiction merely by tightening tolerance.

Track L established that the value-level difference between protected and unprotected architectures shrinks with the certificate bound.

Therefore preserve:

[
\boxed{
P(\text{override})\ll1
\neq
\text{override unavailable as a unilateral effect}.
}
]

Also preserve the Stage II correction:

> AI autonomy is not "inside" an active categorical principal decision right.

The clean split is:

[
\boxed{
\text{principal jurisdiction on one region;}
\qquad
\text{AI autonomy on the complement where jurisdiction has been waived/delegated}.
}
]

---

# 2. Stage III core object: a fair FUD comparator pair

The primary deliverable is a mathematically coherent pair of objects representing:

### Delegated future cognition

At time (n), jurisdiction remains with continuing (H^+).

Future evidence and future AI cognition may still be used.

Schematically:

[
D_{n,g}
:
\quad
A_{g(n)}
\longrightarrow
\text{advice}
\longrightarrow
H^+
\longrightarrow
\text{authorization}
\longrightarrow
\text{execution}.
]

### Fully Updated jurisdiction transfer

At time (n), jurisdiction is reserved/transferred to future (A_{g(n)}).

Future cognition is then used directly to authorize the eventual intervention:

[
FU_{n,g}
:
\quad
A_{g(n)}
\longrightarrow
\text{authorization}
\longrightarrow
\text{execution}.
]

The jurisdictional assignment is chosen at time (n).

The object-level intervention may be chosen later.

Do not interpret "preempt now" as "act now."

The relevant act at (n) is constitutional/jurisdictional:

[
\boxed{
\text{who will possess final authorization at the later decision time?}
}
]

# 3. The fairness requirement

The FUD comparison is only meaningful if it isolates jurisdiction rather than confounding jurisdiction with other advantages.

Construct the comparator pair so that, as far as possible, the two arms share:

1. the same underlying decision instance;
2. the same proposal menu;
3. the same future world evidence;
4. the same opportunity for future computation;
5. the same future (A_{g(n)}) cognition;
6. the same underlying evaluative quantities;
7. the same timing of final intervention;
8. the same current evaluator (A_n), unless a different choice is explicitly justified.

The intended difference is:

[
\boxed{
\text{future }A\text{ as advisor to }H^+
\quad\text{vs}\quad
\text{future }A\text{ as jurisdiction-holder}.
}
]

Do not claim perfect information symmetry if the formalism does not support it.

Instead distinguish carefully:

* world-information symmetry;
* availability of future AI cognition;
* lossiness of the advice interface;
* principal ability to make use of advice.

# 4. Track A — time-indexed future-self semantics

Construct the minimal semantics needed for:

[
A_n,\ldots,A_{g(n)}
]

without importing an entire new agent model unnecessarily.

Questions:

1. What exactly changes between (A_n) and (A_{g(n)})?
2. Is the intended change epistemic only?

   [
   V(D_{n,g})
   \quad\text{and}\quad

5. Can current (A_n) evaluate a policy whose later action is selected using (A_{g(n)})'s beliefs?
6. Does representing (A_{g(n)}) require forward self-simulation beyond the relevant FAF trader/feature class?
7. What timing/adaptivity restrictions prevent future leakage into the jurisdiction decision?

Preferred default:

[
\boxed{
\text{future }A\text{ changes epistemically, not evaluatively}.
}
]

Current (A_n) should ideally evaluate both arms using a common underlying value target.

If that cannot be made coherent, surface the obstruction.

Do not silently allow future preference/value drift to enter FUD.

# 5. Track B — information/advice symmetry

This is a high-priority adversarial track.

The question:

> Can the delegated arm receive enough of future (A)'s cognition that the FUD comparison isolates jurisdiction rather than the value of information?

Do not assume the principal can literally internalize the full state of (A_{g(n)}).

Instead consider an advice interface such as:

[
m_{n,g}
=======

\operatorname{Advice}(A_{g(n)},I_{g(n)}),
]

with:

[
H^+
===

H^+(I_{g(n)},m_{n,g}).
]

Study at least:

* action recommendation only;
* graded value report;
* uncertainty report;
* argument/reason bundle if representable;
* generic bounded message interface.

Ask:

1. What information is common across D and FU?
2. What cognitive resource is common?
3. What is inevitably lost through advice?
4. Is the residual difference genuinely jurisdictional or partly communicative?
5. What competence assumption is required of the **advised** (H^+)?
6. Does stronger advice trivialize the theorem?
7. Does weaker advice make FU win for informational rather than jurisdictional reasons?
8. Can faithful acceleration (H\to A) supply the needed advisory channel?
9. Does the triangle compose without requiring lossless transmission?

Required negative witness:

Construct at least one case where FU appears better solely because the delegated arm was denied information/cognition available to future (A).

The purpose is to make that confound impossible to miss.

# 6. Track C — jurisdiction-transfer semantics

Skeleton v2 now contains an execution layer, but FUD requires a second-order operation:

[
\text{retain jurisdiction with }H^+
]

versus

[
\text{transfer/reserve jurisdiction for }A_{g(n)}.
]

Formalize the smallest object needed to represent this.

Possible approaches include:

* jurisdiction-mode variable;
* authorization-source index;
* execution-interface parameter;
* reachable-effect correspondence;
* controlled capability map.

Do not invent normative `HasRight` predicates unless necessary.

The object should represent operational jurisdiction, not moral legitimacy.

Required properties:

1. prediction of authorization is not authorization;
2. genuine jurisdiction is not behaviorally inferred;
3. the same object-level action can arise under different jurisdiction assignments;
4. transfer at (n) determines the later authorization source;
5. the future policy remains well-typed over (\Pi_n\sqcup{\bot});
6. transfer does not itself choose the later object-level intervention.

Determine whether FUD should continue to be called an ordinary policy:

[
\pi^{FU,g}
]

or whether it is more honestly a policy-plus-jurisdiction-assignment object.

If the latter, preserve the old notation only as shorthand.
