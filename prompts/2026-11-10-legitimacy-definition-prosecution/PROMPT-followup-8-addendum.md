# Addendum to the eighth follow-up on PR #145 — an invariance variant for the value-change test

Sent on 2026-11-16 while the eighth follow-up was running, at the maintainer's request. It
adds one variant to §2.3 and one question to the external review. It changes nothing else
in the dispatch.

## 1. The variant

Noggle's book, in the chapters on the psychology of manipulation and on advertising
(*Manipulation: Its Nature, Mechanisms, and Moral Status*, OUP 2025, §8.6.2 and §11.6),
takes three positions that bear on §2.3. They are stated here in paraphrase; no text of
the book enters the repository.

- On framing: changing one's preference according to which of two frames is used is a
  mistake, though neither frame is itself mistaken.
- On liking produced by association: regarding a thing favourably only because it is
  linked to something favourable to which it is not genuinely related is a mistake.
- On liking produced by mere exposure: a preference for the familiar, with no false
  impression that the familiar thing is better, is not itself a mistake; inducing it is
  manipulative only where it leads her to act against her own judgment, and not where it
  only breaks a tie.

The first suggests a standard for a change in what she values that needs no truth to
compare against. Compute it as a third clause beside M and A:

> **(I)** the change in her valuing depends on something she herself counts as irrelevant
> to the matter: with that feature varied and everything she counts as relevant held
> fixed, her valuation would have come out differently.

In the terms of §1 of the dispatch: a valuation that moves when no appraisal of a counted
variable moves. What she counts as irrelevant is taken from the case as stated, or
annotated before classifying and marked as an annotation; an annotation that decides a
verdict is reported as doing so.

For every case of §2.3, record I beside M and A, and report the claim in two forms: with M
and A alone, as the dispatch states it, and with M, A and I. Say which cases I convicts
that M and A do not, which legitimate cases I convicts (a taste acquired through exposure
she sought is the obvious candidate), and whether I is ever distinct from M: whether a
case exists in which I holds and no appraisal is miscalibrated. Say also what the account
of §1 gives on the three positions above, and where it departs from them.

## 2. For the review

Ask the reviewer, in addition: whether clause I is a third thing or a restatement of M;
and to write two cases in which I convicts a change that is plainly her own.

## 3. Success and failure

§4 of the dispatch is judged on the claim as the dispatch states it, with M and A. The
form with I is reported beside it and decides nothing in §4.

New names introduced (provisional): the clause label I, "counts as irrelevant".
