## Stage two

1. **The core finite mathematics survives, but several examples and bridge claims need correction.** I ran the checks using Python 3.13.13, read the relevant Lean statements, and opened `EXECUTOR-JUDGMENTS.md` only after recording my judgments above; no files were written, and I did not independently build Lean.

2. **Result 3 is correct for nonempty finite move sets.** The convex-combination direction and strict-separation converse are sound, and the checker obtains exact certificates: 179 pairs satisfy hull inclusion and 121 have a separating payoff. The prose should explicitly require nonempty sets where it uses maxima; its aside about infinitely many moves also needs “supremum” rather than “maximum,” unless attainment is assumed.

3. **Result 5’s two displayed guarantees are correct under the adopted reading.** The proof correctly combines the conditional-utility regret bound, the decomposition into \(M+W\), and the comparison with the best unconditional reference move. The checker tests the first form on 982 qualifying pairs and the second on 376, with appropriate exact arithmetic; assigning zero reference miscalibration to newly opened moves does establish \(\bar M_\sigma\leq\bar M_\rho\).

4. **The published conditional-reading example does not refute the prompt’s displayed guarantee.** With the same policy on both sides, its unconditional reference benchmark is 1, so attainment \(19/10\) satisfies the original bound; the checker instead substitutes the conditional benchmark 10. The ambiguity is real and the claimed failure is repairable: let σ offer a perfectly observed gamble paying 10 with probability \(1/10\), otherwise zero, plus a zero move, while ρ offers a sure 5; conditional options compares 10 with 5, but σ attains 1 with \(M=W=R=0\), violating the original unconditional benchmark 5.

5. **The repair to result 1 is real and correctly proved.** Nonspanning appraisals leave the utility *function* unidentified, but leave the random variable \(f(X)\) unidentified only if an occurring point mass lies outside their linear span. In the named example every admissible perturbation vanishes on the sole occurring state, so the prompt’s unqualified “different true utility” is false when “true utility” means \(U_a=f(X,a)\).

6. **The claim about identification from choices alone is overstated.** The positive-weight transformation proves an observational ambiguity, not an exhaustive characterization of all ambiguities; adding a move-independent function of the facts to every utility also preserves choices. Replace “fixed only up to a positive weight” with “not fixed; in particular, these positive-weight transformations preserve choices.”

7. **Result 8 supplies the requested example, but not the stronger non-implication cited in `ACCOUNT.md`.** Its calculations are correct: against idle only options fails; against the role policy only accuracy fails; attainment is \(1/2\), below 2 and \(5/8\). 8. **Result 9’s identity, signs, conditional implication, and two numerical examples are correct.** The checks cover 1,200 laws and 95 qualifying pairs, and correctly distinguish knowing less from using less information. Nevertheless, `conditions()` tests only narrow condition 1, and these examples do not encode protected features or the value-change trigger; they establish the quantitative limitation directly, while a claim about a complete, triggered *wide* value-side case requires an explicit extension.

9. **Bridge §1 correctly distinguishes policy properties from properties of an unannotated realized trajectory.** However, the enriched history must identify the relevant laws and protected-feature maps, either directly or through a fixed surrounding model; `Decision.hist a ω` does not itself guarantee that its output identifies \(a\). Legitimacy is constant across ω for the fixed single-decision comparison described, but that need not remain true for histories containing different subsequent decision moments.

10. **Bridge §1 does not establish an always-available authority-respecting alternative.** Idle satisfies the proposed predicate when idle exists, but this does not establish availability, zero violations, or the adviser’s estimate range, and §11 expressly includes settings without idle. The sentence claiming that an authority-respecting alternative is therefore always available conflicts with §2’s correct admission that `astar_avail`, `astar_range`, and `Respects` are not derived.

11. **Every displayed inequality in Bridge §2 is mathematically correct under its stated single-reference hypotheses.** Mean inflation follows from conditional expectation and Cauchy–Schwarz; the threshold inequality follows by separating the nonnegative \((\hat u_a-\theta)\) contribution on the threshold event from calibration error; the approximate guarantee correctly incurs two additive ε terms and \(|A_\sigma|\epsilon\) inside the square root. These conclusions require an actual witnessing reference, so they do not follow merely from vacuous legitimacy at a moment where the value-change question does not arise.

12. **Bridge §2 correctly identifies the selected-verdict gap.** The bound \(\lvert E[\hat u_c-U_c]\rvert\leq\sqrt{\bar M+\bar W}\) is valid, and the example with zero minimal miscalibration and zero regret but inflation \(1/4\) is genuine. The threshold inequality at \(M=0\) matches `totalTrust_of_correct`’s conclusion for one quantity, not its stronger hypothesis of a correct entire credence map; `UnbiasedFromFeedback` concerns the adviser’s estimates and is indeed independent of these principal-side bounds.

13. **Bridge §3’s account of `Correct` is correct only after making the spaces coincide.** On the joint world-record probability space, full calibration for every world quantity—indicators suffice—is equivalent to equality with the posterior on positive-mass records. If the account’s “facts” are only a coarse projection of Lean’s worlds, calibration for every quantity on those facts establishes only the corresponding marginal correctness.

14. **Bridge §3’s Blackwell converse needs additional hypotheses.** `Sufficient` requires a garbling identity at every world, whereas informed value comparisons under a fixed prior ignore zero-probability worlds; for example, under a point-mass prior an uninformative experiment matches a revealing experiment on every decision problem, although the latter need not be its garbling. Require full support, or restrict the conclusion to prior support, and quantify over sufficiently rich decision problems rather than merely every payoff on an arbitrary fixed move set.

15. **Bridge §3’s description of `Value` is useful as an analogy between conclusions, not as an identification with zero-error hypotheses.** Lean’s `Value` quantifies over every finite decision problem and every best response, comparing it with every reference rule. It does not assume \(M=W=0\): the supplied `value_not_correct` theorem expressly witnesses `Value` without `Correct`, so “value is the guarantee without slack” must not imply equivalence with the sufficient assumptions used to obtain that guarantee.

16. **Bridge §4 conflates two distinct control surfaces.** Its description of `EffRealizes` and charged `CS` is accurate, but `ForecloseAt` ends with `Kphys`, which allows a final uncharged correction; `forecloseAt_iff_shortfall` identifies it with `CSfree`, not `CS`. The deterministic convex-hull correspondence therefore needs matched costs, admissibility, windows, and terminal-response conventions, and concerns only the protected-feature component of condition 1—not its additional \(f_t\) comparison.

17. **Bridge §4’s stochastic comparison is not established by the Lean definitions.** `CS` is defined along a fixed exterior path \(z\), without probabilities, whereas `surface_facts()` treats positive probability as reachability. A recovery that succeeds half the time need not belong to `CS` on an exterior path where it fails; the claimed strictness requires an explicit probabilistic embedding and quantifier convention.
