Accept the §XVII stop-condition verdict.

### Decision 1 — retire PC-1 through PC-4

Treat Track I's result as closing the PC-1…PC-4 statement shape for the main credence-uniform theorem.

Do not attempt to rescue those assumptions by tuning constants or by replacing pointwise regret with Cesàro/selector-relative regret. Record the point-mass dependence of the equivalence explicitly, but do not restrict the admissible credence class away from point masses merely to evade the result. Such a restriction would require independent motivation, especially because the eventual FUD theorem is intended to remain meaningful as the agent becomes highly informed.

The architectural lesson is:

[
\boxed{\text{choice/regret-level competence is circular for the instrumental delegation theorem.}}
]

Move competence into genuinely richer vocabulary, provisionally along Track I's cardinal grade-based direction. Preserve the strongest non-circular decisiveness-gated calibration candidate Track I found, together with its unbounded near-indifference leakage term. Do not canonize it until the Phase-II synthesis compares it with the downstream theorem.

Also record as a standing constraint:

[
\boxed{\text{principal competence conditions instrumental endorsement, not principal authority.}}
]

An inability to prove that following (H^+) is instrumentally good must never disable the protected correction channel or make human authority conditional on (A)'s competence assessment.

### Decision 2 — primary certificate route: calibrated self-estimate of error

Given Track H's impossibility of forcing global magnitude error to zero, do not add an explicit principal-predictability assumption as the main repair.

Reorient the certificate engine from **magnitude control** to **selective validity of low-error self-assessment**.

Let (e_n) denote the eventual certificate-relevant prediction-error statistic and let (q_n) be an (A)-priced contract/estimate settling to (e_n). Investigate a gate of the form

[
G_n = \mathbf 1{q_n\le\tau}
]

(or the weakest soft/lagged admissible analogue).

The target theorem shape is:

[
\frac{\sum_nG_n(e_n-q_n)}
{\sum_nG_n}
\to0
]

and hence, on opened gates,

[
\frac{\sum_nG_ne_n}
{\sum_nG_n}
\le\tau+o(1).
]

Interpretation: LIC need not make the principal predictable. It should make the AI's claims that its prediction error is low statistically trustworthy on the class where those claims license autonomous discretion.

This is measurement-and-gating, not error elimination.

Before using this theorem shape, explicitly verify:

1. that the magnitude/error contract is expressible and settles legally;
2. that the low-(q_n) selector is admissible and does not recreate the quote-responsive diagonal;
3. whether a soft or delayed gate is needed;
4. efficient computability in the pinned FAF model;
5. whether the guarantee is average-on-gated-cases, selector-relative, or stronger — do not silently promote it to pointwise accuracy.

### Role of Track H's other two options

**Self-measured indecision:** retain as the canonical special case and potentially the simplest Lean theorem. H's exact squared-error decomposition may give a particularly clean gate directly from (A)'s own prices. Compare it to the calibrated-error gate and determine whether it is sufficient for the actual multi-intervention certificate statistic. Do not discard it.

**Explicit principal-predictability assumption:** retain only as a baseline/conditional corollary unless later mathematics proves it unavoidable. Do not make it the conceptual engine.

### Consequence for Track L

If Track L can still be steered without invalidating its independence, ask it to distinguish:

[
\text{certificate proves agreement}
]

from the now-preferred

[
\boxed{\text{certificate licenses autonomy when self-assessed epistemic risk is low}.}
]

The protected authority layer supplies categorical fail-closed behavior; the certificate supplies only quantitative permission for autonomous action.

Let Track L report an incompatibility rather than forcing the old certificate theorem into this interpretation.

Continue Phase II under these decisions and preserve Track I's stop-condition result as a headline negative result.
