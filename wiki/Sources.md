# Sources

These works supply inspiration, mathematical tools, or nearby prior art. They do not establish this program's claims unless a page says exactly what is inherited.

## Normative pragmatics and dialogue

- Robert B. Brandom. *Making It Explicit: Reasoning, Representing, and Discursive Commitment*. Harvard University Press, 1994. [Author-hosted Part One](https://sites.pitt.edu/~rbrandom/Courses/2022%20Phil%20of%20Language/Reasons%20texts/Brandom%20MIE%20Part%20One.pdf). Used for commitment/entitlement, perspectival scorekeeping, and default-and-challenge structure.
- Paul Lorenzen and Kuno Lorenz. *Dialogische Logik*. Wissenschaftliche Buchgesellschaft, 1978. [Bibliographic record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/4666882). Nearby dialogue-logic background; not a source of the program's scorekeeping construction.
- John Martin Fischer and Mark Ravizza. *Responsibility and Control: A Theory of Moral Responsibility*. Cambridge University Press, 1998. [Publisher page and DOI](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/responsibility-and-control/54D0EB8AEDEF4D5F4930D691EC214E01). Background for reasons-responsive mechanisms.

## Learning and bounded reasoning

- Scott Garrabrant, Tsvi Benson-Tilsen, Andrew Critch, Nate Soares, and Jessica Taylor. “Logical Induction.” 2016. [arXiv:1609.03543](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03543). Source for logical inductors, markets, traders, and self-trust results used by the deference line.
- Kevin Dorst, Benjamin A. Levinstein, Bernhard Salow, Brooke E. Husic, and Branden Fitelson. “Deference Done Better.” *Philosophical Perspectives* 35, no. 1 (2021): 99–150. [Publisher page and DOI](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/phpe.12156). Source for the decision-based account of epistemic deference and the Total Trust/Value target inherited by the deference line.
- Avrim Blum and Yishay Mansour. “From External to Internal Regret.” *Journal of Machine Learning Research* 8 (2007): 1307–1324. [Official JMLR page](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v8/blum07a.html). Source for the modification-regret reduction used by the finite learning interface.
- Yossi Azar, Ashish Chiplunkar, Shay Kutten, and Noam Touitou. “Set Cover with Delay—Clairvoyance Is Not Required.” *LIPIcs* 173 (ESA 2020), Article 8. [Published paper and DOI](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2020.8). Supplies the repeated-purchase request, action-cost, and delay scheduler class for the restricted dynamic inquiry embedding; it supplies no normative or certified-service semantics.
- Sungjin Im, Viswanath Nagarajan, and Ruben van der Zwaan. “Minimum Latency Submodular Cover.” *ACM Transactions on Algorithms* 13, no. 1 (2016), Article 13. [Publisher page and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2987751). Supplies the metric path, multiple submodular objectives, and sum of cover times for fixed dockets.
- Daniel Golovin and Andreas Krause. “Adaptive Submodularity: Theory and Applications in Active Learning and Stochastic Optimization.” *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research* 42 (2011): 427–486. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.3967). Supplies adaptive coverage, self-certification, and diminishing-returns tractability conditions under a fixed realization model and prior; adaptive submodularity is not treated as legitimacy.
- Andrew Guillory and Jeff Bilmes. “Interactive Submodular Set Cover.” In *Proceedings of ICML 2010*, 415–422. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.3345). Supplies a worst-case finite query/response coverage class with pointwise-submodular objectives; it does not validate the hypothesis class or service objective.

## Reason maintenance and default reasons

- Jon Doyle. “A Truth Maintenance System.” *Artificial Intelligence* 12 (1979): 231–272. [Publisher page and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(79)90008-0). Supplies the justification/node architecture, in/out statuses, non-monotonic justifications, and dependency-directed backtracking that the reason-state work compares against; its conflation of dependency recording with belief choice is what that work rejects.
- Johan de Kleer. “An Assumption-based TMS.” *Artificial Intelligence* 28 (1986): 127–162. [Publisher page and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(86)90080-9). Supplies assumption environments, labels over multiple simultaneous contexts, and nogood recording, read here as cached hypothetical-support queries under a fixed incompatibility structure.
- John F. Horty. *Reasons as Defaults*. Draft #2, August 16, 2006. [Author page](https://www.umiacs.umd.edu/users/horty). Supplies triggered/conflicted/defeated defaults, reasons as premises of triggered defaults, and variable-priority default theories with priorities in the object language; its proper-scenario theory occupies the stance-selection seat this program assigns to the normative learner.

## Imprecise probability and argumentation

- Peter M. Williams. “Notes on Conditional Previsions.” Technical report, University of Sussex, 1975; revised in *International Journal of Approximate Reasoning* 44, no. 3 (2007): 366–383. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijar.2006.07.019). Background for coherent conditional previsions and desirable-gamble readings.
- Peter Walley. *Statistical Reasoning with Imprecise Probabilities*. Chapman & Hall, 1991. [Publisher series catalog](https://www.routledge.com/rsc/downloads/K16386_FebNewsletter_ch1.pdf). Background for lower previsions and imprecise probability.
- Henry Prakken. “Probabilistic Strength of Arguments with Structure.” In *Proceedings of the Sixteenth International Conference on Principles of Knowledge Representation and Reasoning*, 158–167. AAAI Press, 2018. [Author paper](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~prakk101/pubs/probarg3.pdf). Nearby prior art for structured probabilistic support and attack.
- Anthony Hunter, Sylwia Polberg, and Matthias Thimm. “Epistemic Graphs for Representing and Reasoning with Positive and Negative Influences of Arguments.” *Artificial Intelligence* 281 (2020): 103236. [arXiv:1802.07489](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07489). Nearby prior art for graded epistemic support and attack graphs.
