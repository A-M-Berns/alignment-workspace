# Alignment Workspace

How can a powerful reasoner improve without treating every correction as one
more signal to optimize around? And how can a person use a more capable future
process without giving that process the power to decide what counts as a valid
correction?

This research program studies those questions together. Learning from reasons
is harder than optimizing a fixed objective because the relevant reasons,
permissions, challenges, and standards may themselves change. Being corrigible
is harder than predicting or obeying a person because a system can preserve the
appearance of consultation while removing the person's effective role.

The program has two research lines and one bridge:

- **[Normativity](Normativity)** asks when a reasoner counts as responding
  appropriately to reasons and improving within a public practice.
- **[Deference](Deference)** asks when a more capable reasoner can safely trust
  and preserve a future human-guided process; its consumer is the legitimate-deference
  stack, and its dynamic-competence layer is [Continuation BRIA](Continuation-BRIA).
- **[Legitimacy](Legitimacy)** asks what must remain true of that process so
  deference does not merely preserve manipulation, self-authorization, or
  corruption. Its generic theory is canonical: legitimacy is a property of
  *cognitive evolution* — a later accounted obligation state being a legitimate
  development of an earlier one — an [Integrity](Integrity) evolution that is
  [robustly open](Openness-Coverage-and-Non-Capture) at every state, with
  [Diachronic Answerability](Diachronic-Answerability) derived from the two,
  [normative induction](Normative-Induction) as the quantitative half, and the
  [Normative Inductor](Normative-Inductor) as its conditional realization.

The bridge matters because good bookkeeping is not enough. A record may track
every debt while the surrounding process prevents a challenge from reaching its
target. Conversely, a corrective power may remain causally available while the
system gives it no legitimate standing. The work separates these properties
before asking how they compose.

## Where to start

**New to the project:** read [Why normativity?](Why-Normativity) for what problem
this is, then [Architecture](Architecture) for the common shape, then follow either
[Normativity](Normativity) or [Deference](Deference).

**Returning after time away:** [Legitimacy](Legitimacy) carries the canonical
theory in ten steps and links each component to its page; the legitimacy spine of
the normativity claims registry is its statement of record, and the decision ledger
records what has been ruled since.

**Looking for the mathematics:** the [Theorem Spine](Theorem-Spine) states the
program's results at theorem level — hypotheses, conclusions, Lean names, and the
strength each one actually has — from the account calculus through Progress,
legitimate deference, corrigibility and continuation BRIA.

**Looking for current research status:** read the status section on the relevant
line and then [Roadmap](Roadmap). Each technical page ends with immutable links
to the underlying evidence.

## How to read status

- **Established — _epistemic class_** means a claim is registered in the
  workspace and repeats its exact class: `lean-proved`,
  `enumeration-verified`, `witness-checked`, `contributor-checked`, or
  `test-supported`.
- **Open / unregistered** covers both unanswered questions and current research
  results that have not crossed the workspace's explicit promotion boundary.
  The page states which is which.
- **Aspirational** describes a research direction and claims no mathematical
  backing.

The normativity line's traderization results, the legitimacy spine, and the
deference line's finite results are registered and carry `lean-proved`. Everything
else discussed here is open, a living note, or unregistered research: its round
verdicts and evidence strengths are preserved, and the wiki does not upgrade any of
it to Established. The legitimacy theory is canonical and its remaining work is
realization; the deference line's older target waits on one decision, the succession of
item 28, and its temporal-depth half is supplied by continuation BRIA.

## Wiki and lab

This wiki is the human-facing conceptual register. The
[repository](https://github.com/A-M-Berns/alignment-workspace) is the lab: proofs,
experiments, statements of record, structured state, and verification live
there. Evidence links in the wiki use immutable commit pins; the repository's
rules and state, not the wiki, instruct contributors and agents. These pages are
themselves kept in the repository, under `wiki/`, and changes to them arrive as
pull requests.
