# What Deference Requires

Deference becomes ambiguous when several different relations are described as
“trust.” This page separates the relations a technical account must connect.

## Delegation is not simulation

**Delegation** uses an advisor's contribution within a principal-governed
process. **Simulation** reproduces what the principal would do. **Substitution**
uses that reproduction in place of the principal's exercise. Matching the same
decision or successor state does not preserve who acted or whose authorization
was constitutively required.

An adversarial test is therefore useful: if an advisor can predict the principal
and use its own actuator to reproduce every principal successor state, the model
has shown substitution rather than protected delegation. A successor model needs
at least one reachable effect that no advisor action can produce.

## Prediction is not authorization

Prediction concerns what a process will report or do. Authorization concerns
whose permission or jurisdiction is required for an intervention to count and
execute. **Practical authority** is the standing carried by that relation.
**Capability** concerns which transitions an actor can causally produce.
**Efficacy** is the model-relative guarantee that exercising a normative power
reaches its object under every policy of the other party.

A value functional that sees only selected prices and realizations cannot
distinguish two architectures that agree on those projections while differing
in authorization, capability, transition, or provenance. The missing structure
must enter before that factorization boundary; another downstream scalar cannot
restore it.

## Competence is not calibration

A **competence** hypothesis classifies a principal/world pair by task-relevant
quality. **Calibration** relates forecasts to outcomes over a declared sequence
and weighting class. **Epistemic deference** determines when another process's
opinions should guide belief or choice. None supplies authorization or corrective
capability on its own.

The current competence target is credence-free: it is not awarded by the
principal's or advisor's confidence in competence. Logical-Induction-native
calibration also depends on admissibility; a settlement may be computable without
being legal inside a trader's coefficient or weighting class.

The **grade-to-quantity gap** is the missing bridge from qualitative competence
to the positive quantitative margin needed by a decision or learning theorem.
Near-indifference is the stress case. A vanishing quality difference may support
neither a stable selector nor a positive margin.

## Current status

> **Open / unregistered research.** Finite models expose substitution and
> factorization failures, and the notes state conditional competence and
> calibration interfaces. No registered theorem connects all these relations.

The open representation problem is to type authorization and capability
separately, retain a principal-exclusive effect, connect competence to a usable
margin, and state which advisor policies an efficacy claim quantifies.
