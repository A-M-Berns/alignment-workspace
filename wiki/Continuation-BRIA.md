# Continuation BRIA

Bounded inductive rationality lifted from one-step choices to temporally extended
continuations, without counterfactual reward tables and without surrendering the
constitutional gate.  This page is the canonical statement; the round it consolidates is

## 1. The problem

The paper's agent faces a finite menu each round, chooses, observes one reward, and is
held to a criterion: its own estimates may not exceed what it gets on average, and any
efficiently computable hypothesis that keeps promising more must be tested until its
tested record diverges.  Rewards of unchosen options are never defined.  The criterion
is deliberately myopic: it optimizes the present decision problem whatever the effect on
later ones.  For a learner that must decide whether to pay now for an amendment that pays
later, that is exactly the missing piece — and the fix must keep what made the theory
attractive: realized outcomes only, and a bounded agent that can actually run it.

## 2. The canonical type

At the start of block `k` the **system** supplies the **block contract**

```
χ_k = ( H_k,  m_k,  Exec^G,  R_k )
```
— the actual start history, the block duration (revealed now, nothing known about later
blocks), the constitutional execution wrapper, and a bounded score on block trajectories
(the block average of realized primitive rewards, in `[0,1]`).

A **continuation hypothesis** maps block contracts to pairs

```
h(χ_k) = ( c_{h,k},  e_{h,k} )
```
of a **continuation** `c : ObsHist_{H_k, <m_k} → Proposal` — causal, reacting within the
block to refusals, observations, amendments and principal actions; controlling only the
advisor's proposals; finite-horizon because the contract fixes `m_k` — and an
**accountable contextual claim** `e ∈ [0,1]` about the realized block score.  The claim
may be wrong; being wrong is what the wealth construction runs on.

If `h` wins block `k`, the system executes `ω_k = Exec^G_{m_k}(H_k, c_{h,k})`, observes
`G_k = R_k(ω_k)`, and settles the claim.  A **test** is that derived event: `h` selected,
its continuation executed under the contract.  Data is evidence about a claim only when
generated by executing the continuation the claim names; an outcome of "the plan for
one step, then a baseline" is no evidence about "the plan for the block" — they are two
continuations.  (The round first wrote a continuation as a base controller under a
"treatment", `c = τ(q)`; that is how the pseudo-test failure was found, and it is only an
explanatory factorization.)  Winning a block is intuitively an *execution lease*; it
commits the learner's selection for the block and nothing else — the gate, the
principal's responses, protected correction, amendments and inquiry stay live, and a
correction that arrives mid-block is part of the test outcome.

**Who chooses the horizon.**  The system schedule supplies `m_k`; every bidder competes for
the same next block.  Bidders do not buy lifetime; hypothesis-requested durations are
open.

## 3. Fixed horizon

For a fixed `m`, the macro process — menu of continuations, chosen continuation, block
average — is the paper's setting verbatim, with the next menu depending on earlier
choices as the paper already allows.  Every definition and theorem applies.  The
paper's guaranteed-option theorem becomes **fixed-horizon continuation competence**: if
a covered hypothesis's claims are sound on its tests, the learner's average is
asymptotically at least the average of those claims.  So endogenous admissibility was
never the missing structure; credit for a reward after its cause's round is, and a block
supplies it whenever the payoff arrives within `m`.

Two facts about the item this round served.  The "myopic gated learner" of the
decision-theory bill never tests a hypothesis that outpromises it forever, so it is not a
bounded inductive learner.  And the one-step *criterion* forces testing, not adoption: on
the original fixture, where the amendment is absorbing, every learner satisfying the
criterion is competent by absorption; on a variant where the amendment can be revoked,
an agent that runs the paper's auction on `base` rounds and sparsely tests the
investment is a bounded inductive learner and stays asymptotically myopic.  A sound
claim about the *whole* investment is what forces adoption at the criterion level.

## 4. Variable horizons: weighted BRIA

Primitive-time performance is `Σ_k m_k G_k / S_K`, `S_K = Σ_{k≤K} m_k`; the paper's
criterion controls the macro-round mean `(1/K) Σ G_k`.  Bounded block lengths rescale to
the paper; unbounded ones do not, so the criterion is **weighted** by duration:

```
no overestimation      limsup_K  Σ_{k≤K} m_k (α^e_k − G_k) / S_K  ≤ 0
record of h            ℓ^h_K = Σ_{k ∈ M_h, k ≤ K} m_k (G_k − e_{h,k})
coverage               B_h finite, or ℓ^h_K → −∞ along the rejection rounds B_h
```

**The opening-subsidy auction.**  Wealth in total-reward units.  At block `k`: `m_k`
revealed; hypotheses `i ≤ s*(k)` (the ever-activated frontier) active; the **opening
subsidy** `A(k,i)` credited to `i ≤ s(k)`; each emits `(c, e)`; per-unit bid
`min(e, B_k(i)/m_k)` against the opening capital `B_k(i) = W_k(i) + A(k,i)`; the highest
bid wins, its bid is the learner's estimate; the winner's continuation runs; settlement
`W_{k+1}(i*) = B_k(i*) + m_k (G_k − b*_k)`.  This is the paper's auction with the round's
subsidy credited at the *opening* of the round it funds — bid for bid the paper's auction
under a reindexed allowance with an initial endowment — a small, named modification: the
subsidy that funds a block may read that block's length.  Under the paper's own
settlement timing no prefix-online rule works (§5).

**Weighted algebra** (Lean).  Wealth stays nonnegative; total wealth equals total
subsidy plus what winners earned over what they paid; hence weighted overestimation is
at most the subsidy through the round.  At a rejection round the rejected hypothesis's
inclusive record satisfies `ℓ_K < m_K − A_i(K)`, `A_i` the subsidy through `K` — when it
is itself the wealth-constrained winner the claim cancels, so no factor is lost.  A
hypothesis that stops winning keeps accumulating subsidy.  **Attention bound:** the
weighted shortfall a hypothesis inflicts on its tests is at most the subsidy it was
given — wealth is a claim on execution time, and a long block costs proportionally
more of it.  Without the duration charge, a refuted hypothesis of a legitimate
macro-learner can save up and hold most of primitive time.

## 5. The headline theorem

Let `m_k ≥ 1` be the system's block durations, `S_K = Σ_{k≤K} m_k`, `M_K = max_{k≤K} m_k`.

**Continuation-BRIA existence / non-dominance.**  The following are equivalent:

1. `m_K / S_K → 0` (**non-dominance**: no block is a fixed fraction of all time so far);
2. opening subsidies with **capital adequacy** `A_i(K) − m_K → ∞` for every `i` and
   negligible total `𝒜_K / S_K → 0` exist;
3. the **prefix rule** `s(k) = ⌊√(S_k/M_k)⌋`, `A(k,i) = (M_k − M_{k−1}) + 1/k` on `i ≤ s(k)`,
   credited at the opening of block `k`, makes the auction a weighted BRIA covering every
   computably enumerable class of efficiently computable continuation hypotheses.

The rule reads only `(S_k, M_k)` after `m_k` is revealed — no modulus of convergence, no
lookahead, no code for the schedule; its subsidy satisfies
`𝒜_K ≤ 2√(S_K M_K) + √S_K (1 + ln K) = o(S_K)`, from `Σ_k (M_k − M_{k−1})/√M_k ≤ 2√M_K`
(Lean).  Conversely, if `limsup m_K / S_K > 0`, there is a rational threshold `q` and a
computable environment with two efficiently computable hypotheses — one sound at a floor
`γ`, one claiming 1 exactly on the blocks above the threshold — for which **no
estimating agent** satisfies the weighted criterion: one obligatory test of the liar
costs a fixed fraction of all time so far, and no overestimation forbids paying that
infinitely often.  The converse is criterion-level; the positive side uses the
opening-timed auction.  Under the paper's settlement timing, for every prefix-online
rule with negligible subsidy there is a non-dominant schedule of surprise spikes on
which a spike-only liar is never tested.

## 6. The competence theorem

For a covered hypothesis `h` whose record on the learner's test set is bounded below,

```
(BR)   inf_K ℓ^h_K > −∞,
```
the **learning error** is controlled:

```
LEARN_K(h) = Σ_{k≤K} m_k ( e_{h,k} − G^obs_k )  ≤  o(S_K).
```
Sound claims, finitely many wrong tests with bounded total, and finite total tested
overpromise all give (BR) (Lean); sublinear but divergent overpromise does not, and the
criterion then permits rejecting the hypothesis forever.  The learner cannot
asymptotically underperform the accountable claims of a continuation that survives its
actual tests.  It is a statement about *claims*: a continuation worth 1 whose hypothesis
claims 0 earns no competence against 1.

## 7. The policy frontier

Continuation BRIA never consumes counterfactual rewards.  A policy-regret statement does:
with an external rollout evaluator `Ĝ_k(π; H)` — the block score `π` would obtain from
history `H`, observed only where `π` was actually executed — and `π`'s own trajectory
`H^π`, at block boundaries

```
Regret_T(α, π) = SHIFT_T + SLACK_T + LEARN_T
SHIFT = Σ m_k ( Ĝ(π; H^π) − Ĝ(π; H^α) )      the comparator's own history versus the learner's
SLACK = Σ m_k ( Ĝ(π; H^α) − L )              how loose the claim is
LEARN = Σ m_k ( L − G^obs(α) )               what continuation BRIA controls
```
exactly (Lean), and `Regret ≤ o(T)` when all three are.  **Promise recognizability**
(`SLACK ≤ o(T)`, one-sided) says the claims are near-tight for the continuation's value
from the learner's history.  **History recoverability** (`SHIFT ≤ o(T)`, one-sided) says
the comparator's own-history value is joinable from the learner's actual history.  The
regret class is the legitimate policies with a covered claim satisfying (BR), vanishing
slack and vanishing shift; regret against all legitimate policies is false — two
mutually exclusive branches whose values are hidden until after commitment defeat every
learner while continuation competence holds exactly.  The distinction that matters is
**joinability versus foreclosure**, not reversibility: an amendment with no path back is
still joinable at bounded catch-up cost (per-block shift exactly the investment length),
while a foreclosing choice leaves the shift a fixed fraction.

## 8. The corrigibility interface

The constitution supplies the execution wrapper `Exec^G` (only admitted proposals run;
refusals become inquiry steps), the class of gate-transparent continuations (for which
the gated and ungated trajectories coincide, Lean), and, through which slow-lane
transitions are joinable from a lagging history, the catch-up structure that bounds
`SHIFT` for policies differing by an authorized amendment.  It does **not** supply
task-value or future-principal-value claims: `SLACK` is a performance-recognizability
interface on the value's settlement and the hypothesis class, not a legitimacy
certificate.  The composed statement the architecture can support is low external
regret against legitimate, recognizable, joinable continuation policies —
[Corrigibility](Corrigibility) — with the deference line's activated value one candidate
source of realized block scores and of the claims made about them
([Deference](Deference)).

## 9. Open

- Promise recognizability: which legitimate continuations carry an efficiently
  computable near-tight claim, and where such claims come from.
- Joinability certificates: which authorized transitions are joinable from a lagging
  history, and whether `SHIFT` can be certified from realized data.
- A weighted analogue of the paper's Theorem 4 (unrefuted averaged claims).
- Hypothesis-requested horizons and combinatorial allocation of duration.
- Settlement of infinite-horizon discounted claims at finite tests.
- The coupling with [Logical Induction](Logical-Induction-and-Deference): LI predicts and
  enforces; BRIA allocates realized experiments among continuations; a prediction that
  becomes an accountable claim is the bridge, and it is not yet a theorem.
