# Legitimacy

Legitimacy is the bridge between [Normativity](Normativity) and
[Deference](Deference). Normative learning needs public reasons and responses
that the learner cannot rewrite for convenience. Corrigible deference needs a
human corrective power that the advisor cannot quietly preempt. Both therefore
depend on who may alter the process, what the record preserves, and whether a
normative demand actually reaches its object.

## Three properties, not one

The program reserves three names for independent properties:

- **Answerability** is owing an answer. Another participant with standing
  attributes consequences under their practice and can raise a challenge one
  owes an answer to.
- **Auditability** is the record knowing what became of every debt. Each
  liability has one record-computable fate: discharged, mooted by authorized
  revision, suspended, or open and charging.
- **Efficacy** is model-relative causal access: exercising a normative power
  reaches its object under every policy of the other party.

Efficacy is a transition-system property, not a score. The current work names
it and supplies thin finite evidence; it does not provide a general analysis.
An **audit discontinuity** occurs when a liability stated in retired vocabulary
loses its identity. The missing transport object belongs to auditability.

In short: answerability is owing an answer; auditability is the record knowing
what became of every debt; efficacy is the answer-demand actually reaching you.
Response learning uses the first two. Corrigibility uses the first and third.

## Shared relational representation

The bridge fixture uses public acknowledgments, participant-indexed inferential
practices, scoped grants, challenges, exposures, and suspensions. Its central
score is relational rather than global: one participant evaluates another's
acknowledgments under the evaluator's practice.

That structure blocks unilateral self-release. Changing only one's own practice
does not erase what another scorekeeper attributes. It does not block
coordinated drift: participants may revise the practice together while keeping
the acknowledged history fixed. A legitimate process needs to say which such
changes are authorized and what happens to existing liabilities.

## Write access and laundering

Write access is part of a theorem hypothesis. In the current fixture, the
learner writes its own acknowledgments and answering dispositions. It does not
write the critic's practice, acknowledgments, challenges, or exposures. The
surrounding ecology, not the learner, must determine whether a burden is due and
compile that status prospectively into a theorem-facing selector.

The laundering problem appears when an accounted failure depends on a coordinate
its beneficiary controls. A practical-authority loss term once read a grant that
the learner could award itself; self-granting then lowered the loss without
answering the challenge. The theorem-facing loss excludes standing and grants.

Protection creates the opposite pressure. Reserving a grant coordinate outside
an advisor's write set can protect a principal's corrective capability in a
declared model. The same coordinate can become a laundering channel if the
principal controls a gate used to score its own performance. The positive design
lesson is that protection and performance accounting need distinct write
boundaries.

## Current status

> **Open / unregistered research.** The bridge fixtures provide finite evidence
> for relational scorekeeping, write separation, standing–efficacy independence,
> and a grant invariant. No result from the bridge is registered. Efficacy is
> open beyond the declared finite transition models.

The bridge does not yet provide an end-to-end composition. Open work includes a
transport object for audit discontinuities, a treatment of coordinated practice
revision, and a representation that protects corrective authority without
turning self-awarded permission into a loss-reduction mechanism.
