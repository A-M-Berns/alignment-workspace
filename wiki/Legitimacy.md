# Legitimacy

Legitimacy is the bridge between [Normativity](Normativity) and
[Deference](Deference). Normative learning needs public reasons and responses that
the learner cannot rewrite for convenience. Corrigible deference needs a human
corrective power that the advisor cannot quietly preempt. Both depend on the same
thing: what a process may do to the conditions of its own correction.

**Status: research framing, open / unregistered.** No legitimacy predicate is
registered; the components below have Lean spines, exact fixtures, or paper proofs at
the evidence classes their own pages state. What is settled is the *shape*: which
properties legitimacy decomposes into, what each governs, and which parts the theory
proves versus bills to another theory.

## Legitimacy is a property of evolution, not of a state

The central idea, and the one everything else on these pages depends on:

> Legitimacy is a property of cognitive evolution, not an absolute certification of a
> state from first principles.

We can say that a later cognitive or normative state is a **legitimate evolution** of an
earlier one. We cannot, in general, certify the full goodness of the initial state from
nothing. A trajectory may inherit bad commitments — a wrong norm, a captured
evaluator, an unjust allocation of standing — and the theory does not pretend to detect
that from inside. What it constrains is *how those commitments may subsequently be
revised, challenged, answered, defeated, discharged, or extended*. Improvement is
allowed; erasure is not.

This is not a concession. A theory that demanded a provably good starting point would
apply to nothing, and a theory that certified the current state as good would be the
self-authorization it is meant to rule out.

## The decomposition

> **Legitimate Evolution = Integrity / Answerability + Robust Openness.**

In prose:

> A legitimate cognitive trajectory remains answerable to what has legitimately reached
> it and remains robustly open to what may legitimately reach it.

The two halves close two different escape routes:

> Openness prevents evasion by exclusion. Answerability and Integrity prevent evasion
> by revision.

**[Integrity](Integrity)** is the deeper theory under the history side: a trajectory
cannot falsify or silently rewrite its own normative history. It governs authenticated
births, immutable anchors, provenance and lineage, write permissions, faithful carry
under representation change, replayability, and settlement-backed terminal discharge.
It is largely internal and formal, and it rests on one external hypothesis —
settlement integrity — stated on the [settlement interface](Settlement-Interface)
page.

**[Diachronic Answerability](Diachronic-Answerability)** is the conservation principle
stated over a history with integrity: every incurred obligation is answered,
settlement-discharged, or faithfully carried live, with no fourth silent fate. Defeat
carries an obligation onto the grounds for contesting it; it does not extinguish it.
Answerability is answerability *to someone*: preservation of standing is relative to a
protected principal or a declared relation.

**[Robust Openness](Openness-Coverage-and-Non-Capture)** is coverage — an adequate
route from every applicable concern in a declared scope to represented, accountable
consideration — strengthened by a Non-Capture certificate relative to a declared class
of interventions. The scope and the intervention class are declared by the application;
the certificate is billed to an external theory of counterfactual capture.

Integrity and Non-Capture are deliberately distinct: the first asks whether the process
faithfully preserved what entered it; the second asks whether the process could
improperly control what was able to enter, challenge, or evaluate it. A history can have
perfect integrity and a captured process behind it.

Two things are not conjuncts. **Affordability** is a realizability side condition on
any concrete realization, not a source of legitimacy: read as a conjunct it would imply
that a cheaper norm is more legitimate, which the mathematics itself forbids. And the
structural successor and ancestry machinery of the continuity specification is proof
technology for the answerability invariant, not a public component of legitimacy; a
realization with different bookkeeping owes the same three fates.

## What legitimacy exports, and what it does not

Legitimacy exports a **qualitative obligation process**: obligation identities,
anchored specifications, live status, and authenticated lineage. It does *not*
export numerical importance weights. What an obligation is worth relative to another is
decided downstream, by an evaluation protocol the application declares — see
[Normative induction](Normative-Induction), where Progress is stated relative to that
protocol. Keeping the qualitative status separate from the quantitative score is what
stops a process from grading its own homework.

## The quantitative half

Given the exported obligations, **[Normative induction](Normative-Induction)** asks
whether a process learns from them: whether service against what it owes produces
operative uptake, practical response, and anchored Progress, with a three-term bound
whose terms name the three ways it can fail. The concrete candidate realization is the
**[Normative Inductor](Normative-Inductor)**: an ordinary Logical Inductor with one
additive, bounded-liability enforcement trader against a single compiled joint region,
a representation-stable public defect, and an external decision and value plugin. Its
end-to-end theorem is conditional, and its remaining obligations are listed as
implementation and theorem work.

Within a single settled semantics, the fixed-era pages —
[Actionability](Actionability-and-Normative-Force), [Progress](Progress),
[Liability and affordability](Liability-and-Affordability),
[Serviceability](Serviceability) — give the instances with rates.

## Two bills the theory issues on purpose

Two major problems are intentionally externalized, and this is an architectural virtue
rather than missing work.

**The Non-Capture bill.** A theory of counterfactual or institutional capture must
produce the certificate Robust Openness requires: that coverage, standing and the
efficacy of the corrective channel survive the declared interventions. The generic
legitimacy theory does not choose a universal counterfactual semantics; it states
exactly what the certificate must establish and what follows once it does.

**The counterfactual-value bill.** A theory of policy evaluation must produce the
calibration and response certificate the practical-response interface requires: what
the policies not chosen would have been worth, with ambiguity bounds issued before the
response is observed. The Normative Inductor does not solve counterfactual
identification; it specifies the certificate and proves the regret bound from it.

Both bills can be paid by different theories for different applications without
changing the core.

## Where this is going

The longer-run consumer is [Deference](Deference) and its
[corrigibility](Corrigibility) target. The aim is not merely a learner that follows
norms; it is to prove properties of an agent reasoning about a **legitimate future
cognitive process** — one that will remain answerable to what has reached it and open
to what may. A future deference theorem should be able to consume a certified
legitimate-evolution property, a Normative Progress guarantee, and the decision- and
value-facing outputs of a realization, and conclude that there is reason not to
preempt, disable or replace such a process merely because its later judgment may differ
from the current agent's. That theorem has not been proved. What the present work
supplies is a candidate interface for it that did not previously exist.
