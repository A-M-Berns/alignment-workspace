# Diachronic answerability

**Status: canonical, derived.** Diachronic Answerability is not a third pillar of
legitimacy. It is what a legitimate evolution yields: content conservation from
[Integrity](Integrity) and standing and access from
[Robust Openness](Openness-Coverage-and-Non-Capture). The theorem giving that
decomposition is Established `lean-proved`.

## The problem

A reasoner that improves must be able to change: its rules, its procedures, its
evaluators, its concepts, the very way it represents an unresolved obligation. But a
reasoner that can change anything can change its way out of anything. Revise the rule
under which a criticism was raised, and the criticism evaporates. Rewrite the
representation of a debt, and the debt is no longer legible. Nothing was ever refused;
there is simply nothing left to answer.

So the question is not *what must a reasoner keep believing*. The answer to that is
nothing — a later reasoner may reject an earlier norm, defeat an earlier reason,
replace an evaluator. The question is narrower and structural:

> What must remain invariant under self-revision if revision is not to *erase*
> prior answerability?

## The decomposition

> **Diachronic Answerability to `P` = Answerability Conservation from Integrity +
> standing and access from Robust Openness.**

Precisely: a legitimate segment from `O₀` to `O₁` yields

- **Conservation** between `O₀` and `O₁` — no occurrence disappears, every recorded
  answer or closure receipt persists, live content is transported faithfully, every
  account stays anchored to its own occurrence; and
- **openness at both endpoints** — every live in-scope concern has an adequate route
  and the protected party `P` stands on the carrier of every applicable one, on the
  actual branch and under every declared intervention — and, by the definition of a
  legitimate segment, at every state between.

The first half is a theorem of the Integrity evolution alone. The second cannot be:
the exact counterexample is an evolution that carries an obligation's content
faithfully onto a successor at an anchor where only the resolver stands. Every
Integrity condition holds; the protected party can no longer act. Standing is a
question of who may act, not of what is owed, and it lives in Robust Openness.

## The three fates, and there is no fourth

Every account's leaves are drawn from exactly three fates:

> **answered** — an adequate answer receipt; **closed** — a settlement item available
> through the external boundary together with the internal closure judgment made at
> that prefix; **live** — a port of the current docket.

There is no leaf by which content leaves. What remains owed can never grow — new
obligation enters as a fresh occurrence, never by rewriting an old one — and a
transition replaces exactly the live leaves. The consequence that matters:

> Relabelling cannot discharge a debt. Changing a protocol, ontology, evaluator,
> encoding, or label cannot by itself reduce what is owed. Any decrease appears as an
> authenticated answer receipt or a certified closure.

Answered and closed are distinct fates. Both yield a resolution witness — evidence
that the requirement was legitimately accounted for — and nothing in the theory turns
a closure into an answer.

## Defeat carries; it does not extinguish

The **Defeat Principle**:

> A participant may answer an obligation or contest whether it is owed; only an
> authenticated settlement-backed terminal condition may make an unanswered
> obligation disappear.

In the account, a disposal is the identity local law: the successor demands exactly
the parent's content, and the grounds for contesting it are recorded in the law's
authority. Nothing has been answered and nothing has been closed; the obligation has
moved onto the successor. A contest ends the way any obligation ends, by an answer
receipt or by a closure whose internal judgment certifies that the cited settlement
suffices. The alternative reading — disposal as a terminal exit — was rejected because
it makes the disposition term an unaudited hole in every conservation law, and that
hole is exactly the laundering channel a self-improving reasoner would use.

Which rules of a practice license contesting which debts is licence content, supplied
through the protocol's warrants and not derived here.

## Answerability is answerability to someone

Preservation of standing is relative to a protected principal or a declared relation.
A structural condition quantifying over all coalitions cannot exist — take the
coalition to be everyone — so the openness half names a party, and pays for it by no
longer being purely structural. Whether the canonical predicate may name a party is
a ruling still queued in the decision ledger; the mathematics offers no party-free
alternative.

## Being ignored is a failure too

Conservation is a safety property: nothing disappears. It says nothing about anything
ever *happening*. A perfectly conserved obligation can be perfectly ignored forever.
The liveness side — that a case with recurring opportunities is not starved, and that
attention converts into vanishing defect at a rate — is where
[Normative induction](Normative-Induction) begins. Liveness under endogenous contest
remains a research problem.

## Two holes, named

**Semantic authentication is an input.** Everything above turns on the protocol's
predicates meaning what they say: that a claimed answer really is adequate on the
terms the burden was incurred, and that a local law's two maps really relate the
successors' content to the parent's. The theory types the interface and does not
construct it.

**Licence content is upstream.** The Defeat Principle says what a disposal does;
which rules license which contests is the practice's.
