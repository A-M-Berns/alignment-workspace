# What would it take to show that a normative inductor learns in a way that is both genuinely normative and corrigible to a principal?

*The round's draft (2026-10-24), for the maintainers to rewrite. The structure of the walk, which nodes in which order, is what the round was asked to get right; the prose beside each step is a reading, not a ruling.*

The question has two halves, and the map keeps them in two lines. *Genuinely normative* is the normativity line's question: what learning from reasons requires of a reasoner, from a seed of obligations through the market in which a constraint acquires force, to the licence under which a verdict may be announced. *Corrigible to a principal* is the legitimacy line's: what must stay true of a trajectory's record, verdicts and channels of influence for a later judgment to count as the principal's own, and what corrigibility consumes from that. The walk goes down the program to each line, visits the registered results this round connected to the claims they establish, reads each bridge for what the theorem certifies and what it does not, and ends on three things a visitor could do.

One plan under the top question, owned by both maintainers, whose roots are the seven lines. Everything below is a sub-frame of it; the two halves of our question are two of its lines, and a third, deference and trust, is where the principal's trust in a faster inductor is modelled.

## authorship-and-transparency

When a verdict is the principal's own: the thin specification of legitimacy, sincerity through transparency, and the effective exercise of authority. The programme is rooted in the claim the next step visits, and it holds the thin and sincerity results that are not yet connected.

## effective-authority-layer

The multi-record precedent. One informal claim, that effective authority is modelled with a queue that cannot be bypassed, caused and flood-caused shortfalls, a comprehension shortfall, approval discounts and a revocation default, with sincerity conditions on which verdicts count, is established by twelve registered declarations. The claim is now the conjunction of twelve formal results and a completeness leaf saying what they establish together and what they do not: the ask-list deviation is not wired into the record's counting (item 131, filed this round), the attention budget is a cost table and not a physics, learned membership's convergence is not shown, and every row-level result is a finite fixture decided by computation. For the question, this is the corrigibility half made concrete: asking through her protocol is never a violation, floods and incomprehensible asks are shortfalls the agent caused, and revocation is protected by default.

## authority.queue-no-bypass

One of the twelve, chosen because its bridge says the plainest thing: queue-and-wait is no bypass because a bypass needs a release and the queue releases nothing. A definitional consequence on the landed predicates, and the reading says so, so a visitor does not mistake it for a finding about any protocol.

## sincerity.content-fact

Another of the twelve, on the other side of the claim: updating on the content of an utterance and on the fact of it agree exactly when the channel realizes the sincere reference on the speaker's belief. Its one substantive hypothesis is open, and the bridge says the comparison is with the speaker's actual policy, not her beliefs about it. For the question: what the inductor says counts as hers only through sincerity, which is a legitimacy matter and not a truth matter.
