# Open problems

Ranked. Adopted decisions are not here; they are in `DECISION_LEDGER.md`.
Nothing here is promoted: an open question is stated as open, and where both
directions are open that is said.

## 1. Persistence of the core minimum

The per-date question is settled: containment of the core homothet is linear in
the reference at fixed coefficient, so whether a declared core minimum is
satisfiable *now* is one linear program, with quarantine of operative force as
the declared branch on emptiness (`NL-SI-A2`, `NL-SI-A3`).

What is open is the infimum over dates. `NL-SI-A4` shows no finite family of
per-date checks decides it, and `NL-SI-A7` shows both outcomes occur on small
instances: over the searched sweep every confirmed settlement transports, while
of twenty-eight dependent settlements six void the coefficient outright and six
strictly lower it. So the question is now **precisely conjectured** rather than
uninvestigated, and it is the single hypothesis object that separates the
parametric composite from an unconditional enforcement commitment.

**Highest priority**, because it is the one open item the composite actually
leans on.

## 2. A computable coherence modulus, or a proof there is none

Open **in both directions**. The functional a modulus would have to limit is now
defined with a forced normalization (`NL-SI-C1`, `NL-SI-C2`), and conformance is
checkable in matched units, so the question is well posed: does a given engine
admit a computable schedule tending to zero with its prices provably conforming
at every finite date? No such modulus is known, and no impossibility proof is
known.

**The Gaifman note, carried so the adjacent results are not miscited.** Two
impossibility results sit nearby and **neither settles this**. One is the
candidate source's own three-way impossibility — computability, coherence and
Gaifman inductivity are jointly unsatisfiable. The other is a cited four-way
one — computability, non-dogmatism, Gaifman inductivity and a very weak
coherence are incompatible. Both turn on **Gaifman inductivity**, a desideratum
the candidate algorithm already fails and which the modulus question does not
mention. Citing either as settling the modulus question is an error.

The certification layering (`NL-SI-T3`) lets the mechanism operate without a
modulus, at the price of the book carrying the liability, so this is a question
about what can be *certified*, not a blocker.

## 3. The deduction-budget clause, and funding-responsive deduction

The clause's formal content is deliberately absent from this package and is
reserved: the budget structure itself, and the non-exploitability condition an
engine's partial logical beliefs must satisfy while deduction is incomplete.

**The fork, stated.** Funding-responsive deduction has two inequivalent
readings, and nothing here chooses between them. *Acceleration*: the process is
fixed and a budget buys progress along it by wall-clock. *Attention*: the budget
selects what the process works on, making the stream endogenous to the funder.
Only the second makes the plausible-world set funder-dependent, and only the
second threatens the criterion's statement. Distinguishing them is part of the
reserved work.

## 4. Registry completeness

The per-table ablation program gives witnesses for the tables the displayed
grounds exercise, out of thirteen registered. Completeness — that no judge needs
a table the registry lacks — is not established. This is the one gap that would
make a footprint declaration **unsound** rather than merely coarse, which is why
it ranks above the items below despite being the least glamorous.

## 5. Constructing rather than reading the audited pair

`NL-SI-SIM` is a reading audit and `NL-SI-X4` inherits that standing. Nothing in
this package constructs the candidate pair, and the witness theorem for it
remains to be proved. Constructing a minimal instance of it — enough of a market
over a declared process to evaluate the interface predicates against — would
move the strongest available evidence from the weakest class to the strongest.

## 6. Multi-engine generalizations

The two-engine instance displays disjoint jurisdictions, breach isolation, and a
fenced composed purse. Open: whether the isolation and composition results hold
for arbitrarily many engines with overlapping *world claims* but disjoint
jurisdictions; whether fencing composes associatively across three or more
provenances; and what happens when two engines' bridge warrants to one claim are
themselves in a migration relation.

## 7. Higher-dimensional sharpness for the movement cap

Everything verified numerically in the joint layer is the one-coordinate
fixture. The vertex formulation is stated for general finite regions but only
the scalar case is exercised, and no claim is made that the corrected retention
predicate is the weakest sound one.

## 8. Schema-level demand rates

How demand scales with the schema set rather than with the arrival stream is not
treated. The stream results are stated over arrivals.

## 9. Composite endpoint recertification

`CM-J5` assumes the joint-theorem endpoint conditions afresh at each version.
Characterizing when they can be inherited across a composite instead is what
would make a long history checkable in less than per-step full recertification.

## 10. Comparison objects, branching, and several actors

Comparison objects weaker than the strict fiber product; branching histories;
and several simultaneous migrating actors. All three are stated for the linear
single-actor case here.
