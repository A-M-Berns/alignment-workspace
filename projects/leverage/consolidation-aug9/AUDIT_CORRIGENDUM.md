# Corrigendum to the witness audit

A short note appended to the vendored reading audit
(`vendor/WITNESS_AUDIT.md`), which is frozen and is not edited. Two corrections
and one presentational reconciliation. Nothing here disturbs the audit's
substantive findings, all of which stand.

## 1. The citation-integrity flag is too broad

The audit's adequacy-clause entry carries a citation-integrity flag reporting
that three identifiers cited by the interface draft — one witness label and two
repair labels — occur in neither tree.

**Two of the three are genuinely absent.** The two repair labels occur nowhere
in the previous consolidation or in the source tree, under any spelling.

**The witness label is present.** It occurs twice in the previous
consolidation's own ledger, in the necessity columns of the bare-bridge failure
row and the relativized-bridge row, and once in that consolidation's Tier A test
suite, as the comment naming the displayed instance. The audit's grep appears to
have been narrower than its claim.

**What the citation actually points at**, verified against the vendored archive
and stated here as content rather than as identifiers: the previous
consolidation displays a witness in which a true constraint is suspended at
effective age two because resource competition prevents timely service, which
refutes the bare terminal learning bridge; and it repairs that failure by
assuming **operational adequacy** — capped stake, the token envelope,
work-conserving service, and a latency-against-capacity comparison sufficient to
admit and service the required tests. The source tree carries the same content
downstream as a service-inadequacy witness in which response latency exceeds the
available service window.

This is a *better* outcome than the audit reported: the adequacy clause's
lineage is real and traceable, and only the repair's labels were unresolvable.
Theory 11's adequacy clause and Theory 12 §4 state that content directly and
cite no unverified identifier.

## 2. A paper label that does not exist

The audit cites the budget-limiting construction of the candidate engine's
source by a definition-proposition label. **No such label exists in that
source.** The construction appears as an unlabelled definition-proposition
environment inside the subsection that constructs it; the only label in the
environment is on the displayed condition.

The audit's *content* is correct and was verified against the source: the
construction zeroes a participant's strategy as soon as its value reaches the
negative budget in **some** propositionally-consistent world consistent with the
deductive state, which is a worldwise guarantee and strictly more than an
expected-value constraint. That is why the downside-limit clause is the audit's
cleanest finding. This package therefore states the content and cites no label.

Eight further source labels the audit uses were checked and all eight exist.

## 3. Reconciliation of the SI-minus presentation

The audit lists the write-once clause and a starred enforcement clause **inside**
SI-minus, with qualifying bullets. This package lists them **outside**, as named
conditionals — write-once conditional on consistency of the declared theory, and
the enforcement clause conditional on the persistence question.

Both presentations describe the same situation and neither is wrong about it.
This package adopts the second because a conditional inhabitation is not an
inhabitation, and a set membership qualified by a bullet invites exactly the
misreading the audit elsewhere works to prevent.

**One substantive consequence, and it is not cosmetic.** With write-once inside
the set, consistency of the declared theory rides along as a qualifying remark
and can go missing from the sufficiency statement — as it does in the audit's
own delta discussion, which lists the four deltas as jointly sufficient without
naming consistency. With write-once outside, sufficiency must name it. Theory 11
§7 states the sufficiency with consistency named, and explains why it has to be.

## 4. What stands

Everything else. In particular: the pair mapping and the argument that the
alternatives are worse; the finding that the tolerance clause admits vacuous
satisfaction and that soundness and usefulness come apart there; the observation
that the adequacy clause is vacuous on the logical channel; the finding that
only one of the two stopping-neutrality witnesses is available to the candidate;
the proof-carrying modification and its negligible cost; the three unmet
deduction requirements; and the warning that the adjacent impossibility result
does not settle the modulus question.

On that last point this package adds a reason rather than a correction: both the
cited four-way impossibility and the source's own three-way one turn on Gaifman
inductivity, a desideratum the candidate algorithm already fails, whereas the
modulus question involves no Gaifman condition at all.
