# What happened

The learning theorem can now be run, but the whole answerability architecture
cannot yet honestly claim to host its computation.

The implementation follows Blum and Mansour's reduction rather than putting one
Hedge weight on each repair. It keeps 8 rows × 9 lawful-program weights, builds
the transformation-weighted Markov matrix, selects an exact stationary
distribution for the represented numerical weights, incurs expected mixed
charge, and updates each source-action row from the full charge vector.

On the deliberately adverse impediment fixture, uniform play's maximum lawful
repair regret grows from 7.5 at `T=12` to 60 at `T=96`. The Φ-learner grows from
2.879 to 7.748, while regret per round falls from 0.240 to 0.081. These are
numerical observations, not a finite-data proof of big-O. The cited theorem,
together with the item-29 bridge, supplies the expected mixed-loss guarantee.

The important limitation is visible too. On the persistent-interval fixture the
learner has exactly zero regret against all nine repairs while paying charge
`T/2`; ordinary action Hedge pays much less. The nine repair programs leave
costly common fixed behavior untouched. Low regret therefore means self-
correction only relative to this small public repair language.

A sampled action still decodes to an ordinary canonical repository response. It
creates the usual ledger entry, never rewrites prior burdens, and fits response
service capacity. But `ServiceCosts` prices only dispositions. It has no field
for the 72 updates or stationary computation, and the learner state is not part
of the answerability record. Calling that computation free would overstate the
bounded-reasoner synthesis. The round therefore closes the learning construction
but leaves integration blocked on a precise work/record interface.
