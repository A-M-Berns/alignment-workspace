# Prosecution

`THEOREM_MAP.md` has the verdicts. This has the four places the architecture is
weaker than those verdicts read, and the one attack that was expected to land and
did not.

## 1. The critic's copy is not compared against anything either

The obstruction this round was built against: a reasoner holds its own copy of a
relation the environment also holds, revises the copy, and no predicate of the
reasoner's trajectory compares copy with original — witnessed by one trajectory
and two environments differing only in the copy's faithfulness.

What the relational move does is remove the original. There is no environment
relation; there is `practice[C]`, which belongs to a participant. `H` has no move
writing it, so the self-certification attack fails: T2, exhaustively.

**What it does not do is make `C` right.** `practice[C]` is exactly as unjudged as
`practice[H]` was. The round's answer to "why is that better" is structural, not
epistemic: the burden is now a function of two agents' coordinates rather than
one, and neither agent can move both. An adversary who controls `C` as well as `H`
faces nothing here. The architecture buys *separation of the write*, and that is
all it buys.

This is the honest form of the finding, and it is weaker than "self-certification
is repaired". Self-certification by the reasoner alone is repaired. Certification
by a colluding pair is not addressed, and the model has no notion of collusion.

## 2. Consensus is convicted only where the practice already convicts it

T4's witness works because closure under the shared practice meets an
incompatibility in the shared practice. Every agent is defective *by rules they
all endorse*.

That is a real answer to K3, and it is narrower than "unanimity does not settle
correctness". A unanimous position that is internally coherent and simply wrong
about the world is not convicted by anything in this model, because the model has
no world. The second witness — one participant's observation breaking the
unanimity — routes the correction through a participant's assertion, which is
another attitude, not a fact.

The source's claim is that the *form* of the distinction is preserved in every
perspective, not that any perspective is right. The model reproduces the form.
Nothing here shows a practice can detect a shared error with no internal trace,
and a reader taking T4 to show that is taking more than the witness gives.

## 3. The protection is put in, not derived

C5b establishes an unbounded universal quantifier over advisor policies, which is
what the deference line's review asked for and what its predecessor could not
supply. The mechanism is that the advisor holds no reserved authority subject, so
the coordinate the capability reads is closed under advisor moves.

That is a property of the fixture's initial grants. The round did not show that
an authority relation must be arranged this way, and there is nothing here
forcing a real system to withhold the reserved subject from a capable advisor.

What the round does show is narrower and worth stating exactly: **once the
authorization relation is a coordinate that a transition precondition reads, the
protection question becomes a reachability question with an exact answer.** The
predecessor's failure was not that it chose the wrong grants; it was that it had
no authorization coordinate at all, so "the principal can correct" had to be
cashed out in terms the advisor's action set already dominated. Supplying the
coordinate is what makes the question well-posed. Answering it is then a modelling
choice, and this round made a favourable one.

## 4. The applicability object leaks one route

Three of four laundering routes fail. The fourth — disavowing `alpha`, the premise
that grounds the applicability claim — succeeds, and the round classifies it as
legitimate retraction rather than laundering.

That classification is a judgment, not a result. The model cannot distinguish a
reasoner that gives up `alpha` because it no longer believes it from one that
gives it up because `beta` became inconvenient. Selection between arms with the
same public trace is not a property of the trace, which is the same type mismatch
the legitimacy line recorded, reached here by a different route and not repaired.

## 5. The attack that was expected to land and did not

The dispatch's L3 anticipated that state-sensitive comparators would "risk
date-indexed certification and capture". Capture was closed by the sealed guard,
as expected. Date-indexed certification was expected to be the residue, and it is
not there: `test_certification_reads_the_status_and_not_the_date` puts two states
at different depths, reached by different routes, with the same public status, and
every program gives them the same map.

The reason is that the guard reads six booleans and there is no date in the state
for it to read. That is not a deep result — it is what removing the date does —
but it does identify what the earlier round's tension was about. The earlier
comparator class was indexed by the *record*, and a record carries its history;
this one is indexed by *status*, and a status does not. Whether a status-indexed
guard is expressive enough for the repairs a real architecture needs is untested,
and the nine programs here are chosen, not generated.

## What was not attacked

- Collusion between scorekeepers, per §1.
- Any asymptotic statement. The horizons are `12, 24, 48, 96`.
- **Endogenous evolution, and this is the sharpest omission.** L5's recurrence is
  *staged*: the position is re-filed at each date rather than allowed to evolve,
  which makes the loss exogenous and the comparison additive by construction. Let
  the state evolve and a comparator's intervention changes later context —
  acknowledging a consequence changes what is subsequently exposed — which is
  exactly the replay-prefix and endogenous-filing case the learning track's
  applicability audit placed outside its additive reduction. The staging is a
  hypothesis this round inherited and did not discharge, and it bites harder here
  than in the docket fixture, because every repair in the grammar alters the
  scorekeeping position the next date's loss is read from.
- The learner. No online algorithm was implemented and no regret curve measured;
  L5 exhibits the comparator's saving and the arithmetic of the lower bound.
- Multi-step testimony, per `MODEL.md`'s declared simplifications.
- Whether the nine programs are a natural class rather than nine hand-chosen
  records. They are hand-chosen, exactly as the existing learning track's nine
  are, and the comparator-language question that track records as open is
  untouched here.
