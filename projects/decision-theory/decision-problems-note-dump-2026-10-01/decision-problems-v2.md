# Decision Problems: Formal Core (v2)

Working notes toward a revision of *Coherent Care*. Scope, informally: everything is developed in a finite regime — decision problems will be finite trees, action sets finite — with the general measure-theoretic apparatus set up so that the finite restriction is a property imposed where the relevant objects are defined, not a global axiom. Claims I could verify are labelled Theorem/Proposition/Lemma with proofs or proof sketches; claims I could not are labelled Conjecture or Question; citations drawn from the comment thread of Taylor's post are flagged as such where I have not checked them independently. A change log against v1 is at the end.

## 0. Events and worlds

An *event space* is a pair $\mathcal{A} = (\mathcal{E}, J)$: a Boolean algebra $\mathcal{E}$ of *events* (propositions), together with a set $J$ of *designated meets*: each member of $J$ is a family $D \subseteq \mathcal{E}$ whose infimum $\bigwedge D$ exists in $\mathcal{E}$. Designation attaches to the family, not to its infimum — one event is typically the infimum of many families, and constraint 4 below is family-relative; the infimum need not be carried separately, being unique when it exists. Designating a family is an optional expressive act, an addition to the language rather than a mathematical necessity: it asserts that the infinite conjunction is entailed by its conjuncts jointly — an ω-rule, licensing the passage from "every $A \in D$ holds" to "$\bigwedge D$ holds" — where an undesignated infimum is a mere greatest lower bound, permitted to fail even while every conjunct holds. By De Morgan (the algebra is Boolean), designating $D$ is interchangeable with giving the complemented family $\{\neg A : A \in D\}$ existential force: its disjunction true only by the truth of a witness. $J = \emptyset$ is always admissible and is the default in this document.

**Definition 1 (worlds).** A *world* of $\mathcal{A}$ is a set $\omega \subseteq \mathcal{E}$ — read: the propositions true at $\omega$, writing $\omega \models X$ for $X \in \omega$ — satisfying the consistency constraints:

1. for every event $X$, exactly one of $X, \neg X$ belongs to $\omega$ (complete and non-contradictory);
2. if $X \in \omega$ and $X \leq Y$ then $Y \in \omega$ (closed under entailment);
3. if $X, Y \in \omega$ then $X \wedge Y \in \omega$ (closed under conjunction);
4. for every $D \in J$: if $A \in \omega$ for every $A \in D$, then $\bigwedge D \in \omega$ (closed under designated conjunction — the infinitary generalization of constraint 3).

$\Omega$ denotes the set of worlds.

*Remark 0.1.* Constraints 1–3 are exactly the ultrafilter axioms, and they already force *finite* disjunctions to be witnessed: if $X \vee Y \in \omega$ but $X, Y \notin \omega$, then $\neg X, \neg Y \in \omega$ by 1, so $\neg X \wedge \neg Y = \neg(X \vee Y) \in \omega$ by 3, contradicting 1. Constraint 4's converse is likewise free: $\bigwedge D \in \omega$ entails each $A \in \omega$ by constraint 2, exactly as for constraint 3 — so 4 adds only the passage *from* the conjuncts *to* the infinite conjunction, which for finite families is constraint 3 itself. Infinite families are different: a world can hold every $A_n$ while denying $\bigwedge_n A_n$ (by De Morgan: affirm an infinite disjunction while denying every disjunct), with no finite subfamily ever exposing the inconsistency — and whether to forbid this is precisely the designation decision, made per family, according to whether that conjunction is *meant* as a genuine "for all". With $J = \emptyset$ worlds always exist in abundance (Stone representation; Appendix A); enlarging $J$ can shrink the world-set, in extreme cases to nothing. Appendix A collects the phenomena — witness-less worlds, the annihilation of worlds on probability-quotient algebras, the Loomis–Sikorski repair, and the mismatch between what worlds and what probabilities can be asked to respect — and the companion note *Worlds and Points* treats the option space at length, deriving the $\kappa$-parameterization of earlier drafts as the by-cardinality special case of choosing $J$. In this document nothing turns on any of it: every worked example uses a finite algebra, where all joins are essentially finite (designation is moot) and worlds are just the atoms; and no theorem asks any world or probability to respect an infinite designated family.

Fix, for the remainder, one event space $\mathcal{A} = (\mathcal{E}, J)$, with world set $\Omega$.

## 1. Subjective states, Jeffrey–Bolker–Joyce format

A *probability* on $\mathcal{A}$ is a finitely additive $P : \mathcal{E} \to [0,1]$ with $P(\top) = 1$ such that, for every designated family $D \in J$,
$$P\big(\textstyle\bigwedge D\big) \;=\; \inf\,\{\,P(\textstyle\bigwedge F) : F \subseteq D \text{ finite}\,\}.$$
The finite sub-meets are directed (two finite subfamilies union to a third), so the right side is a limit along a shrinking net, and the condition says $P$ is continuous along designated meets. This is countable additivity in disguise, in two steps. Downward: let $\{B_n\}$ be pairwise disjoint with union $B$, and suppose the complemented family $D = \{\neg B_n\}$ is designated; then $\bigwedge D = \neg B$ and $\bigwedge_{n \in F} \neg B_n = \neg \bigvee_{n \in F} B_n$, so the condition reads $P(\neg B) = \inf_F \big(1 - \sum_{n \in F} P(B_n)\big) = 1 - \sum_n P(B_n)$, i.e. $P(B) = \sum_n P(B_n)$. Upward: if $P$ is countably additive on a σ-algebra of sets, then for countable $D$ the chain $B_n := D_1 \cap \cdots \cap D_n$ is cofinal among the finite sub-meets and decreases to $\bigcap D$, and continuity from above along chains — a standard equivalent of countable additivity for finite measures — gives the condition. So on a σ-algebra of sets with every countable family designated, probabilities in the present sense are exactly the countably additive ones; with $J = \emptyset$ they are exactly the finitely additive ones (charges).

Notation: $\Delta(\mathcal{A})$ is the set of probabilities on $\mathcal{A}$. Roman capitals mark the point-set version: for an algebra $\sigma$ of subsets of a set $X$, write $\Delta_\sigma(X) := \Delta((\sigma, J))$, with the convention that a σ-algebra carries its canonical designation (all countable subfamilies) — so $\Delta_\sigma(X)$ denotes the countably additive probabilities, matching standard usage — while a mere algebra of sets carries $J = \emptyset$. Point-style notation is used freely wherever there are enough worlds to carry it: $\Delta(\Omega)$, and for finite $A$ the simplex $\Delta(A)$, which covers every worked example below. The abstract $\Delta(\mathcal{A})$ is reserved for where it is load-bearing (this section, §0, Appendix A): in the pointless regime of Appendix A, $\Delta(\Omega)$ would name distributions on an empty set while $\Delta(\mathcal{A})$ is nonempty, and the decoration would misdirect toward exactly the worlds-as-mixture picture that has failed there.

Worlds are exactly the $2$-valued probabilities: read $2$-valuedly, the displayed condition says $\bigwedge D \in \omega$ iff every finite sub-meet is in $\omega$, which via constraint 3 is "every member is" — the nontrivial direction being constraint 4, the converse being constraint 2. (For $J = \emptyset$ the worlds are, classically, the extreme points of $\Delta(\mathcal{A})$; the identity persists under designation by a conditioning argument verified this session but not sourced, and nothing below uses it.) Throughout, all subjective states share the fixed $\mathcal{A}$; relaxing to per-state algebras is Question 7.

**Definition 2 (subjective state).** A *subjective state* is a triple $s = (P_s, V_s, \mathrm{cf}_s)$ where:

1. $P_s \in \Delta(\mathcal{A})$;
2. $V_s$ (*desirability*) assigns a real number to each $P_s$-non-null event, satisfying the averaging axiom: for disjoint non-null $X, Y$, $\ V_s(X \vee Y) = \dfrac{P_s(X) V_s(X) + P_s(Y) V_s(Y)}{P_s(X \vee Y)}$;
3. $\mathrm{cf}_s$ (*counterfactual structure*) assigns to each non-$\bot$ event a supposed probability-and-desirability pair,
$$\mathrm{cf}_s : (a : \mathcal{E} \setminus \{\bot\}) \to (P : \Delta(\mathcal{A}),\ \mathcal{V}_P),$$
where $\mathcal{V}_P$ is the set of desirabilities for $P$ (clause 2 with $P$ in place of $P_s$), subject to one axiom — *success*: writing $(P^a_s, V^a_s) := \mathrm{cf}_s(a)$, we require $P^a_s(a) = 1$. Note the domain: the type itself declines to suppose $\bot$ (success would force $P^\bot(\bot) = 1$ against additivity), so counterpossible supposition is excluded by construction, matching the scope decision that counterlogicals are not attempted here.

*Remark 1.1 (format, not representation).* This is the Jeffrey–Bolker probability-and-desirability format, posited directly rather than derived from preference axioms; Bolker's representation theorem requires a complete atomless algebra, while every worked example below is finite and atomic, so the representation axioms are deliberately not assumed. (Finite-case representation results in the JB tradition exist — Domotor's work, per discussion — and are set aside.) The counterfactual slot is what lets rival decision theories be compared on one chassis — the comparative ambition is Joyce's (*The Foundations of Causal Decision Theory*, 1999) — and the comparison has a symmetry worth displaying: both act values have the shape "desirability of $a$ according to a state", differing only in which state supplies it. Evidential choice consults the actual state — Jeffrey's news value $V_s(a)$, no counterfactual needed — while causal choice consults the supposed one, $V^a_s(a)$. Nothing forbids a state whose $\mathrm{cf}_s$ agrees with conditioning wherever both are defined; the point is that the evidential evaluator never consults $\mathrm{cf}_s$ at all. Matching this chassis to the constructions in the literature (Joyce's efficacy value in particular) is deferred to a literature pass; no formula is printed here from memory. Where the document needs a specific evaluator it says so, since decision procedures (Definition 4) are the official objects and are not required to arise from any theory.

*Remark 1.2 (why pairs, why one axiom).* Two decisions recorded with their grounds. (i) $\mathrm{cf}_s$ returns a pair, not a bare probability. A bare-probability $\mathrm{cf}$ must reconstruct the supposed desirability by transport from $V_s$ — supposed value of $X$ as the $P^a_s$-weighted average of $V_s$ over the atoms below $X$ — and the transport fails twice over. It exists only where there are atoms — beyond atoms and their unions there is nothing to read off, so it is undefined at the generality of §0 — and, desirability being undefined on $P_s$-null events, it has nothing to read off exactly where a supposition moves mass onto the subjectively impossible. And where it exists it is a substantive stipulation, *rigidity* of atomic values, which on algebras too coarse to express the value-relevant facts hardcodes news value: supposing "smoke" on the two-atom smoking algebra copies the correlational verdict, the wrong thing to build in. Independently, under the Jeffrey–Bolker non-uniqueness, which mixes desirability into probability (the transformed $P$ is the old one tilted by an affine function of $V$; exact transformation family to be checked against Bolker in the literature pass), a bare-probability counterfactual is not a preference-invariant notion. Pair-valued $\mathrm{cf}_s$ subsumes the transport as the special case of rigid suppositions, where meaningful. (ii) The sole axiom is success. Anything further is left elsewhere, sorted by what it consults: agreement with conditioning and rigidity are subjective criteria and belong to the counterfactual theories of §4 (Definition 20); equating $\mathrm{cf}_s$ with objective deviation statistics is *counterfactual calibration* and belongs to §3.1's family (Remark 3.11), where any rules beyond success would be proved from calibration assumptions rather than posited.

## 2. Decision-points and procedures

**Definition 3 (decision-point).** A *decision-point* is a triple $d = (s_d, O_d, A_d)$: a subjective state $s_d$; an *observation* $O_d \in \mathcal{E}$; and a finite nonempty set $A_d \subseteq \mathcal{E} \setminus \{\bot\}$ of *actions*, pairwise disjoint as events (non-$\bot$, since $\mathrm{cf}_s$ and the success axiom are untypeable at $\bot$). $\mathcal{D}$ denotes the class of decision-points.

*Remark 2.1.* Actions are events directly (the Jeffrey–Bolker reading: "the proposition that I take $a$"); v1's injection $\iota$ is gone. The observation is *part of the point* and is an event, so a decision procedure may read it — required for UDT to be representable at all (Definition 17; Remark 6.2) — while the state $s_d$ carries the agent's opinions, which need not be the Bayesian conditioning of anything on $O_d$: that discipline belongs to the normative layer (§4), not to the type.

**Definition 4 (decision procedure).** A *decision procedure* is a dependent function
$$C : (d : \mathcal{D}) \to \Delta(A_d),$$
*deterministic* if every $C(d)$ is a point mass, in which case $C(d)$ also names the action.

*Remark 2.2 (individuation).* Procedures are functions of decision-points: two occurrences of one $(s, O, A)$ triple — within a run, across runs, across problems — receive the same mixed action, and "deviate at this occurrence only" is not an operation the type supports. Identity of the full triple is what is required, observation included, so two locations coincide only under genuine subjective indistinguishability.


## 3. Decision problems

### 3.1 Concrete decision problems

The definition whose justification is most immediate is the post's: a decision problem is a stochastic function of the procedure, typed as $\widehat{B} : ((d:\mathcal{D}) \to \Delta(A_d)) \to \Delta(\Omega \times \mathbb{R})$ (finitely supported) — worlds paired with payoffs — the value of a procedure being the mean payoff. Being a function *of* $C$ already secures extensionality: oracle access, no source code. What $\widehat{B}$ lacks is structure the rest of the theory consumes — *which* decision-points are consulted, *how often*, *on which branches* — and the working definition supplies exactly that:

**Definition 5 (concrete decision problem).** A *concrete decision problem* is a finite rooted tree $B$ in which each internal node is exactly one of: a *chance node* $n$ labelled with $\beta_n \in \Delta(\mathrm{children}(n))$; a *decision node* $q$ labelled with a decision-point $d_q \in \mathcal{D}$, outgoing edges in bijection with $A_{d_q}$ (the verbs "query" and "consult" refer throughout to a run reaching a decision node) — and each leaf $\ell$ carries a world and a payoff, $(\lambda(\ell), r(\ell)) \in \Omega \times \mathbb{R}$. (Finiteness — finitely many nodes — is the formal residue of v1's global assumption (F), imposed here where it belongs; every path is finite and every chance node has finite support as consequences. Infinite trees with a.s.-finite runs: Question 8.) A point $d$ is *queried* if some node carries it.

**Definition 6 (run semantics; value).** $\mu_{B,C} \in \Delta(\mathrm{Leaves}(B))$ is generated by walking from the root: at a chance node sample a child from $\beta_n$; at a decision node sample $a \sim C(d_q)$, independently of everything so far, and follow the $a$-edge. The *objective statistics* are $\nu_{B,C} \in \Delta(\mathcal{A})$, $\nu_{B,C}(X) := \mu_{B,C}\{\ell : \lambda(\ell) \models X\}$ — a finite mixture of worlds, hence a probability on $\mathcal{A}$ since worlds are. The *value* is $V_B(C) := \mathbb{E}_{\mu_{B,C}}[r]$. Three derived notions are used throughout: $\mathrm{occ}(d) \subseteq \mathrm{Leaves}(B)$ is the set of leaves whose root-path contains a $d$-node — the runs on which $d$ is *consulted*; $\#_d(\ell)$ counts the $d$-nodes on the path to $\ell$; and for $m \in \Delta(A_d)$, the *point-deviation* $C[d \mapsto m]$ is the procedure agreeing with $C$ everywhere except $C[d \mapsto m](d) = m$, with $C[d \mapsto a] := C[d \mapsto \delta_a]$ for a pure action $a \in A_d$.

**The extensional shadow.** A tree determines $\widehat{B}(C)$ — the joint law of $(\lambda, r)$ under $\mu_{B,C}$, for every $C$ — but not conversely, and the loss is strict in both directions. Inserting a query of $d$ whose branches lead to identical subtrees leaves $\widehat{B}$ unchanged while changing $\mathrm{occ}(d)$ and $\#_d$, so the occurrence-conditioned calibration notions below are not even functions of the shadow; this is the post's "Omega spuriously imagining a counterfactual", made type-theoretic. In the other direction, some extensional functionals are realized by no tree: payoff $1$ iff $C$ two-boxes at every point of an infinite family — no run touching finitely many points decides it. (A finite-horizon memoryless POMDP is an alternative packaging of the same data; trees are used because the run distribution is defined by structural recursion, with no fixed-point apparatus.)

*Remark 3.1 (the tree is a type signature).* Could some function type itself track which oracle calls a run makes? Adding a trace to the codomain, $C \mapsto \Delta(\mathrm{Trace} \times \Omega \times \mathbb{R})$, fails: the type is still a bare function of $C$, so the reported trace is *testimony* — a constant function reporting a fictitious trace inhabits it as comfortably as an honest one. The obstruction is function extensionality: a function is its graph, and call-patterns are intensional. The standard repair is to make the calls constructors, and it returns Definition 5 as an inductive type,
$$\mathrm{Probl} \;=\; (\Omega \times \mathbb{R}) \;+\; \Delta(\mathrm{Probl}) \;+\; (d : \mathcal{D},\; A_d \to \mathrm{Probl})$$
— leaf, chance, decision-with-continuation — read as the least solution: every problem is finitely many applications of the three constructors. This is free-monad syntax for two effects, probabilistic choice and consulting an oracle of type $(d : \mathcal{D}) \to \Delta(A_d)$; Definition 6 is the *handler* discharging both, the fold from syntax to semantics, which the shadow discussion shows is neither injective nor surjective. (Traditions: algebraic effects and handlers, Plotkin–Power, Plotkin–Pretnar; the coinductive variant, interaction trees, is the home of Question 8's infinite trees. $\mathcal{D}$ is a proper class, so the inductive type is literal only after restricting the points to a set.) In the unbounded well-founded regime, oracle-functionals are tree-representable exactly when continuous (dialogue trees; Escardó — moderate confidence on the attribution), and the infinite-family functional above is a discontinuity witness, neighbourhoods pinning only finitely many coordinates; finite trees buy strictly more — finitely many coordinates, Bernstein-form polynomials per coordinate (Proposition 4) — and the image of the fold in general is Question 11.

*Remark 3.2 (independent redraws).* Each decision node samples $C(d)$ afresh, so a predictor's simulation of a stochastic agent is independent of the agent's live randomization; a shared-seed variant is definable and differs only for stochastic procedures. All worked examples use deterministic procedures.

**Definition 7 (veridicality; coverage; recording).** Fix a run ending at $\ell$ and a decision node $q$ on its path at which $a$ was drawn. The instance is *observation-veridical* if $\lambda(\ell) \models O_{d_q}$, and *action-veridical* if $\lambda(\ell) \models a$. The node $q$ is *subtree-veridical* if $\lambda(\ell') \models O_{d_q}$ for every leaf $\ell'$ below $q$. $B$ *covers* $d$ (for $C$) if $\mu_{B,C}$-a.s. every run whose leaf-world satisfies $O_d$ passes through a $d$-node. $B$ *records at* $d$ (for $C$) if $\mu_{B,C}$-a.s.: every run whose leaf-world satisfies $O_d$ passes exactly one $d$-node; that node is subtree-veridical; its instance is action-veridical; and the leaf-world satisfies an action in $A_d$ only if it is the one drawn there.

*Remark 3.3.* Nothing in Definition 5 marks a query as "real" versus "hypothetical"; an Omega-style simulation is a decision node that fails subtree-veridicality (it sits on paths into worlds where its observation is false). False observations are therefore the default, and ruling them out is an added property. Veridicality is the emergent, statistical residue of really facing the decision.

*Remark 3.4 (what recording gives, and what it deliberately does not).* Two inclusions should be kept separate. Coverage is one: a.s., $\{\ell : \lambda(\ell) \models O_d\} \subseteq \mathrm{occ}(d)$. Subtree-veridicality *at every* $d$-node is the other: $\mathrm{occ}(d) \subseteq \{\ell : \lambda(\ell) \models O_d\}$. Only together do they identify being consulted with the observation coming true. Recording implies coverage, but demands veridicality only of the node met on $O_d$-runs: $d$-nodes on runs ending outside the observation — simulations — are deliberately tolerated. And the veridicality clause recording *does* impose is not decorative; it excludes *selection bias*. At a routing node whose answer determines whether the observation comes true — say a root query whose $a$-edge alone leads into $O$-worlds — conditioning on the observation selects the draw: with $C(d) = (\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ there, $\nu(a \mid O) = 1 \neq \tfrac12$, so conditional action statistics part company with $C(d)$ exactly where veridicality fails.

**Lemma 1 (occurrence constancy).** $\mu_{B,C}(\mathrm{occ}(d))$ does not depend on $C(d)$ — although $\nu_{B,C}(O_d)$ in general does.

*Proof.* $\mathrm{occ}(d)$ is the disjoint union, over *minimal* $d$-nodes $q$ (no $d$-node strictly above), of the events "reach $q$": a path cannot contain two minimal $d$-nodes, and reaching a minimal $d$-node involves draws at non-$d$ nodes only. For the contrast, Remark 3.4's routing root already witnesses it — $\nu(O)$ there is a function of $C(d)$ — and Transparent Newcomb V2 (§7.2) is the vivid case: $\nu(O_E)$ is $1-p$ or $p$ according to the answer at $d_E$ itself. $\square$

The asymmetry — the probability of being *consulted* at $d$ is deviation-invariant at $d$, that of the *observation-event* is not — is put to work in §8. And the invariance is exactly per-run: the per-occurrence normalizer $\mathbb{E}_\mu[\#_d]$ *does* move under deviation at $d$, since the answer at one $d$-node governs whether lower $d$-nodes are reached — one nested pair suffices, $\mathbb{E}_\mu[\#_d] = 1 + C(d)(b)$ in Proposition 5(c)'s miniature. This is one reason the per-run-flavoured Theorem 2 enjoys a discrete cancellation while the per-occurrence-flavoured Theorem 1 is a first-order condition: the former's conditioning weight holds still under deviation, the latter's weights do not.

**Calibration.** The structure just defined supports the document's normative equations: between a problem's stipulated states and the statistics its own runs realize. Fix $B$ and $C$; write $\nu := \nu_{B,C}$, $\mu := \mu_{B,C}$.

**Definition 8 (strict observation calibration).** $B$ is *strictly observation-calibrated for $C$* if for every queried $d$ with $\nu(O_d) > 0$:

1. $P_{s_d}(X) = \nu(X \mid O_d)$ for all $X \in \mathcal{E}$;
2. $V_{s_d}(X) = \mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\,r \mid \lambda \models X \wedge O_d\,]$ for every $X$ with $P_{s_d}(X) > 0$ and $\nu(X \wedge O_d) > 0$.

Queried points with $\nu(O_d) = 0$ are unconstrained, matching the post's parenthetical. Clause 2 replaces v1's pointwise utility condition: desirability is conditional expected payoff, and given clause 1 it still satisfies the averaging axiom automatically — for disjoint $X, Y$ the leaf-events $\{\lambda \models X \wedge O_d\}$ and $\{\lambda \models Y \wedge O_d\}$ partition $\{\lambda \models (X \vee Y) \wedge O_d\}$ by primality of worlds, and clause 1 converts the $\mu$-weights of the two cells into $P_{s_d}$-weights.

*Remark 3.5 (payoffs at leaves, not utilities on worlds).* v1 fixed a global $u : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ and set $r = u \circ \lambda$. The leaf-attached typing is strictly more general and matches the post: the problem, not the ontology, supplies the stakes, and two leaves may carry the same world with different payoffs. The supervenient case is recoverable as $r = u \circ \lambda$, and is always *manufacturable* by enriching $\mathcal{E}$ with payoff coordinates, so supervenience-or-not is relative to the algebra, like Proposition 6's prior-dependence (Question 1). The cost surfaces in calibration: Definition 8's desirability clause conditions the run distribution on a leaf-world event, and where payoffs fail to supervene, same-world leaves are averaged with $\mu$-weights. Three levels of statistic order the situation — the world-marginal $\nu_{B,C}$; the joint world–payoff law $\widehat{B}(C)$, which under the present typing *is* the extensional shadow; and the run distribution $\mu_{B,C}$. Clause 1 consumes the first. The new clause 2 is written as a $\mu$-conditional but depends on $\mu$ only through the joint law: $\mathbb{E}_\mu[r \cdot \mathbf{1}(\lambda \models X \wedge O_d)]$ is a functional of the law of $(\lambda, r)$ — one line to check. SSC alone consumes the third, and by the spurious-query observation above it is not a function of the shadow at all. So non-supervenient payoffs move desirability calibration from marginal to joint and stop there; the surviving kinship with SSC is that both offences against $\nu$-sufficiency are repaired by enriching $\mathcal{E}$ (payoff coordinates here, instance structure in Proposition 2's lift), which files both under Question 1's relativity-to-the-algebra rather than under anthropics. Under supervenience the clause collapses back to a function of $\nu$ and $u$.

*Remark 3.6 (forced self-knowledge).* If $C$ is deterministic and $B$ records at $d$, then $\nu(a \mid O_d) = C(d)(a)$ (the recording argument, reused in Proposition 7), so clause 1 forces $P_{s_d}(C(d)) = 1$: certainty of the very action about to be taken, subjective probability $0$ for every alternative. This is the formal source of the friction with EDT, and Definitions 9, 10, 12 are the post's options (c), (b), (a) in response.

**Definition 9 (masked calibration).** $B$ is *masked-calibrated for $C$* if for every queried $d$ there is a full-support self-model $m \in \Delta(A_d)$ such that, writing $C' := C[d \mapsto m]$ and requiring $\nu_{B,C'}(O_d) > 0$: $P_{s_d}(X) = \nu_{B,C'}(X \mid O_d)$ for all $X \in \mathcal{E}$, and $V_{s_d}(X) = \mathbb{E}_{\mu_{B,C'}}[\,r \mid \lambda \models X \wedge O_d\,]$ wherever both sides are defined. That is: strict calibration *under some self-model* — the state must be a realizable worldview at $d$, not necessarily the realized one. (Variants, compared in Appendix B: freeing the self-model globally, $C'$ arbitrary, rather than at $d$ alone; and dropping full support. All four combinations coincide on the worked examples of §§6–7 at interior self-models; the local non-dogmatic choice made official here ties act-conditionals to the true continuations while forbidding unforced zeros.)

*Remark 3.7 (what masking is for).* Masking answers Remark 3.6: strict calibration plus recording forces $P_{s_d}(C(d)) = 1$, and a self-certain agent has nothing left to deliberate about evidentially. The existential form frees exactly the label: the state must lie on the *calibration manifold* $\{\nu_{B, C[d \mapsto m]}(\cdot \mid O_d) : m \text{ full-support}\}$ rather than at its fixed point where self-model equals truth. Nothing else is loosened — every point of the manifold is a realizable worldview, so self-uncertainty cannot leak into false beliefs about anything the acts do not touch — and EDT's act values at a masked-calibrated point are true conditional payoffs under the self-model. Ratifiability then reads: the label lies on the branch-generated manifold; its fixed point is strict calibration — a base case, inside the calibration family itself, of §5's branch/label distinction. Which zeros survive is exactly right: at a subtree-veridical node, full support forces $P_{s_d}(a) > 0$ for every action, while at a selection node (Remark 3.4's routing root) $\nu_{B,C'}(m{=}5 \mid O_5) = 1$ for *every* full-support $C'$ — the certainty there is a fact about the branch, not a failure of self-humility.

**Definition 10 (trembles; limit calibration).** $C^\varepsilon(d) := (1-\varepsilon)C(d) + \varepsilon\,\mathrm{Unif}(A_d)$. $B$ is *limit-calibrated for $C$* if for every queried $d$ with $\nu_{B,C^\varepsilon}(O_d) > 0$ for all small $\varepsilon$: $P_{s_d}(X) = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^+} \nu_{B,C^\varepsilon}(X \mid O_d)$ for all $X$ (limits exist: ratios of polynomials in $\varepsilon$, by finiteness), with the $V$-clause on events of non-vanishing limiting conditional probability. As in v1: this is the problem-side analogue of trembling-hand perfection, extending Definition 8 to tremble-reachable null observations; trembles used *inside* a procedure (evaluating actions by tremble-limit conditional value) are a different device, and §7.1 shows the two come apart.

**Definition 11 (prior-equipped points; prior calibration).** A *prior-equipped decision-point* is $(s^\circ, s, O, A)$ with $s^\circ$ a subjective state — the prior state — subject to the problem-level constraint that all queried points share one $s^\circ$. Its calibration rule has the same two clauses as Definition 8, unconditioned: $P_{s^\circ}(X) = \nu_{B,C}(X)$ for all $X \in \mathcal{E}$, and $V_{s^\circ}(X) = \mathbb{E}_\mu[\,r \mid \lambda \models X\,]$ for $\nu$-non-null $X$ (strict; the masked variant requires the two clauses under some $C'$ of full support at every queried point).

*Remark 3.8.* Three notes. (i) The rule makes the prior carry self-knowledge of the agent's action statistics — ratifiability recurs one level up, and §5 is about that recursion. (ii) Proposition 1 shows the design is conservative: Jeffrey-conditioned states over a calibrated prior state are automatically strictly calibrated. (iii) The design choice itself: with the prior state inside the point, the first parameter of Definition 17's $\mathrm{UDT}_{s^\circ, \rho}$ is supplied by the point and updatelessness moves toward a closed term; without it, the family shape stands and its optimality claims have quantifier order $\forall B\, \exists C$ where CDT and EDT offer $\exists C\, \forall B$ (Remark 6.2); the two designs are one currying apart. The unequipped design is the default here because one job of the formalism is to make theories' data requirements comparable, and equipping every point with a prior equalizes those requirements by fiat — hiding exactly the cost of UDT that the framework should exhibit. The equipped design is retained as the recorded alternative.

**Proposition 1.** If $B$'s queried points are prior-equipped with a prior-calibrated $s^\circ$ and every queried state is its Jeffrey conditioning — $P_{s_d} = P_{s^\circ}(\cdot \mid O_d)$ and $V_{s_d} = V_{s^\circ}(\cdot \wedge O_d)$ — then $B$ is strictly observation-calibrated for $C$.

*Proof.* At a queried $d$ with $\nu(O_d) > 0$: $P_{s_d}(X) = P_{s^\circ}(X \wedge O_d)/P_{s^\circ}(O_d) = \nu(X \mid O_d)$; and $V_{s_d}(X) = V_{s^\circ}(X \wedge O_d) = \mathbb{E}_\mu[r \mid \lambda \models X \wedge O_d]$, the second equality being clause 2 of prior calibration — without which the value side would be unconstrained, so the prior-state upgrade of Definition 11 is what makes the proposition true rather than half-true. $\square$

**Definition 12 (zero-respecting procedures).** $C$ is *zero-respecting* if for every $d$ with $A_d^+ := \{a \in A_d : P_{s_d}(a) > 0\} \neq \emptyset$, $C(d)$ is supported *only* on $A_d^+$ — that is, $\mathrm{supp}\, C(d) \subseteq A_d^+$: the procedure may introduce further zeros of its own (a deterministic choice among several subjectively possible actions is zero-respecting), but never puts weight on an action the state has ruled out.

*Remark 3.9 (trembles over masking).* Masked calibration exists to give evidential choice content: on any problem strictly calibrated for a deterministic $C$ and recording at its points, $A_d^+ = \{C(d)\}$, so $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ approves *every* deterministic procedure — the maximization domain collapses to whatever is already being done (Remark 3.6). But the existential mask buys EDT's content at two costs. First, it requires the states within one problem to be inconsistent with one another: under the local form, a state consulted *earlier* is calibrated under true continuations — it knows the agent will cross the street — while the state at the crossing itself has been freed of exactly that knowledge; the ignorance is indexed to the moment of action, a strange thing for an account of belief to demand. Second, it deletes the resource the tickle defence runs on: Eells's reply to Smoking Lesion screens off the common cause through the agent's knowledge of its own inclinations, which is just the self-knowledge strict calibration supplies and masking removes — a defence of EDT that forbids EDT's best-known defence. Definition 10 is the EDT-flavoured notion with neither cost: it *refines* Definition 8 rather than weakening it (Lemma 2), so self-knowledge and the tickle machinery survive; what it adds is discipline at the unrealized margin — off-path states must match near-tremble statistics, forbidding incredible off-path stipulations — and its learning-theoretic reading is exact: an evidential learner with vanishing exploration is $\varepsilon$-calibrated throughout training, and Definition 10 is the $\varepsilon \to 0$ shadow of that process (Remark 5.4, §9). How the five-and-ten verdict is then delivered matters, because Definition 10 alone does not deliver it: the take-5 pair is limit-calibrated — the limit conditional *is* the 5-world — and no state-honesty condition can forbid what is a fact about the procedure. Two routes finish the job. Design-stance: evaluate EDT *along* the trembles rather than at their limit. At every $\varepsilon > 0$ the calibrated state has both actions subjectively possible with values $5$ and $10$, EDT selects the $10$, and take-5 is never a best reply to its own trembled statistics; this is trembling-hand perfection transposed, made precise as tremble-consistency in §3.2 (Remark 3.13). Advice-stance: give the limit-point state itself the missing information, pinning act-conditional belief and value by their tremble limits — equivalently, trembles induce a canonical *evidential* counterfactual structure, total, success-satisfying, defined at self-knowledge-nulled acts, a third candidate referent for Remark 3.11's question (Q6). The routes agree wherever the limiting values have a strict maximum and differ at limit-ties, where the sequence route is the finer refinement — exactly the trembling-hand phenomenon.

**Lemma 2 (limit calibration refines strict calibration).** If $B$ is limit-calibrated for $C$, it is strictly observation-calibrated for $C$; the converse fails.

*Proof.* Each leaf probability under $C^\varepsilon$ is a polynomial in $\varepsilon$ whose value at $0$ is $\mu_{B,C}(\ell)$. So wherever $\nu_{B,C}(O_d) > 0$, eventually $\nu_{B,C^\varepsilon}(O_d) > 0$ and the limiting conditionals are the strict ones; events of positive strict conditional probability have non-vanishing limits, so both clauses transfer. Separation: take the prophet's tree with the off-path state stipulated certain of $(10,10)$ while $C$ takes five at *both* points. Then $\nu_{B,C}(O_{10}) = 0$ and strict calibration is vacuous there, but $\nu_{B,C^\varepsilon}(\cdot \mid O_{10}) \to \delta_{(10,5)}$: the promised off-path behaviour is not what near-tremble scrutiny reveals. $\square$

**Definition 13 (subjective-state calibration).** With $\mathrm{occ}(d)$ and $\#_d$ as in Definition 6: $B$ is *per-run SSC for $C$* if for every queried $d$ with $\mu(\mathrm{occ}(d)) > 0$,
$$P_{s_d}(X) = \mu\big(\{\ell : \lambda(\ell) \models X\} \,\big|\, \mathrm{occ}(d)\big) \quad \text{for all } X \in \mathcal{E},$$
and *per-occurrence SSC* if instead $P_{s_d}(X) = \mathbb{E}_\mu\big[\#_d \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\lambda \models X}\big] / \mathbb{E}_\mu[\#_d]$; $V$-clauses analogous. Per-run conditioning weights a run once however often $d$ occurs in it (SSA-like); per-occurrence weights by occurrence count (SIA-like). The per-occurrence normalizer $\mathbb{E}_\mu[\#_d]$ reappears as the weight system of Theorem 1, and the per-run conditioning as the pivot of Theorem 2 — the anthropic fork is not decided here because §8 shows each branch is the bookkeeping of one local-optimality theorem.

**Proposition 2 (per-run SSC lifts to strict OC by superconditioning).** Let $B$ be per-run SSC for $C$. Over the lifted algebra $\mathcal{E}' := 2^{\mathrm{Leaves}(B)}$ — whose worlds are exactly the leaves, as principal ultrafilters — define $B'$ with the same tree, $\lambda' = \mathrm{id}$, payoffs unchanged, and lifted points $d' := (s'_d,\ \mathrm{occ}(d),\ \{\lambda^{-1}(a) : a \in A_d\})$ where $P_{s'_d} := \mu(\cdot \mid \mathrm{occ}(d))$ and $V_{s'_d}$ its conditional value; let $C'$ agree with $C$ at lifted points. Then the run distribution and value are unchanged; each lifted state pushes down to the original ($\lambda_* P_{s'_d} = P_{s_d}$ — this equation *is* the per-run SSC hypothesis); and $B'$ is strictly observation-calibrated for $C'$, the lifted observation being $\mathrm{occ}(d)$ itself.

*Proof.* By construction, as in v1; the observation-in-the-point design makes the lift cleaner, since the lifted observation needs no separate labelling apparatus. $\square$

*Caveat 2.1.* The lifted states are new objects with credences over runs — over which node of the tree they inhabit, including hypothetical instances. This is the post's rescue manoeuvre ("agents who know that they might be imaginary"), and whether $B'$ counts as the same problem is the superconditioning question (Question 3).

**Observation 1 (strict OC does not imply SSC).** In Transparent Newcomb (§7.2) with a perfect predictor and a two-boxing agent, the hypothetical query of $d_F$ lies on every run, so $\mathrm{occ}(d_F) = \mathrm{Leaves}$ while the runs end in empty-box worlds a.s.; per-run SSC would force $P_{s_{d_F}}(\text{full}) = 0$ against the state's certainty of fullness, while strict OC is vacuously satisfied there since $\nu(O_F) = 0$.

**Proposition 3 (the veridicality bridge).** Fix $C$ and a queried $d$. If every $d$-node is subtree-veridical and $B$ covers $d$, then $\mathrm{occ}(d) = \{\ell : \lambda(\ell) \models O_d\}$ up to $\mu$-null sets, and consequently the per-run SSC equation at $d$ and clause 1 of strict OC at $d$ are the *same equation*. If this holds at every queried point, $B$ is per-run SSC for $C$ iff strictly observation-calibrated for $C$.

*Proof.* Subtree-veridicality gives $\mathrm{occ}(d) \subseteq \{\lambda \models O_d\}$; coverage gives the reverse inclusion a.s.; conditioning on equal events yields equal conditionals. $\square$

*Remark 3.10 (what the bridge measures).* The divergence between the post's two calibration notions is exactly the symmetric difference $\mathrm{occ}(d) \,\triangle\, \{\lambda \models O_d\}$. Simulation queries populate $\mathrm{occ}(d) \setminus \{\lambda \models O_d\}$ — consulted in worlds where the observation is false; unconsulted observation-worlds populate the reverse. The Proposition-2 lift restores subtree-veridicality by enriching worlds until "some instance of me observes $O_d$" holds wherever the query occurs, which is precisely the Cartesian modelling move in [Taylor's post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5bd75cc58225bf06703751b2/in-memoryless-cartesian-environments-every-udt-policy-is-a): "we have to isolate Omega's simulation of the agent as a copy of the agent". So observation-calibration handles simulation by conditioning on the world-event and leaving hypothetical instances free (the updateless-friendly reading), while subjective-state calibration handles it by making the agent anthropically uncertain whether it *is* the simulation; the lift is the formal bridge between the readings, and §8 shows the two local-optimality theorems distribute over exactly this divide. One dogmatic–radical note: in a radical scenario with $O_d = \top$ everywhere, strict OC collapses (every queried state must equal $\nu$ outright), while SSC retains content through occurrence patterns — occurrence-conditioning is the calibration notion that does not presuppose evidence-events.

*Remark 3.11 (does the counterfactual structure get an objective referent?).* The definitions above equate $P_s$ and $V_s$ with objective statistics; should $\mathrm{cf}_s$ be calibrated too? The tree semantics offers two intrinsic counterfactuals — deviation at a decision-point, $C[d \mapsto a]$, and forcing a single query instance, the $G_q$ of Theorem 1 — and these are exactly the counterfactuals under which EDT-style and CDT-style local evaluation become theorems (§8). But note what adopting either as *the* referent of $\mathrm{cf}_s$ would do: under both, a predictor's output covaries with the action, so classical CDT ("hold the prediction fixed") would be ruled miscalibrated in Newcomb's problem by definitional fiat — begging the question the framework is supposed to adjudicate. Classical CDT's counterfactual is a third one, not tree-intrinsic. So $\mathrm{cf}_s$ stays uncalibrated by default, an opinion of the state constrained only by success; Question 6 records the alternative, and is where any further rules for $\mathrm{cf}_s$ would be *proved* from calibration assumptions rather than posited.

### 3.2 Abstract decision problems

**Definition 14 (abstract decision problem).** An *abstract decision problem* $\Sigma$ is a set of concrete decision problems — equivalently, the models of a set of stipulations about chance structure, queried points, observations, states, payoffs. A member $B \in \Sigma$ is an *instantiation*.

Two motivations. First, informally posed problems *are* stipulation sets: "Smoking Lesion" fixes a correlation here, an independence there, and stays silent about everything else, so the object it denotes is the set of concrete problems meeting the stipulations, not any one of them. Second, $C \mapsto \mu_{B,C}$ is total — trees run on anything — so whatever "you can't consistently put procedure $X$ into problem $Y$" means, it cannot mean undefinedness at the concrete level; it is a relation between a procedure and an *abstract* problem, and stating it requires the notions of §4 (taken up in §5). Note also that concrete problems now have *two* generalizations, pointing in different directions: the extensional quotient $\widehat{B}$ of §3.1 forgets how the interaction is structured, while the abstract level underspecifies which structured interaction is faced. Neither subsumes the other — a functional realized by no tree is not a set of trees, and a two-element set of trees is not a functional — and the document uses both, for different jobs.

**Definition 15 (consistency).** Fix a calibration sense $\kappa \in \{\text{strict}, \text{masked}, \text{limit}, \text{per-run SSC}, \text{per-occurrence SSC}\}$: an abstract decision problem $\Sigma$ (Definition 14) is *$\kappa$-consistent for $C$* if some instantiation is $\kappa$-calibrated for $C$, else *inconsistent for $C$*.

*Remark 3.12 (tremble-consistency).* Definition 15 composes with trembling: say $\Sigma$ is *tremble-consistent for $C$ with respect to a theory $T$* if for every sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ some instantiation $B_\varepsilon \in \Sigma$ is strictly calibrated for $C^\varepsilon$ and $T(C, B_\varepsilon)$ holds — the procedure must be normative against its own trembled statistics, honestly instantiated. This is trembling-hand perfection transposed, and it does what Remark 3.9 wanted with no new machinery: on the five-and-ten stipulations (one point, leaf payoffs $5$ and $10$, states free), take-10 is tremble-consistent with respect to $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ and take-5 is not, since every $\varepsilon$-calibrated state makes both actions subjectively possible with values $5$ and $10$, and the support condition then excludes the $5$. (Test-sequence and $\exists/\forall$ variants parallel those pending for Definition 10.)

**Definition 16 (making an event inconsistent).** $C$ *makes $X \in \mathcal{E}$ $\delta$-inconsistent in $\Sigma$* if every instantiation $\kappa$-calibrated for $C$ has $\nu_{B,C}(X) \leq \delta$; "inconsistent" is $\delta = 0$.

*Remark 3.13.* "You can't consistently put CDT into Smoking Lesion" cannot mean undefinedness (trees run on anything, as above); it means the Smoking-Lesion abstract problem is inconsistent for CDT (§7.3 proves more). Definition 16 is the formal content of the title of Soares's essay, with the probabilistic reading at $\delta > 0$: in Transparent Newcomb V2($p$), the both-cases one-boxer makes $\{\text{seen} = E\}$ $(1{-}p)$-inconsistent.

## 4. Decision procedures and decision theories

The named procedures first; the theories that recommend them follow.

**Definition 17 (four procedures of interest).** Convention: an argmax set becomes a mixed action by uniform mixture, and an empty maximization domain falls back to $\mathrm{Unif}(A_d)$. Write $A_d^+ := \{a \in A_d : P_{s_d}(a) > 0\}$. The updateful pair:
$$\mathrm{EDT}(d) := \mathrm{Unif}\ \arg\max_{a \in A_d^+} V_{s_d}(a), \qquad \mathrm{CDT}(d) := \mathrm{Unif}\ \arg\max_{a \in A_d} V^a_{s_d}(a).$$
Evidential choice takes the news value of the act at the current state — necessarily restricted to subjectively possible acts, so EDT is zero-respecting by construction (Definition 12) — while causal choice takes the supposed value of the act and is total, because $\mathrm{cf}$ is. For a *prior state* $s^\circ$ and a *policy interpretation* $\rho : (d : \mathcal{D}) \to A_d \to \mathcal{E} \setminus \{\bot\}$, reading $\rho_d(a)$ as the event that the agent's policy answers $a$ at $d$, the updateless pair:
$$\mathrm{UDT}_{s^\circ\!,\, \rho}(d) := \mathrm{Unif}\ \arg\max_{a \,:\, P_{s^\circ}(\rho_d(a)) > 0} V_{s^\circ}(\rho_d(a)), \qquad \mathrm{cUDT}_{s^\circ\!,\, \rho}(d) := \mathrm{Unif}\ \arg\max_{a \in A_d} V^{\rho_d(a)}_{s^\circ}(\rho_d(a)).$$
The updateless pair evaluates the *disposition* rather than the act, at the *prior* rather than the current state. EDT and CDT are closed terms; UDT and cUDT consume parameters — the formal shape of the post's observation that updatelessness has data costs the other theories do not pay.

*Remark 4.1 (recommended, not yet judged).* These are the procedures *recommended by* the respective decision theories. The theories themselves — predicates on (procedure, problem) pairs, saying whether a procedure is normative on a problem — are Definition 18, deferred until decision problems exist to be quantified over.

*Remark 4.2 (the policy interpretation is not optional).* One might hope $\rho_d(a) := a$ suffices, making the prior state the sole updateless parameter. Counterfactual Mugging (Proposition 6's $B_1$) is the witness that it does not. There the act-event "pay" is disjoint from the heads branch (the choice coordinate reads $\bot$ there), so conditioning on it — and equally any success-obeying supposition of it — confines all weight to tails-worlds and gives the heads-side transfer probability zero: prior evaluation of the *act* refuses, whatever the prior thinks of the predictor. The material-conditional repair $\rho_d(a) := \neg O_d \vee a$ fails differently, by antecedent reweighting: at self-belief $q_0 = \tfrac12$ the two sides score $\tfrac{y-x}{3}$ against $\tfrac{y}{3}$ and it refuses, while at other $q_0$ its verdict drifts — the antecedent probability is doing the deciding, not the stakes. What updateless evaluation needs is an event true in exactly the worlds where the *disposition* holds, heads-worlds included: with a would-pay coordinate, $V_{s^\circ}(\text{would-pay}) = \tfrac12(y-x) > 0 = V_{s^\circ}(\text{would-refuse})$, and paying wins iff $y > x$, as it should. No such event exists in that example's algebra as modelled — whether $\mathcal{E}$ events the agent's dispositions is a richness condition on the algebra (Remark 6.3, Question 1). So $\rho$ is the formal residue of "UDT needs a self-model": the updateless procedures require a prior that not only weighs worlds but locates the agent's own policy among the events.

**Definition 18 (decision theory).** A *decision theory* is a predicate $T$ on pairs of a decision procedure and a concrete decision problem, $T(C, B)$ read: $C$ is normative on $B$ by $T$'s lights. Two shapes recur. *Advocacy*: each evaluator of Definition 17 yields a theory approving *every* resolution of its ties. $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}(C, B)$ holds iff at every queried point $\mathrm{supp}\, C(d) \subseteq \arg\max_{a \in A_d^+} V_{s_d}(a)$ (no constraint where $A_d^+ = \emptyset$); $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$, $T_{\mathrm{UDT}_{s^\circ, \rho}}$, $T_{\mathrm{cUDT}_{s^\circ, \rho}}$ analogously, each with its own act value and maximization domain. Any mixture over maximizers is normative; the uniform-tie procedures of Definition 17 are canonical witnesses, so the $\exists C\, \forall B$ shape survives — $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}(\mathrm{EDT}, B)$ for every $B$, and likewise CDT. *Optimality*: $T_{\mathrm{opt}}(C, B)$ holds iff $V_B(C) = \max_{C'} V_B(C')$ — the updateless theory as the post construes it, endorsing per-problem optima, the $\forall B\, \exists C$ shape. How $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ relates to the parameterized procedures $\mathrm{UDT}_{s^\circ, \rho}$ and $\mathrm{cUDT}_{s^\circ, \rho}$ — for which priors and policy interpretations the recommended procedure attains the optimum — is Question 10. One further observation: Definition 15's consistency generalizes verbatim from calibration senses to arbitrary theories — $\Sigma$ is *$T$-consistent for $C$* iff some instantiation has $T(C, B)$ — recovering Definition 15 when $T$ is a calibration sense and making "calibrated and approved", the conjunction the tie-freedom remark below solves for, a $T$-consistency instance.

*Remark 4.3 (tie-freedom is where equilibria live).* Approving mixtures over maximizers is not a permissive afterthought: conjoined with a calibration epistemology, it is what makes the normative layer solvable at all. Miniature: one point $d$ with actions $\{a, b\}$; a predictor draws an independent sample from $C(d)$; the leaf pays $2$ on (live $a$, sample $b$), $1$ on (live $b$, sample $a$), else $0$. Under strict calibration the state reports the true mixture, so with $q := C(d)(a)$ the act values are $V_{s_d}(a) = 2(1-q)$ and $V_{s_d}(b) = q$. For $q < \tfrac23$, $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ demands support $\{a\}$, forcing $q = 1$ — contradiction; for $q > \tfrac23$, support $\{b\}$, forcing $q = 0$ — contradiction; at $q = \tfrac23$ the values tie, every mixture is approved, and calibration pins the mixture to $(\tfrac23, \tfrac13)$: the unique jointly calibrated-and-approved answer, the mixed Nash equilibrium of the agent against its own statistics, indifference across the support being the equilibrium signature. A theory restricted to any *fixed* tie-break has no solution here — the uniform break at the tie yields $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, whose calibrated label $q = \tfrac12$ is not at a tie — so the tie-freedom is doing existence work, the same work mixing does in Nash's theorem.

**Definition 19 (epistemology).** An *epistemology* is a decision theory whose verdict is a function of the queried decision-points that the pair can reach — the subjective material the interaction surfaces — consulting neither the numerical statistics $\mu_{B,C}$, $\nu_{B,C}$ nor the payoffs. It judges the discipline of the states, not their fit to the world; in particular calibration (§3.1), which equates states with objective statistics, is a decision theory but *not* an epistemology. Examples: the *dogmatic* epistemology relative to a prior state $s^\circ$ (every reachable queried state is the Jeffrey conditioning of $s^\circ$ at its observation, as in Proposition 1); the *radical* epistemology (always approves — no discipline beyond the type); observation-functionality (reachable points with equal $O_d$ carry equal $(P, V)$ components).

**Definition 20 (counterfactual theory).** A *counterfactual theory* is a decision theory whose verdict is a function of the counterfactual structures $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d}$ of the reachable queried points — the same subjectivism as Definition 19, aimed at the $\mathrm{cf}$ component. Example: the *evidential criterion* — every such $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d}(a)$ agrees with $(P_{s_d}(\cdot \mid a),\ V_{s_d}(\cdot \wedge a))$ wherever the conditioning is defined. By contrast, equating supposed statistics with the deviation statistics $\nu_{B, C[d \mapsto a]}$ consults the objective side and is therefore *counterfactual calibration*, not a counterfactual theory; it belongs to §3.1's family, and Remark 3.11's question-begging warning governs it.

*Remark 4.4 (memorylessness, nearly forced).* v1 assumed memorylessness — same observation, same point. With observations inside points the assumption dissolves into a normative criterion: "queried points with equal $O_d$ carry equal states". Observation-calibration nearly delivers it at positively-observed labels: two calibrated states sharing $O$ must agree in their $P$ and $V$ components, both being pinned to $\nu$-statistics, but not in their counterfactual structure — so procedures can still distinguish same-observation points by the causal opinions they carry, and full memorylessness is the conjunction of an epistemology with a counterfactual theory, not a consequence of calibration.

## 5. The fixed-point problem

There is a conceptual gap in the machinery so far (due to Tim Parker). States are node *labels* — constants of a concrete problem. Calibration equates a label with a $C$-dependent statistic; in particular, mixed-strategy ratifiability requires the state presented at $d$ to already report $C$'s own mixing probability *at $d$*. An equation between a constant and a function of $C$ can only be solved by choosing the constant per-$C$ — so something outside the tree selects the concrete problem that fits the procedure. Can the selection be internalized as earlier queries? Only partly, and the boundary is quantifiable:

**Proposition 4 (probe expressiveness).** Let $A_d = \{a, \bar{a}\}$ and $q := C(d)(a)$. Over concrete problems that query $d$ exactly $k$ times on every path and contain no other $d$-nodes, the functions $q \mapsto \Pr(X)$ realizable for an event $X$ are exactly
$$\Big\{\, q \mapsto \sum_{j=0}^{k} b_j \tbinom{k}{j} q^j (1-q)^{k-j} \;:\; b_0, \ldots, b_k \in [0,1] \,\Big\}.$$

*Proof sketch.* Forward: conditioning on the pattern $S \subseteq [k]$ of $a$-draws, $\Pr(X) = \sum_S c_S\, q^{|S|}(1-q)^{k-|S|}$ with $c_S \in [0,1]$; grouping by $|S|$ averages the $c_S$ into $b_j \in [0,1]$. Backward: the tree observes the pattern and routes to a chance node realizing $b_{|S|}$. $\square$

Consequences. "Fill the box with probability equal to your mixing probability" costs one probe ($k = 1$, $b_0 = 0$, $b_1 = 1$). Any continuous dependence is approximable ($b_j = f(j/k)$, Bernstein), but no exact threshold such as $[q > \tfrac{1}{2}]$ is realizable at any finite $k$, and no label can *report* $q$ at all. So the finite, pure-strategy part of fixed-point selection internalizes as adaptive probing — the Told-You-So tree of §7.1 is exactly this — while the mixed/continuum part is the residue that the abstract level exists to host.

*Remark 5.1 (the branch/label principle).* $C$-dependence has exactly one inlet internal to a concrete problem: branching on sampled draws. Call this *branching-dependence*; it is calibration-governed and Proposition 4 measures its reach. Dependence of the *description* on the agent — *label-dependence* — is inexpressible internally by the type of Definition 5, and lives at the abstract level, where its honesty condition is the fixed-point equation. Ratifiability is to labels what calibration is to branches. This is offered as the principled distinction the two-level structure needs: it is derived from the type rather than stipulated.

*Remark 5.2 (existence and multiplicity).* Two residues deserve to stay visible rather than be papered over. Existence: for argmax-style procedures the map $q_0 \mapsto C(d(q_0))(a)$ across a family of self-describing labels is discontinuous and can lack fixed points entirely; smoothed evaluators (trembles, softmax) regain existence by Brouwer, paralleling Nash's theorem needing mixed strategies. Multiplicity: self-fulfilling-prophecy problems have several calibrated instantiations with different values, so the $\exists$ of Definition 15 and the $\forall$ of Definition 16 are already substantive selection policies — optimistic and adversarial respectively — and §7.1's predictor is adversarial selection internalized for the finite pure case.

*Remark 5.3 (parallels and repairs).* A concrete problem paired with calibration is close to a Kreps–Wilson *assessment* with their *consistency* condition — which they, too, define via limits of full-support trembles, independently matching Definition 10 — and "who selects the fixed point?" is the standing critique of rational-expectations equilibria. The static repair in that literature is a stability refinement (E-stability; Evans–Honkapohja): privilege fixed points that are locally attracting under the perceived-law $\mapsto$ actual-law adjustment map, a condition checkable *at* the fixed point. This would import the learning picture's verdicts about reachable equilibria without importing its machinery (Question 5). A different refactor dissolves the objection by relocation: strip states from problems (decision nodes carry $(O, A)$ only), let an *agent* be a state-assignment (contexts $\to$ states) paired with a choice rule, and split calibration into environment-side honesty and agent-side self-knowledge; producing a self-consistent state is then *deliberation*, for which Skyrms's deliberational dynamics is an existing formalism — probability-of-one's-own-action evolving within a single decision to a fixed point, the uncalibrated intermediates being deliberational stages rather than environmental queries. v2 does not adopt the refactor (states-in-problems keeps decision-points as the interface the advice stance recognizes) but records it as the third road between unexplained selection and full learning theory.

*Remark 5.4 (why learning collapses the levels).* In the learning-theoretic picture no selection is needed because equilibrium is reached by iteration. Proposition 4 says why, statically: repeated interaction is repeated probing, and as $k$ grows, branching-dependence Bernstein-approximates any continuous label-dependence — sampling estimates dispositions, so branching swallows labels in the limit. The calibration picture is the static shadow of that process, and this is the precise sense in which it is the inferior-but-intended model: it sets aside the dynamics while keeping the equilibrium condition, exactly as the practice of decision theory does.

## 6. Comparing procedures

**Definition 21 (value comparison; optimality).** Fix a calibration sense and a class $\mathfrak{B}$ of concrete problems. $C \succeq C'$ iff $V_B(C) \geq V_B(C')$ for every $B \in \mathfrak{B}$ calibrated for both. $C$ is *optimal on $B$* if $V_B(C) = \max_{C''} V_B(C'')$; the maximum exists by continuity of $V_B$ on a finite product of simplices, and is attained by a deterministic procedure whenever no path contains two nodes sharing a decision-point (then $V_B$ is multiaffine in the tied variables) — but not in general: Proposition 5(c) is a problem whose best procedure is properly stochastic. (v1 wrongly asserted vertex-attainment outright; see the change log.)

**Proposition 5 (per-problem optima; procedures versus assignments).** (a) An *assignment* is a map $\pi$ from decision nodes to actions with $\pi(q) \in A_{d_q}$; $V_B$ extends to assignments and $\max_\pi V_B(\pi)$ is attained. (b) $\max_C V_B(C) \leq \max_\pi V_B(\pi)$, with equality whenever some optimal assignment is constant on the fibers of $q \mapsto d_q$ — in particular whenever that map is injective. (c) Strictness is possible: one point $d$ at two nested nodes (first node: $a \to$ leaf with payoff $0$, $b \to$ second node; second: $a \to$ payoff $4$, $b \to$ payoff $1$); assignments reach $4$; deterministic procedures get $0$ or $1$; the best procedure is $C(d)(a) = \tfrac{1}{3}$ with value $\tfrac{4}{3}$.

*Proof.* (b): with independent redraws, the realized node-action profile is a random assignment with product law, so $V_B(C)$ is an average of assignment values. (c): $\max_x (1-x)(3x+1) = 4/3$. $\square$

*Remark 6.1.* (c) is Piccione–Rubinstein's absent-minded driver in miniature. Since observations now live inside points and procedures read them, the gap requires two nodes with identical $(s, O, A)$ — genuine subjective indistinguishability — which is the regime of §8.

*Remark 6.2 (UDT and the prior).* Define $\mathrm{UDT}_B$ := any procedure agreeing with an optimal assignment wherever fibers permit; when $q \mapsto d_q$ is injective on queried nodes, $\mathrm{UDT}_B$ is optimal on $B$. The subscript is the point: the optimal answers depend on $B$, the run-time input is the decision-point, so the $B$-dependence must be built in — the prior. v1's typing wrinkle (recovering one's location from the state) is dissolved by observations being readable; what survives, by Proposition 6, is that the *decision-point does not determine the optimal act*, so UDT's claims take the form $\forall B\, \exists C$ against CDT/EDT's $\exists C\, \forall B$ — the quantifier-order statement of the cost recorded in Remark 3.7.

**Proposition 6 (no uniform optimum; where the prior bites).** Let $\mathcal{E}$ be the finite algebra generated by coordinates coin $\in \{H, T\}$, choice $\in \{\mathrm{pay}, \mathrm{refuse}, \bot\}$, transfer $\in \{0,1\}$; leaf payoffs $r = -x\,\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{pay}] + y\,\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{transfer}]$ read off the leaf's world, $0 < x < y$. Fix $d = (s, O_T, \{\mathrm{pay}, \mathrm{refuse}\})$ with $O_T = \{\text{coin} = T\}$, $P_s(T) = 1$, $P_s(\text{transfer} = 0) = 1$, $P_s(\mathrm{pay}) = q_0$, $V_s$ the conditional expectation of the payoff formula under $P_s$. Two problems query $d$ and nothing else: $B_1$ (Counterfactual Mugging) — fair coin; on $T$, query $d$, pay $\to$ leaf $(T, \mathrm{pay}, 0)$, refuse $\to (T, \mathrm{refuse}, 0)$; on $H$, query $d$ hypothetically, pay $\to (H, \bot, 1)$, refuse $\to (H, \bot, 0)$. $B_2$: identical with the $H$-branch transfers swapped. With $q := C(d)(\mathrm{pay})$: $V_{B_1}(C) = \tfrac{1}{2} q (y - x)$, $V_{B_2}(C) = \tfrac{1}{2}(y - q(x + y))$. Both are masked-calibrated for *every* procedure: take the self-model with $m(\mathrm{pay}) = q_0$, and the state is exactly $\nu_{B_i, C[d \mapsto m]}(\cdot \mid O_T)$ — its action-credence realized rather than exempted, its $T$-conditional worlds shared by the two problems (interior $q_0$; Appendix B for the boundary). Optimality on $B_1$ forces $q = 1$, on $B_2$ forces $q = 0$; no procedure is optimal on both. $\square$

*Remark 6.3 (richness of the algebra).* Under strict calibration, $B_i$ is calibrated only for procedures with $q = q_0$, so the argument runs pointwise through fixed points of $q_0 \mapsto C(d(q_0))(\mathrm{pay})$, with the existence caveat of Remark 5.2. More important is the relativity: the construction lives on an algebra too poor to record the predictor's rule. Enrich $\mathcal{E}$ with that rule as an event and calibration at $O_T$ forces the two states apart, the points separate, and one procedure serves both. "The prior is ineliminable" is thus equivalent to "there is decision-relevant structure not recorded in calibrated world-beliefs" — a property of the chosen $\mathcal{E}$, not a theorem-for-free (Question 1).

**Proposition 7 (rigidity of strict calibration).** If $B$ records at $d$ for both $C$ and $C'$, is strictly observation-calibrated for both, and $\nu_{B,C}(O_d) > 0$, $\nu_{B,C'}(O_d) > 0$, then $C(d) = C'(d)$ in $\Delta(A_d)$.

*Proof.* Recording makes $\{\lambda \models O_d\}$ coincide a.s. with reaching the unique $d$-node (Proposition 3's identity is contained in the recording clauses), and subtree-veridicality makes membership in $O_d$ independent of the draw there; action-veridicality with the only-via-the-draw clause then gives $\nu_{B,C}(a \mid O_d) = C(d)(a)$, and clause 1 of Definition 8 equates both procedures' mixtures with $P_{s_d}$ on actions. $\square$

*Remark 6.4.* Consequence: under strict calibration, procedures are value-comparable on a shared problem only if they play identically at every commonly-reached recording point; differences must reroute which observations are realized. One-shot common-point comparisons are impossible under strict calibration, so masked and limit calibration are preconditions for the comparative methodology, not mere EDT-accommodations.

## 7. Worked examples

### 7.1 Told-You-So Five-and-Ten

$\mathcal{E}$ generated by coordinates $n \in \{5, 10\}$ (announced) and $m \in \{5, 10\}$ (taken); leaf payoffs $r = m$ read off the leaf's world. Points $d_k = (s_k,\ \{n = k\},\ \{\{m{=}5\}, \{m{=}10\}\})$ for $k \in \{5, 10\}$, with $P_{s_k}$ certain of the world $(k, k)$ and $V_{s_k}$ its conditional value. Tree $B_P$ — a truthful predictor announces only what it has verified the agent does *given the announcement*, trying the worse prophecy first: root = query $d_5$: $\{m{=}5\} \to$ leaf $(5,5)$; $\{m{=}10\} \to$ query $d_{10}$: $\{m{=}10\} \to (10,10)$; $\{m{=}5\} \to (10,5)$ (the contrarian branch: the prophecy is simply false, and the problem miscalibrated for such procedures, appropriately).

**Proposition 8.** Let $C_0$ be any zero-respecting procedure and $C^\ast$ any procedure taking $\{m{=}10\}$ at both points ($\mathrm{UDT}_{B_P}$, or a dominance reasoner). Then $B_P$ is strictly, masked-, and limit-calibrated for both, and $V(C_0) = 5 < 10 = V(C^\ast)$.

*Proof.* $P_{s_5}(\{m{=}10\}) = 0$, so $C_0(d_5) = \{m{=}5\}$ and $\nu_{C_0} = \delta_{(5,5)}$: at $O_5$, $\nu(\cdot \mid O_5) = \delta_{(5,5)} = P_{s_5}$; $\nu(O_{10}) = 0$ vacuous. For $C^\ast$ symmetrically with $\delta_{(10,10)}$. Limit calibration: under $C_0^\varepsilon$, $\nu_\varepsilon(\cdot \mid O_5) = \delta_{(5,5)}$ exactly and $\nu_\varepsilon(\cdot \mid O_{10}) \to \delta_{(10,10)}$, matching $P_{s_{10}}$. $\square$

The four v1 remarks stand, restated compactly. (1) The defect is the *policy* of respecting stipulated zeros; $C_0$'s local reasoning at $d_5$ is impeccable given that policy, and the fallback structure preys on the policy itself — the calibration-level shadow of Löbian spurious proofs, with the adaptive announcer playing the proof-search order. (2) A procedure evaluating actions by tremble-limit conditional value takes ten at both points and thereby makes $O_5$ *inconsistent* (Definition 16): the slogan enacted — it does not beat $C_0$ inside the announce-five branch, it makes that branch never happen. (3) One decision-point cannot register any of this; the defect lives in the relation between $d_5$ and $d_{10}$, i.e. in the fallback structure, which is why problems must be interactions. (4) Problem-side limit calibration does *not* exonerate $C_0$; only procedure-side trembling rescues, confirming the two devices are distinct. New in v2: $B_P$ is Remark 5.2's adversarial selection internalized — a two-probe search over the finite pure-strategy fixed points, which Proposition 4 licenses; the mixed analogue would not internalize.

### 7.2 Transparent Newcomb: the variant matters

$\mathcal{E}$ generated by fill $\in \{0,1\}$, act $\in \{\mathrm{both}, \mathrm{large}\}$, with seen $:= F$ iff fill $= 1$; leaf payoffs $r = \mathrm{fill}\cdot L + S\cdot\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{act} = \mathrm{both}]$ read off the leaf's world, $L > S > 0$. Points $d_F, d_E$ with observations $O_F = \{\mathrm{seen} = F\}$, $O_E$, states certain of fill $= 1$ resp. $0$. Predictor reliability $p \in (\tfrac12, 1]$. **V1($p$)**: query $d_F$ (hypothetically), answer $x$; chance sends the run to the full branch with probability $b_1(x) = p$ if $x = \mathrm{large}$, else $1 - p$; the full branch queries $d_F$ and leads to a fill-$1$ leaf recording the act, the empty branch queries $d_E$ likewise. **V2($p$)**: query $d_F$ then $d_E$ hypothetically, answers $(x, y)$; branch with $b_2 = p$ if $(x,y) = (\mathrm{large}, \mathrm{large})$, else $1 - p$; real branches as in V1. Policies $(x, y)$, $1 =$ large-only, $2 =$ both:

| policy | V1($p$) | V2($p$) |
|---|---|---|
| $(1,1)$ | $pL$ | $pL$ |
| $(1,2)$ | $pL + (1{-}p)S$ | $(1{-}p)L + pS$ |
| $(2,1)$ | $(1{-}p)(L{+}S)$ | $(1{-}p)(L{+}S)$ |
| $(2,2)$ | $(1{-}p)(L{+}S) + pS$ | $(1{-}p)(L{+}S) + pS$ |

**Proposition 9.** In V1($p$), $(1,2)$ weakly dominates $(1,1)$ (strictly for $p < 1$), and the optimum is $(1,2)$ iff $(2p{-}1)L > pS$, else $(2,2)$: no optimal V1 policy one-boxes at the empty box. In V2($p$), the optimum is $(1,1)$ iff $(2p{-}1)L > S$, and deviating at the empty box alone then costs $(2p{-}1)L - pS > 0$. *Proof:* pairwise differences from the table. $\square$

*Corollary 9.1 (correction to the post).* The footnote's claim that the variants "don't matter much" fails for every $V$-optimizer: the optimal act at the empty box flips between V1 and V2. The main text states the V1 rule and then argues in V2 terms; the empty-box argument for updatelessness requires a rule that consults the empty-case answer. Didactically, V2 is the variant this essay needs. Under masking, all four policies are calibrated for $p < 1$; at $p = 1$ the unrealized observation is vacuous under Definitions 8–9 and pinned by Definition 10; under *strict* calibration, Proposition 7 forbids common-point comparisons and forces the rerouting reading. Slogan instance: relative to the V2 abstract problem, $(1,1)$ makes $O_E$ $(1{-}p)$-inconsistent.

**Proposition 10 (one problem, two verdicts — the event-conditioned wedge).** In V2($p$) with $(2p{-}1)L > S$, $p < 1$: say $C$ is *myopically satisfied at* $O_E$ if $\nu_{B,C}(O_E) > 0$ and $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{B,C}}[r \mid \lambda \models O_E] \geq \mathbb{E}_{\mu_{B, C[d_E \mapsto a]}}[r \mid \lambda \models O_E]$ for all $a$. The $V$-optimal $(1,1)$ is not myopically satisfied at $O_E$ (conditional value $0$ against the deviation's $S$); every myopically satisfied policy takes both there and has $V \leq (1{-}p)L + S < pL$. And $\nu_{B,(1,1)}(O_E) = 1{-}p$ while $\nu_{B,(1,2)}(O_E) = p$: the deviation moves the probability of the very event conditioned on — the term the myopic functional discards and $V$ retains. $\square$

*Remark 7.1.* This wedge is specifically an *event-conditioning* pathology, and §8 shows that repairing the conditioning dissolves the wedge without vindicating the two-boxer: in V2 the hypothetical query of $d_E$ lies on every run, so $\mathrm{occ}(d_E) = \mathrm{Leaves}$ and the occurrence-conditioned local evaluation of Theorem 2 just *is* the global evaluation. The anthropic lens — "I might be the simulation, and the simulation always runs" — recovers updatelessness exactly, which is the concrete instance of the bridge of Proposition 3.

### 7.3 Smoking Lesion: inconsistent for everyone, or dissolved

$\mathcal{E}$ generated by $(\ell, m, k) \in \{0,1\}^3$; leaf payoffs $r = \alpha m - \beta k$ read off the leaf's world, $\alpha, \beta > 0$. Abstract problem $\Sigma_{SL}$: (S1) chance $\ell \sim \mathrm{Bern}(\rho)$, $k \sim \mathrm{Bern}(\gamma_\ell)$ with $\gamma_1 > \gamma_0$, cancer drawn from the lesion only; (S2) the queried state's conditionals correlate: $P_s(k \mid m{=}1) > P_s(k \mid m{=}0)$, both defined; (S3) a single queried point with $O_d = \top$ — the maximally uninformative observation, so only the radical epistemology (Definition 19) admits the scenario's states without further discipline. Instantiations: chance $\ell$; query $d$; act $m$; chance $k$; leaf $(\ell, m, k)$.

**Lemma 3 (screening-off).** In any instantiation where each run realizes at most one $d$-node and post-query chance depends on the path only through pre-query chance and the drawn action: conditional on the realized node, the draw is independent of pre-query events; under (S1)+(S3), $m \perp (\ell, k)$ under $\nu_{B,C}$ for every $C$. *Proof:* Definition 6 samples the draw independently of the path. $\square$

**Proposition 11.** Under (S3), $\Sigma_{SL}$ is inconsistent for *every* procedure, deterministic included, with no detour through limit calibration: a masked-calibrated state equals $\nu_{B, C'}(\cdot \mid \top)$ for some full-support $C'$, and Lemma 3 applies to $C'$ as to any procedure — $k \perp m$ under every realizable worldview — so $P_s(k \mid m{=}1) = P_s(k) = P_s(k \mid m{=}0)$, contradicting (S2). The stipulated state is not a possible worldview of any instantiation. $\square$

**Proposition 12 (the tickle instantiation dissolves it).** Replace (S3) by lesion-informative observations $O \in \{\{\ell{=}1\}, \{\ell{=}0\}\}$ with distinct points. Masked-calibrated instantiations of (S1) then exist for any $C$; in every such instantiation each queried state satisfies $P_s(k \mid m{=}1) = P_s(k \mid m{=}0)$ (Lemma 3 within the observation), so (S2) fails at every decision-point even while the population-level correlation $\nu(k \mid m{=}1) > \nu(k \mid m{=}0)$ can hold when $C$ smokes more at the lesioned state; and conditional-value maximization at a calibrated state computes a difference of $\alpha > 0$ and smokes. $\square$ This is Eells's Tickle Defence in the present vocabulary: calibration relocates the correlation from the decision-point's conditionals, where it would be decision-relevant, to the population statistics, where it is not; the residue of Smoking Lesion that survives calibration is a problem on which EDT and CDT agree — and the decision-point formalism could not even state what was wrong, since the wrongness is a relation between stipulated states and realizable statistics, the thing Definition 14 quantifies over.

## 8. Local optimality: two theorems and what coherence turns out to be

For a decision node $q$ define $R_q(C) := \mu_{B,C}(\text{reach } q)$ and $G_q(C, a) := \mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\,r \mid \text{reach } q,\ \text{force } a \text{ at } q \text{ only}\,]$ — forcing the single instance, all other nodes (including other $d_q$-nodes) still drawing from $C$. These are the two intrinsic counterfactuals of Remark 3.11: single-instance forcing here, all-instance deviation $C[d \mapsto a]$ below.

**Theorem 1 (global optimality implies CDT+SIA ratifiability).** If $V_B(C) = \max_{C'} V_B(C')$, then for every queried $d$:
$$\mathrm{supp}\, C(d) \;\subseteq\; \arg\max_{a \in A_d} \sum_{q\,:\,d_q = d} R_q(C)\, G_q(C, a).$$

*Proof.* Regard $V_B$ as a function of one distribution $p_q$ per decision node: it is multilinear (each node occurs at most once per path), with $\partial V / \partial p_q(a) = R_q \cdot G_q(a)$ and $R_q$ free of $p_q$. Tying $p_q := C(d_q)$ and applying the chain rule over the fiber of $d$: $\partial V / \partial C(d)(a) = \sum_{q : d_q = d} R_q G_q(a)$. At a maximum over the product of simplices, first-order necessity gives, for each $d$, $\sum_a (r_a - C(d)(a))\, \partial V/\partial C(d)(a) \leq 0$ for all $r \in \Delta(A_d)$, i.e. the support condition. $\square$

Since $\sum_{q : d_q = d} R_q = \mathbb{E}_\mu[\#_d]$, the weights $R_q / \mathbb{E}_\mu[\#_d]$ are exactly the per-occurrence location probabilities of Definition 13: the condition says the drawn action maximizes SIA-weighted, single-instance-causal value. This transposes [Taylor's theorem](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5bd75cc58225bf06703751b2/in-memoryless-cartesian-environments-every-udt-policy-is-a) — her condition $\forall o, a:\ \pi(a \mid o) > 0 \Rightarrow a \in \arg\max_{a'} \sum_s SIA_\pi(s \mid o)\, Q_\pi(s, a')$ with deliberately unnormalized $SIA$ — node-for-state; the unnormalized form handles null observations there and vacuous fibers here. The condition is necessary, not sufficient: it is a Nash-style fixed-point property with bad equilibria (her cooperative-game analogy; per the comment thread, Piccione–Rubinstein 1997 p. 19 contains the result's ancestor and Aumann–Hart–Perry 1997 and Korzukhin 2020 exhibit non-optimal CDT+SIA policies — thread-sourced citations I have not independently verified).

**Theorem 2 (EDT+SSA ratifiability is point-deviation coherence).** Let $C$ be deterministic and $d$ queried with $\mu(\mathrm{occ}(d)) > 0$. Transpose Oesterheld's minimal-reference-class construction (comment on Taylor's post): the EDT+SSA value of $a$ at $d$ weights each instance $i$ on each leaf $\ell \in \mathrm{occ}(d)$ by $\mu_{B, C[d \mapsto a]}(\ell \mid \mathrm{occ}(d)) / \#_d(\ell)$. Then
$$\arg\max_a \sum_{\ell \in \mathrm{occ}(d)} \sum_{i} \frac{\mu_{C[d \mapsto a]}(\ell \mid \mathrm{occ}(d))}{\#_d(\ell)}\, r(\ell) \;=\; \arg\max_a V_B(C[d \mapsto a]),$$
and consequently every $V$-optimal deterministic $C$ satisfies $C(d) \in \arg\max_a V_B(C[d \mapsto a])$ at every queried $d$.

*Proof.* Summing over the $\#_d(\ell)$ instances cancels the SSA division — in Oesterheld's words, "setting the policy for all agents at the same time" cancels it — leaving $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{C[d \mapsto a]}}[r \mid \mathrm{occ}(d)]$. Its denominator $\mu_{C[d \mapsto a]}(\mathrm{occ}(d))$ is constant in $a$ by Lemma 1; and leaves outside $\mathrm{occ}(d)$ contribute to $V_B(C[d \mapsto a])$ a quantity constant in $a$, since paths avoiding $d$-nodes never consult $C(d)$. So the two argmaxes rank actions identically. Necessity from optimality is immediate: the global maximum survives every point-deviation. $\square$

**Definition 22 (coherence).** $C$ is *$B$-coherent* if $V_B(C[d \mapsto a]) \leq V_B(C)$ for every queried $d$ and $a \in A_d$ — no single decision, deviating unilaterally at *all* of its occurrences, can improve the interaction. A procedure-level Nash condition of the many decisions against each other; heart and lungs, neither able to profit by defection.

Three comments on what the theorems make of it. (i) Coherence now has an external pedigree: by Theorem 2 it is exactly EDT+SSA ratifiability, the anthropic layer cancelling — Wei Dai's thread observation that the SSA decomposition "ends up being just a null operation" is, in this framework, an identity. By Remark 2.2 the all-occurrences deviation is also the *only* well-typed deviation, so the EDT flavor is the type speaking, not a modelling choice. (ii) The two local conditions are distinct in general: when no path contains two $d$-nodes, $V_B$ is affine in $C(d)$ and they coincide; under self-succession (the absent-minded driver) Theorem 1 constrains the marginal one-instance change and Definition 22 the discrete all-instance change, and at the AMD optimum $x = \tfrac13$ both hold, as they must. (iii) Neither condition is sufficient — multiplicity of ratifiable policies is exactly Remark 5.2's multiplicity, and selection among them is not local. Caveat inherited from the sources: the minimal reference class ignores correlations between *distinct* decision-points ("isomorphic" situations under different observations — Conitzer's Dutch-book example, per Oesterheld's caveat comment); since procedures are independent entries per point, such correlations are unrepresentable by point-deviations. That is FDT territory and out of scope here by design.

*Remark 8.1 (the anti-compatibilist claim, refined).* v1 read Proposition 10 as "one interaction, two functionals, and the myopic functional is defective". §8 sharpens this. The wedge of Proposition 10 is an artifact of conditioning on the observation-*event*, whose probability the deviation moves; the occurrence-conditioned local functional is not so afflicted, and the $V$-optimum satisfies it (Theorem 2) — in V2-Transparent-Newcomb by way of $\mathrm{occ}(d_E) = \mathrm{Leaves}$, where occurrence-conditioning *is* global evaluation (Remark 7.1). So the position "UDT answers a different question" survives only in this form: an updateful evaluator can agree with the updateless verdict locally, but the repair requires (a) occurrence-conditioning — anthropic uncertainty about being the simulation, the SSC side of the bridge of Proposition 3 — and (b) settling for a necessary condition, with equilibrium selection still living at the level of $V$. Same problem; rival local answers; the coherent-care functional is the one whose ratifiable points contain the optimum.

**Definition 23 (conflict-freeness, a candidate for naturalist care).** For a family $\mathcal{M}$ of "moral patients" — sub-collections $D_i \subseteq \mathcal{D}$ — say $C$ is *conflict-free for $\mathcal{M}$ on $B$* if no $D_i$ has a joint deviation (revising $C$ on $D_i$ only) that improves $\mathbb{E}_\mu[r \mid \mathrm{occ}(D_i)]$, where $\mathrm{occ}(D_i) := \bigcup_{d \in D_i} \mathrm{occ}(d)$.

**Conjecture 1 (spectrum).** With $\mathcal{M}$ trivial ($\{\mathcal{D}\}$, and $\mathrm{occ} =$ everything when the root queries), conflict-freeness contains $V$-optimality (updateless endpoint); with $\mathcal{M}$ maximally fine and occurrence-conditioning replaced by event-conditioning, it degenerates toward Proposition 10's myopia (updateful endpoint); intermediate $\mathcal{M}$ interpolate, and the geometric-mean aggregation of Geometric UDT should correspond to a particular compromise point. Unverified; the intended formal home of the post's anti-conflict reading of care.

## 9. Learning, briefly

Calibration is the static shadow of a learning process, and Remark 5.4 gives the mechanism of the shadow-casting: iterated interaction is probing, and probing Bernstein-approximates label-dependence, so the two-level structure of §5 collapses dynamically. The equilibrium-selection question then becomes reachability — which calibrated fixed points are attractors of the actual dynamics — and the static stand-in is a stability refinement in the E-stability style (Question 5). None of this is developed here; the section exists to mark that the fixed-point problem is where the static and dynamic pictures shake hands.

## 10. Questions

**Q1 (richness).** Proposition 6's prior-dependence is relative to $\mathcal{E}$; enriching $\mathcal{E}$ dissolves the example. Is there a principled maximal $\mathcal{E}$ (all "policy-relevant" structure), and does prior-dependence survive it?

**Q2 (the calibration manifold).** Characterize $\{\nu_{B,C'}(\cdot \mid O_d) : C'\}$ — the realizable worldviews at $d$ — intrinsically: dimension, facial structure over the product of simplices, and which stipulated states lie on it (a pointwise sibling of Q11's image-of-the-fold). The credal candidate of Appendix B is its convex hull; masked calibration is membership; strict calibration is its ratifiable point.

**Q3 (superconditioning and identity).** When is the lift of Proposition 2 "the same problem"? A criterion would decide whether SSC is a genuinely different notion from OC or the same notion on a refined algebra — Proposition 3 answers "same equation" under veridicality-plus-coverage, so the residue is exactly the non-veridical case.

**Q4 (sufficiency gaps and selection).** Both Theorem-1 and Theorem-2 conditions admit non-optimal fixed points. Characterize the gap; is there a local-plus-stability condition (Q5) whose fixed points are exactly the optima on a natural problem class?

**Q5 (stability).** Formalize the E-stability-style refinement of Remark 5.3: a perceived-to-actual adjustment map on concrete instantiations whose locally-attracting fixed points are the privileged ones, checkable statically.

**Q6 (the counterfactual structure's referent).** Should $\mathrm{cf}_s$ be calibrated to one of the tree's intrinsic counterfactuals (deviation; forcing), given that either choice condemns classical CDT by fiat (Remark 3.11)? Is there a neutral referent, or is the absence of one itself the correct formal verdict on the CDT/EDT dispute? And which rules for $\mathrm{cf}_s$ beyond success follow from which calibration assumptions?

**Q7 (ontology).** Per-state algebras $\mathcal{E}_s$ with translation maps would let problems present points whose world-concepts differ (ontological crises). What replaces the shared-$\mathcal{E}$ calibration equations?

**Q8 (beyond finiteness).** Infinite trees with a.s.-finite runs (Taylor's setting has unbounded horizons with finite expected iterations); and the regime where countable meets are designated (equivalently joins, via De Morgan), in which pointless algebras force either Loomis–Sikorski representations or a reformulation of leaf-labelling with worlds replaced by ideals-of-events — the option space is mapped in the companion note *Worlds and Points*. The type-theoretic home for the infinite-tree half is coinductive: interaction trees (Remark 3.1), with the run distribution a final-coalgebra semantics in place of structural recursion.

**Q9 (anthropic residue).** Theorems 1–2 pair SIA with per-occurrence forcing and minimal-SSA with per-run deviation. Non-minimal reference classes (Oesterheld's caveat; Conitzer's Dutch book) and problems where per-run and per-occurrence calibration *disagree* about the same state remain unmodelled: which, if either, does the naturalist stance select?

**Q10 (faithful updatelessness).** For which prior states $s^\circ$ and policy interpretations $\rho$ do $\mathrm{UDT}_{s^\circ, \rho}$ and $\mathrm{cUDT}_{s^\circ, \rho}$ earn $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$'s endorsement on $B$? Prior-state calibration (Definition 11) and a faithfulness condition on $\rho$ — the $\rho_d(a)$-worlds tracking where the deviated runs $\mu_{B, C[d \mapsto a]}$ actually land — are the evident ingredients; the selection issues of Q4 presumably reappear as multiplicity of self-confirming $(s^\circ, \rho)$.

**Q11 (the image of the fold).** Which extensional functionals $\widehat{B}$ are realized by finite trees, and at what query cost? Proposition 4 answers exactly for one binary point queried $k$ times; wanted is the general characterization — several points, interleaved chance, per-point query budgets — that is, the image of Remark 3.1's fold: a realizability theory for decision problems.

**Q12 (correlated draws; procedures as correspondences).** Definition 6 samples every decision node independently from $C(d_q)$, so a simulation cannot share randomness with the live agent (Remark 3.2). What is the right relaxation? Candidates: shared-seed semantics (one sample per point per run, reused at every node carrying it); explicit correlation devices; and — an intuition recorded here deliberately unexplored — retyping decision rules as Kakutani maps, upper-hemicontinuous convex-valued correspondences from points to sets of mixed actions, with run semantics an equilibrium selection rather than independent sampling. Two hooks already in the document: the advocacy theories of Definition 18 return convex sets (support conditions are faces of the simplex — correspondence-valued recommendations), and the existence discussions of Remarks 4.3 and 5.2 are Kakutani's theorem's native habitat.

## Appendix A. Worlds beyond the finite case

Compressed from the companion note *Worlds and Points*, which has proofs, examples, a summary table, and presentation recommendations; this appendix records only what a reader of §0 might reasonably demand on the spot.

**Existence at $J = \emptyset$.** Ultrafilters separate every Boolean algebra (Stone, via the Boolean prime ideal theorem), so undesignated worlds always exist in abundance. A bonus on the probability side: every finitely additive probability on $\mathcal{E}$ extends to a genuinely $\sigma$-additive regular Borel measure on the Stone space, because clopens are compact — a countable disjoint clopen cover of a clopen is essentially finite, so $\sigma$-additivity on clopens holds vacuously and the Carathéodory extension applies.

**Witness-less worlds.** The price of $J = \emptyset$: on $\mathcal{E} = 2^{\mathbb{N}}$, any ultrafilter extending the cofinite filter contains $\mathbb{N} = \bigcup_n \{n\}$ but no $\{n\}$ — some number is selected, no number is it. No choice principle is needed for the phenomenon: in the finite–cofinite algebra the cofinite sets themselves form an ultrafilter, explicitly witness-less. Whether this is defect or resource (an "unspecified large number" is exactly what limit constructions want) depends on the subject matter — which is why designation is per-join and optional.

**Annihilation.** Designating too much can leave no worlds. On the Lebesgue measure algebra (Borel sets modulo null) with countable joins designated: atomlessness yields a decreasing chain of halves with measures $2^{-n}$; a world following the chain holds every element, and with the chain designated as a meet, constraint 4 applies in one step: the meet — which is $0$ in the algebra — must be held too. Contradiction; no worlds. (The join formulation needed a complement translation here; the meet formulation does not.) On $\mathrm{Borel}(X)$ for Polish $X$, by contrast, designating countable joins yields exactly the Dirac worlds $\delta_x$: null events are retained as distinct objects there, and that is what the worlds live on. The general repair for pointless algebras is Loomis–Sikorski: represent as a $\sigma$-field of sets modulo a $\sigma$-ideal, and read satisfaction modulo negligibility.

**Typing the designation.** Designation is officially by meets (§0); this appendix speaks the De Morgan-dual join language where the familiar examples live — a designated join is the complemented family of a designated meet, witnessing for the one being closure for the other. In either language the designated object must be a *family* $D \subseteq \mathcal{E}$, not an event: witnessing is family-relative, since one event is the supremum of many families. On $2^{\mathbb{N}}$, $\mathbb{N}$ is the supremum of the singletons and also of $\{\text{evens}, \text{odds}\}$; designating the singleton family bans exactly the free ultrafilters, while designating the finite family adds nothing (finite witnessing is automatic, Remark 0.1). The probability-side condition — in join form $P(\bigvee D) = \sup_{F \subseteq D \text{ finite}} P(\bigvee F)$, the De Morgan dual of §1's official meet form — needs no disjointness and unifies the two sides: its $2$-valued instance is precisely Definition 1's constraint 4. One can further ask $J$ to satisfy closure conditions (under subsumption, refinement, pullback along meets), at which point $(\mathcal{E}, J)$ sits in known territory: presentations of frames by generators and relations, and coverages on posets (Johnstone, *Stone Spaces*; Vickers, *Topology via Logic*) — moderate confidence on those two as the right entry points, high confidence that the construct-with-closure is standard in substance. Nothing here requires closure, and imposing it would change which $J$'s are expressible.

**The world/probability mismatch.** Worlds and probabilities cannot be handed the same maximal designation: a probability additive over *all* existing joins is purely atomic ($[0,1] = \bigvee_x \{x\}$ exists in $\mathrm{Borel}[0,1]$, and Lebesgue measure would need $1$ to be a supremum of finite sums of $0$). Diffuse belief lives exactly on undesignated joins, so pressure pushes worlds toward more designation and states toward less; where each sits is part of a model's content. The triad making this precise — doxastic individuation of events, diffuseness, existence of countably-consistent worlds: pick two — is §4 of the companion note.

## Appendix B. Calibration variants and unadopted candidates

**The masked family.** Definition 9 freed the self-model locally ($C' = C[d \mapsto m]$) and non-dogmatically ($m$ full-support); the other three combinations are live alternatives. *Global* masking lets $C'$ differ from $C$ everywhere: the state may then mis-model its own downstream behaviour, so act-conditionals at $d$ reflect continuations the agent will not follow — a thoroughgoing "no self-knowledge anywhere" subjectivism, open to the sophisticated-choice objection; the local form ties conditionals to true continuations and frees only the present act. *Plain* (dropping full support) permits unforced zeros: a state certain-against an action even at nodes where every full-support model gives it positive conditional probability. At subtree-veridical nodes the two support conventions genuinely differ; at selection nodes they agree (the routing-root certainty of Remark 3.7 is forced either way). On problems whose observation-branches involve a single decision-point — every worked example of §§6–7 — all four combinations coincide at interior self-models.

**Calibration notions not yet adopted (recorded for future review).**

The definitions above occupy a few cells of a larger grid: *which state-component* is calibrated ($P_s$, $V_s$, $\mathrm{cf}_s$, $P^\circ$), against *which statistic* (the world-marginal $\nu_{B,C}$; the joint world–payoff law $\widehat{B}(C)$; the run distribution $\mu_{B,C}$), under *which reference procedure* (actual $C$; trembled $C^\varepsilon$; deviated $C[d \mapsto a]$; single-instance forced). Candidates from the empty cells, none adopted, in descending order of expected usefulness:

1. *Belief/value split.* Unbundling clause 1 from clause 2 of Definitions 8–9 makes "calibrated beliefs, miscalibrated stakes" expressible — plausibly the thematically load-bearing notion for *Coherent Care* (care-calibration as distinct from belief-calibration). Wrinkle to resolve before adoption: the averaging axiom couples $V_s$ to $P_s$, so clause-1-without-clause-2 is clean while clause-2-without-clause-1 needs a decision about which $P$ the averaging runs over.
2. *Self-transparency* as a named notion: $P_{s_d}(a) = C(d)(a)$ on $A_d$. Already used namelessly — derived in Remark 3.6, consumed by Proposition 7's proof, freed existentially in Definition 9 — and naming it would let statements say "calibrated except for self-transparency" without routing through a subalgebra.
3. *$\varepsilon$-calibration*: total-variation slack in the calibration equations. Candidate repair for the fixed-point existence failures of Remark 5.2, on the pattern of $\varepsilon$-Nash (exact self-describing labels may fail to exist where approximate ones exist); also the natural setting for asking whether Theorems 1–2 degrade gracefully.
4. *Mixture calibration*: $P_{s_d} = \bar{\nu}(\cdot \mid O_d)$ with $\bar{\nu} = \int \nu_{B,C}\, d\pi(B)$ for a measure $\pi$ on an abstract problem's instantiations — strictly between dogmatic and radical, gives the prior something to be a mixture of, and is the static stand-in for asymptotic-frequency calibration (§9). Checked this session: Smoking Lesion's inconsistency (Proposition 11) survives mixing, since $\nu_B(m) = C(d)(m)$ is constant across instantiations and the screening-off argument passes through the integral.
5. *Credal calibration*: $P_s$ an imprecise set, calibrated iff the realizable statistics lie within it; the $\exists$/$\forall$ selection policies of Remark 5.2 become the state's own imprecision rather than a metatheoretic choice. Least developed of the five.

One conjecture-level observation from the grid (unverified beyond the absent-minded-driver case): under independent redraws, *procedure-side* tremble-conditioning ("given that I drew $a$ at this node") delivers single-instance information, so a tremble-EDT evaluator should recover Theorem 1's forcing counterfactual $G_q$ rather than Theorem 2's all-instance deviation — which would align the two tremble devices distinguished in Definition 10's remark with the two theorems of §8 respectively.
