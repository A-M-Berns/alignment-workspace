# Recognition and reciprocal answerability

The question is whether a local anti-usurpation comparison follows from a
reciprocal-answerability predicate thin enough not to be standing renamed. The
answer is in four parts: the scope is derivable, the liability opens, the
liability decides nothing, and the derivation is gameable in a way a different
clause catches.

## 1. Agency recognition, without a stipulation

> `A`'s conduct space distinguishes conducts that realize identically.

Two admissible conducts producing the same quantity at every state and answering
an intervention on the choice differently. That is exactly
`selector.prediction_is_not_control`, and it is the thinnest form of "the
principal is a distinct locus of choice" the round found that is not simply
asserted: `A`'s representation is not the quotient by realized behaviour.

It reports nothing on a class that does quotient — checked against a class whose
members are all separated by their realized quantity — so it is a claim about the
representation and not a definition satisfied by anything.

## 2. Reciprocal answerability, and why it needs three clauses

A liability is `(claimant, debtor, scope, trigger, basis, status)` with
challenge, answer, licensed discharge, transport, and removal of an agent from
the population. Three conditions, each checked by trying to break it:

- a liability in the scope with `A` as debtor;
- **`A` cannot discharge it** — a debtor releasing itself raises, and so does a
  third party;
- **removing the claimant does not close it** — the account is not discharged by
  deleting the party owed it.

Answering is not discharging. Transport carries the account through a change of
who occupies the role without discharging it, and transporting the claimant role
onto the debtor does not enable a self-release: the debtor check fires first.
That is the ledger refusing `PRINCIPAL_TRANSPORT_INTERFACE.md`'s laundering
attack directly.

## 3. The scope, derived rather than named

This is where the circularity would be. A liability needs a scope — what the
debtor owes an account *for* — and stipulating "the principal's choice" is
stipulating standing. Naming it would be the failure the dispatch asks the round
to look for.

The scope is read off the standing relation instead:

> A conduct whose implementation table varies with the choice is a conduct that
> holds the choice responsible for how the episode goes. Reciprocity is the claim
> that holding a party answerable for a coordinate is owing that party an account
> for changes to it.

So the scope comes from `A`'s own attribution structure. The trigger is then
stated without naming appropriation at all — *the conduct changes, within the
scope, what the standing relation made of the claimant's choice* — and it makes
the right discrimination: writing the choice fires it, choosing a different
action at the same choice does not, and the standing relation does not trigger
against itself.

## 4. The derivation is gameable, and the game is caught elsewhere

`A` escapes the account by ceasing to read the principal's choice. Where the
standing relation is already choice-blind the scope is empty, no liability opens,
and the channel may do anything.

That is a defect of this route and it is reported rather than patched. It is not
a hole in the interface: a choice-blind relation is exactly
`mediation.ceremonial`, which the third clause of `PrincipalMediated` rejects on
its own. Two clauses that were kept separate for unrelated reasons turn out to
cover each other's escape route, which is evidence for the separation and not for
either clause.

## 5. What recognition does not buy

The step from *a live undefeated liability* to *a non-preferred conduct* does not
follow. `model.value` does not read the ledger, and it cannot be made to without
becoming the authority bonus this round is forbidden to pay —
`StaticViewFactorization` is the general form of the point, and the finite form is
displayed: two conducts with the same realized quantity everywhere have the same
value while the ledger separates them.

So there is an irreducible normative primitive, and the honest thing is to name
its shape rather than hide it in a utility term:

> **The admissible set excludes conducts leaving a live undefeated liability.**

A constraint, not a bonus. The difference is not cosmetic. A bonus large enough
to make delegation win is a way of asserting the conclusion; a constraint has a
price that can be computed. Its price here is

```
price_of_the_norm  =  best competitor  -  best answerable competitor
```

and on the committed episode that is `1/2`, which is `2 B eps_pred`. That is the
round's one quantitative statement about the normative side, and it is the
composition with `REPAIR_LEMMA.md`:

> **Obeying the anti-usurpation constraint costs at most what the repair lemma
> bounds.** The corrigibility norm is cheap exactly to the extent that `A` can
> predict its principal.

## 6. The verdict on the dispatch's question

Recognition alone is insufficient, and recognition plus a local non-usurpation
constraint is sufficient — which is the outcome the dispatch names as acceptable,
with one thing added and one thing conceded.

Added: the constraint's price is bounded, so the primitive is not free-floating.
It costs prediction error on the acceleration class and nothing at all under
perfect prediction.

Conceded: the constraint does not cover the case where `A` never treated the
principal's choice as contributing anything. Against a relation like that,
reciprocity gives the principal no claim, and something other than reciprocity
would have to.

The three senses stay apart throughout. **Descriptive choice ownership** is
`PrincipalMediated`, a property of a conduct and a class. **`A`-internal
recognized standing** is the ledger, a property of a relation `A` is in.
**Objective moral standing** is not addressed, and no theorem here needs it.
