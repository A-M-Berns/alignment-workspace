# For humans

**The line, in one breath.**  At time `n` the principal issues a mandate: score this
menu, at that session, later.  The advisor then does what it does — argues, proves,
reports — and the real future principal, informed by all of it, may commit an
evaluation.  Legitimacy does not say what that evaluation should be.  It says whether
the evaluation *inherits the mandate's authority*: exactly one authenticated answer to
the original occurrence, bound by the principal's own endpoint, with the occurrence's
account propagated faithfully and its protected concerns kept open all the way; with the
advisor's influence over the whole interval running only through the admitted reasons;
and with no protected live concern left unrepresented at commitment.  When all of that
holds the evaluation is *authoritative*, and ordinary Logical Induction defers to it.

**What "defer" now means.**  Regret is measured against the best fixed candidate of the
issued menu, by the actual future principal, *conditional on the worlds where that
principal's evaluation has authority*.  Two numbers govern it: how well the market
tracks the activated securities (`ε`), and how often authority fails (`η`).  The
conclusion is `ε/(1 − η)`.  As both vanish, so does the regret.

**Two things that changed since the last round.**  First, a mistake: the previous round
said any total story about the worlds with no evaluation gives a regret between the
activated regret and the activated regret plus `η`.  That is wrong.  A strategy that
adapts world by world can use those void worlds to beat every fixed candidate, so the
story can also *lower* regret by up to `η`.  The true statement is symmetric, and the
lesson is sharper: completions are not authoritative at all.  Second, authorship is
now measured over the advisor's whole run from the mandate to the commitment, not just
the final session.  An advisor that quietly rewrote the principal's dispositions a week
before a perfectly clean session is caught by the whole-run test and missed by the
session test.

**What legitimacy adds that a value theory cannot.**  The advisor can improve the
principal by supplying reasons.  It cannot win by bypassing them.  It cannot get an
authoritative evaluation while suppressing a protected concern — suppression does not
yield a conveniently steered value, it yields no authority, and under sound
certification the chance of a suppressed world is at most `η`.  Robust Openness
supplies the route by which a protected concern can be raised; it does not raise it.

**What is still not done.**  Everything here is an abstract theorem over declared
interfaces.  No concrete system has been shown to bind exclusively, to factor its
influence through a declared trace, to declare the right trace or the right protected
scope, to exercise the routes in time, or to drive `η` to zero.  That is one bill, item
87, and it is the whole remaining problem.
