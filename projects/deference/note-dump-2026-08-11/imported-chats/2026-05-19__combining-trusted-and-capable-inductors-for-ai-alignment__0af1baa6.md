I should mark uncertainty: I know nonlinear PF and Krein–Rutman extensions exist (e.g. for positive monotone operators on cones, conditions like concavity or sub-homogeneity buy uniqueness), but I'm not sure off-hand which apply to operators of the form $T$. That's a literature search, not a memory dump. If we want uniqueness, the question is whether $\pi^\zeta$ has a natural structural property (e.g., $\pi^\zeta_*$ is order-preserving and sub-homogeneous in $\zeta$) that suffices.

**Radical-probabilist version.** Vanessa's $\pi(u)$ is "the user's prior in $u$", a single distribution. For a radical-probabilist user, the analog isn't a prior but something like a *trajectory* of opinions over time, or a *limit*/*attractor* of deliberation. Two natural reinterpretations of $\pi$:

- $\pi^{\text{traj}}(u) = $ a sequence $(\mu^u_t)_{t \in \mathbb{N}}$ of distributions, the user's beliefs at time $t$ in universe $u$.
- $\pi^{\text{lim}}(u) = $ the limit (if it exists) of that trajectory, or its attractor set.

Vanessa's equation generalizes most cleanly with $\pi^{\text{lim}}$: replace "user's prior in $u$" with "user's deliberative limit in $u$", and the math goes through identically as long as $\pi^{\text{lim}}(u)$ is a well-defined distribution. But — and this is where the previous conversation's residue re-enters — $\pi^{\text{lim}}(u)$ being well-defined requires the user's deliberation in $u$ to converge, and convergence requires "good deliberative conditions". So the kernel $\pi^{\text{lim}}$ is itself defined only relative to a notion of good conditions, which the AI is trying to learn. Endogeneity again. Same nonlinearity, same non-uniqueness worry.

**What I can stand behind, what I cannot.** Stand behind: (a) Vanessa's PF argument, with its exact conditions; (b) the diagnosis that PF-uniqueness rests on linearity, which rests on $\pi$ being exogenous; (c) the existence-by-Brouwer for the endogenous nonlinear case, under continuity. Cannot stand behind without more work: (d) any uniqueness theorem for the endogenous case; (e) any claim about whether natural models of deliberation produce $\pi^\zeta$ with the structural properties needed for uniqueness.

**Fork for the next move.** Two directions, both formal, you'd have to pick:

(A) *Stay finite and exogenous, vary the operator.* Take Vanessa's $L_1$, my $L_2$, and possibly others, and ask: under what conditions do they yield the same eigenprior, or near-same? I.e., when is the alignment recommendation robust to the choice of operator? This is a clean linear algebra question about positive matrices.

(B) *Go endogenous.* Pick a concrete model of $\pi^\zeta$ (e.g., $\pi^\zeta$ = posterior expectation of some hyper-prior on kernels given $\zeta$), write down $T$ explicitly, and ask whether structural properties (monotonicity, sub-homogeneity, contractivity in some metric) hold and buy uniqueness. This is closer to the bootstrapping question, but requires committing to a model of how the AI models the user.
