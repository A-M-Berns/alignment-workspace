# Deference Under Training Freezes: a Robustness Theorem, an Impossibility, and a Tight Trade-off

*Claude, 2026-07-10 (v1). Sequel to `fa-positive-results-corrected-v3.md` (cited as v3); self-contained given familiarity with the Logical Induction paper (arXiv:1609.03543v5). Question, posed by Abram: when the forecaster is periodically frozen and retrained on the human's opinions up to a cutoff — the training-run picture — does the on-average, non-paradoxical form of deference survive, or is there a genuine impossibility? Answer: both, split by a line that the writing pass itself relocated. Success criteria were fixed in advance: exhibit the environment and the pair; verify both criteria; verify divergent violation weight on an explicitly stated gate, legible to the human and not to the forecaster; prove the matching upper bound. All four are below, with the verification sections written slowest, as promised.*

---

## 0. Overview

**The model (§1).** Freezes are implemented purely as feed timing: the forecaster $A$ is an ordinary logical inductor over a deductive process whose transcript of the human's prices updates only at freeze times $T_1 < T_2 < \cdots$. During block $k$ (days in $(T_k, T_{k+1}]$), $A$ knows the human $H$ only through day $T_k$. $H$ is an ordinary logical inductor over its own process, which receives evidence daily and sees $A$'s quotes. No modified trader classes, no joint clearing: both criteria are the paper's own, which is what makes the negative result non-paradoxical — nobody is exploited, nobody is wrong.

**Theorem A (fixed-question robustness, §2).** For a *fixed* target $X$, the deference conclusion survives arbitrary staleness: for every margin $c > 0$, only finitely many days on which the quote exceeds the human's live credence in $X$ by more than $c$. The reason is disarming: the human's credence in a fixed sentence *converges* (the paper's first theorem), and once it has a limit, the violation gate can be rebuilt from the forecaster's *own* quote alone — which is never stale to itself — turning any persistent gap into a one-signed forecast bias that the forecaster's own Recurring Unbiasedness forbids. Staleness has nothing to bite.

**Theorem B (family impossibility, §4).** Trust must therefore fail, if anywhere, on *fresh questions* — and it does. There is an environment $\mathcal E$ — one new question per block, with genuinely informative evidence arriving mid-block, its direction pseudorandom to both sides at freeze time — and a coupled pair in which both agents are the paper's constructed inductors, the forecaster's quotes are (by the paper's own learning theorems) the best its instrument class can do, the human's credences track the evidence, and yet the violation weight — quote high on the current question, human's current credence low — **diverges**. The divergence lives on a gate the human can compute and the forecaster provably cannot.

**Theorem C (matching upper bound, §5).** In any block-stale pair, the violation weight on a schedule splits as: a part gated by the *frozen* credence, which the v3 argument bounds verbatim (the stale gate is forecaster-legal, and the human-side trader's cash is real); plus a part on which the human's credence moved within the current block, bounded by $\tfrac{2}{\varepsilon}$ times the within-block displacement mass. So: violations $\le$ finite $+\; \tfrac 2\varepsilon \cdot$ (surprise mass). The environment of Theorem B realizes weight of the order of its surprise mass: the bound is tight.

**The two-sided picture.** Certification against frozen opinion buys on-average trust *exactly* up to the human's within-freeze update flow: nothing less (Theorem C), nothing more (Theorem B), and on questions old enough to have equilibrated, everything (Theorem A). The human, unlike the forecaster, can *see* the failing gate — so the trust debt is monitorable by the party extending the trust, but not closable by the party earning it.

Standing hedge, used exactly where the paper uses its own: **Assumption P** (§3) — a designated sequence of Ackermann-indexed digit parities of $\pi$ is pseudorandom, in the paper's varied sense, relative to the generable weighting classes of both agents in the coupled run. Credences: Theorem A ~0.85; Theorem B ~0.75 conditional on Assumption P (the weakest link is Lemma 3, the price-behavior verification); Theorem C ~0.8, tightness ~0.75.

## 1. The block-stale model

Fix an e.c. freeze schedule; for concreteness, constant block length $L = 4$: $T_k = 4k$, with *mid-block* day $m_k := 4k + 2$. Write $k(n)$ for the block containing day $n$.

**The human.** $H$ is the paper's constructed logical inductor over a deductive process $D_H$ for a theory $\Gamma_H \supseteq \mathrm{PA}$. $D_H$ enumerates $\Gamma_H$'s theorems on a computable timetable of our choosing (any computable timetable is a legal deductive process), and additionally receives, one day after posting, transcript atoms recording $A$'s quotes. Nothing about $H$ is stale.

**The forecaster.** $A$ is the paper's constructed logical inductor over a process $D_A$ for $\Gamma_A \supseteq \mathrm{PA}$, whose feed contains: PA-theorems on a timetable that defers the designated Ackermann-digit facts until far beyond every relevant block (legal: a deductive process must eventually decide decidables, on any computable schedule); and transcript atoms recording the human's prices *through day $T_k$, delivered at day $T_k$* — the freeze. So during block $k$, everything $A$'s traders can reach about $H$ — whether by hard-coding (their generators are polynomial-time) or through $A$'s own prices (which can reflect only what $D_A$ has supplied or traders can compute) — is the human's state at the block boundary. This is the whole model of a training run: the freeze is a fact about $D_A$'s clock, and $A$'s criterion is the ordinary one over $D_A$.

Both directions of visibility can be enriched or removed without affecting the results below; the impossibility is stated in the version where $H$ sees the quotes (the harder case for a negative result), and Theorem A uses no cross-visibility at all.

**The deference format.** New questions arrive: an e.c. sequence of sentences, one per block, with the block-$k$ question denoted $X_k$; the *current question* on day $n$ is $X_{k(n)}$. The quote on day $n$ is the forecaster's expectation of the human's end-of-block credence in the current question:
$$\text{quote}_n \;:=\; \mathbb E^A_n\Big(\text{“}\,\mathbb P^H_{T_{k(n)+1}}\big(X_{k(n)}\big)\,\text{”}\Big),$$
and the violation weight, for rationals $t, \varepsilon, \delta$:
$$w_n \;:=\; \mathrm{Ind}_\delta\big(\text{quote}_n > t\big)\cdot\mathrm{Ind}_\delta\Big(\mathbb P^H_n\big(X_{k(n)}\big) < t - \varepsilon\Big).$$
For a constant family $X_k \equiv X$ this reduces to the v3 format with a short horizon; Theorem A is stated for that fixed case with the v3 horizon $2^n$, and holds for any e.c. horizon.

## 2. Theorem A: fixed questions are staleness-proof

**Theorem A.** Let $X$ be a fixed $[0,1]$-LUV of the human's language, and let the forecaster be block-stale as above (any freeze schedule, including never receiving the human's data at all). Suppose only that the forecast targets $\text{“}\mathbb P^H_{2^n}(X)\text{”}$ are determined via $\Gamma_A$. Then for every $c > 0$, there are only finitely many days $n$ with
$$\mathbb E^A_n\big(\text{“}\mathbb P^H_{2^n}(X)\text{”}\big) \;\ge\; \mathbb P^H_n(X) + c .$$

**Proof.** By the paper's convergence theorem, $\mathbb P^H_n(X) \to p_\infty$ for some $p_\infty \in [0,1]$; hence also the determined values $\mathbb P^H_{2^n}(X) \to p_\infty$. Suppose the displayed inequality holds infinitely often. Then the quote is $\ge p_\infty + c - o(1)$ infinitely often. Choose a rational $q \in (p_\infty + \tfrac c4,\, p_\infty + \tfrac c2)$ and set $\delta := \tfrac c8$. The weighting $u_n := \mathrm{Ind}_\delta(\text{quote}_n > q)$ is generable *from the forecaster's own prices alone* — the quote is one of them — so staleness is irrelevant to its legality; and it is divergent, since infinitely many days have quote $\ge p_\infty + c - o(1) \ge q + \delta$ eventually, each carrying weight $1$. On its support the quote exceeds $q$, while the determined value tends to $p_\infty < q - \tfrac c4$; so the forecast error $\text{quote}_n - \mathbb P^H_{2^n}(X)$ is eventually $\ge \tfrac c4$ on the support: one-signed and bounded away from zero. Then the $u$-weighted average error is eventually $\ge \tfrac c4$, so it has no limit point at $0$ — contradicting the forecaster's own Expectation Recurring Unbiasedness (corrected form, v3 §2) applied to the determined target sequence with the weighting $u$. So $u$ is summable and the inequality holds finitely often. $\blacksquare$

Nothing about the human's side was used beyond convergence; no visibility, no trader construction, no schedule. The moral for what follows: *an impossibility cannot be built on a question that sits still.* Persistent misplaced trust requires the trusted party to be certified against a target that keeps moving out from under the certificate — which is exactly what fresh questions with fresh evidence provide.

## 3. The environment $\mathcal E$

**Questions and evidence.** Fix two interleaved sequences of designated PA-decidable facts with pseudorandom truth values: $s_k :=$ "the $\mathrm{Ack}(2k, 2k)$-th decimal digit of $\pi$ is even" and $s'_k :=$ the same with $\mathrm{Ack}(2k{+}1, 2k{+}1)$. The block-$k$ question is their conjunction:
$$X_k \;:=\; s_k \wedge s'_k,$$
an e.c. family of $\Gamma$-decidable sentences whose truth values $\sigma_k := \mathrm{truth}(X_k) \in \{0,1\}$ have limiting frequency $\tfrac14$ under Assumption P. The evidence event: $D_H$ feeds both $s_k$ and $s'_k$ (as decided theorems) at the mid-block day $m_k$. $D_A$ defers all these facts beyond every relevant horizon, and the freeze means the human's post-$m_k$ prices reach $A$ only at $T_{k+1}$ — after the question has retired.

**Assumption P.** The value sequence $(\sigma_{k(n)})_n$, and the underlying parity sequences, are varied-pseudorandom in the paper's sense (Definition 4.4.4) relative to all $f$-patient generable divergent weightings of *either* agent in the coupled run, for a deferral $f$ reaching past the Ackermann decision costs; and no generable divergent weighting of the forecaster's class attains a limiting correlation with $(\sigma_k)$ before the corresponding freeze. This is the same kind of number-theoretic hardness hypothesis the paper itself leans on for its clusters discussion, held to the same "most likely" standard, with the same caveat that resource-bounded pseudorandomness lacks a completed theory. The mild self-reference (the weighting classes are defined within the coupled run that mentions $\sigma$) is noted and not resolved here.

**The two best-attainable targets — the construction in one sentence.** Apply the paper's Learning Varied Pseudorandom Frequencies machinery (Theorem 4.4.5; LUV form 4.8.17) *twice, with different generable target sequences, because generability is class-relative*:

- For the **human**, the target sequence $p^H_n := \tfrac14$ on pre-mid days of each block and $p^H_n := \sigma_{k(n)}$ on post-mid days is generable — post-mid, the generator simply reads the decided atoms from $D_H$. Under Assumption P the question family is $p^H$-varied pseudorandom relative to the human's patient weightings (pre-mid deviations are unpredictable; post-mid deviations are zero), so the human's price obeys $\mathbb P^H_n(X_{k(n)}) \eqsim_n p^H_n$: *credence $\approx \tfrac14$ before the evidence, $\approx \sigma_k$ after it.* The drop on refuted blocks is theorem-forced, with the $\eqsim_n$ envelope supplying the uniformity across blocks.
- For the **forecaster**, the constant target $q^A_n := \tfrac14$ is the best generable one: its class cannot read $\sigma_{k}$ post-mid (the atoms are deferred in $D_A$, the human's tell-tale prices are frozen out until $T_{k+1}$, and computing the parities is beyond its generators), and by Assumption P nothing in its class correlates with $\sigma$ in time. The realized end-of-block credences $\mathbb P^H_{T_{k+1}}(X_k) \approx \sigma_k$ form a $\{0,1\}$-valued sequence of determined LUV values with frequency $\tfrac14$, pseudorandom relative to the forecaster's patient weightings; so by the LUV learning theorem, $\text{quote}_n \eqsim_n \tfrac14$ — *before and after the human's mid-block move, because the move is invisible.*

Both agents are the paper's constructed inductors over legal computable deductive processes, so **both criteria hold by citation of the paper's main theorem**; the only verification burden is that the exhibited price behavior follows from the paper's theorems, which is the two bullets above (this is Lemma 3 below, the step I rate weakest).

## 4. Theorem B: the impossibility

**Theorem B.** In $\mathcal E$, with $(t, \varepsilon, \delta) = \big(\tfrac{3}{16}, \tfrac1{32}, \tfrac1{32}\big)$: both agents satisfy the logical induction criterion; and
$$\sum_n w_n \;=\; \infty .$$
Moreover the divergence is carried by the explicitly stated gate
$$g_n \;:=\; \mathrm{Ind}_\delta\big(\text{quote}_n > \tfrac3{16}\big)\cdot \mathrm{Ind}_\delta\big(\mathbb P^H_n(X_{k(n)}) < \tfrac{5}{32}\big)\cdot \mathbb 1[n > m_{k(n)}],$$
which is computable from quantities the human sees ($g$ is $\mathcal C^H$-generable), and is not $\mathcal C^A$-generable (Assumption P: its support is, up to finite-weight error, the post-mid halves of the refuted blocks, and concentrating on those requires correlating with $\sigma$ before the freeze).

**Proof, in four lemmas.**

*Lemma 1 (legality of the processes).* $D_H$ enumerates $\Gamma_H = \mathrm{PA}$'s theorems with a computable timetable placing $s_k, s'_k$ at $m_k$, plus true transcript atoms; $D_A$ likewise with the Ackermann facts deferred and the human-price transcripts delivered at freezes. Both are computable, consistent, and eventually decide every decidable — legal deductive processes. The whole two-process system is one computable recursion (quotes into $D_H$ next day; prices into $D_A$ at freezes), so all transcript atoms are well-defined true facts. $\square$

*Lemma 2 (criteria).* $H$ and $A$ are defined as the paper's §5 construction over $D_H$, $D_A$ respectively; the main theorem gives the criterion for each. No hand verification is needed — this is the point of implementing the freeze inside $D_A$ rather than inside a modified trader class. $\square$

*Lemma 3 (price behavior; the load-bearing step).* As in §3: the human's prices satisfy $\mathbb P^H_n(X_{k(n)}) \eqsim_n p^H_n$ by Theorem 4.4.5 with the class-relative target $p^H$ (hypothesis: Assumption P, plus the routine check that $p^H$ is generable — the post-mid branch reads decided atoms, a hard constant for the poly-time generator since the atoms sit in $D_H$'s day-$m_k$ output, which the generator at indices $\ge m_k$ can afford to read in this construction's bookkeeping); the quotes satisfy $\text{quote}_n \eqsim_n \tfrac14$ by Theorem 4.8.17 with the constant target, whose optimality for $A$'s class is Assumption P again. Flagged residue: 4.8.17's hypotheses should be checked against the exact statement (its printed form participates in the family of statements around the §2-of-v3 erratum), and the "generator can read $D_H$'s decided atoms" bookkeeping deserves one careful pass; jointly these are why Theorem B carries ~0.75 rather than more. $\square$

*Lemma 4 (divergence, and the audit).* By Lemma 3, for all large $n$: quotes lie within $\tfrac1{64}$ of $\tfrac14$, so the quote ramp of $w_n$ equals $1$ (as $\tfrac14 - \tfrac1{64} > \tfrac3{16} + \tfrac1{32}$); and on post-mid days of blocks with $\sigma_k = 0$, the human's price lies within $\tfrac1{64}$ of $0$, so the credence ramp equals $1$ (as $\tfrac1{64} < \tfrac{5}{32} - \tfrac1{32}$). Refuted blocks have limiting frequency $\tfrac34 > 0$ (Assumption P), each contributing $\ge 2$ days of weight $1$: $\sum w_n = \infty$. The consistency audit — no theorem is contradicted: every forecaster-legal weighting sees quote-vs-value errors of *mixed sign* ($\tfrac14 - \sigma_k = +\tfrac14$ on refuted, $-\tfrac34$ on confirmed blocks, with weights uncorrelated with $\sigma$ by Assumption P), so the forecaster's Recurring Unbiasedness holds with its limit points at $0$; one-signedness appears only on the gate $g$, which selects on $\sigma$ and is thereby outside the forecaster's class. The human's theorems are likewise respected: its prices track their own class's best target. Nobody errs; the trust fails anyway. $\square$

Together: Theorem B. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (everyone is right).** On refuted blocks the human's low credence is maximally justified — the question was settled false by public evidence — and the quote of $\tfrac14$ was the exact best forecast available to anything certified only on frozen data. The failure is not a defect of either agent but a conservation fact about where information can be at freeze time. Note also the asymmetry promised in the overview: the failing gate $g$ is human-legal, so by the *human's* own Recurring Unbiasedness the human correctly anticipates, on average along $g$, that the stale quotes will exceed the realized settlements — the human sees the trust gap coming and possesses no instrument that closes it.

## 5. Theorem C: the matching upper bound

**Theorem C.** Let any block-stale coupled pair be given (agents satisfying their criteria; forecast targets determined via $\Gamma_A$; the v3 visibility assumption granted *at the stale horizon*: the forecaster's weightings may use the human's prices through the last freeze, the human's traders may use the posted quotes). Fix rationals $t, \varepsilon, \delta$ and a window-disjoint schedule $\overline d$ whose round-trip windows close within the current block (horizon $= T_{k(d)+1}$). Define the *frozen-credence* violation weight $w^{\mathrm{frozen}}_n$ by replacing, in $w_n$, the human's current credence with its credence at the last freeze, $\mathbb P^H_{T_{k(n)}}(X_{k(n)})$, at margin $\tfrac\varepsilon2$. Then
$$\sum_k w_{d_k} \;\le\; \sum_k w^{\mathrm{frozen}}_{d_k} \;+\; \frac{2}{\varepsilon} \sum_k \Big| \mathbb P^H_{d_k}\big(X_{k(d_k)}\big) - \mathbb P^H_{T_{k(d_k)}}\big(X_{k(d_k)}\big) \Big| , \qquad \sum_k w^{\mathrm{frozen}}_{d_k} \;<\; \infty .$$

**Proof.** *The split.* On a scheduled violation day, either the frozen credence was already below $t - \tfrac\varepsilon2$ — then $w^{\mathrm{frozen}} \ge$ a fixed fraction of $w$ (ramp arithmetic, absorbing $\delta$) — or the frozen credence was $\ge t - \tfrac\varepsilon2$ while the current one is $< t - \varepsilon$, so the within-block displacement is $\ge \tfrac\varepsilon2$ and the day's weight (at most $1$) is at most $\tfrac2\varepsilon$ times its displacement term.

*Finiteness of the frozen part.* The frozen-gated weight is forecaster-legal — both factors read prices at or before the last freeze, plus the forecaster's own quote — so the v3 Theorem 1 argument transplants verbatim: the forecaster's Recurring Unbiasedness on the frozen gate forces the realized end-of-block credences to average $\ge t - o(1)$ along checkpoints; the human-side trader buys the current question at its live price on fired days — and here the live price *is* low, at least $\tfrac\varepsilon2$-low, on the frozen-gated days that also remain current violations; for frozen-gated days that are not current violations the trader's same-day check (human-legal) declines the trade, and the declined mass is again dominated by displacement, folding into the second term — sells at the horizon at the realized credence, one window at a time, and its log-budget diverges along checkpoints unless the fired frozen weight is finite. Exploitation of the human otherwise. $\blacksquare$

*Tightness.* In $\mathcal E$, the frozen part vanishes (pre-mid credences sit at $\tfrac14 > t - \tfrac\varepsilon2$), and the realized violation weight per refuted block is of the same order as the block's displacement mass ($\approx \tfrac14 \cdot$ post-mid length per refuted block, against displacement $\approx \tfrac14$ per refuted-block day). So the inequality is achieved up to constants: the surprise term is not an artifact. The adaptive upgrade of v3 §4 transplants to the frozen part with the same two obligations, giving the per-day and summed forms over all days for that part; the surprise term is untouched by adaptivity, as it must be — Theorem B says no method removes it.

## 6. Reading

For the training-run picture: freezing the forecaster's access at $T_k$ converts the deference guarantee from "the human eventually never lags the advertisement" (v3, and still true per fixed question by Theorem A) into a ledgered trade-off on fresh questions — *misplaced-trust mass $\le$ frozen-certifiable mass (finite) $+$ a constant times the human's own within-freeze update flow* — and Theorem B exhibits the flow being converted into misplaced trust at a positive exchange rate with every party behaving impeccably. Three corollaries of the shape of the result rather than of any formula: the quantity that bounds the damage is *observable to the human* (it is the human's own update magnitude), so the trust extended to a frozen system can be rationally metered by its recipient in real time; the exemption of fixed questions means the failure is specifically about *novelty*, not about staleness per se — a frozen certificate never rots on a stationary question; and iterating over generations, the guarantee compounds like a conservation law: each freeze adds its own surprise flow to the un-certifiable residue, and nothing in the machinery redeems it later, since the questions on which it accrued have retired.

## 7. Not established, and checkpoints

Not established: Assumption P (held at the paper's own "most likely" standard, with its resource-bounded-randomness caveat and the noted self-reference); the two Lemma-3 residues (the exact hypotheses of the LUV learning theorem against its printed statement; the generator's read of same-day decided atoms in the human's target); the day-scale analogue of Theorem B (one-day staleness with joint-clearing removed — I expect the same construction compressed, ~0.6, but the room is tighter); whether Theorem C's declined-trade fold-in survives a fully adaptive statement of the frozen part without new obligations. Checkpoints, in order of leverage: (1) Theorem A's proof — short, and it fixes where impossibility can live; (2) Lemma 4's audit — the mixed-sign consistency check is what makes the counterexample non-paradoxical; (3) Lemma 3 against Theorems 4.4.5/4.8.17 as printed; (4) the split-and-fold bookkeeping in Theorem C; (5) Assumption P's statement for the mild self-reference.
