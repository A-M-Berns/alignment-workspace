## 1. Was the conjecture ever *proven* false? Partially yes — but only for one strong reading, in a setting Sam disputes

The corpus split "Eisenstat's merge conjecture" into two halves (stated in [[eisenstat-lookahead-construction]]: §1): the **trust half** ($H$ comes to defer to the quote $a_n = \mathbb E^A_n(\ulcorner\mathbb E^H_{f(n)}(X_n)\urcorner)$) and the **inductor half** ($B_n$ is itself a logical inductor). The status of each is very different:

**Genuinely proven false (in-setting): the timely *pointwise* trust reading.** The home of this is [[no-timely-pointwise-tower]] plus deference-v6 §4.3/§4.8. The tower `Mart(H→A)` reduces (via read-off of the published quote + the free self-trust theorem `cee`) to tracking $a_n \approx_n Y_n$, and tracking dies by an explicit counterexample — the "Gödel-coin" diagonal: take $g_n \leftrightarrow (a_n \le \tfrac12)$. Since $H$ records $A$'s quote in its ledger by stage $n{+}1$, $g_n$ is *decided* in $H$'s world, provability induction pins $H$'s credence to the indicator $\mathbb 1[a_n \le \tfrac12]$, and no $a_n \in [0,1]$ is ever within $\tfrac12$ of that indicator. The defect is a constant $\ge \tfrac12$, independent of $A$'s compute (an oracle doesn't help — the settlement is *defined* as the anti-inductive negation of the quote). This is rigorous: elementary, and its arithmetic core is kernel-checked in Lean (`SelfRefTarget.no_exact_quote`, `tracking_fails`). Via the Total Trust ⟹ ... ⟹ Tower loop, full-strength cross-process Total Trust over quote-referencing-closed bet classes dies with it — what the corpus calls "the paradox route."

**Everything else was *not* proven false:**
- On **quote-free** question families the pointwise tower was only shown **underivable** (2b, the cost-circularity regress: timely prediction of the coupled target would require $A$'s trader class to contain its own coupled simulation cost $R_A \circ F$). The corpus itself labels this "underivable, not disproven" and rates its soft joint ~75–80%.

## The reconstruction: two separate failures, neither of them a wrong proof

Tracing the corpus, your belief "Sam's conjecture is false" was formed by two independent slippages, and notably **neither one is a math error** — every proof involved appears to be fine:

**Slippage 1: attribution drift.** The name "Eisenstat's conjecture" was attached to the object in May–June (AGENDA, the lab) with your contemporaneous record of the hypothesis ("good feedback", and your li-deference formalism even had the human-side publication delay e(n) — the closest thing to Sam's intent anywhere in the corpus). Then the object was re-formalized roughly four times — prompt-ledger mutual legibility (where tower-death was proven), joint clearing, A-side freeze, and the one-way setting (A never reads H at all) — and **the name silently rode along through every re-wiring**. By July, "Eisenstat's lookahead construction" named a setting whose information structure was point-for-point reversed from what Sam says he meant. No step did anything wrong locally; there was just no step whose job was to ask "does the name still fit?" — that question is checkable only against a person, and the person was never consulted.

**Slippage 2: status compression.** The corpus's page-level bookkeeping was actually meticulous — "REFUTED at the timely pointwise grade, in-setting", "underivable, not disproven", "UNTOUCHED, zero cases discharged", "OPEN, tools pointing weakly toward true". But what a human carries out of a months-long ledger is a cached summary vibe, and yours crystallized around the tower-death era (June–July) as "false" — then went stale as the ledger moved *positive* underneath it (Theorem A, Theorem SS, both impossibility attempts self-destructing in late July). So when Sam asked *why*, there was nothing under the cache: the reasons had never passed through you, and the cache was two months out of date besides. Your self-diagnosis ("I took 'couldn't prove it' for 'probably false'") is real but is the smaller part; the bigger part is that the one genuine refutation was of a *differently-wired, stronger* statement, and the identification of that statement with Sam's conjecture was never anyone's vetting target.

And here is the part I'd underline: **the vetting effort went where vetting was tractable, not where the risk was.** Proofs got adversarial passes, Lean cores, confidence numbers. The *bridge claims* — "this object formalizes Sam's idea", "this refutation is about his conjecture", "this is what to say on the slide" — got zero vetting protocol, because they don't look like theorems. The corpus even wrote sections literally titled "What to say about Eisenstat's conjecture" — speech-ready text — without ever flagging that the construal was unconfirmed with Eisenstat. That's an AI-side failure, not a you-side failure, and it's why I don't accept "maybe it'll just have to be on my end": the sessions handed you slide-ready compressions with the scope qualifiers already shed.

One more observation, which I think you'll appreciate: this research program is *about* when deference to a stronger reasoner is justified, and its own repeated finding is that trust is earned only on the vetted/feedback-covered subsequence and the deferred-to system is unconstrained off it. Your process failure is your own theorem instantiated: you extended trust off the vetted subsequence. The fix the theory suggests is the fix the postmortem suggests — **scope the deference to what's been checked, and make the boundary of "checked" impossible to lose track of.**

## The policy I propose

Split by who can actually execute each piece:

**Your side (the part only you can do):**
1. **Public-claim gate.** A headline claim gets asserted only if you can answer the obvious follow-ups from reasons you've personally traced — otherwise the assertion is the provenance-honest one: "AI-assisted work I haven't fully vetted suggests…". 3. **Named-person claims require the person.** Before asserting anything in someone's name (including its falsity), either confirm the construal with them or attach the flag out loud.

**Session side (rules for the AIs working in this directory):**
1. **Register on every headline sentence.** Compressions must carry their scope in the same sentence — never "the tower is dead", always "dead at the pointwise grade, in the prompt-ledger construal". The place this discipline matters most is exactly the summary/"what to say" text, because that's what human memory caches.
2. **Bridge claims are their own register.** "X formalizes P's idea" / "X refutes P's conjecture" are claims about a person's intent — mark them ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED until the person confirms; every named idea gets a canonical-statement page quoting the original record verbatim (the [[eisenstat-conjecture-attribution]] pattern, now standing).
3. **Prep-for-public text must be provenance-split three ways:** vetted-by-Abram / proved-in-corpus-but-not-vetted-by-Abram / construal-dependent. Only the first bin goes on a slide unflagged.
4. **Failure-to-prove is recorded with its diagnosis, never as "probably false"** — and updates state their direction (dying impossibility attempts are evidence *for*).
5. **A defensible-claims ledger** — one page holding exactly the claims you could state publicly, each with statement, grade, the two-sentence why, the expected follow-up questions with answers, and a vetted-by-Abram bit **that only you may set**. That last rule is what keeps the ledger from becoming one more AI artifact you defer to: it holds provenance, not truth. 6. **The belief audit ("Sam test") before any public presentation:** a session interviews *you* with the follow-up questions, you answer from memory, and mismatches against the ledger get surfaced. It converts "take time to work everything through" into a concrete ritual.
