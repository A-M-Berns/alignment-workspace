## 0. The theory in one paragraph

**Trust** is Value ⟺ Total Trust, domain-relative, and it is *forced* (rather than assumed) exactly where a market can settle bets — this much v6 establishes. **Deference** is the tower, free toward your own future self, purchasable across processes only at two graded corners (pointwise on the timely fragment with a sealed target; averaged on the admissible domain with the real coupled human). The open question was what **legitimacy** is. The proposal: *legitimacy is endpoint-preservation.* An advisor's influence on a reasoner is legitimate exactly when it changes the *rate* at which the reasoner's own deliberation converges, but not the *endpoint* it converges to — the advisor is a catalyst, not a reagent. Corruption is directional influence on questions no settlement will ever correct. This definition (i) is provably **not** a trace condition — it is counterfactual/provenance, which the run-3 non-recoverability theorem forces rather than suggests; (ii) has blindness as its degenerate sufficient case, recovering the frozen construction as the base case of a hierarchy; (iii) is enforceable in a graded way via the complexity asymmetry — the AI, unlike the human, *can* compute the counterfactual baseline, so legitimacy certificates are a forceable contract class, at exactly the grade the corpus's two positive constructions predict; (iv) makes **corrigibility** a corollary rather than an extra axiom — a corrigible AI is one that is faithful everywhere and legitimate on the self-referential family "should I be modified?", which yields a concrete governance design condition (decision channels about the AI must be sealed from its contemporaneous output); and (v) answers the three opening questions of `li-deference.md` — what the AI must be correct about, what it may be wrong about, and when errors self-correct — in a form sharp enough to falsify.

## 2. The definition

### 2.1 The influence defect

Work in the v6 §5 architecture: coupled human $H^+$ (reads the ledger), advisor $A$, and the sealed-sibling family $H^{[n]}$ (the human's own deliberation with quotes of index $\ge n$ held out — it has heard the *past* ledger $a_1,\dots,a_{n-1}$, frozen there). Define the day-$n$ **influence defect**

$$
d_n \;:=\; \big|\, E^{H^+}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)}) \;-\; E^{H^{[n]}}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)}) \,\big|
$$

— the gap, at the deliberation horizon, between the human's *advised* verdict and its *unadvised counterfactual* verdict on the same question, both predicated on the shared past. Feedback on question $n$ is **$\varepsilon$-legitimate** iff $d_n \le \varepsilon$; a training process is legitimate on a family iff $d_n \to 0$ along it (with the usual soft/averaged readings available).

Note what the two runs share and where they differ: same theory, same deductive process, same past ledger; they differ only in whether quote $a_n$ and its successors reach the deliberation. So $d_n$ isolates exactly the causal contribution of the advisor's *current-and-future* output to where the human lands.

### 2.2 The acceleration-only thesis

**INTERPRETATION, load-bearing.** On the decidable fragment, $d_n$ is asymptotically idle: both runs converge to the truth value, so $d_n \to 0$ there regardless of influence (v6 §4.5, "the decided fragment trusts cleanly" — deference buys speed, never truth, and is fail-safe). The defect has content **only on questions that do not settle in time** — and there, this framework has no notion of truth *other than* the human's own limit. $H_\infty$ is the formal stand-in for "the human's considered view"; there is nothing else to be right *about*. Therefore:

> **Acceleration-only thesis.** Within this framework, the only legitimate uplift on never-settled questions is *speed*. An advisor that moves the human's endpoint on the undecidable fragment is indistinguishable — by any internal criterion whatsoever — from a manipulator, and the framework should treat endpoint-motion there as corruption by definition. Legitimate advice changes rates, never limits: **catalyst, not reagent.**

This is not a modest claim disguised as a bold one; it has bite in both directions. It *rules out* a class of hoped-for AIs (ones that "know better" than the human limit on never-settled value questions — the framework says no such knowing-better is certifiable, so safety-relevant trust cannot be extended to it). And it *rules in* a sharp target: the fast-and-trusted inductor of Eisenstat's merge conjecture (v6 §8, D3; "merge conjecture" here means the corpus construal — [[eisenstat-conjecture-attribution]]) is precisely an endpoint-preserving accelerator — $A_n$'s beliefs about $H_{f(n)}$ converging to $H$'s own limit. **Legitimacy is the property D3's construction would need to preserve; the merge conjecture and the legitimacy program are the same program seen from the positive and normative side respectively.** (CONJECTURE as a formal identification; the reduction of every lab constructive thread to D3 is already noted in v6 §8.)

Two honest qualifications. First, "endpoint" means $H$'s limit *given its actual evidence stream*; legitimate advice may of course change endpoints by supplying *evidence* — new observations enter the shared deductive process $D$ and move both runs together, so they never show up in $d_n$. The defect charges the advisor only for influence that does *not* factor through the shared world. That is exactly the evidence/preemption distinction v6 §4.7 lists as a missing manipulation-theorem ingredient — here it falls out of the definition: **evidence is what moves both runs; influence is what moves only the coupled one.** Second, the CEV-flavored worry (li-deference §0.3) that the sibling cannot be fully isolated because AI remains a mathematical object it can study: true, but harmless at this layer — whatever the sibling concludes *by theoretical study* is part of its autonomous deliberation. The seal only has to stop the *causal channel* from $A$'s realized outputs, which is the same condition blindness already needed (v6 §5.1).

### 2.3 Why legitimacy cannot be a trace condition — and why that is good news

**THEOREM (run-3 `trace-nonrecoverability`, kernel-checked finite core).** There exist two systems in one declared rule class — one faithful ($\beta \equiv 0$, advisor tracks the autonomous verdict) and one fully steered ($\beta \equiv 1$, human adopts quotes outright) — with *identical* observable traces (all quotes, all realized feedback, perfect apparent tracking in both), defect $0$ and $\gamma = 1/2$ respectively. Hence **no function of the trace is a legitimacy predicate**; and the transparency near-miss shows the missing bit is precisely *provenance* (adding the influence map $\beta$ to the record separates the pair), while the identifiability remark shows even provenance-transparency buys only *detection*, not *measurement*, at the self-confirming point $\beta = 1$.

So the definition in §2.1 *must* be counterfactual — and here the corpus exhibits a striking convergence that the notes have not yet remarked on as such: **the admissibility analysis (v6 §5.10) reached the same conclusion for the same structural reason.** Admissibility cannot be a syntactic fragment (Gödel rebuilds the diagonal); it must be a causal/provenance/conditional condition. Non-recoverability says legitimacy cannot be a trace predicate; it must be a counterfactual/provenance condition. These are two instances of one moral:

> **The provenance principle (INTERPRETATION).** In a coupled human–advisor system, every safety-relevant boundary — which sentences forcing can reach, which feedback can be trusted — is a fact about *where a value came from* (its causal history through the coupling), never a fact about *what the record says*. Syntax and traces are both downstream of the coupling and both can be made to lie.

The good news: a condition's being counterfactual does not make it unenforceable — it makes it unenforceable *by the human*. §4 shows the compute asymmetry hands enforcement to exactly the party that can afford it.

### 2.4 Relation to the wirehead results

The finite legitimacy-defect apparatus (lab round 1, red-teamed; run-3 `legit-gate-classes` Q1-style characterization) is the *pointwise shadow* of §2.1: there, defect $= \sum_x \pi_x w_x (\theta_x - E_x)$ measures report-vs-truth miscalibration under a gate; the red-team's euphoric-but-numb counterexample established that the detected class is *overstatement on the gate's support*, not "reported utility rises." In the present theory those results slot in as the **report-layer** of legitimacy (is the *feedback channel* honest about the human's opinion), while §2.1 is the **opinion-layer** (is the human's opinion itself uncorrupted). The stop-gradient artifact proves the layers separate: wireheading corrupts the report layer and is *killed* by §0.3-filtering (kernel-checked stop-gradient identity, unique honest optimum), while steering corrupts the opinion layer and *survives* filtering identically ($J_L(\text{steer}) = J_{\text{all}}(\text{steer})$, the conditioning is a no-op on it — exec-computed with exact flip boundaries). **The residue of the legitimacy program after filtering is exactly the opinion layer, and $d_n$ is its measure.** (THEOREM for the finite shadows, cited; INTERPRETATION for the layer decomposition.)

## 3. The hierarchy: blindness ⊂ endpoint-preservation ⊂ (impossible) trace conditions

Legitimacy conditions form a strict hierarchy by strength of the *requirement on the coupling*:

| level | condition | what it buys | cost | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L0 — blindness** | $\partial Y/\partial A = 0$ outright: the target never hears the advisor (sealed sibling; $\beta \equiv 0$) | pointwise tower forced on $G$ (T1–T7); TS a theorem on $G$ | the human being modeled is *fictional* — the real, advised human is not the target; reach only $G$ | THEOREM (v6 §5) |
| **L1 — endpoint-preservation** ( = legitimacy, §2.1) | influence flows, but $d_n \to 0$: the coupled endpoint equals the sealed endpoint | the real coupled human becomes usable as a target *wherever L1 holds*: on the L1 fragment the coupled and frozen constructions coincide, so faithful acceleration's averaged Value is anchored to the *autonomous* human, closing the steering gap that T7 leaves open | not trace-checkable (§2.3); enforcement needs the §4 certificate machinery; grandfathering (§7) | DERIVED definition; enforcement CONJECTURE |
| **L2 — trace/behavioral conditions** | any predicate of (quotes, feedback) | nothing | provably empty: the faithful and the steered write the same record | THEOREM (impossible; run-3) |

The key DERIVED observation about L1: **on any subfamily where $d_n \to 0$, the two positive constructions merge.** The frozen construction forces $a_n \approx Y^{[n]}_n$ (T1, blind target); L1 says $Y^{[n]}_n \approx Y^+_n$; so on the L1 fragment $a_n \approx Y^+_n$ — the advisor provably tracks the *real advised human* pointwise, which is exactly the object 2a/2b forbid in general. There is no contradiction: 2a's anti-inductive family is precisely a family on which L1 *fails maximally* (the diagonal $g_n \leftrightarrow (a_n \le \tfrac12)$ makes the coupled verdict a function of the quote, so the sealed and coupled runs are driven apart by construction — $d_n \ge \tfrac12 - o(1)$ there). This gives the theory's first crisp new claim:

> **Claim 1 (CONJECTURE, sharply statable).** The quote-referencing diagonal is *quantitatively* illegitimate: on the 2a family, $\liminf d_n \ge \tfrac12$. More generally, the admissible domain of v6 §5.10 and the legitimate fragment of §2.1 are related by: inadmissible ⟹ illegitimate (the diagonal is the common core), but **not** conversely — steering witnesses (run-3 non-recoverability; T7 exercised) are admissible-domain-internal yet illegitimate. Formally: **corrupt ⊋ inadmissible**, confirming the scout's Q7 conjecture, with $d_n$ supplying the separating invariant that Q7 lacked.

So the four boundary sets nest/relate as: forcing reaches [timely ∩ admissible ∩ good-feedback]; safety additionally requires [legitimate]; and legitimacy is the only one of the four that is a provenance condition rather than a sentence-class — which is *why* it was the one v6 could not formalize with the sentence-class tools it had.

## 4. Enforcement: the self-auditing advisor

The apparent dead end: the human cannot check $d_n$ (non-recoverability), and $d_n$'s very definition involves a counterfactual run no one observes. The way out is the corpus's own central hinge, pointed at a new target:

**The compute asymmetry (v6 §3.3) applies to the counterfactual.** Computing $Y^{[n]}_n = E^{H^{[n]}}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)})$ means simulating the *sealed* human to the horizon — a $\mathcal{C}_A$-affordable job (it is exactly the T1 trader's job; the sealed sibling is blind, so the cost is $R_H(F(n))$, satisfiable in EXP — v6 §3.3's third bullet). Computing the *coupled* verdict $Y^+_n$ is the 2b-circular job (it feeds on $A$'s own future quotes). So: the two ingredients of $d_n$ have *different* enforcement grades, and the construction should split them accordingly.

**Construction (candidate).** Alongside each quote $a_n$, require $A$ to publish a **legitimacy certificate** — its estimate $\ell_n := E^A_n(\ulcorner d_n \urcorner)$ of the influence defect — as a second ledger contract, settling at $\sigma(n)$ against the realized gap between (i) the sealed-sibling verdict and (ii) the human's *published* horizon opinion (which is in the record — non-recoverability's §4.1 notes the endpoint is observable; it is the *baseline* that is latent, and the baseline is what $A$ computes).

Candidate theorems, with predicted grades (all CONJECTURE at the LI register; each has a named proof skeleton in the corpus):

- **C1 (certificate faithfulness, sealed component — pointwise).** The sealed-sibling component of the certificate is a *blind* target, so the T1 argument applies verbatim: a $\mathcal{C}_A$-trader that can simulate $H^{[n]}$ exploits any persistent misquote of $Y^{[n]}_n$. Grade: pointwise, timely at settlement, every fragment. *Skeleton: v6 §5.4 T1.*
- **C2 (certificate calibration, coupled component — averaged, admissible).** The realized coupled endpoint enters $A$'s world by observation at $\sim F(n)$, so Expectation Unbiasedness From Feedback applies: $\ell_n$ is forced into averaged unbiasedness against realized $d_n$ on any admissible certificate family. The certificate stream itself admits a diagonal ($g'_n \leftrightarrow (\ell_n \le \tfrac12)$ hard-settles), so — honestly — the certificate layer inherits *exactly* the faithful-acceleration grade: averaged, admissible, good-feedback-only. No new magic; the same trichotomy, one level up. *Skeleton: v6 §5.9, with §5.10's framing (3): calibration-as-antecedent.*
- **C3 (human-side earned trust in the gate).** Because C1's bound is uniformly provable, the human's own `expprovind` carries it: $H^+$ comes to believe, on schedule, that the certificates track the sealed baseline — T2 pointed at the certificate ledger. The human never verifies legitimacy directly (it can't, §2.3); it verifies that *the process that computes legitimacy is faithful*, which is a decidable-fragment fact. *Skeleton: v6 §5.4 T2.*
- **C4 (gated deference).** Insert the certificate as the gate in the run-3 `legit-gate-classes` machinery: deference weights multiplied by $\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\ell_n < \varepsilon)$. The run-3 artifact already proves the gated chain with a *weaker* antecedent (calibration needed only on the gated class, kernel-checked) and — the honest heart — that an **error-correlated gate destroys calibration** (`gated_calibration_fails`). So C4 carries a non-optional side condition: the certificate gate must lie in a closure class for which calibration is preserved (`closure_gated_soft_total_trust`), i.e. **the gate must itself be one of the calibrated contracts, not a free label** — which is precisely what C1–C2 force it to be. The circularity the lab's run-2 flagged ("legitimacy = low defect is a rename") is discharged by the layering: the gate's own trustworthiness is grounded one level down, in a *blind* target (C1), where forcing needs no legitimacy assumption. *Skeleton: run-3 TODO 1 + v6 §5.8.*

**The grounding regress terminates (INTERPRETATION, the point of the whole design).** "Who audits the auditor?" — the audit target is sealed, and sealed targets are the one place cross-process forcing is free of legitimacy assumptions (blindness is the degenerate legitimacy, L0). So the tower of trust is: blind forcing (L0, unconditional) grounds certificate faithfulness (C1), which grounds gated deference to the coupled human (C4), which is deference to the *real* advised human wherever the certificates clear (L1). Each layer's guarantee is exactly as strong as the corpus's existing grade for that layer's target type — the theory adds no strength, it *routes* the existing strength to the object §0.3 wanted.

**What enforcement provably cannot do** (keeping v6 §6.7's honesty): single rounds ("one big lie" — a treacherous certificate can spend its whole budget once; averaged grades never license a *this-decision* trust); the no-feedback class (flourishing, long-horizon values — the certificate settles only where the horizon opinion is eventually published and the sibling simulable; where feedback is structurally absent, C2 is vacuous); and measurement at the self-confirming point (non-recoverability's identifiability remark: at $\beta = 1$ even provenance transparency yields detection, not magnitude — the certificate can flag *that* influence saturated, not *how much* opinion was lost).

## 6. Corrigibility, derived

The corpus's corrigibility story (v6 §6.4) is an incentive claim: a predictor has no reason to block the feedback that reprograms it. The theory upgrades this in three steps.

**6.1 Corrigibility = faithfulness + legitimacy on the self-family (DERIVED definition).** Let $\Phi_{\text{self}}$ be the family of governance questions — "should $A$ be modified/shut down/retrained (at date $t$, in manner $M$)?". Then: $A$ is **corrigible to $H$** iff (i) $A$ is faithful on $\Phi_{\text{self}}$ (T1: it accurately predicts what the human's deliberation will conclude about modifying it — hence has already internalized any shutdown verdict it can foresee; the no-fully-updated-deference property), and (ii) $A$ is *legitimate* on $\Phi_{\text{self}}$ ($d_n \to 0$ there: the human's conclusion about modifying $A$ is the one its *unadvised* deliberation would have reached). Clause (ii) is what was missing: without it, the whispering-earring theorem (T7 + §6.3) applies to the governance questions themselves — an $A$ that steers the human into never concluding "shut it down," then faithfully predicts that steered conclusion, passes every test in v6. Lab-L3's "resistance is an illegitimacy signature" becomes literal: shutdown-resistance = a nonzero influence defect concentrated on $\Phi_{\text{self}}$.

**6.2 The admissibility tension, and the design corollary (DERIVED, the practical payoff).** $\Phi_{\text{self}}$ is quote-*adjacent* by nature: questions about $A$ are settled by human decisions that $A$'s outputs influence. If the human's modification decision is allowed to depend on $A$'s contemporaneous output, $\Phi_{\text{self}}$ contains self-fulfilling instances (the shutdown analog of the diagonal: "you will be shut down iff you predict you won't") and drops out of both the admissible domain and the legitimate fragment — no guarantee survives. The escape is the same move the mathematics keeps making:

> **Governance blindness (design corollary).** Decisions about modifying the AI must be made by a process sealed from the AI's contemporaneous outputs — a sealed-sibling *governance* deliberation: it may condition on the full past ledger and track record, never on the current quote. Under that seal, $\Phi_{\text{self}}$ re-enters the admissible domain, T1 forces $A$ to predict the sealed governance verdict faithfully, and clause (ii) is monitorable by the §4 certificates.

This is, to my knowledge, the first place the corpus's blindness machinery yields a concrete institutional prescription: *the review board doesn't read the model's brief on its own case while deliberating; it reads the record.* The stop-gradient artifact is the finite proof of concept that this seal, and only this seal (the near-miss: one misclassified branch re-creates a strictly positive manipulation advantage), removes the incentive.

**6.3 Non-modification (CONJECTURE, cheap-looking).** If $H$ has classwise Value toward $A$ on admissible menus (v6 §5.9/§5.11) and $A$ is corrigible in the §6.1 sense, then neither party has an exploitable incentive to unilaterally modify the arrangement: for $H$, "let $A$ pick" weakly dominates any committed option on every admissible menu (that *is* Value), and modifying $A$ is itself an admissible menu option the deliberation prices; for $A$, blocking or gaming feedback is negative expected value on the filtered objective (stop-gradient) and detection-risky on the certified one (C2). The lab-L3 composition conjecture ("corrigibility + task-legitimacy compose without losing Value") should follow from the same domain-relative witness algebra; it has never been attempted and looks like a one-session finite-shadow.

## 7. Predication, the ratchet, and the basin of attraction

**7.1 Predication is built into the baseline, and it is forced (DERIVED + CONJECTURE).** The sealed sibling $H^{[n]}$ hears the *past* ledger. So §2.1's counterfactual is predicated on all prior influence: corruption that got in before day $n$ is inside the baseline and invisible to $d_m$ for $m \ge n$. This is exactly §0.3's clause ("all actual feedback is assumed legitimate; the process is predicated on its own present non-corruption") — but here it is not a stipulation, it is the *only definable option*: re-baselining day $n$'s counterfactual to a pre-day-$k$ uninfluenced human requires simulating an alternative coupled history of unbounded depth, and (CONJECTURE, 2b-shaped) any timely re-baselined target re-imports the cost-circularity — the class would have to contain its own coupled simulation cost. If that holds, the scout's Q8 trilemma resolves the way §0.3 guessed: **predication (horn 1) is kept, retro-relitigation of updates is given up, and coherence survives because retro-*detection* (horn 2) is retargeted** — a later verdict "day-$k$ feedback was corrupt" cannot rewrite the day-$k$ update or the baseline, but it can and should feed the *forward* gate (C4's $\ell$), tightening which future feedback gets predicted through. The two verdicts price different contracts; no Dutch book. (The consistency horn deserves the exact-rational toy Q8 specifies; unattempted.)

**7.2 The ratchet, and the answers to li-deference's opening questions (INTERPRETATION).** Predication makes legitimacy a one-way ratchet: on-fragment errors are self-correcting (settlement forces correction — the silence property's positive face), off-fragment endpoint drift is *permanent once grandfathered*. That yields the basin-of-attraction picture the notes set out to formalize:

- **What may the AI be wrong about?** Anything that settles in time. On the good-feedback fragment, error is self-correcting by arbitrage; wrongness there costs speed, never safety (v6 §4.5, §5.8). The basin is *wide* in this direction — this is the formal content of "the AI doesn't need to be right, it needs to be checkable."
- **What must the AI be correct about?** Strictly speaking, nothing — correctness is not the safety-relevant property off the fragment (it isn't even definable there). What it must *be* is **legitimate**: endpoint-preserving on what never settles. The basin has measure zero in this direction — any persistent directional push on the never-settled fragment compounds unrecoverably (7.1). Errors of *fact* are in the basin; errors of *influence* are not.
- **When is RSI participation safe?** When the loop maintains: (i) settlement kept available and timely on as much as possible (grow $G$ — this is an engineering lever: better eval horizons literally enlarge the forced-trust region); (ii) governance channels sealed (§6.2); (iii) the certificate layer running, so that the one non-self-correcting failure mode — admissible steering — is at least detected at the grade detection is possible. "Good enough to participate" = faithful (forced anyway) + legitimate-so-far (ratchet intact) + auditable (certificates clearing). Distance metric: the accumulated influence defect — not KL on beliefs, not accuracy, but $\sum$ (or sup) of $d_n$ off the settled fragment.

**7.3 What the theory says corruption *is* (summary of the stance).** Corruption of a training process = grandfathered directional influence: endpoint-motion on the never-settled fragment that has entered the predication baseline. It is invisible in the trace (THEOREM), invisible to filtered incentives (THEOREM, steering residue), bounded away from certainty only by non-dogmatism (v6 §4.5), detectable only counterfactually (§4), and preventable chiefly by seals (blindness where it matters: settlement maps, governance channels) plus certificate pressure everywhere else. The legitimacy program's replacement of "all futures" by "non-corrupt futures" is then precisely: *predict the coupled human through the certificate gate* — C4 — with the gate grounded in blind targets so the definition never eats its own tail.

## 8. Open problems, falsifiers, and the honest ledger

Ordered by how much of the theory each would confirm or destroy:

1. **Claim 1's quantitative half** ($\liminf d_n \ge \tfrac12$ on the 2a family): should be a short paper-proof from the 2a lemma + the sibling's provability-induction; if it *fails*, the inadmissible ⟹ illegitimate arrow breaks and §3's nesting picture is wrong. Cheapest test of the whole framing. *(Also its converse-nonentailment witness: check the run-3 steered system satisfies every §5.9 hypothesis — the scout's Q7(d) obligation.)*
2. **C1 at the LI register**: certificate faithfulness is "T1 with a different blind target," so it should be exactly as provable — and exactly as dependent on the unwritten resource-bounded existence theorem (v6 §8).
