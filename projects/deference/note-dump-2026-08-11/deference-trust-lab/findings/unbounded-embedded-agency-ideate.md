## 0. The thread in one paragraph, and the lens I bring

Cole Wyeth's reflective-oracle AIXI uses EDT on single actions and learns its own decision procedure
empirically (source code unknown, behaviour of any code known). Main result: `(1-δ)`-confidence in
your decision procedure ⇒ `ε`-optimal. The principal's three critiques are (a) **improbable actions**
(confidence in source code forces tiny probability on un-taken actions; reasoning about them needs
contrived worlds; could spurious-proof / Troll-Bridge pathologies recur?); (b) the self-knowledge
assumption **sneakily rules out self-modification** (and so environments where updatelessness is
needed-and-reachable); (c) reflective-oracle independence makes **Ω-prediction look impossible**, and
the `ε`-optimality notion **interleaves conditionals** so updateful behaviour always looks fine
("AIXI converges to AIXI"). The principal's proposed fix: a theorem *"if a UDT1.0 agent
`(1-δ)`-believes its policy is UDT1.1, then it is `ε`-optimal,"* where belief-you're-UDT1.1 does the
work of Cole's hand-built oracle (it forecloses the bad self-coordination / Stag-Hunt equilibria).

The lens I bring: **this is a deference/endorsement problem in disguise, and the lab already has the
right machinery for it.** "Believing your policy is UDT1.1" is exactly *deferring to an expert* — the
expert being "the optimal-whole-policy procedure" — and the ε-optimality conclusion is exactly DDB
**Value**. The principal's fix is therefore a structural cousin of v2 §10: it replaces the engine of
self-trust (there, the conditional martingale `ccee`; here, Cole's constructed oracle) with a single
clean cross-agent premise (there, LUV-Total-Trust; here, the `(1-δ)`-belief in UDT1.1). The
`udt-representation-theorem/formal-single-agent.md` "split (UDT1.0) vs unified (UDT1.1)" picture,
including its open question **"Approximate unity"**, is *literally* this theorem. So the cleanest
first move is to prove the algebraic shadow in that finite split/unified setting (Idea 1, Lean'd),
then push toward the reflective-oracle / LI renderings.

## Idea 1 (TOP PICK; partially Lean-formalized) — Belief-you're-UDT1.1 ⇒ ε-optimal as a finite mixture-deference bound

**Candidate proposition (PROVED at the finite-algebra level; see `lean/UDT11Belief.lean`,
UNCHECKED).** Work in the `formal-single-agent.md` setting: a multi-situation problem with policies
`σ ∈ Pol`, a "true" (UDT1.1) value-of-a-policy map `Vstar : Pol → ℝ`, and an arbitrary "other code"
value map `Vother : Pol → ℝ`, both valued in `[lo, hi]` (bounded utility). A UDT1.0 agent that
`(1-δ)`-believes its policy is generated by UDT1.1 evaluates a candidate policy by the mixture
`Bel(σ) = (1-δ)·Vstar(σ) + δ·Vother(σ)` and commits to a `Bel`-maximizer `s`. Then, with `pstar` a
genuine `Vstar`-optimum (the definition of UDT1.1),

$$(1-\delta)\,\big(V_\star(p_\star)-V_\star(s)\big)\ \le\ \delta\,(hi-lo),
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad V_\star(p_\star)-V_\star(s)\ \le\ \tfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}\,(hi-lo).$$

So the agent's actually-chosen policy is `ε(δ)`-optimal with `ε(δ)=δ/(1-δ)·range` (≤ `2δ·range` for
`δ≤½`; exactly `0` at `δ=0`). **This is the honest form of the principal's theorem.** Note the
correction the math forces: the naive `ε=δ·range` is only the first-order approximation — the bad
δ-world is weighed against the `(1-δ)` good-world *mass*, not against `1`, so a `1/(1-δ)` factor is
real and the bound genuinely degrades as `δ→1` (you can be arbitrarily bad if you barely believe you
are UDT1.1). The Lean file proves the exact `(1-δ)`-form (`epsilon_optimal_of_belief`), the clean
`2δ·range` corollary for `δ≤½` (`epsilon_optimal_clean`), and the `δ=0` exact-optimality corner
(`optimal_of_certain`).

**Why it bears on trust.** This is the single cleanest "when can you trust a sub-optimal-by-design
reasoner" statement in the thread. A UDT1.0 agent is *known to be improvable* (it can sit in a
Stag-Hunt bad equilibrium with itself — a bad whole-strategy that no single-action change fixes). The
theorem says: a human (or the agent itself) can justifiably treat it as near-optimal **exactly to the
extent it believes a globally-coordinating optimizer is in charge of the whole policy.** The belief
is the trust-warrant, and the bound makes the warrant quantitative. This is the decision-theoretic
analog of the lab's epistemic-trust results: there, *endorsing the expert's beliefs* ⇒ deference;
here, *believing your decisions come from the optimal coordinator* ⇒ near-optimal decisions.

**Cleanest formal setting.** Finite `Pol`, real value maps — the `formal-single-agent.md` /
`LeanDeference.Deference` finite algebra, deliberately stripped of LI and reflective oracles. This is
the honest *shadow* (cf. how `LeanDeference.value_of_CM` is the `δ=0` shadow of the LI §3 chain). The
Lean mirrors that discipline: boundedness + a believed-argmax hypothesis, conclusion = a Value gap.

**Fidelity caveats (the gap to flag — this is the most important part).**
1. The Lean models the agent as choosing among *whole policies* with a single believed-value
   functional. A genuine UDT1.0 agent chooses *actions pointwise*. The reduction "pointwise UDT1.0
   that believes it is UDT1.1 ⇒ effectively chooses the believed UDT1.1 policy" is the **informal
   bridge the Lean assumes, not proves.** This is exactly where the Stag-Hunt subtlety lives, so it
   is smuggled — *honestly* — into the `hsel` hypothesis. Removing this smuggling is Idea 2.
2. `Vother` is left *fully arbitrary* (subject only to the bound). That is faithful and is the
   strength: the `(1-δ)` mass on UDT1.1 dominates *whatever* the bad world says. It also means the
   theorem is **not** about the agent's belief being *accurate* — only about its belief being
   *concentrated* on UDT1.1. (A `(1-δ)`-belief that is concentrated but wrong still yields the bound;
   what you actually get is closeness to `Vstar`, the map you believe in. If `Vstar` is not really
   optimal, "ε-optimal" is ε-optimal-by-your-own-lights. Flag for the trust reading: **concentration
   of self-belief ≠ correctness of self-belief.**)
3. Non-vacuity: the bound is tight-ish and `hi>lo` makes it informative; at `δ=0` it is exact
   optimality, not `True`-style.

**Difficulty.** Low for the finite shadow (Lean written, plausibly kernel-checkable today). Medium-
hard to lift `Vstar`/`Vother` to genuine reflective-oracle *believed continuations* (the real
theorem). **This is the most promising single idea to develop** because it (i) already has a checkable
artifact, (ii) corrects the principal's stated bound, and (iii) cleanly exposes where the real
content (Idea 2) must go.

## Idea 2 — Make the smuggled bridge a theorem: when does pointwise-UDT1.0-believing-UDT1.1 actually implement the UDT1.1 policy? (the Stag-Hunt closure)

**Candidate proposition (CONJECTURE).** In a multi-situation problem with cross-situation dependence
(`formal-single-agent.md` §"Pattern Dependence"), let a UDT1.0 agent choose, *at each situation `o`
independently*, the action `argmax_a E[U | my action at o is a]`, but compute that conditional
expectation under the belief that its action at *every other* situation is the UDT1.1 action
`π*(o')`, with confidence `(1-δ)` (and with prob `δ` distributed over arbitrary alternative
profiles). Then the resulting pointwise-best-response profile **coincides with `π*`** whenever `π*`
is the *unique* `(1-δ)`-robust correlated equilibrium of the induced self-coordination game, and in
general lies within `O(δ)` value of `π*`. Equivalently: **belief-you're-UDT1.1 selects the good
equilibrium of the Stag Hunt that UDT1.0-with-itself plays.**

**Why it bears on trust.** This is the actual mechanism by which the principal's fix replaces Cole's
constructed oracle. Cole's special reflective oracle exists to *break ties / avoid self-coordination
failure*; the conjecture says the `(1-δ)`-belief does the identical job by acting as a **correlation
device that points all situation-copies at the same (optimal) policy.** Trust reading: you can trust
a fleet of independently-acting copies (or an agent across time, or a slow human + fast AI both
modeled as "situations") *to the extent they share the belief that an optimal coordinator authored
the joint policy.* Shared belief = shared correlation device = no treacherous bad equilibrium.

**Cleanest formal setting.** A finite **self-coordination game**: players = situations, each player's
payoff = the agent's global utility, each plays a best response under the `(1-δ)`/`δ` belief about the
*others'* play. This is precisely the reflective-oracle ↔ correlated-equilibrium correspondence in
`internal-fixpoint/reflective-oracles-project/` (their Δ²(X) fixed points = correlated equilibria).
The `(1-δ)`-belief is a *biased prior over the correlation signal*; the question is whether biasing
toward `π*` makes `π*` the unique fixed point.

**Difficulty.** Medium. The "unique equilibrium ⇒ exact" half is a standard `ε`-best-response /
perturbed-equilibrium argument (potential games / Stag Hunt selection); fully formalizable. The "in
general `O(δ)`" half is harder and may need a smoothness/uniqueness hypothesis to rule out
multiple bad equilibria surviving the perturbation — that hypothesis is the real content and the
analog of Cole's *choice* of oracle. **Cross-connect:** this is the constructive twin of v2 §10.4's
open "characterization" problem — there, *which inductors LUV-Total-Trust which*; here, *which
situation-profiles the `(1-δ)`-belief stabilizes*. Both ask when a coordination/trust premise is
self-fulfilling rather than assumed.

## Idea 3 — Improbable actions without source-code knowledge: a soft-conditioning analog of the chicken rule, and whether Troll-Bridge recurs

**Candidate proposition (SKETCHED + CONJECTURE).** Cole avoids spurious proofs by *not knowing his
source code* (forcing all action-probabilities positive), which the AGENDA calls "a weak defense."
Claim, in two parts:

- **(3a, SKETCHED, structural analogy to v2 §0.3 / §5.2.)** The pathology the principal worries about
  — "as the agent becomes confident of its code, evaluating an un-taken action requires conditioning
  on a vanishing-probability, increasingly contrived world" — is *exactly the hard-vs-soft
  conditioning split that LI already diagnoses.* In LI, **hard** conditioning on a measure-zero / liar
  event is paradox-prone and the relevant theorem (Self-Trust 4.12.4) is **false** in its hard form
  and **true** in its soft (`Ind_δ`) form. Translating: an embedded agent that *hard*-conditions on
  "I take the un-taken action `a`" (a `P→0` event under code-confidence) is in the regime where
  Troll-Bridge-style Löbian collapse is possible; an agent that evaluates `a` via a **soft
  counterfactual** of width `δ_n↓0` (a smeared "what if my code were slightly different") is in the
  regime LI proves safe. **Candidate:** the chicken rule and Cole's not-knowing-your-code are *two
  hard ways* to keep probabilities positive; the *soft-counterfactual* (Conditional-Oracles-flavored)
  way is the LI-native one, and it is the one immune to spurious proofs *and* to the hard-Troll-Bridge
  Löb argument.

- **(3b, CONJECTURE — the failure mode the principal asks about.)** Even *without* explicit source-
  code knowledge, if the agent is *mostly confident* about its code, a Troll-Bridge-like
  pathology recurs in evidential dress: taking an action the agent has near-proven it won't take has
  *negative evidential consequences* (it is evidence for an alternative, worse, code-hypothesis), so
  the agent avoids it, which confirms the near-proof. **This is Troll Bridge with "inconsistency"
  replaced by "I must be running worse code than I thought."** Candidate test: exhibit a concrete
  small environment (a 2-action "evidential Troll Bridge": crossing is good, but crossing is also
  evidence you're the buggy code that crashes) where Cole's empirical-self-model agent provably
  declines the good action for `δ` bounded away from `0`, *whether or not* it literally knows its
  source. If so, the principal's worry is vindicated: not-knowing-your-code is genuinely a weak
  defense.

**Why it bears on trust.** A reasoner that can be Troll-Bridged is one whose *avoidance of an option
is uninformative about the option's quality* — its choices are not truth-tracking about value, so a
human cannot read its refusals as evidence of badness. Distinguishing "declines because bad" from
"declines because Löb/evidential-self-doubt" is exactly the legitimacy distinction (AGENDA "Modeling
Legitimacy") at the level of *actions*. A trustworthy agent's refusals must be legitimate.

**Cleanest formal setting.** For (3a): the LI soft/hard apparatus directly (`Ind_δ`, 4.12.3/4.12.4);
this is the most reusable since the lab's machinery is built for it. For (3b): a tiny reflective-
oracle environment, or — to stay in checkable territory — a finite decision problem with an explicit
"code-hypothesis" latent variable and an evidential (EDT) `argmax`, where "negative evidential
consequence of a low-probability action" can be written as a finite conditional expectation and the
Löb-like fixed point is a small algebraic self-reference.

**Difficulty.** (3a) medium, mostly re-interpretation of existing LI theorems — payoff is conceptual
unification. (3b) medium-hard but high-value: a clean counterexample would be a *publishable*
sharpening of the AGENDA's "weak defense" remark and is the kind of failure-mode the SCOPE rules ask
us to hunt. **Red-team flavored.**

## Idea 4 — Separating self-knowledge from environmental modification (rescuing the optimality interpretation)

**Candidate definition + proposition (CONJECTURE).** The principal's critique (b): Cole's
`(1-δ)`-confidence in "I mechanically work like *this* (my initial code)" *sneakily rules out self-
modification*, hence rules out environments where updatelessness is needed-and-reachable-via-self-mod
— so the "self-knowledge" hypothesis covertly restricts the *environment class*, muddying the
optimality reading. **Fix:** split the confidence into two independent parameters.

- Define **behavioural self-knowledge** `(1-δ_b)`: confidence about *the function from
  (history)→(action-distribution)* the agent implements, i.e. about its **policy**, not its code.
- Define **substrate stability** `(1-δ_m)`: confidence that this policy is *not going to be
  modified* (by the environment or by the agent itself) over the horizon.

**Candidate proposition.** Cole's theorem (and Idea 1) needs only `δ_b` small — confidence about
*what you do*, the **extensional** policy — and is *agnostic* to `δ_m`. The conflation in Cole/the
principal's worry is that "confidence in my source code" bundles `δ_b` and `δ_m` together (knowing
the code pins both behaviour *and* immutability). Once split: (i) an environment that *rewards*
self-modification has small `δ_m` but can still have small `δ_b` *for the post-modification policy*,
so an optimality theorem stated about the **realized** policy survives; (ii) the "self-knowledge
rules out interesting environments" objection becomes "self-knowledge-of-*code* does, self-knowledge-
of-*behaviour* need not." This is the AGENDA's own suggested fix ("distinguishing self-knowledge from
environmental concerns, or self-modification from environment-forced modification") made precise.

**Why it bears on trust.** This is the tiling-relevant core. A human trusting an AI cares about
*behavioural* predictability (Vingean: "I trust it will win, not how"), **not** about the AI's code
being frozen. Conflating the two is exactly the mistake that makes "trustworthy" sound like
"unmodifiable," which is both false and anti-corrigible. Separating `δ_b` from `δ_m` is the formal
hook for **corrigibility** (AGENDA "Modeling Legitimacy"): a corrigible AI has small `δ_b` (you can
predict its behaviour) *and small `δ_m` toward legitimate human-initiated modification* (it expects
and endorses being changed). Trust should rest on `δ_b` + *legitimacy of* `δ_m`, never on `δ_m=0`.

**Cleanest formal setting.** A two-stage decision problem: stage 1 the agent may modify its policy
(a meta-action), stage 2 it acts. Self-knowledge enters as a prior over the *stage-2 realized policy*.
Show the Idea-1 bound holds with `δ := δ_b` and *no* dependence on `δ_m`. Finite and Lean-adjacent
(it is Idea 1 with `Pol` = realized post-modification policies and the belief placed there).

**Difficulty.** Medium. The definitional split is the contribution; the accompanying bound is a
re-indexing of Idea 1. The hard/open part is characterizing when `δ_b` *can* be kept small across a
self-modification (Vingean self-trust across the mod — connects to v2 §10.4 tiling register).

## Idea 5 — Why updateful AIXI "looks fine": formalizing the interleaving-conditionals artifact and a correlation-restoring repair

**Candidate proposition (SKETCHED, diagnostic; CONJECTURE for the repair).** The principal's
critique (c): Cole's `ε`-optimality samples histories by drawing **observations from `ξ`** (rOSI) but
**actions from `π`**, *interleaving* conditionals so the environment can only react *after* an action
occurs — hence Ω cannot be modeled as predicting you, and updateful behaviour always looks optimal
("AIXI converges to AIXI"). Two formal targets:

- **(5a, SKETCHED — name the artifact.)** Make precise that this evaluation map is a **causal /
  do-operator** semantics: `V_interleave(π) = E_{o∼ξ, a∼π}[U]` is the value of an *intervention*
  `do(actions := π)`, which by construction breaks any environment→action back-channel. Claim: under
  this semantics, *every* policy is its own best response (no acausal leverage exists to exploit), so
  "updateful is fine" is **true but trivial** — it is optimality in the strict subclass of
  **Cartesian / do-fair** environments (the "playing a video game from outside" case the principal
  notes Cole may be implicitly targeting). This is the decision-theoretic statement of v2's "bounded
  + no-acausal" hygiene, here as a *fairness restriction on the environment* rather than on utility.
- **(5b, CONJECTURE — the repair.)** Replace the interleaving with a **fixed-point / correlated**
  evaluation `V_corr(π) = E_{(o,a)∼μ_π}[U]` where `μ_π` is the reflective-oracle fixed point that
  *allows* Ω's oracle calls to correlate with `π`'s. Conjecture: under `V_corr`, the Idea-1/Idea-2
  belief-you're-UDT1.1 hypothesis becomes *load-bearing* (it is no longer free), and the theorem
  "belief-UDT1.1 ⇒ ε-optimal" now has nontrivial content precisely because `V_corr` *can* reward
  updatelessness. In the principal's words, fixing the oracle (allow correlation) and fixing the
  optimality notion (the interleaving) are **interchangeable**; this conjecture says the
  belief-you're-UDT1.1 premise is what *connects* the two repairs.

**Why it bears on trust.** Whether an optimality theorem secretly assumes a do-fair / Cartesian
environment is exactly whether it certifies the AI in the *world it actually faces* (embedded, with
predictors and acausal channels) or only in a sandbox. A trust certificate that silently assumes
"nothing predicts you" is worthless against a manipulative or predictive environment — and
manipulation/prediction is the core safety concern. Surfacing the do-operator hidden in `ε`-optimality
is a trust-relevant honesty check on the whole genre of AIXI-optimality results.

**Cleanest formal setting.** Reflective oracles for the genuine version (needs the rOSI /
correlated-fixed-point apparatus in `internal-fixpoint/reflective-oracles-project/`). A finite
two-node causal model (agent node, Ω-predictor node, with/without the Ω→agent or agent→Ω edge)
suffices for the *diagnostic* (5a) and is checkable; it cleanly exhibits "interleaving = cutting the
Ω→agent edge."

**Difficulty.** (5a) medium and tractable — a causal-semantics reframing with a finite witness.
(5b) hard — requires the correlation-allowing reflective oracle, the deepest technical object in the
thread. High payoff but I would not start here.

## Idea 6 — Belief-you're-UDT1.1 inside Logical Induction (the Vingean / bounded analog)

**Candidate proposition (CONJECTURE; the direct v2-§10 bridge).** Port Idea 1 into LI, so it inherits
*computability* and the lab's free coherence. Let the "expert" be **the optimal-policy procedure
`π*`**, supplying a sequence of estimated policy-values `𝔼_exp(V(π*))`. Model "the UDT1.0 inductor
`(1-δ)`-believes its policy is UDT1.1" as: the inductor's *own* day-`f(n)` self assigns price `≥1-δ`
to the sentence `"my realized policy = π*"`. **Conjecture:** if this self-endorsement price holds in
the timely-manner (`≳_n 1-δ`) sense, then the LI **Value** gap is `≲_n δ·range` — i.e. the
§3/§10 Value bound holds up to an additive `δ`. The engine is, as in v2 §10, the single cross-agent
premise **LUV-Total-Trust toward `π*`**, here softened by the `(1-δ)` self-belief weight `w_n :=
Ind_δ(ℙ_{f(n)}("policy=π*"))` plugged into `ccee` (4.12.3) — *exactly the licensed soft future-state
weight*.

**Why it bears on trust.** This is the bounded, Vingean version, and therefore the one closest to
real AI: a *computable* reasoner that comes to (approximately) trust that it is the optimal coordinator
and is thereby (approximately) optimal — with all the trust derived dynamically from the no-Dutch-book
criterion (no hand-built oracle, the v2 "win" over both Cole and DDB). It is the decision-theoretic
sibling of v2's epistemic self-trust.

**Cleanest formal setting.** Logical induction, reusing the v2 §3/§10 five-line chain verbatim with
`O^j` reinterpreted as policy-value LUVs and the selection weight carrying the self-belief. Crucially
this is **expert-agnostic in the §10 sense** — the §10 audit already shows lines 2/4/5 are free, so
only the self-endorsement-of-`π*` premise must be supplied. **Cross-connect (sharp):** v2 §10.4's
genuinely-open "when does inductor `N` LUV-Total-Trust inductor `M`?" *is* the question "when does a
UDT1.0 inductor justifiably believe it is UDT1.1?" — `M` = `π*`. The decision-theoretic thread and the
epistemic-deference thread **collapse to the same open characterization.** That is the most important
structural observation in this note after Idea 1.

**Difficulty.** Medium-hard. The plumbing (re-typing `O^j` as policy values, checking `π*`-value is a
legal `[0,1]`-LUV and the self-belief weight is market-generable) is real but bounded; the §10 chain
does the heavy lifting. The open subtlety: is "policy = π*" even a *well-typed market-generable*
event for an embedded inductor (self-reference!), or does it hit the diagonal problem of v2 §1.2 /
AGENDA "Representational Issues"? If it does, the *soft* `Ind_δ` self-belief is again the surviving
form — which would be a satisfying unification.

## Idea 7 (lateral, lower priority) — UDT1.0/1.1 as a Blackwell refinement, recovering v2 §10.3(a)

**Candidate proposition (INTERPRETATION → theorem, CONJECTURE).** v2 §10.3(a): *the future self is
the join of all observable experts* (Blackwell-maximal). Lateral claim: **UDT1.1 is the Blackwell
refinement of UDT1.0** in the policy lattice. UDT1.0 chooses action-by-action (a coarse,
situation-local "experiment"); UDT1.1 chooses the whole policy (the finest joint "experiment"). By
Blackwell–Geanakoplos value-of-information monotonicity, deferring to UDT1.1 weakly dominates UDT1.0,
*and* the value gap is governed by how far the UDT1.0 information partition falls short of the joint
one — which is `0` exactly when there is no cross-situation dependence (recovering the
`formal-single-agent.md` "split = unified when no cross-situation dependence" theorem). Then Idea 1's
`δ`-belief is a *probabilistic interpolation* between the coarse and fine experiments, and `ε(δ)` is
the Blackwell value-gap scaled by `δ`.

**Why it bears on trust.** Casts "trust UDT1.1 over UDT1.0" as a *comparison of experiments* — the
same currency as the lab's epistemic deference (v2 §1.1, §10.3). It would unify the decision-theoretic
(Stag-Hunt) and information-theoretic (Blackwell) readings of why whole-policy optimization dominates,
and tie the thread to the lab's existing finite-frame Blackwell machinery.

**Cleanest formal setting.** Finite probability frames + Blackwell ordering on policy-partitions
(the v2 §1.1 / Geanakoplos apparatus). Likely a finite, Lean-adjacent statement once "UDT1.0 partition
⊑ UDT1.1 partition" is pinned.

**Difficulty.** Medium. The interpretation is clean; making "UDT1.0 ⊑ UDT1.1 as experiments" precise
(and matching its value-gap to Idea 1's `δ(hi-lo)`) is the work, and risks the usual Weatherson
infinite-frame caveats if pushed past finite. Lower priority than 1/2/6 but a nice unifier.

## Ranking and the single most promising idea

**Top pick: Idea 1**, developed toward **Idea 2** then **Idea 6**.

- **Idea 1** is the only one with a concrete checkable artifact (`lean/UDT11Belief.lean`), it
  *corrects* the principal's stated bound (the real `ε` is `δ/(1-δ)·range`, not `δ·range`), and it
  pins down the load-bearing fidelity gap (the pointwise→whole-policy bridge) that the rest of the
  thread must close. It is the honest finite shadow, in exactly the discipline of the confirmed
  `LeanDeference.lean`.
- **Idea 2** turns that smuggled bridge into the real theorem (belief-you're-UDT1.1 = a correlation
  device selecting the good Stag-Hunt equilibrium), using the lab's own reflective-oracle ↔
  correlated-equilibrium correspondence.
- **Idea 6** lifts it into LI, where v2 §10's audit shows the work reduces to *one* cross-agent
  premise — and reveals that "when may a UDT1.0 inductor believe it is UDT1.1?" is **literally** v2
  §10.4's open "when does one inductor LUV-Total-Trust another?" The decision-theoretic and epistemic
  threads share one open characterization; closing it is the thread's center of gravity.

The red-team obligation (SCOPE rule 5) is discharged mainly by **Idea 3b** (a concrete evidential-
Troll-Bridge counterexample would show Cole's not-knowing-your-code is genuinely a weak defense) and
by the fidelity caveats of Idea 1 (concentration of self-belief ≠ correctness; the `1/(1-δ)` blowup).
