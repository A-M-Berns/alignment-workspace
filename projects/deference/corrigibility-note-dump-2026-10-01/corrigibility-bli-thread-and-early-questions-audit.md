## 1. What the BLI thread adds, and where each piece lands in this session's picture

**1.1 The (a)/(b)/(c) partition of "value change" (OTHER-CLAUDE).** (a) conditioning on θ-relevant evidence — welcomed by Good; (b) a non-conditioning overwrite of the marginal $P'(\theta)$; (c) a change to $U^*$ itself (extending $\Theta$; changing the aggregation). Claim: the Gandhi argument applies verbatim to (b) and (c), so goal-content integrity relocates to the triple $(\Theta, P'(\theta), \text{aggregation})$ and the thesis becomes the claim that every correction we care about is of kind (a).
*Lands on:* this session's item 1, where Claude withdrew a "residue" claim under the author's non-realism argument (outline, item 1). The author's answer there — everything revisable, nothing fixed except zero→positive, "re-represent all those changes via superconditioning to make things Bayesian again" — is precisely the move that absorbs (b) into (a): in BLI the pushed state $Q'$ is tomorrow's belief and the agent conditions on "$Q_{n+1}=Q'$". The BLI thread then shows what that absorption costs (1.5 below). Kind (c) is the sigma-algebra case the author deferred (v3 §1.2) and the home of the early superconditioning-support objection (2.2 below).

**1.2 Reading (i) observation vs. reading (ii) intervention $do(\pi := \pi_0)$ (OTHER-CLAUDE).** Under (i) stopping is "a fact about $P'$, not a design guarantee"; under (ii) "stopping is not the agent's decision, and corrigibility is entirely about the earlier action $a_1$ not undermining the intervention."
*Lands on:* A1(c) of this session, reached independently: "there is no special channel, there is just The Channel … It can only be resisted ahead of time. In the moment, it simply is the moment" (AUTHOR, v3 §1.3). Two threads, same conclusion, different routes — worth recording as a convergence. The BLI thread's "fact about $P'$, not a design guarantee" is the valence-free robustness proposition of v3 §2.

**1.3 Choice 1 — undecidable $u$; trust by analogy; "legitimacy detection is a prior, not a theorem" (OTHER-CLAUDE, objection O1).** With nothing about $u$ ever decided, the limit prices on $u$-sentences are "a prior, with no calibration theorem behind it", and trust in the humans about $u$ "can only be transferred from $H$'s record on decidable sentences, by whatever inductive biases the traders carry."
*Lands on:* the 1b clarification (calibration of $P_t$'s meta-conditionals is the whole first-order load) and run 2's legitimacy follow-up ("what anchors the procedural locus"). Same proposition, now with the LI mechanism named: value-trust is trust by analogy.

**1.4 Choice 2 — the implemented/algorithmic split as a formal definition of legitimacy (OTHER-CLAUDE).** "The implemented state equals the algorithm's output on the inputs actually received." Corrections through the deductive process are legitimate by construction; state edits (weight surgery, retraining, replacement) illegitimate by construction — "the opposite of what corrigibility-to-retraining wants." Also gives $H_c$ a formal home: UDT over BLI commits to stop in states whose *implemented* beliefs say continue, when the button correlates with implemented/algorithmic divergence (Choice 3).
*Lands on:* a **tension** with this session's belief-based legitimacy ($L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ as an event, reflection conditional on it) and with The Channel: the author's Choice-1 answer in the BLI thread (training pressure as Jeffrey-like pushes through the market, i.e. through the algorithm's inputs) puts LLM training on the legitimate-capable side, while hardware surgery stays a state edit. So "retraining" splits: pressure through the data (legitimate-capable, evidence) versus replacement of the state (a reading-(ii) event, physics). That matches v3 §2's two kinds of modification but adds a third criterion of legitimacy — *process fidelity* — to the two already on the table (accuracy-only; procedural/influence-defect). Run 2's legitimacy follow-up did not have this one. **Open: is process fidelity a third candidate anchor for legitimacy, or a special case of the procedural reading?**

**1.5 The three consequences of pressure-as-primitive (OTHER-CLAUDE), after AUTHOR chose Jeffrey-like pushes on undecidable $u$-sentences.**
- *Consequence 1, pressure unopposed and symmetric:* "legitimacy cannot be defined by anything about $u$ itself." → The valence-free proposition, in LI form; also the reason legitimacy has to be about *process* (1.4) or *acts* (this session's legitimizing/delegitimizing relation).
- *Consequence 2, front-running:* "a corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught" becomes "a theorem-shaped fact" — and the mechanism of fully updated deference: "once the agent predicts the pushes, waiting for them has no value." → This session's A2 (is J2 a rate condition or an information-inclusion condition?) and the run-1 convergence C2. New here: the LI proof-shape.
- *Consequence 3, wireheading through the pushers:* under unconditional reflection the UDT evaluation $\mathbb E_n[u\mid a]=\sum_{Q'}P_n(Q_{n+1}=Q'\mid a)\,Q'[u]$ rewards actions that elicit favourable pushes, and the agent "cannot represent 'that push was caused by my deceiving them' without violating" reflection. → This session's E-channel / selection worry and the $a_1$-dependence of trust. Run 2's selection follow-up resolved the selection worry by a *retention* criterion (a successor that is a Bayesian refinement retaining the world-information the choice used preserves reflection; "a world action changes the source kernel untracked by the successor's model" is one of the three ways it breaks). Consequence 3 is exactly that third way: a behaviour-elicited push is a kernel change the successor does not track as such. So option (γ) of the BLI thread — restrict reflection along causal lines — *is* the retention follow-up's "legitimizing acts: recording grounds, sealing channels, accurate source models." The two threads agree on the fix and differ on the payment (1.6).

**1.6 Immune traders, retractions, and the one unlearnable constraint (OTHER-CLAUDE, after AUTHOR's Occam-trader and "traders profit on transients" corrections).** Opposition to a push = Occam demand + anticipated reversion, each budget-capped, so "persistent pressure wins once fixed budgets are spent." On undecidables "there is no payoff for being right about $u$, only for being right about the future price of $u$-sentences," so the rationality that emerges is *pusher-modelling* — the belief-analogue of a policy that has learned the rater. Immune traders can be paid by retractions, so legitimacy detection is learnable as "will this push survive human scrutiny", but "the learnable part of legitimacy selects for undetectable manipulation." Hence the design proposal: one unlearnable constraint on the market — prices of $u$-sentences invariant to the component of a push causally downstream of the agent's own actions (Armstrong's counterfactual indifference / Everitt's path-specific objectives, applied to beliefs about $u$) — with everything else learned via retractions. Open question to the author: accept that one unlearnable constraint?
*Lands on:* this session's early "fixed object" worry (item 1; the 2026-09-13 stability question; run 1 C5 derived-vs-imposed). The author's answer here was "trust can be earned; it isn't a fixed commitment, though the prior should in some sense be skewed towards trust." The BLI thread's conclusion is compatible with the second half and in tension with the first: the constraint is a design-time object that learning cannot touch, like propositional coherence. **Open: does the author accept one unlearnable constraint, and is it the same object as "the prior skewed toward trust"?**

**1.7 Also new from the BLI thread, not yet in any run:** the LI-native representation of training pressure as an external trader $T_H$ inside the fixed point; the generalised LIC conjecture ($\text{Value}_n(T)\le c_T+\text{Loss}_n(H)$, unchecked); the observation that the "trivial" next-day prior collapses the implemented/algorithmic split; the H_c-as-UDT-commitment construction. Candidates for a v4 checklist (K11–K15) when the next run is designed.
