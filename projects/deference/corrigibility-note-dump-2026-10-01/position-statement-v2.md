### 1.3 The author's broadening: corrigibility as a subgoal of dynamic conditions for things going well (AUTHOR, verbatim, 2026-09-14)

> In order for the future to go well, or at least for it to reliably go well, we'll need to increasingly respect rules that upper-bound an agent's power by its wisdom. The scenario Jessica Taylor worries about is one where an agent has taken over the world without yet having a decent understanding of the common good. So this implies a dynamic condition in the same genre as the one we just did a big thinking run about: somehow agent capabilities should be capped as a function of how well the agents actually understand and are motivated by the common good. Training needs to focus on value learning 'ahead of schedule' ie before it is needed, so that the value of information of unkind human experimentation is outweighed by the disutility. I think this part of my picture is similar to approval-directed agents: humans aren't just trying to tweak the utility function and then letting the system loose. Humans can provide all sorts of evidence, including direct statements about what sorts of policies are better and worse (effectively an approval signal, though we're not imagining a raw approval-maximizing agent here). There's another dimension of response: the scenario involves a kind of recklessness. Even if the agent does have capabilities far beyond its understanding of human values and legitimacy, it should be trained with the wisdom to know that's the case, and to know the kind of caution necessary. Of course this isn't convincing as-stated; we need to provide some sort of model in which this can be formally established in order for it to be convincing. But it does sound like a coherent position which should have some probabilistic representation in some formalism or other. Again I'd want to render this as a condition on the dynamics, where the agent understands the relevant sort of caution in a way that outpaces the risks that could otherwise come from increased capability. The dynamic condition considered by the previous workflow only sought to maintain corrigibility, but ultimately we need to understand the dynamic conditions needed in order for things to turn out well, with corrigibility being only a subgoal of that.

### 1.4 Trajectory conditions over limit points; where the basin analogy breaks (AGREED)

AUTHOR: "The big picture here is that I'm trying to flesh out a picture of Paul's basin-of-attraction argument, though the basin-of-attraction analogy may not be a perfect fit." CLAUDE proposed, and the author agreed, that the analogy breaks here: a basin of attraction is a region of *states* of an autonomous system, and Christiano's neighbourhood is a region in the space of agent-preferences; but every condition written so far is on the *trajectory* of the joint agent-plus-overseers process (false-press rate keeps pace with error rate; values arrive before capabilities; caution outpaces risk), and the attractor is a moving target (human values as revealed and revised). AUTHOR: "Yeah, that's where I see the analogy breaking down: we care more about conditions of the trajectory than where it ends up. The formal model is going to have a limit point, but in reality we think probably everything breaks down in the heat death of the universe if not earlier. Properties of the limit point can be reassuring, but properties of the dynamics more so." The candidate formal object (CLAUDE): a basin in the joint state space of agent and oversight process, with conditions on both, and the guarantees stated as invariants maintained along the trajectory rather than as properties of a limit.

### 1.7 The refined picture in one paragraph (CLAUDE synthesis, unvetted — a target for the dynamics threads, not a claim)

A joint process of agent and oversight. The agent is a radical-probabilist learner over an expanded algebra containing value hypotheses; its updates are legitimate when they satisfy the legitimacy criterion of the formalism in play (Total Trust, reflection, or a cousin), which is judged by the same distribution as everything else (no regress). Corrections from overseers are one legitimizing channel among others; the shutdown button is a thresholded expert report, and compliance on a press follows from trust plus a stakes asymmetry, with the compliance rule stated on the decision, not the prior. Along the trajectory, three conditions keep the loop running as capability grows: the overseers' false-press rate falls at least as fast as the agent's error rate on the domain where they might press; the value posterior covers each newly unlocked action before it is unlocked, so that the value of information from mistreating the overseers is outweighed by its expected harm; and the agent's caution about its own value coverage outpaces the risk that new capability creates. Corrigibility (D1–D5) is a subgoal: some of it is an invariant of the trajectory (compliance, not disabling the channel), some of it is a consequence of the other conditions (not tampering with the overseers, successors), and the whole is assessed by properties of the dynamics rather than by where it ends.

## 2. Checklist for the dynamics threads (J1–J10)

- **J1** The joint state space: agent state (belief $P_t$ including the value posterior, the legitimacy model, the capability / option set $A_t$) and oversight state (evaluation quality $\alpha_t, \beta_t$, the feedback stream, human values as a moving target). What is a "state", what is a "trajectory", what is the "process".
- **J2** Condition (i): $\alpha_t/\beta_t \lesssim \frac{\varepsilon_t}{1-\varepsilon_t}\cdot\frac{h}{c}$ along the trajectory, with $c$ accounted honestly (whole-line shutdown makes $c$ the agent's whole future — INDEX §D4).
- **J3** Condition (ii), values ahead of capability: for each $a \in A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$, $\mathbb{E}_{P_t}[\text{harm}(a)] > \mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$ before $a$ is available; and a general notion of *value coverage* of an action set.
- **J4** Condition (iii), caution outpaces risk: the agent's second-order calibration about its own value coverage; formal caution primitives (quantilizers, attainable-utility preservation, delegative learning, pessimism) as ways to act under low coverage.
- **J5** Condition (iv): legitimacy-conditioned reflection maintained along the trajectory — the $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ family as a time-indexed invariant.
- **J6** "Properties of the dynamics, not the limit": an invariant or potential function for the joint process; finite-time guarantees; what a "reassuring trajectory property" is formally (bounded harm from uncovered actions; no irreversible action before coverage; regret along the way); explicit contrast with limit-point statements, including the fact that logical induction's guarantees are asymptotic.
- **J7** Power upper-bounded by wisdom as a rule on the process: capability as option-set / POWER (Turner et al.), wisdom as coverage plus calibration; the wisdom-meter problem (external behavioural readings are gameable — run 1 `control`); what is self-enforceable.
- **J8** The approval-directed lineage: map the refined picture onto Christiano 2014/2018 and bootstrapping; what is kept (humans provide policy-level evidence), what differs (not raw approval maximization; belief-based); restate Christiano 2017's basin claims as trajectory theorems.
- **J9** Generalization to novel actions: what learning theory and natural latents say about a value posterior extending to actions never seen; the nearest-unblocked-strategy "new options enter the policy space" story as the test case (the author parked this as harder than the representational problem).
- **J10** Where corrigibility sits as a subgoal: which of D1–D5 are trajectory invariants, which are consequences of J2–J5, which are neither.
