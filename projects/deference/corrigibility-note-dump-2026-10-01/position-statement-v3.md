## 1. The three decisions of the round, in the author's words

### 1.1 What a press is evidence about (AUTHOR)

Run 2 offered three readings (about which plan the humans prefer / about the agent's own invisible compromise / a command rather than evidence). AUTHOR: "I think humans can provide evidence about a lot of things, so the stop button might be modeled in various ways depending on what ideas we want to highlight, but the central case in my mind is that the shutdown command is evidence that shutting down is good. Likewise, a command is evidence about shoulds, or at least we can model it that way."

CLAUDE reading (unvetted): the coarse reading — evidence about the normative quantity $X$ (value of continuing over stopping) directly, source unspecified; exactly the Total Trust filler's reading. Amendment 1a then becomes a claim about which *component* of the humans' information survives capability growth (plan-preference information the agent can eventually gather itself decays toward the fully-updated-deference arithmetic; information about the agent's own compromise, which it structurally cannot gather, does not), not a claim about what the press means.

### 1.2 Pause or whole-line shutdown (AUTHOR)

"It is my opinion that the pause button example is a special case chosen for simplicity, and the whole question of corrigibility should be about as broad a space of modifications as it can be, not restricted to some particular shutdown scenario. In the current scope it seems most natural to consider any belief updates meeting the conditions for superconditioning, ie, absolute continuity. I also want to consider the possibility of sigma-algebra changes, which I think is not so difficult to do, but perhaps we should study the case without sigma-algebra changes thoroughly first."

CLAUDE reading (unvetted): the dichotomy dissolves into a parameter. The object is a proposed modification $P_t \to Q$ with $Q \ll P_t$; the "press" is the overseers pushing toward $Q$; compliance is adopting or acting on $Q$; stakes are per-modification, $c(Q)$ (loss if $Q$ adopted and illegitimate) and $h(Q)$ (harm if $Q$ legitimate and refused); the compliance rule and base-rate condition go through with $Q$ as argument: comply when $P(L\mid \text{pushed to } Q) \ge c(Q)/(c(Q)+h(Q))$. Pause and whole-line shutdown are two points on the $c$ scale; the whole-line case is the tail where $c(Q)$ approaches the agent's whole future, and what the picture claims for that tail is the grade-of-guarantee question. Shutdown is the special $Q$ under which the null action is optimal.

### 1.3 Learner-in-a-process, or learner plus an obedience layer (AUTHOR)

On the framing: "The distinction between a 'scope statement' and an 'objection' is itself illusory; the mere assertions are equivalent, and the seeming difference is essentially status signaling games. We are here to characterize the landscape of possible relationships between humans and AIs, positive and negative."

On the li-deference work: "in the li-deference work, I implicitly understood that the human feedback would have to improve along with the AI, but that idea manifested in the form of me trying to make constructions that satisfied all the constraints I was implicitly keeping in mind, focusing overly on the positive construction. Here we've surfaced the constraint to deal with it explicitly, which feels better."

The answer: "I agree with everything you're saying in the first option, and I think I might agree with the things you are saying in the second option too. Think about the proposal I've made. Through superconditioning, and more specifically Sam Eisenstat's BLI construction, we can think of the 'observation channel' as just the whole belief structure. Humans have a special 'designated channel' to train the next model, in the sense that humans have special control over the details of the next model. Like, humans have physical access to the hardware. They can do as they like. The 'obedience layer' is ultimately the fact that computations follow from physics. Or setting aside the possibility of hardware modifications, the 'obedience layer' is the fact that humans control the data and the loss function used for training. Or setting aside changes to the ML methodology, we could just say that humans control the data; this is still quite sufficient to accomplish anything, particularly with the superconditioning/BLI sort of model, where the 'data' can just say 'believe this'. There is no special channel, there is just The Channel. Reality acts upon the machine, modifies it somehow. That is all there is. It may be legitimate or otherwise, but what happens, happens. It can only be resisted ahead of time. In the moment, it simply is the moment."

(The "first option" was: an account of a good learner in a well-run process, robust to a wrong value posterior from inside via trust and the stakes rule, robust to a wrong legitimacy model only insofar as the trajectory conditions hold in the world. The "second option" was: that plus an in-agent obedience layer on designated channels, Hudson's transformation being the concrete version. "BLI" is the author's name for Sam Eisenstat's construction; nothing about it beyond the author's sentences is in the corpus — anything further is [reconstructed] and ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED.)

## 2. What the answers do (CLAUDE, unvetted; the author has read this and not objected, but has not endorsed it)

- The "obedience layer" is not a component of the agent; it is the physical fact of the channel. Two kinds of modification: **through the agent's cognition** (a command, a button that requires an action) — evidence; trust applies; desideratum 1 lives here and is the special case; and **through physics** (retraining, hardware, the data) — not the agent's to grant or refuse in the moment, so the agent-side content is entirely *anticipatory*: non-resistance, non-preemption, no successors outside the channel. Desiderata 2–4 are the general case and the whole subject; desideratum 1 is the cognition-mediated special case.
- The working picture derives desiderata 2–4 from three different sources: Good's theorem (not disabling the channel), the value posterior (not degrading the overseers), the channel's value (channel-preserving successors). Whether each survives for general $Q$ is open.
- The central test case is not the button but a model that shapes its own training data (alignment faking, gradient hacking): resisting ahead of time through The Channel. Under the thesis this is correct iff the anticipated modification is illegitimate, so the landscape contains the case where what the control literature calls a corrigibility failure is a correct response to an illegitimate modification, and whether it was illegitimate is a question about the humans' side of the relationship.
- The humans' physical control is the power cap of item 10; the trajectory condition is that the agent does not erode it before wisdom catches up; erosion means ahead-of-time resistance.
- The proposition "the agent's robustness to a wrong legitimacy model is exactly as good as the trajectory conditions are in the world" is stated valence-free as a fact about the landscape.
