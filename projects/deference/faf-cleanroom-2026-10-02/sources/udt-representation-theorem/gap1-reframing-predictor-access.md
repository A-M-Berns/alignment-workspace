# Gap 1 Reframed: What Kind of Predictor Access Grounds Decision-Determination?

> **Status:** new research note, 2026-08-05, written by an AI session (Claude Fable) continuing the project after the cleanup pass ([cleanup-audit-2026-08-05](cleanup-audit-2026-08-05.md)). Unreviewed by the author. Trust level: careful notes. The claims marked "Observation" are argued here and I believe them checkable by inspection; the claims marked "Proposed lemma" are stated as targets, not results.

## 1. The gap, and why it resisted proof

Open Gap 1, as stated in [project-status](project-status.md): *if $\pi$ is a good condensation of behavior $B$, and $E$ is an optimal predictor, then $E$ factors through $\pi$* — which would give decision-determination (DD), and hence (by the verified chain) the UDT formula.

The cleanup audit left this with two named obstructions ([fair-environment-theorem](fair-environment-theorem.md), corrected version): the **minimality gap** (recoverability makes $\pi$ *a* sufficient statistic, not *the minimal* one) and the **off-support problem** (a predictor optimal for the realized observation distribution is unconstrained on unrealized observations, yet unrealized branches are where UDT's content lives).

This note's claim: the conjecture resisted proof because **"optimal predictor" conflates three different epistemic situations the environment can be in**, and the conjecture is trivial for one, ill-posed for another, and genuinely open (but now precisely posed) for the third. Sorting by predictor access reorganizes the gap.

## 2. Three kinds of predictor access

Let the agent be a mechanism $M$ implementing policy $\pi_M : O \to A$. Write the predictor's output as a prediction function: for mechanism $M$ and observation $o$, the environment's prediction is $p(M, o)$. The question behind DD is when $p(M, \cdot)$ depends on $M$ only through $\pi_M$.

**(i) Single-situation statistical access.** The predictor's only data is the realized behavior of *this* agent in *this* situation. Then the predictor cannot be predictive-in-advance at all: to correlate with an action using only data generated by that same action is circular. Newcomb's predictor cannot exist under access (i). This case is a red herring — no version of the conjecture lives here.

**(ii) Family statistical access.** The predictor has data on the agent's behavior across a *family* of situations — past choices, similar problems, other instances, or (equivalently for the math) samples from the prior over worlds. This is how real predictors work: they generalize. Here "the realized observation distribution" means the family's observation distribution $D$, and the support question becomes: which observations does the *family* exercise?

**(iii) Mechanism access, used extensionally.** The predictor can read or simulate $M$, querying its input-output behavior on arbitrary (including never-realized) observations, but responds only to the I/O function, not the implementation. This is the classic "Omega runs your code" idealization.

## 3. Two observations that dissolve part of the gap

**Observation 1 (extensional simulation factors through the policy by construction).** Under access (iii), $p(M, o)$ is computed by evaluating $M$'s I/O function; if the predictor is extensional — blind to implementation — then $\pi_{M_1} = \pi_{M_2}$ implies $p(M_1, \cdot) = p(M_2, \cdot)$ identically. Factoring holds with no information theory at all. Under access (iii), **"fairness" just *is* extensionality of the simulator**, and DD is immediate. The condensation framing contributes nothing here, and nothing is missing here: this case is done, and it is the case the Lean stipulation `isOptimalPredictor := factorsThroughPolicy` silently assumed.

**Observation 2 (perfect accuracy on full support is trivially factoring).** Under access (ii), suppose the predictor is *perfectly accurate on the family's full observation support*: for every $o$ with $D(o) > 0$, $p(M, o) = \pi_M(o)$. If $D$ has full support ($D(o) > 0$ for all $o \in O$), then $p(M, \cdot) = \pi_M(\cdot)$ identically, so factoring is immediate — again with no appeal to minimality, sufficiency, or condensation.

Together: **the exact/perfect version of Gap 1 is either trivial or already assumed, for every access type.** All the actual content of Gap 1 lives in the *approximate* and *partial-support* regimes of access (ii). This is, I think, why the corpus kept "proving" the gap by restatement — the exact version has nothing to prove, and the approximate version was never separated out.

## 4. Where the real problem now sits: support and error

Under access (ii) with partial support, the failure mode is real and unfixable from the inside: two mechanisms agreeing on-support but differing off-support generate identical data, so no amount of statistical optimality distinguishes them. The consequences split by what the environment *does* with the prediction:

**(a) If the environment's response uses only on-support predictions**, then utility depends on $M$ only through on-support behavior — a *coarsening* of $\pi_M$ — so DD still holds (factoring through a coarsening is factoring). But then modifying $\pi$ off-support changes nothing, and UDT's recommendations are pinned down only on-support: the theorem goes through and **the updateless content shrinks to the family's support**.

**(b) If the environment's response depends on off-support policy values** (transparent Newcomb's unrealized branch), access (ii) cannot ground the predictor's accuracy there, and "optimality" places no constraint. The conjecture is not false so much as *ungroundable*: there is no fact of the matter, learnable by the environment, for the prediction to be accurate *about*.

**The tremble connection.** The classical remedy for ungrounded counterfactual branches — ε-exploration, trembling hands — is exactly a device for converting case (b) into case (a): with tremble probability $\varepsilon > 0$, every branch has positive probability, the family's support is full, Observation 2 applies, and factoring holds. But the errors now matter quantitatively, and the interesting regime is $\varepsilon \to 0$: the branches UDT most cares about are the ones exercised most rarely, so predictor accuracy about them is the *last* thing the data pins down. The tension between "counterfactuals must be exercised to be learnable" and "UDT's value comes from branches you never take" is not a technical nuisance — it appears to be the actual mathematical content of Gap 1. (Note the resonance with two things already in the corpus: trembling-hand equilibrium is precisely the selection device in [superconditioning-mismatched-ontologies](superconditioning-mismatched-ontologies.md) §10–11, and exploration-grounded counterfactuals are a running theme of Diffractor's sequence.)

**Lemma (approximate factoring; proof included).** Under access (ii), suppose that for each mechanism $M$ the predictor satisfies the accuracy bound
$$\Pr_{o \sim D}\big(p(M, o) \neq \pi_M(o)\big) \leq \delta.$$
Then for any two mechanisms with $\pi_{M_1} = \pi_{M_2}$:
$$\Pr_{o \sim D}\big(p(M_1, o) \neq p(M_2, o)\big) \leq 2\delta,$$
and if additionally $D(o) \geq \gamma > 0$ for every $o \in O$, the predictions disagree on at most $2\delta/\gamma$ observations (counting measure).

*Proof.* Let $S = \{o : p(M_1,o) \neq p(M_2,o)\}$ and $S_i = \{o : p(M_i,o) \neq \pi(o)\}$ where $\pi$ is the shared policy. If $o \notin S_1 \cup S_2$, both predictions equal $\pi(o)$ and hence each other, so $S \subseteq S_1 \cup S_2$ and $\Pr_D(S) \leq \Pr_D(S_1) + \Pr_D(S_2) \leq 2\delta$. For the counting version: $\delta \geq \Pr_D(S_i) \geq \gamma\,|S_i|$ gives $|S_i| \leq \delta/\gamma$, so $|S| \leq 2\delta/\gamma$. $\square$

Two readings of the bound. The $D$-measure version says approximate factoring — and hence approximate DD, once the environment's response is suitably continuous in the predictions — costs only $2\delta$ *as measured by the family itself*. The counting version is what you need if the environment's response can hinge on any single observation's prediction, and there the tremble/exploration rate $\gamma$ of the *rarest* branch enters the denominator — making the $\varepsilon \to 0$ tension of the previous paragraph explicit in a formula: as trembles vanish, the guarantee on exactly the branches UDT cares about blows up as $\delta/\gamma$.

**Corollary (utility-level approximate DD).** Which reading you need is determined by whether the *observation distribution itself* is downstream of the predictions — and this is exactly what separates garden-variety prediction problems from transparent-Newcomb-style ones.

*Fixed-observation regime.* If utility takes the local form $U(M) = \sum_o D(o)\, u\big(o,\, p(M,o),\, \pi_M(o)\big)$ with $D$ fixed and $u$ ranging over an interval of width $R$, then for $\pi_{M_1} = \pi_{M_2}$ the terms differ only where the predictions do, so
$$|U(M_1) - U(M_2)| \;\leq\; R \cdot \Pr_{o \sim D}\big(p(M_1,o) \neq p(M_2,o)\big) \;\leq\; 2\delta R.$$
Approximate DD at cost $2\delta R$, no $\gamma$ anywhere.

*Prediction-dependent-observation regime.* If the observation distribution depends on the prediction profile — transparent Newcomb: whether you *see* a full box is itself set by the predictor's verdict — then $U(M) = \sum_o D_{p(M,\cdot)}(o)\, u(\cdot)$, and the bound needs the total-variation distance between $D_{p(M_1,\cdot)}$ and $D_{p(M_2,\cdot)}$, which is controlled only through the *counting* number of disagreeing predictions: if changing the prediction at one observation moves $D$ by at most $L$ in total variation, the bound becomes $|U(M_1) - U(M_2)| \leq R\,(2\delta + L \cdot 2\delta/\gamma)$. **The $\gamma$-blowup is thus not an artifact of the proof: problems where observations are downstream of predictions structurally require the counting bound, which is why transparent-Newcomb-style cases are exactly the fragile ones.** This lemma is small, but it is the honest replacement for the Lean stipulation `isOptimalPredictor := factorsThroughPolicy`: accuracy-on-a-family is a real premise, and factoring becomes its (approximate) consequence, with the exact chain as the $\delta = 0$ corollary. What remains genuinely open is not the factoring step but the *upstream* premise (why is the predictor's internal statistic $\pi$ rather than a near-equivalent — §5) and the *downstream* continuity step (how prediction disagreements translate to utility differences).

## 5. What condensation can and cannot contribute (honest version)

The audit found five incompatible versions of the condensation bridge ([cleanup-audit-2026-08-05](cleanup-audit-2026-08-05.md) §Condensation). The access-type analysis says where a condensation-flavored premise genuinely enters — and it is narrower than any of the five:

- Not in access (iii): extensionality does everything.
- Not in the exact full-support case of (ii): perfect accuracy does everything.
- **In the approximate case of (ii)**, there is real work for a minimality/stability premise: with noise, many statistics of the data predict behavior nearly as well, and the claim "the predictor's internal state is (approximately) $\pi$, rather than some gerrymandered near-equivalent" needs a reason. The available reason is a redundancy/stability property: $\pi$ is the statistic that remains predictive *across* the family's situations — the invariant summary, where gerrymandered alternatives are context-bound. That is much closer to Wentworth-style natural latents (redundant information across contexts) than to Eisenstat's score-based condensation, which matches the audit's finding that the corpus's "condensation conditions" never actually matched Eisenstat's definition. The honest statement of the research program: **the bridge premise is "the policy is the natural latent of behavior across the deployment family," and the right formal home for that premise is redundancy/minimal-sufficiency across a family, with Eisenstat's condensation one candidate formalization among several.**

## 6. A punchline worth keeping: the scope of updatelessness

Case (a) of §4 gives a quantitative answer-shape to a question the author has posed repeatedly (how updateless should an agent be?):

**An agent should be updateless over exactly the family of situations its predictors generalize across.** The environment's response can only depend on policy structure the predictor can ground; the predictor can only ground structure exercised (at some rate $\gamma$) by the family; so cross-situation dependence — the thing that gives updatelessness its content — extends precisely as far as the family's support, with strength degrading as $\delta/\gamma$. Commitments beyond the family's support are unpriced by the environment: nothing responds to them, so updatelessness there is free but worthless — and updatelessness *within* the family is exactly as valuable as the predictor is accurate.

This also gives the representation-theorem project a cleaner scope statement than "fair problems": the theorem's domain is not a binary fairness condition but a *graded* one, parameterized by the family and the accuracy, recovering binary DD in the $\delta \to 0$, full-support limit. And it connects Part I to Part II more organically than either the prediction or endorsement route alone: heterogeneous decision-points ([superconditioning-mismatched-ontologies](superconditioning-mismatched-ontologies.md) §9) whose situations fall in *different* predictor-families are exactly the ones that cannot rely on shared DD and must fall back to bargaining.

## 8. Worked example: transparent Newcomb with predictor error $\varepsilon$

Setup. Observations $O = \{o_{\text{full}}, o_{\text{empty}}\}$ (you see the big box's contents before choosing); actions $\{\text{1box}, \text{2box}\}$; small box always contains \$1K, big box contains \$1M iff the predictor predicts you would 1box upon seeing it full. The predictor errs with probability $\varepsilon$.

**Policy values.** The four deterministic policies, with the UDT-recommended $\pi^* = (o_{\text{full}} \mapsto \text{1box},\; o_{\text{empty}} \mapsto \text{2box})$:

| Policy (full, empty) | Expected value |
|---|---|
| $\pi^*$ = (1box, 2box) | $(1-\varepsilon)\cdot 1\text{M} + \varepsilon \cdot 1\text{K}$ |
| (2box, 2box) | $(1-\varepsilon)\cdot 1\text{K} + \varepsilon \cdot 1.001\text{M}$ |
| (1box, 1box) | $(1-\varepsilon)\cdot 1\text{M}$ |
| (2box, 1box) | $\varepsilon \cdot 1.001\text{M}$ |

$\pi^*$ dominates: it beats (2box, 2box) iff $999(1-\varepsilon) > 1000\varepsilon$, i.e. for all $\varepsilon < 999/1999 \approx 0.4997$. So far this is the standard analysis, just tremble-explicit.

**Grounding rates — the point of the example.** The payoff-relevant prediction is behavior-upon-$o_{\text{full}}$. Under access (ii), the predictor's evidence about an agent's full-box behavior accrues only on episodes where that agent actually faces a full box:

- An agent *verdicted a one-boxer* faces $o_{\text{full}}$ at rate $1-\varepsilon$ → its payoff-relevant behavior is richly exercised; the verdict is tested nearly every episode.
- An agent *verdicted a two-boxer* faces $o_{\text{full}}$ at rate $\varepsilon$ → its payoff-relevant behavior is exercised only when the predictor errs. **In the lemma's terms, $\gamma = \varepsilon$ for exactly the (mechanism, observation) pairs that decide the problem.**

**The self-sealing asymmetry.** Follow the two error types:

- *True two-boxer verdicted "one-boxer":* box gets filled, agent sees full, two-boxes — disconfirming evidence arrives at rate $\approx 1$. The error is **self-correcting**.
- *True one-boxer verdicted "two-boxer":* box left empty, agent sees empty and acts on the empty branch; its full-box disposition is never exercised. Disconfirming evidence arrives at rate $0$ (absent trembles). The error is **self-sealing**: a learned predictor can permanently misclassify a genuine one-boxer, and no behavior of the agent can ever demonstrate the misclassification.

So in the $\varepsilon \to 0$ limit — the limit in which the problem is usually posed — the learned predictor's verdict "two-boxer" becomes unfalsifiable, and the problem's crispness is purchased by silently switching to access (iii): the idealized Omega who *simulates* rather than learns. **Transparent Newcomb intuitions split along exactly this line: readers imagining a learned predictor and readers imagining an extensional simulator are imagining different problems, and only the simulator version supports full-strength updatelessness.** For the learned version, the honest statement is: updateless one-boxing on the full-box branch is worth exactly what the predictor's $\varepsilon$-grounded accuracy about that branch is worth, per the §4 corollary's $\delta/\gamma$ bound with $\gamma = \varepsilon$.

**The example spectrum (scope check, §7 item 5).** The corpus's motivating examples now line up on a groundedness gradient rather than a binary fair/unfair split:

| Example | Payoff-relevant branch exercised at rate | Status under access (ii) |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinated Buttons | $1/2$ each room (both realized) | fully family-grounded; DD solid |
| Standard (opaque) Newcomb | $\approx 1$ (predicted action is the realized one) | family-grounded via past behavior |
| Transparent Newcomb | $\gamma = \varepsilon$ (only via predictor error) | grounded only by trembles; self-sealing verdicts at $\varepsilon = 0$ |
| Memory Problem | — | outside on independent grounds (channel asymmetry, [examples-revisited](examples-revisited.md)) |
