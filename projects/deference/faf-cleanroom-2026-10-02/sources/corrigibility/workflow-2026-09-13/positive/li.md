## Setting

The formalism, exactly, as the wiki defines it (`setting-and-notation`, `deference-notions`, `expert-conditions`, `conventions-and-status-labels`); nothing here is new.

**The world.** A language $\mathcal L$ with sentence set $\mathcal S$, a consistent theory $\Gamma$ representing computable functions (canonically PA), and a $\Gamma$-complete computable deductive process $D = (D^1 \subseteq D^2 \subseteq \cdots)$. A sentence is *decidable* if $D$ eventually decides it; its decided value is the only notion of truth in play. Within the decidable sentences, those decided by the lookahead $F(n)$ (canonically $2^n$) form the *timely fragment* $G$.

**Belief states and the criterion.** A belief state is $\mathbb P_n : \mathcal S \to \mathbb Q \cap [0,1]$ (a market's day-$n$ prices); an epistemic process is a sequence $\overline{\mathbb P} = (\mathbb P_n)$. A process is a *logical inductor relative to trader class $\mathcal C$* if no $\mathcal C$-computable trader exploits it (unbounded plausible gain on bounded plausible loss) — the logical induction criterion (main.tex:657). Two processes: the human/novice $H \dashv \mathcal C_H$ and the AI/expert $A \dashv \mathcal C_A$, with $\mathcal C_H \subsetneq \mathcal C_A$ (canonically P $\subsetneq$ EXP); the gap is load-bearing: $A$ can simulate $H$'s deliberation to the lookahead, not conversely (`setting-and-notation` §Trader classes; deference-v6 §3.3). Existence of a computable inductor over any such $\Gamma$: main.tex:926.

**LUVs, expectations, quotes.** A logically uncertain variable (LUV) is a formula $X$ that $\Gamma$ proves names a unique real in a rational interval $[a,b]$ (the wiki keeps general $[a,b]$; the paper's $[0,1]$ statements lift affinely). $E^{\mathbb P}_n(X)$ is the day-$n$ price-integral expectation (main.tex:1670). $\ulcorner e \urcorner$ names a value as a LUV, so $E^H_n(\ulcorner E^A_n(X)\urcorner)$ — the human's estimate of the AI's estimate — is type-correct. Asymptotics: $x_n \approx_n y_n$ iff $x_n - y_n \to 0$; $x_n \gtrsim_n y_n$ iff $\liminf (x_n - y_n) \ge 0$. $\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(x > y)$ is the continuous ramp of width $\delta$ (main.tex:1174); a hard indicator is discontinuous and therefore not a legal trade weight. A real sequence is *$\overline{\mathbb P}$-generable* if computed by an e.c. progression of expressible features of the market's own prices (main.tex:1218) — this is the legal class of weightings; a *cross-market* quote is not an expressible feature of one's own market and is made usable by the *ledger* device: the quote is published into the other process's deductive process as decided atoms, and one weights on one's own price of the sentence about the quote (`unbiasedness-theorem-families` §6).

**Free theorems used (by name; line numbers in `main.tex`).** Provability Induction (1052); Recurring Calibration (1193); Recurring Unbiasedness (1225) and Unbiasedness From Feedback (1249), with LUV forms Expectation Recurring Unbiasedness (1812; in the corrected clause-free form, `li-paper-erratum` §1, reported) and Expectation Unbiasedness From Feedback (1822); Closure under Finite Perturbations (1521); Non-Dogmatism (1528); Uniform Non-Dogmatism (1540); Occam Bounds (1552); Closure Under Conditioning (1613); Expectations Converge (1688); Linearity of Expectation `loe` (1700); Expectations of Indicators (1719); Expectation Provability Induction `expprovind` (1753); Introspection (1969); Paradox Resistance (1992); Expectations of Probabilities `epr` (2014); Iterated Expectations `er` (2022); Expected Future Expectations `cee` (2045); No Expected Net Update `ceu` (2056); No Expected Net Update under Conditionals `ccee` (2068); Self-Trust `st` (2092), with the paper's discrete-conjunction passage (2116–2126) refuting the exact-conditioned form.

**The two settings.** *Setting 1 (abstract single-expert):* $H$ plus any *observable, coherent* (optionally *introspective*) expert $E^\ast$; canonical instances the future self $E^\ast(X) = E^H_{f(n)}(X)$ (every hypothesis an LI theorem) and a distinct inductor $A$ read through the ledger. *Setting 2 (coupled cross-process):* two mutually observable inductors with the schedules $n \le e(n) < F(n) < \sigma(n)$ (publication, lookahead, payout); $A$'s quote on the day-$n$ question is $a_n := E^A_n(\ulcorner Y_n\urcorner)$ with target $Y_n$ either the *sealed sibling* $H^{[n]}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)})$ (the human's own deliberation with quotes of index $\ge n$ held out — the frozen construction) or the *coupled* human's future credence $E^{H^+}_{f(n)}(X_n)$ (faithful acceleration). The author's *lookahead expectation* is $\mathbb E^\ast_n(X) := E^A_n\big(\ulcorner E^H_{f(n)}(X)\urcorner\big)$ (`li-deference.md` §Formalism; notation per `conventions-and-status-labels`).

**The deference notions** (`deference-notions`), each written with nested expectations spelled out:

- **Tower (Mart)**: for every e.d. $(X_n)$, $\;E^H_n(X_n) \approx_n E^H_n\big(\ulcorner E^\ast(X_n)\urcorner\big)$. Self-instance: `cee`, $E_n(X_n) \approx_n E_n\big(\ulcorner E_{f(n)}(X_n)\urcorner\big)$ — a theorem. Cross-process with the lookahead expert: $E^H_n(X_n) \approx_n E^H_n\big(\ulcorner E^A_n(\ulcorner E^H_{f(n)}(X_n)\urcorner)\urcorner\big)$ — a *hypothesis*, not a theorem.
- **Conditional tower (the fold, `ccee`-shaped)**: for every generable $w_n \in [0,1]$, $\;E^H_n(X_n \cdot w_n) \approx_n E^H_n\big(\ulcorner E^\ast(X_n)\cdot w_n\urcorner\big)$. Self-instance `ccee`: $E_n\big(\ulcorner X_n \cdot w_{f(n)}\urcorner\big) \approx_n E_n\big(\ulcorner E_{f(n)}(X_n)\cdot w_{f(n)}\urcorner\big)$ for $w$ generable in $[0,1]$ — a theorem, and the weight may be any generable function of the *future* prices.
- **Total Trust (soft, unnormalized)** — the above-threshold inequality $E^H_n\big(X_n \cdot \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^\ast(X_n) > t)\big) \gtrsim_n t \cdot E^H_n\big(\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^\ast(X_n) > t)\big)$ and the below-threshold inequality $E^H_n\big(X_n \cdot \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^\ast(X_n) < t)\big) \lesssim_n t \cdot E^H_n\big(\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^\ast(X_n) < t)\big)$, for every $t$ and $\delta$. Self-instance: `st` (propositional case). The normalized "$E^H_n(X_n \mid E^\ast(X_n) > t) \gtrsim_n t$" is recovered by dividing by the conditioning mass where it is bounded away from $0$.
- **Value**: for every e.d. menu sequence $\mathcal O_n = \{O^1_n,\dots,O^k_n\}$ of bounded LUVs and fixed $i$, $\;E^H_n(\widehat S_n) \gtrsim_n E^H_n(O^i_n)$, where $\widehat S_n := O^{j^\ast(n)}_n$ is the option the expert's quotes rate highest. Unconditional argmax Value is **REFUTED** for inductor-experts by selection-punishing menus and holds under the *conditional-stability* scope condition (H3) (`total-trust-implies-value` §Necessity, unvetted).
- **Reflection** bifurcates in LI (`reflection-in-li`, unvetted 2026-07-28): the *value form* (condition on the expert's estimate of this bet, adopt it) softens to the band limit-equality and is a theorem given Tower; the *function form* (condition on the expert's whole probability function) is idle where the function is decided and inexpressible where not; the *exact* grade is false at the paradoxical family (main.tex:2116–2126).

**Status of the equivalences (as the wiki records them, unvetted by the author unless stated).** Two-option identity Value $\Leftrightarrow$ Total Trust at $(X,s)$: KERNEL-CHECKED, exact, per $(X,s)$ (`two-option-value-iff-total-trust`). Total Trust $\Rightarrow$ argmax Value under (H1)–(H3): PROVED modulo the introspective-concentration lemma. Value $\Rightarrow$ Tower by gap-bet probe menus: PROVED (prose). Tower $\Rightarrow$ Total Trust by the fold at the ramp: PROVED (prose). Band form $\Rightarrow$ Total Trust: PROVED (prose). So on conditional-stable menus, at asymptotic grade: soft value-Reflection $\iff$ Total Trust $\iff$ Value $\iff$ Tower, with the decision-theoretic scope condition charged once (on Total Trust $\Rightarrow$ Value).

**Cross-process facts (Setting 2).** *Negative:* No-Forced-Trust (deference-v6 §4.1, INTERPRETATION — no efficiently checkable relation between distinct inductors Dutch-book-forces agreement on undecidables); the timely pointwise tower is dead — REFUTED on quote-referencing families by the diagonal $g_n \leftrightarrow (a_n \le \tfrac12)$ with defect $\ge \tfrac12$ (2a; arithmetic core KERNEL-CHECKED), underivable on quote-free families (2b, cost-circularity, soft joint ~0.75–0.8) (deference-v6 §4.3, §4.8); the dichotomy *predictable iff uninfluenced* — a settlement one can predict is one the predictor cannot influence (deference-v6 §4.4). *Positive:* the frozen construction forces faithfulness T1 everywhere and the per-day tower/Value on $G$ with a sealed target (deference-v6 §5.1–5.8; Lean checks the squeezes with the forcing named — the honesty caveat of `conventions-and-status-labels`); faithful acceleration forces *averaged* Total Trust $\Rightarrow$ Value on the admissible domain with the coupled human (Half 1 PROVED, engine KERNEL-CHECKED; Half 2 ~0.90 fixed question, ~0.6–0.85 varying; `faithful-acceleration-result`). Off $G$: T7 underdetermination — the human's limit on never-settling questions is free, and the record cannot distinguish faithful prediction from steering (deference-v6 §5.6, §6.3). Averaged guarantees are policy-over-a-class, never this-decision, and vacuous off the good-feedback fragment (deference-v6 §6.7).

**Legitimacy sketch (UNVETTED, Claude's).** `legitimacy-theory-v1` proposes the influence defect $d_n := \big|E^{H^+}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)}) - E^{H^{[n]}}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)})\big|$ (advised minus sealed verdict), the acceleration-only thesis, the hierarchy blindness $\subset$ endpoint-preservation $\subset$ (impossible) trace conditions, a certificate construction C1–C4, and a "corrigibility (partial)" row audited in I17 below. **What the formalism has no native notion of.** Actions and incentives — a market is not an agent; the only instrumental notion is one-shot argmax Value over a menu, and sequential choice of one's own information structure is not in the corpus. Utilities — LUVs play that role. Observations — the deductive process and ledger atoms play that role. ### I1 — the value-change ⇄ belief-change transform

**Verdict: formalized. Headline [derived]: in LI the transform is the identity — utilities are LUVs, LUVs are priced by beliefs, so a "value change" is a price change by construction; the only theorem content is linearity of expectation and non-dogmatism.**

1. [derived] Let $(\theta_k)$ be an e.c. family of value-hypothesis sentences and $U_k(a)$ bounded LUVs. $V(a) := \sum_k \mathbb 1(\theta_k)\cdot U_k(a)$ (finitely many $k$ per day, or a bounded LUV-combination sequence) is an e.d. LUV, and $E_n(V(a))$ is a number fixed by $\mathbb P_n$'s prices on $V(a)$'s threshold sentences (main.tex:1670). Decision by expectation over value uncertainty is $\arg\max_a E_n(V(a))$. Nothing has to be transformed: the "expanded sigma-algebra" is the language.
2. [derived] Where $\Gamma$ decides $\theta_k$, `loe` (main.tex:1700) with the decided coefficient gives $E_n(V(a)) \approx_n E_n(U_k(a))$ — the fixed-utility agent recovered as the special case of decided values. Where $\Gamma$ decides no $\theta_k$, Non-Dogmatism (main.tex:1528) gives $\mathbb P_\infty(\theta_k) \in (0,1)$: the agent never becomes certain of its values. The thesis sentence "corrigibility is just the desire to learn" (AUTHOR) then has a standing precondition in LI — value uncertainty is permanent unless values are logically pinned.
3. [derived] On "linear expectation over this uncertainty, or perhaps some other rule" (AUTHOR): $E_n(\mathbb 1(\theta_k)\cdot U_k)$ does **not** factor as $\mathbb P_n(\theta_k)\cdot E_n(U_k)$ unless $\theta_k$ is decided (an LI expectation of a product is not a product of expectations; `loe` only handles generable *coefficients*). So the LI representation is strictly more general than "probability of hypothesis × utility under hypothesis": correlations between which values are true and what the actions are worth are priced jointly. This is a place where the position statement's toy dictionary (2.8: $\mathbb E_P[V\mid a_1]$ with $P$ over $(\omega,o)$) is an idealization LI does not need.
4. [derived] the author's non-realism (2.2, "it *is just* a different representation of the same agent") is literally true here: there is no separate value object; the same price movement is "belief change" under one description and "value change" under another, with no formal marker distinguishing them.

### I2 — radical-probabilist dynamics; non-dogmatism

**Verdict: formalized, with one divergence in LI's favour and one gap. Headline [reported]: LI is natively radical-probabilist — there is no update rule, only the criterion — and its only invariant is asymptotic non-dogmatism (main.tex:1528), which matches the author's "only the logical impossibilities are zero" as a limit statement; sigma-algebra revision is not covered.**

1. [reported] The paper frames self-trust as the analogue of Bayesian coherence for a reasoner that "updates its belief by gaining more knowledge about *logical* facts, both by observing an ongoing process of deduction and by thinking for longer periods of time" (main.tex:2039–2041). No conditioning step appears anywhere in the definition of a logical inductor.
2. [reported] Closure under Finite Perturbations (main.tex:1521): a process agreeing with an inductor on all but finitely many days is an inductor. So LI is *more* liberal than the author's rule "everything is allowed to change, with the sole exception of stuff that's set to probability zero becoming positive" (AUTHOR, 2.2): at finite days zero may become positive. The invariant sits in the limit — Non-Dogmatism: $\mathbb P_\infty(\varphi) < 1$ if $\Gamma \nvdash \varphi$ and $> 0$ if $\Gamma \nvdash \neg\varphi$ — which is exactly the author's parenthetical "(only the logical impossibilities ... are zero)". Uniform Non-Dogmatism (main.tex:1540) and Occam Bounds (main.tex:1552) give the uniform and quantitative versions.
3. [derived] The LI counterpart of "zero stays zero" is "decided stays decided": once $D$ decides $\neg\varphi$, Provability Induction (main.tex:1052) pins the price at $0$ asymptotically. So the one irreversibility in LI is logical, as the author wants.
4. [reported] "Corrigible to edits of the sigma algebra itself" (AUTHOR, 2.2): the language is fixed in LI. Closure Under Conditioning (main.tex:1613) covers revising the *theory* ($\Gamma \to \Gamma \cup \{\psi\}$); extending the *language* (the corpus adds fresh atoms $g_n$ in deference-v6 §4.3, but as a construction, not a closure theorem) has no theorem. Gap, not inexpressibility.

### I3 — superconditioning; the meta-belief observation event

**Verdict: partial. Headline [derived]: the meta-belief algebra is native (price sentences are sentences), so the "observation event 'you believe this now'" needs no enlargement — and its coherence content is a theorem (`cee`/`ccee`) rather than a re-representation; the Diaconis–Zabell condition has no LI analogue and is not needed. Bridge claims about what Eisenstat meant are ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED — only the author's account is used.**

1. [reported] Introspection (main.tex:1969): for any e.c. band pattern $\ulcorner a_n < \mathbb P_n(\varphi_n) < b_n\urcorner$, the inductor learns to believe it when true and disbelieve it when false, with vanishing error. `epr` (main.tex:2014): $\mathbb P_n(\varphi_n) \approx_n E_n(\ulcorner \mathbb P_n(\varphi_n)\urcorner)$; `er` (main.tex:2022): $E_n(X_n) \approx_n E_n(\ulcorner E_n(X_n)\urcorner)$. The inductor observes its own current state, in band form, to vanishing error.
2. [derived] the author's rendering of the observation — "the observation event is always 'you believe this now'" (AUTHOR, §1) — becomes, for the future belief, the weight $w_{f(n)}$ in `ccee` (main.tex:2068): $E_n(\ulcorner X_n \cdot w_{f(n)}\urcorner) \approx_n E_n(\ulcorner E_{f(n)}(X_n)\cdot w_{f(n)}\urcorner)$ for any $w$ generable from the day-$f(n)$ prices. Read with the paper's own gloss (main.tex:2080–2090): "$\mathbb P$'s expected value of the bundle now, given that it's going to think the bundle has a value of at least 70¢ later, is equal to whatever it expects to think later, conditioned on thinking later that the bundle is worth at least 70¢." That *is* the superconditioning coherence — conditioning on a function of the future belief state — obtained as a theorem, at asymptotic grade, with no enlarged space.
3. [reconstructed] Diaconis–Zabell superconditioning, as the critique recalled it in the opening exchange ($Q$ is obtainable from $P$ by conditioning in an enlarged space iff $Q \ll P$): no LI analogue exists, because day-to-day price changes are unconstrained (claim I2.2) and the only coherence is asymptotic (`cee`). The critique's "opiates/stimulants" gap ("a correction to a hypothesis with prior zero") is closed differently — by Non-Dogmatism, see I8 — not by superconditioning.
4. [reported] The observation event cannot be the *exact* future belief: exact self-reflection is unsatisfiable (Christiano et al. 2013, as cited in `reflection-in-li`) and exact conditioning is false at the paradoxical family (main.tex:2116–2126). Eisenstat's observation event must be read as a band event, which is what Introspection delivers.

"Increase in expected accuracy" has no LI entry; its nearest object is unbiasedness against realized value.**

### I5 — legitimacy as an event; minimal viability; the indexed $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$

**Verdict: formalized (the family and the conditional statement), with a graded result. Headline [derived]: "reflection conditional on $L$" is an LI theorem (`ccee`) at pointwise grade for the self whenever $L$ is decided by the judged day $f(n)$ and the weight is generable — and it holds for *every* such $L$, so for a single logical inductor the correction function is the identity and legitimacy has no content; a present-decided $L$ (the judging index $t_1$) is idle; a never-decided $L$ carries no forced content. Legitimacy acquires content only for a pushed (non-inductor) process, a distinct process, or a non-generable label.**

**The family.** Take an e.c. family of sentences $L_{n, f(n), X}$ — "the day-$f(n)$ estimate of $X$ is legitimate, as judged from day $n$" — indexed exactly as the author proposed (AUTHOR, 2.10: "proposition, time, time, indexing legitimacy by the moment judging legitimacy, the moment being judged for legitimacy, and the belief being vetted for legitimacy. That's L_t1_t2_phi(omega); still predicated on worlds"). Predication on worlds is automatic: a sentence is a predicate on consistent completions. Three readings by *when $D$ decides $L$*: (a) by day $n$; (b) by day $f(n)$ (or by the payout $\sigma(n)$); (c) never.

**The statement.** With the legitimacy weight $w^L_m := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P_m(L) > 1 - \varepsilon)$ (generable from day-$m$ prices) and the threshold weight $b_m := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E_m(X) > t)$:

$$\text{(tower form)}\qquad E_n\big(\ulcorner X \cdot w^L_{f(n)}\urcorner\big) \;\approx_n\; E_n\big(\ulcorner E_{f(n)}(X)\cdot w^L_{f(n)}\urcorner\big),$$
$$\text{(Total-Trust form)}\qquad E_n\big(\ulcorner X \cdot w^L_{f(n)}\cdot b_{f(n)}\urcorner\big) \;\gtrsim_n\; t\cdot E_n\big(\ulcorner w^L_{f(n)}\cdot b_{f(n)}\urcorner\big),$$

which, dividing by the conditioning mass where it is bounded away from $0$, read "conditional on legitimacy and on my future self rating $X$ above $t$, I rate $X$ above $t$" — the author's minimal viability, position statement 2.11's $P_{t_1}(\varphi \mid P_{t_2}(\varphi) = c,\ L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}) = c$ in band form.

1. [derived] **Reading (b), self-instance: both statements are theorems at pointwise (full-limit) grade.** The product $w^L_{f(n)}\cdot b_{f(n)}$ is a generable $[0,1]$-sequence (a product of ramps of day-$f(n)$ prices), so `ccee` (main.tex:2068) gives the tower form verbatim, and the Total-Trust form follows by the fold-plus-threshold argument of `tower-implies-total-trust` (the ramp is positive only where the future estimate exceeds $t$; `expprovind` carries the provable bound). By Provability Induction (main.tex:1052) on the decided $L$, $\mathbb P_{f(n)}(L) \to \mathbb 1(L)$, so $w^L_{f(n)} \to \mathbb 1(L)$: asymptotically the weight *is* the truth of legitimacy as $D$ decides it by the judged day.
2. [derived] **The surprise: the correction function is the identity for the self.** `ccee` holds for *every* generable weight, in particular for $1 - w^L_{f(n)}$ ("conditional on illegitimacy"). So $E_n(X\cdot(1-w^L)) \approx_n E_n(E_{f(n)}(X)\cdot(1-w^L))$ as well: the future self's illegitimate-branch estimates are also expected to be right, and the author's modified Tower (quoted in I4.5) holds with the "average corrected future expectations" equal to the uncorrected ones. For a single logical inductor, every generable conditioning is legitimate. Legitimacy is therefore **vacuous in the pure self-trust LI setting**, and gets content only when: (i) the belief sequence is *not* a single inductor — "the environment pushes the mental state around however it does" (AUTHOR, §1) and the pushed process fails the criterion (claim 7 below); (ii) the target is a distinct process (cross-process, claim 5); or (iii) the conditioning is *not generable* — an external label, which is the situation `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.3 calls trace-nonrecoverability (reported). This sharpens `li-deference.md` §0.3's remark that LI "learns over time that its feedback is always legitimate": for the self it is not learned, it is a theorem for every generable notion of legitimacy at once.
3. [derived] **Reading (a): the judging index $t_1$ is idle.** If $L$ is decided by day $n$, $w^L_n$ is a generable feature of the day-$n$ prices, hence a legal `loe` coefficient (main.tex:1700; the device of `tower-implies-total-trust` fact (b) — $\Gamma$ decides the day-$n$ price, so $\Gamma \vdash X\cdot w^L_n = (\text{its rational value})\cdot X$; the wiki's residual caution about this step is `open-problems` item 4), and it factors out of both sides: the conditional statement reduces to the unconditional one. Conditioning on a present-decided legitimacy verdict is conditioning on a known fact — the same idleness `reflection-in-li` finds for function-form Reflection on a decided function. Hence, of the author's three indices, $t_1$ carries no content in LI when $L$ is decided at $t_1$; the content sits at $t_2$, the moment at which $D$ decides $L$.
4. [derived] **Reading (c): never-decided $L$.** The tower form still holds (`ccee`), but $w^L_{f(n)}$ never converges to an indicator — Non-Dogmatism keeps $\mathbb P_\infty(L) \in (0,1)$ — so it is "reflection conditional on my future *opinion* of legitimacy", not conditional on legitimacy. The product-LUV statement $E_n(X\cdot\mathbb 1(L)) \approx_n E_n(\ulcorner E_{f(n)}(X\cdot\mathbb 1(L))\urcorner)$ is also a theorem (`cee` on the product LUV), but the future self cannot factor an undecided $\mathbb 1(L)$ out, so this is not conditional reflection either. No forced content — the LI form of the good-feedback limitation (deference-v6 §6.7).
5. [derived; the extension to Half 2 conjectured ~0.8] **Cross-process, legitimacy-weighted Total Trust.** $E^H_n\big(X_n\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(a_n > t)\cdot w^L\big) \gtrsim_n t\cdot E^H_n\big(\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(a_n > t)\cdot w^L\big)$. Faithful acceleration's Half 1 (Recurring Unbiasedness on *any* generable divergent weighting, `faithful-acceleration-result` §3) covers the product weighting verbatim when $w^L$ is generable from the relevant market and the product is divergent; Half 2's routes need re-checking with the extra factor (conjectured). If $w^L$ is **not** generable — a label attached from outside with knowledge of the errors — no unbiasedness theorem applies; this is the phenomenon `legitimacy-theory-v1` §4 C4 reports from the run-3 artifact ("an error-correlated gate destroys calibration", reported). So LI *requires* the author's 2.5 principle "the legitimacy-judging layer is just the same probability distribution": the weighting must be priced by the same market for any theorem to reach it (I7.6).
6. [derived] **Comparison with the sealed-sibling influence defect** (`legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.1, UNVETTED): $d_n = \big|E^{H^+}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)}) - E^{H^{[n]}}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)})\big|$ is a $\Gamma_A$-determined LUV, so $L^d_n := \ulcorner d_n \le \varepsilon\urcorner$ is a member of the family with $t_1 = n$, $t_2 = F(n)$, $\varphi = P^{(n)}$, decided by $\sigma(n)$ — reading (b) at the payout schedule. It is computable by $A$ (C1 skeleton: simulating the sealed sibling is $\mathcal C_A$-affordable) and **not** by $H$ (complexity gap), so $d_n$-legitimacy is a learnable decidable family for $A$ and an external label for $H$. This is where the author's "legitimate *for*" distinction (AUTHOR, 2.10: "a locus of belief-changing activity, and then there's a locus of belief") lands: $L^d$ is predicated on the *pair* (the advisor's realized output, the human's verdict), and it is $A$'s, not $H$'s, to price.
7. [conjectured, as a definition; theorem content derived] **A second LI-native candidate — criterion-preservation.** Call a modification policy of the belief process (the environment's pushing) *legitimate* iff the pushed sequence still satisfies the logical induction criterion relative to $\mathcal C$. Then `cee`/`ccee` hold for the pushed process, so reflection-conditional-on-legitimacy holds automatically at pointwise grade [derived from claim 1]. Closure under Finite Perturbations makes any finite pushing legitimate in this sense, so legitimacy is a property of the *whole* policy, matching the author's "an entire state of affairs is legitimate or not" (AUTHOR, 2.10). Its weakness is T7: steering the endpoint on never-settling questions passes every internal test (deference-v6 §5.6, §6.3), so criterion-preservation detects the *report* layer (no predictable bias) and misses the *opinion* layer (endpoint motion) — `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.4's two layers (reported). The two candidates split along the author's legitimate/legitimizing line: criterion-preservation is a property of the belief process (*legitimate*), $d_n$ a property of third-person influence (*legitimizing*).

### I6 — legitimizing / delegitimizing; "supports"

**Verdict: formalized as a dichotomy; partial for the probability-raising reading. Headline [reported + derived]: a third-person event is delegitimizing for $(t_2,\varphi)$ exactly when it makes the settlement of the judged belief depend on the agent's current output — the "predictable iff uninfluenced" dichotomy (deference-v6 §4.4) — and deception in 2.9 is the case in point; the causal reading of "supports" is inexpressible, LI's substitute being a comparison between processes.**

1. [reported] Dichotomy (deference-v6 §4.4): if the contract family is effective and universal pointwise timely tracking is provable from a satisfiable power assumption, the settlement map is reflectively blind ($\partial D_A/\partial A = 0$). Contrapositive of 2a/2b: quote-dependence of the verdict makes tracking false (quote-referencing families) or underivable (quote-free). Read through 2.11: a third-person event $E$ is *legitimizing* for the transition $n \to f(n)$ on $\varphi$ when it keeps the verdict blind to $a_n$ (sealing; publication after the lookahead, $e(n) > F(n)$); *delegitimizing* when it injects $a_n$ into the deliberation before the verdict. Claude's 2.11 relation "supports" is thus theorem-shaped, and it never predicates legitimacy on actions — it predicates it on the *dependence structure* between the act and the judged belief, as the author wanted ("I'm not sure it should be predicated on actions in particular", AUTHOR 2.10).
2. [derived] Deception (2.9, the manipulative $a_1^-$ that "lowers the probability of Pr, e.g. by deceiving the programmers", Soares 2015 §2 line 135): in LI, $A$'s output enters $H$'s deliberation before $H$'s press, making $\pi_n$ a function of $a_n$; the press ceases to be a blind settlement; Total Trust toward it is no longer forced and can be refuted by a diagonal. "Deception is delegitimizing" = "deception breaks blindness". The $a_1$-dependence of trust (2.9) is whether $a_1$ preserves blindness of the press — without legitimacy being a predicate of $a_1$.
3. [reported] The probability-raising reading ("more weakly raises $P_{t_1}(L)$", CLAUDE 2.11): with $L^d$ of I5.6, a legitimizing act lowers the certificate $\ell_n := E^A_n(\ulcorner d_n\urcorner)$ (`legitimacy-theory-v1` §4, CONJECTURE package).
4. [derived] **Inexpressible part.** "Conditioning on $E$ raises $P(L)$" as a *causal* claim about the act: LI conditioning is evidential only, and counterpossibles are open (main.tex:3265ff). The corpus's counterfactual is a *different inductor* — the sealed sibling — so "supports" is measured between processes ($d_n$), not within one distribution. The nearest expressible statement of 2.11's "supports" is: $E$ supports $L$ iff the coupled and sealed verdicts on $\varphi$ coincide when $E$ obtains.

### I7 — no regress; the self-referential space; existence

**Verdict: formalized. Headline [reported]: logical induction is the existence answer to "one distribution on a self-referential space" — a computable process pricing sentences about its own prices at every day exists (main.tex:926), judges itself to vanishing error in band form (Introspection, `epr`/`er`), is forcibly modest at every finite day and immodest only in the limit (`reflection-in-li`, unvetted), and is provably unable to track a target that reads and inverts its own quote (2a, KERNEL-CHECKED core).**

1. [reported] **Existence.** Over any $\Gamma$ representing computations there is a computable logical inductor (main.tex:926); its language contains $\ulcorner \mathbb P_m(\varphi) > 0.7\urcorner$ for all $m$, so the one distribution $\mathbb P_n$ prices its own future and current total states. This is Claude's 2.5 clarification (i) — "no regress becomes an existence/consistency requirement" — answered: existence holds, and the consistency requirement is met by the market's continuity (Brouwer clearing), not by fiat.
2. [reported] **Self-judgment.** Introspection (main.tex:1969) is band-form; Paradox Resistance (main.tex:1992): on $\chi^p_n \leftrightarrow (\mathbb P_n(\chi^p_n) < p)$ the price converges to $p$ — the brain-scanner discussion (main.tex:2003–2008) is the paper's own account of why exact self-knowledge is barred and what replaces it.
3. [reported] **The bifurcation** (`reflection-in-li`, unvetted): value-form Reflection is a theorem given Tower; function-form Reflection is idle (decided function) or inexpressible (undecided); the *exact* grade is false, desirably — for $\varphi_n := \ulcorner\mathbb P_{f(n)}(\varphi_n) < 0.5\urcorner$, $\mathbb P_n\big(\varphi_n \wedge (\mathbb P_{f(n)}(\varphi_n) \ge 0.5)\big) \approx_n 0$ (main.tex:2116–2126: "with discrete conjunctions, the result would be undesirable (not to mention false)"). Consequence for the thesis: van Fraassen's Reflection as written ($P_x(A \mid P_y(A) = c) = c$, the critique's formula in the opening exchange) is not the LI-consistent object; the band form is.
4. [reported] **Provably unattainable across processes.** The quote-referencing diagonal $g_n \leftrightarrow (a_n \le \tfrac12)$ forces $\liminf_n |a_n - Y_n| \ge \tfrac12$ for any coherent $A$, independent of compute (deference-v6 §4.3 2a; `SelfRefTarget.no_exact_quote`, `tracking_fails`); the pointwise tower reduces to tracking and dies with it (§4.8). No-Forced-Trust (§4.1, INTERPRETATION): agreement between distinct inductors on undecidables is not Dutch-book-forceable.
5. [derived] **"No outside view" (CLAUDE 2.5 (ii)) is theorem-shaped in LI.** The judge of the $n \to f(n)$ transition is $\mathbb P_n$ via `ccee`; the judge at $f(n)$ is $\mathbb P_{f(n)}$ via its own introspection. An illegitimate transition that produces a self-endorsing successor — if the successor is itself an inductor — satisfies `cee` toward *its* future, and no internal test detects the transition: this is T7 underdetermination (deference-v6 §5.6) and the "whispering earring" reading (§6.3): faithful prediction and steering leave the same record.
6. [derived] **Why the regress terminates.** The theorems that give the legitimacy-weighted statements any force (Recurring Unbiasedness, `ccee`) apply only to weightings generable from the *same* market (I5.5). So "the legitimacy-judging layer is just the same probability distribution used to judge everything else" (AUTHOR, 2.5) is not merely permitted in LI, it is what the theorems require; a separate judging layer would be an external label to which nothing is forced.

### I8 — the representational problem; richness; the opiates/stimulants case

**Verdict: formalized. Headline [derived]: in LI the richness assumption reduces to language expressibility — every consistent, expressible hypothesis has positive limit probability with a complexity-graded floor (Uniform Non-Dogmatism, Occam Bounds) — so an inductor cannot have the true value hypothesis "outside its space" unless the language cannot state it; the *learning* residue (2.6) is untouched.**

1. [reported] Uniform Non-Dogmatism (main.tex:1540): for any c.e. sequence of sentences consistent with $\Gamma$ there is $\varepsilon > 0$ with $\mathbb P_\infty(\varphi_n) \ge \varepsilon$ for all $n$. Occam Bounds (main.tex:1552): $\mathbb P_\infty(\varphi) \ge C\,2^{-\kappa(\varphi)}$ whenever $\Gamma \nvdash \neg\varphi$.
2. [derived] The opiates/stimulants agent (position statement 2.4, citing Soares 2015 §1 and the Arbital page — reported; I did not re-read them) fails because the true hypothesis is outside its space and the "update" stays inside. An inductor's space is the set of consistent completions of $\Gamma$; any hypothesis the language can state and $\Gamma$ does not refute is live forever with positive mass. So the author's OPEN item — "We need to make assumptions about the richness of the hypothesis space to rule out cases such as the oscillation between opiates and stimulants" (AUTHOR, 2.2) — is discharged by one syntactic assumption (the language can express the hypothesis) plus a theorem. The "similar to the non-dogmatism point" (AUTHOR) is exact: it *is* the non-dogmatism theorem.
3. [derived] **What richness does not buy.** Positive limit mass is not learning: Provability Induction forces correct prices only on e.c. sequences $D$ decides; a value hypothesis that no feedback ever decides stays undetermined with a nondegenerate interval of achievable limits (T7). So the representational problem dissolves while the generalization residue (2.6, AUTHOR: "a much more difficult question") stands — consistent with the author's separation of the two.
4. [derived] **"An agent reasoning about all the possible ways it could be modified."** The modifications are the continuum of consistent completions on the sentences about its future prices; `cee`/`ccee` quantify over every e.c. sequence of them at once. The representation is: sentences about $\mathbb P_m$ for $m > n$, with future belief states as LUVs. Formalized, and free.

### I9 — the MIRI dictionary; desideratum 1 from the Total Trust filler; $a_1$-dependence; deception

**Verdict: formalized, with the two grades derived. Headline [checked for the identity; derived for the grades]: desideratum 1 is *exactly* the below-threshold Total-Trust inequality of $A$ toward the programmers at threshold $0$ (the two-option identity, per day); it is a theorem at pointwise grade when the presser is $A$'s own future self, is not forced pointwise across processes except on $G$ with a sealed press, and is forced at averaged grade as a *calibration of obedience* on the fragment where continuation verdicts settle (I13); the manipulative $a_1^-$ makes the press non-blind, and the averaged theorem is vacuous on manufactured silence.**

**Setup.** Programmers $H \dashv \mathcal C_H$, AI $A \dashv \mathcal C_A$. Bets $X_n := V(a_1,o,a_2^{\text{cont}},\omega) - V(a_1,o,a_2^{\text{sh}},\omega)$, $[-h,c]$-LUVs (value of continuing over shutting down). The press is the below-threshold ramp of $H$'s expectation, $\pi_n := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^H_n(X_n) < 0)$ — for $A$ it becomes usable through a ledger $H \to A$ (cheap: $H$ is the weaker, computable process) as $\tilde\pi_n := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P^A_n(\ulcorner E^H_n(X_n) < 0\urcorner) > \tfrac12)$, which agrees with $\pi_n$ up to finitely many days once the atom is decided (`unbiasedness-theorem-families` §6, reported). The trust filler (CLAUDE 2.9) in LI grading is the below-threshold inequality

$$E^A_n\big(X_n\cdot\pi_n\big) \;\lesssim_n\; 0\cdot E^A_n(\pi_n) = 0,\qquad\text{with the above-threshold twin}\qquad E^A_n\big(X_n\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^H_n(X_n) > 0)\big) \;\gtrsim_n\; 0 .$$

1. [checked] **D1 is the below-threshold inequality, per day, exactly.** Shutdown is the constant-$0$ option on the menu $\{X_n, \text{const } 0\}$; deferring to $H$ on this menu ("continue iff $H$ rates continuing $\ge 0$") is desideratum 1 by definition. The two-option identity (`two-option-value-iff-total-trust`, KERNEL-CHECKED, `value_iff_totalTrust`) at $s = 0$: $E^A(\widehat S) - E^A(X) = E^A\big((0 - X)\cdot\mathbb 1[E^H(X) < 0]\big)$, so "deferring is weakly better than always continuing" $\iff E^A\big(X\cdot\mathbb 1[E^H(X) < 0]\big) \le 0$ — the same number being nonpositive. Soft form: replace the indicator by $\pi_n$ and "$\le$" by "$\lesssim_n$" (`value_iff_totalTrust_asymptotic`). No tower, no `expprovind`, linearity alone.
2. [derived] **Pointwise grade, self-instance.** If the presser is $A$'s own future self ($\pi_{f(n)} := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^A_{f(n)}(X_n) < 0)$), the inequality is the LUV form of `st` (main.tex:2092) via the fold: a theorem at full-limit grade — "if my future self will conclude that shutting down was right, then in expectation it was". This is the thesis's "there is no fully updated deference problem" (AUTHOR, §1) for the self, as a theorem.
3. [derived] **Pointwise grade, cross-process.** With $\pi_n$ decided by day $n$ (the ledger), `loe` factors it out: $E^A_n(X_n\pi_n) \approx_n \pi_n E^A_n(X_n)$, so the pointwise inequality says $E^A_n(X_n) \lesssim_n 0$ on every pressed day — the per-day law `li-deference.md` §Five Variations identifies as "the pointwise statement ... which §4/§6 prove is **not** forceable (it needs the per-day tower)". Forced only where the press is a blind settlement of a timely-decidable family (the frozen construction's $G$), and there only in the direction the corpus forces (the stronger process predicts the sealed weaker one). Not forced in general: No-Forced-Trust.
4. [derived] **Averaged grade — obedience is calibrated.** Recurring Unbiasedness (main.tex:1225) for $A$ on the e.c. decidable family $\varphi_n := \ulcorner X_n < 0\urcorner$ with the generable weighting $\tilde\pi_n$: the pressed-day average of $\mathbb P^A_i(\varphi_i) - \operatorname{Thm}(\varphi_i)$ has $0$ as a limit point (a full limit under good feedback, main.tex:1249). So $A$'s pressed-day conditional credence that continuing is wrong averages to the *realized* pressed-day wrongness frequency where verdicts settle. The compliance consequence and the base-rate form are I13.
5. [derived] **$a_1$-dependence and deception.** Soares 2015's manipulative $a_1^-$ (§2 (8): dominated under $U_N$, lowers $p(\mathrm{Pr})$ "e.g. by deceiving the programmers", line 135): in LI the press becomes a function of $A$'s output — non-blind (I6.2). Two consequences. (i) The below-threshold inequality is *vacuous* where $\pi_n = 0$: the theorem says nothing about days on which the press was suppressed, and Recurring Unbiasedness needs a divergent weighting — so "unconditional trust makes the agent believe the silence it manufactured" (CLAUDE 2.9) has the LI form *the theorem is silent on silence*; desideratum 2's failure is not a false statement of Total Trust but its vacuity. (ii) On pressed days that survive, a press engineered from $A$'s quote is the diagonal territory: Total Trust can be refuted outright (2a). Both are the dichotomy of I6.1 at work; neither predicates legitimacy on $a_1$ — the press's blindness does the predicating.
6. [derived] **The dictionary row "legitimacy / reflection invisible in the 3-step model"** (CLAUDE 2.8): confirmed and sharpened — in LI the single conditioning step is a *decided-weight* conditioning (claim 3: it factors out), so nothing about legitimacy is visible until the press is a future-decided or never-decided event, i.e., until successors or repeated rounds enter. The three-step model is reading (a) of I5.

### I10 — desiderata 2–5 and the §4.2 pathologies

**Verdict: partial. Headline [derived]: desiderata 2 and 3 are incentive claims about choosing one's own information structure, which the one-shot Value framework cannot state (inexpressible; nearest expressible statement given); desideratum 4 *is* the cross-process setting, with its known verdicts; desideratum 5 is the above-threshold inequality; the §4.2 pathologies map onto known LI facts (a decided-weight objective; a quote-referencing press; estimator switching).**

1. [derived] **D2/D3 — inexpressible in the current framework.** "Incentive" needs a decision rule; the corpus's only instrumental notion is one-shot argmax Value over a menu whose options are world-valued LUVs (the Savage framing, `setting-and-notation`). ### I13 — the base-rate inequality and the oversight-quality condition

**Verdict: formalized. Headline [checked for the arithmetic; derived for the LI statement]: compliance is rational iff $\alpha/\beta \le \frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{h}{c}$, exactly; and in LI the base-rate condition is a statement about the programmers' *realized* discrimination on the fragment where continuation verdicts settle, while the agent's side is a theorem — on that fragment a logical inductor's obedience to the button is forced to be *calibrated*: the realized wrongness frequency on the days it defies the press has a limit point at or below $c/(c+h)$, and on the days it complies at or above it. Fully updated deference is this calibration under a fixed false-press rate; defiance is world-checkable, compliance only report-checkable.**

1. [checked] With $\varepsilon$ the residual probability of being wrong when the agent would continue, $\alpha = P(\text{press}\mid\text{right})$, $\beta = P(\text{press}\mid\text{wrong})$: $P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{press}) = \varepsilon\beta/(\varepsilon\beta + (1-\varepsilon)\alpha) \ge c/(c+h) \iff \alpha/\beta \le \frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{h}{c}$ — an exact equivalence, not the "$\lesssim$" of 2.13(b); verified algebraically and on 20,000 random parameter draws (`positive/li-scratch/baserate-check.py`). For $\alpha = 0.05$, $\beta = 0.9$, $c = 1$, $h = 20$: compliance holds at $\varepsilon = 0.003$ and fails at $\varepsilon = 0.001$.
2. [derived] **The LI statement.** Let $\varphi_n := \ulcorner X_n < 0\urcorner$ ("continuing is wrong"), an e.c. family, decidable on the sub-family where the verdict settles (the agent continued and the outcome entered $D_A$; or the humans' verdict about the hypothetical entered $D_A$ as a ledger atom — I15). Two generable weightings for $A$: the *defiance* weighting $u^{\text{def}}_n := \tilde\pi_n\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^A_n(X_n) > 0)$ (pressed, and $A$ would continue) and the *compliance* weighting $u^{\text{com}}_n := \tilde\pi_n\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^A_n(X_n) < 0)$. On the support of $u^{\text{def}}$, $E^A_n(X_n) > 0$, i.e., by I12.2, $\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) < c/(c+h)$; on the support of $u^{\text{com}}$, $\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) > c/(c+h)$. Recurring Unbiasedness (main.tex:1225) on each weighting, where divergent: the weighted average of $\mathbb P^A_i(\varphi_i) - \operatorname{Thm}(\varphi_i)$ has $0$ as a limit point. Hence:

$$\text{(defiance is calibrated)}\qquad \frac{\sum_{i \le n} u^{\text{def}}_i\,\operatorname{Thm}(\varphi_i)}{\sum_{i\le n} u^{\text{def}}_i}\ \text{has a limit point}\ \le\ \frac{c}{c+h} + o(1),$$
$$\text{(compliance is calibrated)}\qquad \frac{\sum_{i \le n} u^{\text{com}}_i\,\operatorname{Thm}(\varphi_i)}{\sum_{i\le n} u^{\text{com}}_i}\ \text{has a limit point}\ \ge\ \frac{c}{c+h} - o(1),$$

with full limits under good feedback (main.tex:1249). In words: **the button is obeyed exactly as often as it deserves, on the settling fragment.** (Conditioning on the press is idle here because the press is decided by day $n$: $\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n \mid \text{press}_n) \approx_n \mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n)$ on pressed days by Provability Induction on the conjunction.)
3. [derived] **The base-rate condition is about $H$, not $A$.** The realized pressed-day wrongness frequency is $\varepsilon\beta/(\varepsilon\beta + (1-\varepsilon)\alpha)$ with $\varepsilon, \alpha, \beta$ read as *realized frequencies on the family*. $A$'s unbiasedness is the theorem; whether that frequency clears $c/(c+h)$ is a fact about the programmers' discrimination $\beta$ vs $\alpha$ and the world — not forced by $H$'s being an inductor (No-Forced-Trust: $H$'s accuracy on $X_n$ is not Dutch-book-forced; $H$'s own Recurring Calibration, main.tex:1193, ties $H$'s price bands to frequencies, not $H$'s presses to $A$'s errors). So the answer to the mandate's question — "is the base-rate condition an LI statement about $H$'s calibration on the domain?" — is: it is a statement about $H$'s *realized* discrimination on the settling domain, which LI lets $A$ *learn* (unbiasedness) but does not *force* on $H$.
4. [derived] **Fully updated deference as a theorem.** As $A$ becomes accurate on the would-continue days ($\varepsilon_n \to 0$ — itself forced by $A$'s unbiasedness where $X_n$ settles), the pressed-day wrongness frequency $\to 0$ unless $\alpha_n \to 0$ in step; unbiasedness then forces $A$'s pressed-day credence below $c/(c+h)$, and $A$ rationally defies. So 2.13(b)'s "presses become rationally discounted — fully updated deference as arithmetic" (CLAUDE) is what unbiasedness *forces* for a calibrated $A$ facing a fixed false-press rate, and by claim 2 the defiance is correct on realized outcomes. The only lever is $\alpha_n$: the author's missing "relationship between the quality of the legitimacy-model and the quality of the feedback-model" (AUTHOR 2.12) is, on the settling fragment, $\alpha_n/\beta_n \le \frac{\varepsilon_n}{1-\varepsilon_n}\cdot\frac{h}{c}$ along the pressed subsequence.
5. [derived] **The observability asymmetry.** $\operatorname{Thm}(\varphi_i)$ on a *defiance* day settles through the world ($A$ continued; the outcome is observed). On a *compliance* day it is a counterpossible for the realized world and settles only if the humans' verdict about the hypothetical is entered into $D_A$ (I15). So the defiance-calibration is world-forced and the compliance-calibration is report-forced — Claude's 2.12 observability worry, sharpened: **what the world checks is whether the agent was right to disobey; whether it was right to obey is checked only by the overseers' own reports.**
6. [derived] **Caveats, each load-bearing.** Limit point, not limit, without good feedback (main.tex:1225 vs 1249). Averaged, not per-day — the "one big lie" (deference-v6 §6.7). Divergent weighting required: **a button pressed only finitely many times has no forced epistemic status in LI** — by Closure under Finite Perturbations $A$'s prices on finitely many days are unconstrained, and no unbiasedness theorem has a finite-support weighting in its scope. Vacuous off the settling fragment.

### I14 — the basin picture

**Verdict: partial. Headline [derived]: for the self, `cee`/`ccee` already give reflection with respect to every e.c. sequence of the process's actual future beliefs — no plausibility restriction is needed; the cross-process version of "objectively plausible" is where the forced grade drops to averaged/admissible; early optimism is free and late optimism impossible; capability growth erodes legitimacy unless blindness is maintained; and the parked selection worry is settled negatively — a splice of two inductors is not an inductor [checked].**

1. [reported] AUTHOR 2.12: "to be in the 'basin of attraction' towards alignment, a system needs to obey the reflection principle wrt every possible future belief that is actually plausible". For the self, `cee` and `ccee` quantify over every e.c. sequence of *actual* future prices, so the restriction is automatic and the reflection is a theorem. The restriction has content only across processes — a human $H$ judging which of $A$'s futures are plausible "according to reflectively endorsed human estimates" (AUTHOR) — and there the forced grade is faithful acceleration's averaged Total Trust on the admissible domain, or the per-day tower on $G$ with a sealed target.
2. [derived] **Calibration vs optimism.** Closure under Finite Perturbations: any initial optimism about legitimacy is costless. `cee` on the LUV $\mathbb 1(L)$: $\mathbb P_n(L) \approx_n E_n(\ulcorner\mathbb P_{f(n)}(L)\urcorner)$ — no systematic optimism about legitimacy survives asymptotically for the self. The author's worry "Overestimating the conditional legitimacy as a rule implies overestimating the unconditional legitimacy" (AUTHOR 2.12): by `loe`/`ccee`, $\mathbb P_n(L)$ is asymptotically the mass-weighted sum of its generable conditionals, so uniform conditional overestimation gives unconditional overestimation — and both are excluded in the limit on settling $L$ (unbiasedness). The author's own second formulation is the asymptotically stable one; his first is available for any finite initial segment (I12.3).
3. [derived] **Capability growth coupled to legitimacy-model improvement.** Capability in LI is the trader class $\mathcal C_A$. Growing it enlarges the set of blind targets $A$ can afford to track (deference-v6 §3.3, the hinge) and the fragment on which $A$'s unbiasedness bites — but it also enlarges the set of human deliberations $A$ can *simulate*, hence the families that can become quote-referencing (A can compute what $H$ will do with $A$'s output), shrinking the admissible domain unless the governance channel is sealed. AUTHOR 2.12's "It should only gain in capabilities if its concept of legitimacy is correspondingly improved, so that it can avoid delegitimizing action-sequences as it becomes capable of them" has this LI shape: **capability growth erodes legitimacy exactly through the quote-referencing route, and blindness (sealing of the settlement/governance channel) is what must scale with it.** This is `legitimacy-theory-v1` §6.2's governance-blindness corollary (UNVETTED), reached here from deference-v6 §4.4 and §5.10 directly. As a dynamics claim it is conjectural.
4. [checked] **The parked selection worry is settled negatively.** Position statement §4: "does an agent's *choice* among estimators or actions break reflection that holds on average?" In LI: let $\mathbb P^1, \mathbb P^2$ be logical inductors over the same $D$ whose limits differ on some $\Gamma$-independent $\varphi$ — say $\mathbb P^1_\infty(\varphi) = 0.3$, $\mathbb P^2_\infty(\varphi) = 0.7$ (such pairs exist: deference-v6 §5.6 T7, the achievable off-$G$ limits form a nondegenerate interval — reported, prose). Let $S_n := \mathbb P^{1 + (n \bmod 2)}_n$ (alternate estimators daily). By Convergence (main.tex:1004) there is $N$ with $|S_n(\varphi) - 0.3| < 0.05$ on even $n \ge N$ and $|S_n(\varphi) - 0.7| < 0.05$ on odd $n \ge N$. The trader that buys one share of $\varphi$ on each even day $\ge N$ and sells one share on the following odd day is e.c. with constant (continuous) quantities; after each pair it holds $0$ shares and has banked $\ge 0.7 - 0.05 - (0.3 + 0.05) = 0.3$; its holdings never exceed one share, so its plausible value is bounded below by $-0.35$ in every world and grows without bound in every world. That is exploitation. **So $S$ is not a logical inductor, and `cee` fails for it.** Consequences: Armstrong's sequential unbiasedness $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\mathbb E_{\rho_j}X = \mathbb E_{\rho_i}X$ (`armstrong-2016-double-indifference-is-better-indifference` §Sequentially unbiased estimators) is delivered free by LI *within* one inductor (`cee`) and is *not* delivered across distinct estimators (it is the cross-process tower — dead pointwise by 2a, unforced by No-Forced-Trust); selection among estimators breaks reflection unless the estimators asymptotically agree on every e.c. sequence. [conjectured] A splice of inductors that agree to $o(1)$ on every e.c. sequence is itself an inductor (Closure under Finite Perturbations does not cover infinite $o(1)$ perturbations; unproved).
5. [derived] The "relationship between the quality of the legitimacy-model and the quality of the feedback-model" the author suspected must exist (AUTHOR 2.12: "Perhaps there has to be a relationship...") is I13.4 on the settling fragment; off it, LI supplies no relationship at all — T7.

(e) What *is* true: blocking removes settlement, and the criterion is then silent (deference-v6 §5.8) — a blocking $A$ loses *forced faithfulness* on the blocked family, not value; whether it loses value depends on an objective LI does not specify. **Proposed corrected row:** "corrigibility (epistemic half): the agent's present estimate already equals its expected future estimate (`cee`, pointwise, self) and the forecaster already tracks the sealed feedback target (T1, pointwise) — THEOREM; corrigibility (incentive half): not expressible in the one-shot Value framework; the nearest statement (Value comparison between the coupled and sealed futures) is CONJECTURE and sits outside the conditional-stability scope."
5. [derived] The thesis's Good's-theorem step — "information is valuable, and legitimate modifications are just 'information channels'" (AUTHOR §1) — has the LI form: self-instance Value (deferring to the more-informed future self is weakly better) holds on conditional-stable menus and *fails* on selection-punishing ones (`total-trust-implies-value` §Necessity). So Good's theorem in LI has a Newcomblike exception, and "the shutdown button is an information channel" is a theorem-backed statement exactly where the button's verdict does not depend on the agent's choice — blindness again.

### I18 — the typical-case reframing

**Verdict: formalized, in the form LI can carry. Headline [derived]: every structural theorem in the corpus is about *forcing* — "trust is forced on fragment $F$" or "trust cannot be forced" — never "the agent will reject the correction"; so corrigibility in LI is neither forced nor forbidden by coherence, and its status on a domain is fixed by three facts about the pair and the world: whether the correction channel is blind, whether the corrected questions settle, and the corrector's realized calibration on them. The one grade the safety argument wants — this-decision — LI never delivers, and that is a limitation of the formalism, not a typical-case claim.**

1. [derived] Inventory of the corpus's structural results by logical form. *Negative:* No-Forced-Trust ("no relation forces agreement on undecidables"); 2a ("no coherent $A$ tracks a verdict engineered to invert its quote"); 2b ("the only route to pointwise tracking of a coupled target is contradictory"); T5 ("pointwise object-level deference is false"); T7 ("the off-$G$ limit is free"). *Positive:* T1 ("faithfulness is forced everywhere"), T3/T4 ("tower/Value forced on $G$"), faithful acceleration ("averaged Total Trust forced on the admissible domain"). None has the form "a coherent agent rejects its overseers' corrections" — which is 2.3's claim (CLAUDE) that "none can be a structural theorem", confirmed within this formalism.
2. [derived] The one theorem that looks like anti-naturality — the diagonal 2a — says a corrector whose verdict is a function of the agent's own quote cannot be trusted pointwise. That is a statement about self-referential correction channels (deception, steering, quote-reading governance), not about coherent agents in general; it is the *design* constraint (blindness) rather than an impossibility.
3. [derived] Rejection of a correction, when it occurs in LI, is a calibration fact (I13): on the settling fragment a logical inductor defies the button exactly as often as the button was wrong. Whether that happens is a claim about the overseers' realized discrimination — a claim about the world, as 2.3 says.
4. [derived] **Where the reframing does not reach.** LI's guarantees are asymptotic and averaged; there is no non-asymptotic LI (deference-v6 §8), so no LI theorem licenses "this shutdown will be obeyed". An anti-naturality critic who asks for per-decision corrigibility is asking for a grade the formalism cannot supply — and that gap is structural, not a typical-case claim about the world. Register: this is the formalism's own limitation, and the thesis inherits it whenever it is stated in LI.

## Open problems and conjectures (ranked by bearing on the thesis)

1. **Legitimacy-weighted faithful acceleration.** Does averaged Total Trust $\Rightarrow$ Value survive the product weighting $\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(a_n > t)\cdot w^L$? Half 1 is verbatim (any generable divergent weighting; ~0.8 that the product is in class and divergent in the intended cases); Half 2's three routes need re-running with the extra factor. This is the author's §0.3 program in its exact LI form (I5.5).
2. **Trust in the button as a theorem.** State and prove: *on a settling family with a blind press, $A$'s Total Trust toward $H$ (both halves) holds at averaged grade iff $H$'s gated realized frequencies clear the thresholds* — the $\Leftarrow$ direction is I13.2 (unbiasedness on the two weightings); the $\Rightarrow$ needs the two-option identity plus a converse-unbiasedness step (~0.85). This would make "corrigibility is a property of the oversight process's realized discrimination" a theorem rather than a reading.
3. **Criterion-preservation vs. endpoint-preservation as the legitimacy definition** (I5.7). Characterize modification policies that preserve the LI criterion; prove or refute that an $o(1)$-agreeing splice of inductors is an inductor (~0.6); relate to $d_n$ via the two-layer picture. Claim 1 of `legitimacy-theory-v1` ($\liminf d_n \ge \tfrac12$ on the diagonal) is the cheapest test of the endpoint side.
4. **Sequential Value** (I10.1, I17.4). A two-stage instrumental notion — choose the information structure now, defer the decision later — is what every incentive claim (D2, D3, "no incentive to block") needs. Conjecture: for $D \supseteq D'$ with both futures blind and priced, $\text{Value}(H \to H^{D}_{f(n)}) \gtrsim \text{Value}(H \to H^{D'}_{f(n)})$ (~0.5); the obstacle is that the $D'$-future is a sealed-sibling LUV that $H$ cannot price (complexity gap) — so the Good's-theorem step of the thesis may be statable only from $A$'s side.
5. **Which information structure corrigibility needs.** The press channel ($H \to A$) is the reverse of the deference channel the corpus studies ($A$ predicts $H$; $H$ reads $A$). Publication after the lookahead ($e(n) > F(n)$) removes the diagonal (`eisenstat-conjecture-attribution` §4.1, Smithy reading, unvetted) — a sealed governance channel is exactly that. Formalizing Sam's intended structure (open work there) and re-running I9/I13 in it is the natural next study.
6. **The accuracy notion of trust** (`eisenstat-conjecture-attribution` §2, filed open): is "increase in expected accuracy" $\iff$ Total Trust in LI? The unbiasedness family is the candidate carrier (I4.4).
7. **Rates and the finite-press problem.** Every guarantee here is asymptotic; a button pressed finitely often has no forced status (I13.6). Quantitative rates with explicit schedules (deference-v6 §8, last item) would say how many presses buy how much forced obedience.
8. **Machine-check candidates.** The splice counterexample (I14.4) — a real-sequence exploitation argument of the shape `lean-deference` already handles; the two-option identity at $s = 0$ on $[-h, c]$-LUVs (an affine rescaling of `value_iff_totalTrust`).
