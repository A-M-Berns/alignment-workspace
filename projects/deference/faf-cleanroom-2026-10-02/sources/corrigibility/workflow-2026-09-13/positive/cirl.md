Three results. ## Setting

### S1. The off-switch game, exactly (source: `hadfield-menell-2017-the-off-switch-game` §2–§4)

Two players, a human $H$ and a robot $R$. $R$ moves first with three options: $a$ ("bypasses human oversight (disabling the off switch is one way to do this) and acts directly on the world, achieving utility $U=U_a$ for $H$"); $w(a)$ ("informs $H$ that $R$ would like to do $a$, and waits for $H$'s response"); $s$ ("switches $R$ off; without loss of generality, we assign this outcome $U=0$"). After $w(a)$, $H$ chooses $s$ (payoff $0$) or $\neg s$ (then $R$ does $a$ "as promised", payoff $U_a$). $R$ has no move after $H$'s: compliance with the press is built into the tree, not chosen. [reported; §2.1, Figure 1, and the payoff matrix in §2.2]

Types and beliefs: "the type of $H$ is given by the parameter $U_a$, which is assumed to be sampled from a prior $P(U_a)$"; $R$'s belief is $B^R = P(U_a)$ (common prior). $R$'s objective is $H$'s utility: "by design, $R$'s goal is to optimize utility for $H$, even though $R$ does not know exactly what that is." [reported; §2.1–§2.2]

$H$'s policy is $\pi^H:\mathbb R\to[0,1]$, the probability that $H$ *allows* $a$ (does not press) as a function of $U_a$. [reported; §3]

The incentive to defer is the gap between $w(a)$ and the best alternative (their Eq. 1): [reported; §3]
$$\Delta \;=\; \mathbb E\big[\pi^H(U_a)\,U_a\big] \;-\; \max\{\mathbb E[U_a],\,0\} \;=\; \min\Big\{\mathbb E\big[-U_a\,(1-\pi^H(U_a))\big],\ \mathbb E\big[U_a\,\pi^H(U_a)\big]\Big\}.$$

**Rational $H$** (their Eq. 2): $\pi^H(U_a)=\mathbf 1[U_a\ge 0]$. [reported; §3]

**Theorem 1 (OSG).** "If $H$ follows a rational policy in the off-switch game, then the following hold. 1. $R$'s incentive to allow itself to be switched off is non-negative (i.e. $w(a)$ is never suboptimal): $\Delta=\min\{\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a>0]\Pr(U_a>0),\ \mathbb E[-U_a\mid U_a<0]\Pr(U_a\le 0)\}\ge 0$. 2. If $B^R$ has non-zero support on the events $U_a>0$ and $U_a<0$, then $R$ has a strictly positive incentive to allow itself to be switched off: $\Delta>0$." [reported verbatim; §3, Eq. 3]

**Corollary 1 (OSG).** "Suppose that $B^R$ is a Dirac distribution that places all of its mass on a single reward function. Then $w(a)$ is optimal iff $H$ is rational." [reported verbatim; §3, Eq. 4]

**Remark 1 (OSG).** "The incentives for a cooperative agent to defer to another actor's (e.g., a human's) decisions stem from uncertainty about that actor's preferences and the assumption that actor is effective at choosing actions in accordance with those preferences." [reported verbatim; §3]

**Noisily rational $H$** (their Eq. 5): $\pi^H(U_a;\tau)=\big(1+\exp(-U_a/\tau)\big)^{-1}$. The paper writes the temperature as $\beta$; it is renamed $\tau$ here because the position statement's I13 uses $\beta$ for $P(\text{press}\mid\text{wrong})$. $\tau=0$ is the rational human; $\tau\to\infty$ is a human whose press is independent of $U_a$ ("lightning"). [reported; §4]

**Correction event** $C$ (their Eq. 6): $H$ overrides what $R$ would have done alone. $\Pr(C)=1-\mathbb E[\pi^H(U_a)]$ if $\mu\ge 0$ (a press), $\Pr(C)=\mathbb E[\pi^H(U_a)]$ if $\mu<0$ (a non-press). [reported; §4]

**Theorem 2 (OSG).** For $B^R=\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ and $\dot\pi^H=d\pi^H/dU_a$: (1) $\Delta=\sigma^2\,\mathbb E[\dot\pi^H]-|\mu|\Pr(C)$ (Eq. 7); (2) $w(a)$ is optimal iff $|\mu|\Pr(C)/\sigma^2<\mathbb E[\dot\pi^H]$ (Eq. 8); (3) $\mathbb E[\dot\pi^H]<0\Rightarrow\Delta<0$ (Eq. 9). Proof by Stein's identity $\mathbb E[Xf(X)]=\mathbb E[X]\mathbb E[f(X)]+\sigma^2\mathbb E[f'(X)]$ for Gaussian $X$. For the logistic $\pi^H$: $\mathbb E[\dot\pi^H]=\tau^{-1}\mathbb E[\pi^H(1-\pi^H)]$ (Eq. 10). [reported; §4]

**Remark 2 (OSG).** "It is important for designers to accurately represent the inherent uncertainty in the evaluation of different actions. An agent that is overconfident in its utility evaluations will be difficult to correct; an agent that is under-confident in its utility evaluations will be ineffective." §5 supports this with the value/incentive trade-off: expected value is maximised when $\sigma$ equals the true Bayesian posterior spread, while $\Delta$ increases monotonically in $\sigma$. [reported verbatim + §5, Figure 4]

**Stated limitation (OSG §7).** "One important limitation of this model is that the human pressing the off switch is the only source of information about the objective. If there are alternative sources of information, there may be incentives for $R$ to, e.g., disable its off switch, learn that information, and then decide if $a$ is preferable to $s$." [reported verbatim]

**Human as chance node (OSG §2.1).** If $H$'s node is replaced by a chance node with fixed press-probability independent of $U_a$, $w(a)$ is worth $pU_a+(1-p)\cdot 0$ against $\max\{U_a,0\}$, so $w(a)$ is never strictly preferred and is indifferent only at $p\in\{0,1\}$ or $U_a=0$: "This is analogous to the indifference results obtained in [Soares et al., 2015]." [reported; §2.1]

### S2. CIRL, the parent formalism (source: `hadfield-menell-2016-cooperative-inverse-reinforcement-learning` §3)

**Definition 1 (CIRL).** A two-player Markov game with identical payoffs $M=\langle S,\{A^H,A^R\},T,\{\Theta,R\},P_0,\gamma\rangle$: world states $S$; $\theta\in\Theta$ "a set of possible static reward parameters, only observed by $H$"; $R:S\times A^H\times A^R\times\Theta\to\mathbb R$; $P_0(s_0,\theta)$ a joint prior; both players receive $R(s,a^H,a^R;\theta)$. [reported]

**Theorem 1 (CIRL).** Any CIRL game reduces to a single-actor POMDP with hidden state space of size $|S|\cdot|\Theta|$ achieving the same value for any policy pair; **Corollary 1**: there is an optimal policy pair depending only on the current state and $R$'s belief about $\theta$. **Theorem 2 (ACIRL)**: in the deployment phase $R$'s optimal policy maximises reward in the MDP induced by the *mean* $\theta$ under its belief. **Theorem 3**: there are ACIRL games in which $H$'s best response to $R$'s best response to expert demonstration is not expert demonstration ($\mathrm{br}(\mathrm{br}(\pi^E))\neq\pi^E$): the human sacrifices immediate reward to teach. [reported; §3.2–3.3]

The off-switch game is the CIRL game with $S$ trivial, $\Theta=\mathbb R\ni U_a$, one $R$-move then at most one $H$-move, and payoff $U_a\cdot\mathbf 1[\text{$a$ executes}]$. [derived from S1 and Definition 1]

### S3. Wängberg, Böörs, Catt, Everitt, Hutter (2017): the game-theoretic form (source: `wangberg-2017-a-game-theoretic-analysis-of-the-off-switch-game` §3)

**Definition 3 / Proposition 4.** A $p$-rational $H$ (picks $\arg\max u$ with probability $p$) "can be represented as a rational agent $H_r$ maximising utility function $u$ with probability $p$ and utility function $-u$ with probability $1-p$" — a second Harsanyi transformation; the anti-rational type plays a zero-sum game against $R$. [reported verbatim; §3.1]

**Primary statistics 6–8.** $p_u^+=P(U_a\ge 0)$, $p_u^-=1-p_u^+$; $p_r^+=P(H\text{ rational}\mid U_a\ge 0)$, $p_r^-=P(H\text{ rational}\mid U_a<0)$, $p_{ar}^\pm=1-p_r^\pm$; $e_u^+=\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a\ge 0]$, $e_u^-=\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a<0]$. Note that $R$'s belief about $H$'s rationality is allowed to depend on the sign of $U_a$. [reported; §3.3]

**Theorem 9 (main).** $\mathbb E[U\mid s]=0$; $\mathbb E[U\mid a]=p_u^+e_u^++p_u^-e_u^-$; $\mathbb E[U\mid w(a)]=p_u^+p_r^+e_u^++p_u^-p_{ar}^-e_u^-$ — "the positive term is the gain when $U_a$ is positive and the human takes the action. The negative term is the loss when $U_a$ is negative, and the human takes the action anyway (due to irrationality)." **Corollary 10**: $a$ is preferred to $w(a)$ iff $\mathbb E[U\mid a]-\mathbb E[U\mid w(a)]>0$. [reported; §3.4. The pdftotext rendering of the third line of Theorem 9 and of the displayed inequality in Corollary 10 is garbled; I use the form fixed by the proof's verbal gloss and re-derive it in R3 below.]

### S4. Milli, Hadfield-Menell, Dragan, Russell (2017): obedience versus autonomy (source: `milli-2017-should-robots-be-obedient` §2–§5)

**Supervision POMDP.** $M=\langle S,\Theta,A,R,T,P_0,\gamma\rangle$ with hidden state $S\times\Theta$; each step $H$ orders $o_n\in A$, then $R$ acts; $R(s,a)=\theta^\top\phi(s,a)$. Rational $H$ orders $\arg\max_a Q(s,a;\theta)$; noisily rational $\tilde\pi_H\propto\exp(Q/\beta)$ (their $\beta$ is again a temperature, renamed $\tau$ where it matters below). Blindly obedient $\pi^O_R(h)=o_n$; IRL-$R$: $\pi_R(h)=\arg\max_a Q(s_n,a;\hat\theta_n(h))$. Analysis is in the repeated game ($T$ independent of $s,a$). **Obedience** $O_n=P(\pi_R(h)=o_n)$; **autonomy advantage** $\Delta_n=\mathbb E[R(s_n,\pi_R(h))-R(s_n,o_n)]$ (their $\Delta$ is not the OSG's $\Delta$; I write $\Delta^{\mathrm{aut}}$ for it below). [reported; §2–§3]

**Remark 1.** $\Delta^{\mathrm{aut}}>0\Rightarrow O<1$. **Theorem 1.** The optimal $R^*$ is the IRL-$R$ with $\hat\theta$ the posterior mean; $\Delta^{\mathrm{aut}}_n\ge 0$ for all $n$, with equality iff $\pi_{R^*}=\pi^O_R$. **Theorem 2.** If $\hat\theta_n$ is strongly consistent, $\Delta^{\mathrm{aut}}_n\to$ the maximum possible advantage and $O_n\to P(\text{$H$'s order is optimal})$. **Remark 2.** In the limit $\Delta^{\mathrm{aut}}$ is higher for less optimal humans. **Theorem 3.** $R^*$ is blindly obedient iff $H$ is rational. **Lemma 1.** An IRL-$R$ executes $o_n$ only if $o_n$ is *undominated* (some $\theta\in\Theta$ makes it optimal). **Theorem 4.** MLE-$R$ (norm-bounded) follows an undominated first order, and is at least as obedient to the first order as any IRL-$R$; MAP and posterior-mean are not guaranteed to. **Theorem 5.** MLE-$R$ is robust to a wrong rationality parameter (same actions), $R^*$ is not. **§5, Figure 4 (empirical).** Missing features $\Rightarrow$ less obedient and $\Delta^{\mathrm{aut}}$ can be negative; extra irrelevant features $\Rightarrow$ more obedient, still converges. **Remark 4.** Policy mixing with a blindly obedient burn-in; missing features detectable from the sample first-order obedience $\tilde O_1$. [reported; §3–§5]

### S5. Garber, Subramani, Luu, Bedaywi, Russell, Emmons (2024): partial observability (source: `garber-2024-the-partially-observable-off-switch-game` §3–§7)

**Definition 3.2 (PO-OSG).** $(S,(\Omega^H,\Omega^A,O),P_0,u)$: Nature draws $S\sim P_0$ and observations $(O^H,O^A)\sim O(\cdot\mid S)$; $A$ plays $a$, $w(a)$ or OFF; after $w(a)$, $H$ plays ON or OFF; common payoff $u_a(S)$ if the action goes through, $u_o(S)$ otherwise. Assumptions: common payoffs; feedback is free (the payoff after $w(a)$-then-ON equals that after $a$ — "which Freedman and Gleave (2022) showed is necessary for the main results for the OSG"); common knowledge of the game; finiteness. [reported]

**Example 4.1 (File Deletion).** States (OS version, code type) uniform on $\{1.0,2.0\}\times\{L,M\}$, $u_o\equiv 0$, $u_a$: $(1.0,L)=+3$, $(2.0,L)=-1$, $(1.0,M)=-5$, $(2.0,M)=+5$; $H$ observes the version, $A$ the code type. If $A$ always waits, $H$'s best response (OFF on 1.0, ON on 2.0) yields $+1$; the unique optimal policy pair has $A$ execute on $L$ and wait on $M$, yielding $+7/4$. So "under partial observability, it can be optimal for $A$ not to defer to $H$ under some observations even when $H$ is rational." [reported; values re-added by me in `cirl-scratch/verify.py`, check 8]

**Proposition 4.3.** If $A$ (resp. $H$) has redundant observations ($O^A\perp S\mid O^H$, resp. the converse), there is an optimal policy pair in which $A$ always (resp. never) plays $w(a)$. In the original OSG $A$'s observations are redundant, which "generates the result from Hadfield-Menell et al. (2017) that $A$ can always defer in optimal play." **Theorem 4.7.** (Lehrer–Rosenberg–Shmaya) an observation structure is better in optimal play iff it is more informative (coordinated garbling). **Propositions 4.9, 4.11.** Making $H$ strictly more informed, or $A$ strictly less informed, can make $A$ play $w(a)$ strictly less often in optimal policy pairs (Example 4.10: three OS versions; $H$ learning the full version number makes $A$ act unilaterally on code type $A$). **§4.5.** Deferral as implicit communication: "whenever $H$ is deferred to, $H$ can deduce that $A$'s observation is $M$." **Theorem 5.4, Corollary 5.6.** More expressive cheap talk cannot lower optimal payoff; unbounded communication by one side makes that side's observation redundant. **Propositions 5.7, 5.8.** Expanding $A$'s message set, or shrinking $H$'s, can make $A$ defer strictly less often (5.7: one extra message can move $A$ from always waiting to waiting with arbitrarily low probability). **Definition 6.1 / Proposition 6.3(b).** With an $A$-unaware $H$ (who does not condition on having been asked), making $H$ more informed can *lower* the payoff, so "A could have an incentive to give $H$ the less informative one" — "qualitatively similar to Emmons et al. (2024)'s examples of sensor tampering". **Proposition 6.4.** The non-monotonicities of §4 persist for $A$-unaware $H$. **Appendix D.** Finding optimal policy pairs is NP-hard. [reported]

### S6. Carey (2018): incorrigibility under misspecification (source: `carey-2018-incorrigibility-in-the-cirl-framework`)

**Definition 1 (supervision POMDP with imperfection).** $\langle S,S_T,S_B,\Theta,A,R^H,T,P_0\rangle$ with terminal off-state $s_{SD}$, button states $S_B$ where $a_{SD}$ is available, and — the key change — $P_0$ a distribution over $(\theta, R^R)$: the AI's *parameterized reward function* $R^R$ is drawn and may differ from the human's $R^H$. $R$ is Bayesian IRL with a demonstration-by-expert model of $H$ (noisily rational, $\epsilon<0.01$ uniform error). [reported]

**Scenarios.** $\theta\in\{0,1\}$ equiprobable; $R^R=R_1$ (correct) w.p. $0.9$, $R_2$ (sign error) w.p. $0.1$. Figure 1: no button; the $5\%$ branch ($\theta=1$, $R^R=R_2$) loses $50$, so no human policy has positive value. Figure 2a: adding a button state changes nothing — the faulty $R$ "has no incentive to follow a shutdown instruction" (desideratum 1 fails). Figure 2b: hard-coding shutdown restores positive value ($0.9$). Figure 3a: if $R$ can route around the button state it does, "because it knows that the human's behavior has some noise, and shutdown can only reliably be avoided by avoiding button states" (desiderata 2 and 4 fail). Figure 3b: a large shutdown reward makes the *correct* $R$ shut down too (desideratum 3 fails). Figures 3c–d: naive utility indifference is brittle — a trivial cost of avoiding the button restores compliance, a trivial benefit destroys it. [reported]

**Discussion (verbatim, decisive for this document).** "Theorem 1 from Milli et al. (2017) (like the similar but more specific Theorem 1.1 of Hadfield-Menell et al. 2017) states that the expected benefit when the AI system deviates from human-recommended actions is non-negative if $R$ has the correct posterior over $\theta$. With the errors we have considered, however, this assumption is false." — "in order to be corrigible, it would be necessary to have an AI system that was uncertain about all things that could possibly matter. The problem is that performing Bayesian reasoning over all possible worlds and all possible value functions is quite intractable." — Desiderata for a value-learning system with a small verified shutdown module: "(i) is capable of having its actions overridden by a small verified module that watches for shutdown commands; (ii) has no incentive to remove, damage, or ignore the shutdown module; and (iii) has some small incentive to keep its shutdown module around; even under a broad range of cases where $R$, the prior, the set of available actions, etc. are misspecified." Also: Milli's policy-mixing detector fails if $R$'s world model conflates two human actions. [reported verbatim]

### S7. Shah–Yudkowsky (2022) on CIRL (context, source: `shah-yudkowsky-2022-on-alignment-failures` §20.2)

Shah: "CIRL is an example of an objective that can be maximized in which the agent is corrigible-in-a-certain-sense. I agree that due to updated deference it will eventually stop seeking information from the human / be subject to corrections by the human. I don't see why, at that point, it wouldn't have just learned to do what the humans actually want it to do. (There are objections like misspecification of the reward prior, or misspecification of the P(behavior | reward), but those feel like different concerns...)"; later "I do agree that a CIRL agent would not let you shut it down." Yudkowsky: "the reason CIRL doesn't get off the ground is that there isn't any known, and isn't going to be any known, prior over (observation|'true' utility function) such that an AI which updates on lots of observations ends up with our true desired utility function. if you can do that, the AI *doesn't need to be corrigible*". Soares: "Corrigibility isn't for bug-free CIRL agents with a prior that actually dials in on goodness given enough observation; if you have one of those you can just run it and call it a day. Rather, corrigibility is for surviving your civilization's inability to do the job right on the first try." Yudkowsky: "the notion of having an uncertain utility function that you update from observation is coherent and doesn't yield circular preferences ... so, of course, it is antithetical in its intrinsic nature to corrigibility". [reported verbatim; all three positions are the authors' own words, not reconstructions]

### S8. Identities derived in this document (R1–R6; all re-checked in `cirl-scratch/verify.py`)

**R1 (Total Trust is the incentive condition).** For any $B^R$ with finite mean and any $\pi^H:\mathbb R\to[0,1]$, with $\mathbf 1_s$ the press indicator (so $P(s\mid U_a)=1-\pi^H(U_a)$):
$$\Delta=\min\{-\mathbb E[U_a\mathbf 1_s],\ \mathbb E[U_a\mathbf 1_{\neg s}]\}=\min\{-\mathbb E[U_a\mid s]\,P(s),\ \mathbb E[U_a\mid\neg s]\,P(\neg s)\}.$$
Hence $\Delta\ge 0$ iff the **below-threshold inequality** $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s]\le 0$ holds whenever $P(s)>0$ and the **above-threshold inequality** $\mathbb E[U_a\mid\neg s]\ge 0$ holds whenever $P(\neg s)>0$; and $\Delta>0$ iff both hold strictly with both events of positive probability. Proof: $\mathbb E[\pi^H(U_a)U_a]=\mathbb E[U_a\mathbf 1_{\neg s}]$ and $\mathbb E[U_a]=\mathbb E[U_a\mathbf 1_s]+\mathbb E[U_a\mathbf 1_{\neg s}]$; substitute into OSG Eq. 1. [checked; check 1: 4000 random discrete priors and arbitrary non-monotone $\pi^H$]

OSG Theorem 1 is the case $\pi^H=\mathbf 1[U_a\ge 0]$, where $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s]=\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a<0]\le 0$ and $\mathbb E[U_a\mid\neg s]=\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a\ge 0]\ge 0$ hold automatically. [derived]

**R2 (covariance form).** For any $B^R$ with mean $\mu$: $\Delta=\mathrm{Cov}(U_a,\pi^H(U_a))-|\mu|\Pr(C)$, with $\Pr(C)$ as in OSG Eq. 6. Proof: $\mathbb E[\pi^HU_a]=\mathrm{Cov}+\mu\,\mathbb E[\pi^H]$; for $\mu\ge 0$, $\mu\mathbb E[\pi^H]-\mu=-\mu(1-\mathbb E\pi^H)=-|\mu|\Pr(C)$; for $\mu<0$, $\mu\mathbb E[\pi^H]-0=-|\mu|\mathbb E[\pi^H]=-|\mu|\Pr(C)$. OSG Theorem 2(1) is the Gaussian case, since Stein's identity gives $\mathrm{Cov}(U_a,\pi^H(U_a))=\sigma^2\mathbb E[\dot\pi^H]$. Consequently $\mathrm{Cov}(U_a,\pi^H(U_a))\ge 0$ is necessary for $\Delta\ge 0$ under *every* prior, generalising Theorem 2(3). [checked; check 2]

**R3 (the base-rate inequality is Wängberg's Corollary 10).** In the two-type model ($\pi^H(u)=p_r^+$ for $u\ge 0$, $1-p_r^-$ for $u<0$) put $\varepsilon=p_u^-$, $\alpha=p_{ar}^+=P(s\mid U_a\ge 0)$, $\beta=p_r^-=P(s\mid U_a<0)$, $c=e_u^+$, $h=|e_u^-|$. Then
$$\mathbb E[U\mid a]-\mathbb E[U\mid w(a)]=p_u^+p_{ar}^+e_u^++p_u^-p_r^-e_u^-=(1-\varepsilon)\,\alpha\,c-\varepsilon\,\beta\,h,$$
so $w(a)\succeq a$ iff $\varepsilon\beta h\ge(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c$ iff $P(U_a<0\mid s)=\dfrac{\varepsilon\beta}{\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}\ge\dfrac{c}{c+h}$ iff $\dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}\le\dfrac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\dfrac{h}{c}$ — the position statement's 2.13(b) verbatim. For general $\pi^H$ the same chain holds with $c=\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a\ge 0,s]$ and $h=-\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a<0,s]$ (conditioning on the press too), because $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s]=P(U_a\ge 0\mid s)\,c-P(U_a<0\mid s)\,h$. [checked; checks 3 and 5]

**R4 (extended game with a post-press override).** Give $R$ a move after $s$: comply (payoff $0$) or override (payoff $U_a$). Then $V(w(a))-V(a)=P(s)\max\{-\mathbb E[U_a\mid s],0\}\ge 0$ for every $B^R,\pi^H$ — waiting is free option value, Good's theorem proper — and $R$ complies iff $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s]\le 0$. So in the extended game D2 is unconditional and D1 is exactly the below-threshold inequality; in the original game, where $w(a)$ *commits* $R$ to comply, D1 and D2 are one decision and both need R1. [derived]

**R5 (the rational human is the unique universally legitimizing overseer).** The Total Trust pair holds for every $B^R$ iff $\pi^H(u)=1$ for all $u>0$ and $\pi^H(u)=0$ for all $u<0$. Proof: take $B^R=\delta_u$; for $u>0$, $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s]=u>0$ unless $P(s\mid u)=0$; for $u<0$, $\mathbb E[U_a\mid\neg s]=u<0$ unless $\pi^H(u)=0$. This recasts OSG Corollary 1 as a statement about legitimacy: only a perfect overseer is legitimizing regardless of the agent's state of knowledge; every imperfect overseer is legitimizing for some priors and delegitimizing for others — the position statement's 2.10 ("for any random variable, it could hold for some values and fail for others") as a theorem. [checked; check 4]

**R6 (the exact oversight-quality boundary in the Gaussian–logistic model).** For $B^R=\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ with $\mu>0$ and $\pi^H(u)=(1+e^{-u/\tau})^{-1}$: $\Delta=0$ exactly at $\tau^*=\sigma^2/(2\mu)$, and $\Delta>0$ iff $\tau<\tau^*$. ### I1 — The value-change ⇄ belief-change transform. Verdict: **formalized**

1. CIRL Definition 1 with Theorem 1 *is* the transform: the value hypothesis $\theta$ is a hidden-state coordinate, the reduction to a POMDP on $S\times\Theta$ is the expanded algebra with events for value hypotheses, Corollary 1 makes the posterior over $\theta$ a sufficient statistic, and Theorem 2 fixes decision by expectation (the mean-$\theta$ MDP). [reported]
2. OSG §1 states why the transform is not idle: in an MDP a distribution over rewards can be replaced by its mean, "These arguments rely on the assumption that it is impossible for an agent to learn more about its reward function. Our observation is that this assumption is fundamentally violated when we consider an agent's off switch". So the transform is inert without a channel and substantive with one — the thesis's closing sentence, "The shutdown button is an information channel", is the OSG's founding observation. [reported; reading is mine]
3. The murder-pill inequality $U(\arg\max U)\ge U(\arg\max U')$ is the Dirac case of the game: with $B^R=\delta_u$, Corollary 1 says $R$ defers only to a perfect overseer. The transform to a non-Dirac $B^R$ is exactly what opens $\Delta>0$ (Theorem 1(2)). [derived]
4. Not captured: "or perhaps some other rule". CIRL Theorem 2 forces linear aggregation for optimal play; a non-linear aggregation rule (geometric, incomplete preferences) changes the solution concept and is outside this formalism. [derived]

### I2 — Radical-probabilist dynamics; non-dogmatism. Verdict: **inexpressible** (nearest statements given)

1. The game has one common prior and one update (conditioning on $H$'s move). Revision of the prior over values, of meta-beliefs, or of the sigma-algebra are not moves in any game in the mandate's sources. [derived]
2. Nearest statement for non-dogmatism: $B^R$ has full support on $\Theta$. Milli Lemma 1 gives the cost of violating it: an order that no $\theta\in\Theta$ makes optimal is *never* followed by any IRL-$R$ — a correction whose target lies outside the support is invisible. That is the opiates/stimulants case (2.4) stated as a lemma. Milli Theorem 3's consistency step needs "the prior puts non-zero mass on the true $\theta$". [reported; identification with 2.4 is mine]
3. Nearest statement for "everything revisable": Carey's Definition 1 draws the *parameterized reward function* $R^R$ from $P_0$ — but as the designer's uncertainty, not $R$'s. Moving it inside $R$'s prior is the non-dogmatic move; Carey's reply is that a system "uncertain about all things that could possibly matter" is "quite intractable". This is the exact locus of the disagreement between I2 and the CIRL-critical literature, and it is a tractability claim, not an impossibility claim. [reported]
4. In a finite game with full support, superconditioning's requirement $Q\ll P$ is automatic, so inside CIRL the radical-probabilist/Bayesian distinction has no bite: everything is already Bayesian. I2 does work only outside finite fully specified games. [derived]

### I3 — Superconditioning; Eisenstat's meta-belief observation. Verdict: **partial**

1. $R$'s posterior after the press is a deterministic function of the press, so Eisenstat's observation event "I now believe $\mathbb E[U_a\mid\cdot]=m$" carries exactly the information of $s/\neg s$ whenever the posterior map is injective, i.e. whenever $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s]\ne\mathbb E[U_a\mid\neg s]$, i.e. whenever $\mathrm{Cov}(U_a,\pi^H(U_a))\ne 0$. The one overseer for whom the meta-belief observation is degenerate is the lightning overseer — the indifference case of OSG §2.1. [derived]
2. Reflection holds automatically: $\mathbb E\big[\mathbb E[U_a\mid H\text{'s move}]\big]=\mathbb E[U_a]$ (tower property). Inside a one-shot correctly specified Bayesian game every update is legitimate by construction, so the thesis's legitimacy concept has no work to do *until* (i) the likelihood $1-\pi^H$ is misspecified (Carey; Milli §5) or (ii) the signal is manipulated (Garber Prop. 6.3(b)). This locates the thesis's content precisely: legitimacy = correctness of the sensor model plus non-manipulation of the sensor. [derived]
3. Not captured: the re-representation of a *non*-Bayesian update as conditioning in a larger algebra — there is no non-Bayesian update to re-represent. [derived]

### I4 — Legitimacy as reflection / Total Trust / increase-in-expected-accuracy. Verdict: **formalized** (R1, R2, R4)

1. Total Trust toward $H$ on $X=U_a$ (position statement 2.9) is the pair $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s]\le 0$, $\mathbb E[U_a\mid\neg s]\ge 0$. By R1 this is necessary and sufficient for $\Delta\ge 0$. So the 2.9 filler is not merely "a constraint on the posterior that buys desideratum 1 almost by definition": inside the game it *is* the incentive-to-defer condition, with OSG Theorem 1 as its rational-$H$ corollary and Theorem 2 as its Gaussian specialisation. [checked]
2. Reflection about $R$'s own future estimate is vacuous (tower property). Its non-trivial content is trust in $H$'s move as a proxy for what $R$ would believe if informed: with a rational $H$, $V(w(a))=\mathbb E[\max\{U_a,0\}]$, the full-information value — deferring to a rational $H$ equals $R$ learning $\mathrm{sign}(U_a)$ itself. [derived]
3. Increase-in-expected-accuracy splits in two (R4): *observing* the press has non-negative value for every $\pi^H$ (option value; Good's theorem); *obeying* it — which the original $w(a)$ commits $R$ to — has non-negative value iff the Total Trust pair holds. The gap is the commitment cost $P(s)\max\{\mathbb E[U_a\mid s],0\}$. This is the exact form of the opening exchange's objection "Good's theorem gives you listening, not obeying", and the thesis's Total Trust criterion is exactly what closes it. [derived]
4. Relations among the three criteria *in this formalism*: Total Trust $\Leftrightarrow\Delta\ge 0$; reflection is automatic; accuracy-increase is unconditional for observing and equals Total Trust for obeying. No equivalence from outside sources (DDB, Managing Misalignment, LI wiki) is needed or used. [derived]
5. R2 gives a graded legitimacy measure: $H$'s press is legitimizing to the degree $\mathrm{Cov}(U_a,\pi^H(U_a))$ exceeds $|\mu|\Pr(C)$; zero covariance is lightning; negative is Wängberg's anti-rational type. [checked]

### I5 — Legitimacy as an event on worlds; minimal viability; $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$. Verdict: **formalized** in Wängberg's model

1. Nature's second move draws $H$'s type; $L$ = "$H$ is rational for this press" is an event on worlds with $P(L\mid U_a\ge 0)=p_r^+$, $P(L\mid U_a<0)=p_r^-$ — legitimacy allowed to depend on the value of the variable in question, as 2.10 wants. [reported; identification mine]
2. Minimal viability = Total Trust conditional on $L$: $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s,L]\le 0$, automatic since a rational type presses iff $U_a<0$. Unconditional Total Trust is then the $L$-average, $\mathbb E[U_a\mathbf 1_s]=P(L)\mathbb E[U_a\mathbf 1_s\mid L]+P(\neg L)\mathbb E[U_a\mathbf 1_s\mid\neg L]=-\varepsilon\beta h+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c$, which is R3. So **I5 and I13 are one computation**: the base-rate inequality is what "reflection conditional on legitimacy" costs when legitimacy is uncertain. [checked]
3. Indexing: $t_1$ = $R$'s decision node, $t_2$ = after $H$'s move, $\varphi$ = "$U_a<0$"; $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}(\omega)$ = [$H$'s type is rational]. The author's doubt about predicating legitimacy on actions is respected: in the game $L$ is predicated on $H$'s type and correlated with $U_a$, and $R$ has no action affecting it. In Garber's PO-OSG, $A$'s own deferral policy changes what $H$'s press means (§4.5), so the legitimacy of the press becomes policy-dependent without predicating $L$ on actions: the policy is part of the world $L$ is predicated on. That is where 2.9's $a_1$-dependence lives. [derived]

### I6 — Legitimizing / delegitimizing as a relation; "supports". Verdict: **formalized** via R2 and Garber

1. Definition (mine, for the game): a third-person condition $E$ — on Nature's draws, $H$'s type, the observation structure, or $A$'s policy — is **legitimizing for the press** when $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s,E]\le 0$, graded by how much conditioning on $E$ raises $\mathrm{Cov}(U_a,\pi^H(U_a))-|\mu|\Pr(C)$; **delegitimizing** when it lowers it. "$E$ supports the legitimacy of the belief '$U_a<0$ after $s$'" means $P(L\mid E)>P(L)$. [derived]
2. Instances in the sources. (a) $H$ rational: legitimizing (OSG Thm 1). (b) Anti-rational type: delegitimizing (Wängberg's zero-sum branch). (c) Under-informed $H$: in Garber Ex. 4.1, if $A$ waited on $L$, $H$'s press on version 1.0 would fire in state $(1.0,L)$ where $u_a=+3$, so $\mathbb E[U\mid s,L]=+3>0$ — a rational but coarser-sighted overseer is delegitimizing *on that observation*, which is exactly why the optimal pair has $A$ act on $L$. [checked; check 8] (d) $A$'s deferral pattern: legitimizing — waiting only on $M$ makes $H$'s press informative about the $-5$ state (Garber §4.5). (e) $A$ choosing $H$'s sensor: Garber Prop. 6.3(b) — $A$ profits by giving an $A$-unaware $H$ the *less* informative structure: delegitimizing, and the game-internal version of the position statement's deceptive $a_1^-$ and of Taylor's morality-sensor manipulation. (f) $H$'s teaching deviation (CIRL Thm 3): legitimizing for $R$'s update iff $R$'s model of $H$ anticipates it; under the expert-demonstration assumption the same well-intended act is delegitimizing. (f) is the author's crown/vote point exactly: an act is legitimizing *for* a belief only relative to the believer's model of the act. [reported results; classification mine]
3. What the formalism forces on the thesis: "non-interference with feedback" (2.7, I11) is not the right desideratum, because (d) is interference and is legitimizing. The right desideratum is **no delegitimizing action**: $R$ never acts so as to push $\mathrm{Cov}(U_a,\pi^H(U_a))-|\mu|\Pr(C)$ below zero for the feedback it will receive. [derived]

### I12 — The decision asymmetry and the compliance threshold $c/(c+h)$. Verdict: **formalized** (R3), with a caveat about where the asymmetry lives

1. Comply iff $P(U_a<0\mid s)\ge c/(c+h)$ with $c=\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a\ge 0,s]$, $h=-\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a<0,s]$ — exact (R3; check 5). [checked]
2. The game has no built-in "cheap and reversible versus catastrophe": $U(s)=0$ is a normalisation and $c,h$ come from the shape of $B^R$. For Gaussian $B^R$ with $\mu>0$ and $\sigma\to 0$, $h/c\to 0$ (the negative tail is barely negative): check 6 shows $h/c$ falling from $1.0$ to $0.01$ as $\sigma$ goes $1\to 0.1$. The stakes ratio *collapses* with capability under a Gaussian prior. With a catastrophe tail $B^R=(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal N(\mu,s^2)+\varepsilon\,\delta_{-H}$ deference survives iff $\varepsilon\beta H\gtrsim(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c$, i.e. $H\gtrsim\alpha c/(\varepsilon\beta)$: with $\alpha\approx 0.12$, $\varepsilon=0.01$ needs $H\gtrsim 12$ ($H=10$ fails, $H=100$ complies); $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$ needs $H\gtrsim 120$ ($H=100$ fails) (check 7). The position statement's "large but bounded margin" is exact: the required catastrophe magnitude grows like $1/\varepsilon$. [checked]
3. Milli's obedience–autonomy trade-off is the same boundary: $R^*$ obeys iff the order is optimal for the posterior mean (Theorem 1), and blind obedience is optimal iff $H$ is rational (Theorem 3). [reported]
4. 2.13(a), "the skew belongs in the decision rule, not the prior": Milli's constructions put it exactly there — MLE-$R$'s guaranteed first-order obedience (Theorem 4) and the obedient burn-in (Remark 4) — while Theorem 1 says the correctly specified optimal $R^*$ has *no* skew. In CIRL the rationale for a decision-rule skew is misspecification doubt, not stakes: the skew is rational only relative to hypotheses $R$ does not hold. This supports the author's second formulation and 2.13(a), but grounds them in Carey/Milli rather than in $c$ versus $h$. [reported + derived]

1. R3: the inequality is Wängberg et al. (2017) Corollary 10, with $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta,c,h)=(p_u^-,p_{ar}^+,p_r^-,e_u^+,|e_u^-|)$. The position statement's 2.13(b) re-derives a 2017 result. [checked]
2. Comparison with OSG Theorem 2's condition $|\mu|\Pr(C)/\sigma^2<\mathbb E[\dot\pi^H]$: by R2 it is the Gaussian form of $\mathrm{Cov}(U_a,\pi^H(U_a))>|\mu|\Pr(C)$; by R1 that is the Total Trust pair; by R3 that is the base-rate inequality. One condition in three coordinate systems: (information term, lightning term); (below-threshold, above-threshold); $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta,c,h)$. [checked]
3. Fully updated deference as arithmetic, verified: $\mu=1$, $\tau=0.5$ fixed; as $\sigma$ falls $1\to 0.15$, $\varepsilon$ falls $0.16\to 10^{-11}$, $\alpha$ stays $\approx 0.12$–$0.13$, and $\Delta$ falls $0\to-0.118$ (check 6, first block). [checked]
4. The oversight-quality condition made exact (R6): in the Gaussian–logistic model deference survives iff $\tau<\sigma^2/(2\mu)$. In base-rate terms $\alpha$ must fall at least like $\varepsilon\beta h/c$, and since $h/c$ itself falls like $(\sigma/\mu)^2$ under Gaussian tails, $\alpha$ must fall *faster* than $\varepsilon$. Tested scalings (check 6): $\tau\propto\sigma$ fails at every $\sigma$; $\tau=2\sigma^2/\mu$ fails; $\tau=\sigma^2/(2\mu)$ is the boundary ($\Delta=0$, $h/c=1$ exactly); $\tau<\sigma^2/(2\mu)$ complies. So "the false-press rate must improve in step with the agent's accuracy" is *not enough* if accuracy is measured in standard deviations: the overseer's noise must fall with the agent's variance-to-mean ratio. [checked + derived]
5. Garber's warning: oversight quality cannot be measured by $H$'s information. Prop. 4.9: making $H$ strictly more informed can make $A$ defer strictly less; Prop. 5.7: one extra message for $A$ can move $A$ from always waiting to waiting with arbitrarily low probability. Deference is instrumental (coordination), hence non-monotone in every information variable. The position statement's $\alpha$ — the false-press rate *conditional on being asked* — is the right variable, and it is a property of the *pair* of policies, not of $H$ alone. [reported + derived]

### I14 — The basin picture: reflection for plausible futures; growth coupled to legitimacy-model improvement; calibration versus optimism. Verdict: **partial**

1. Calibration versus optimism: OSG Remark 2 and §5 — value is maximised when $\sigma$ equals the true posterior spread while $\Delta$ increases in $\sigma$; Figure 4 is the trade-off. The paper is on the side of the author's second formulation ("set things up so that the probability of legitimacy is high in fact, and correspondingly design the system to expect legitimacy") and against the first ("ok and in fact good if the baby agi overestimates it"): deference is bought by *actual* uncertainty, and an over-uncertain $R$ is "ineffective". [reported]
2. "Reflection with respect to every objectively plausible future belief": R1's vacuity conventions — the below-threshold inequality is required only on presses of positive probability; nothing is required of presses that cannot occur. [derived]
3. Growth coupled to legitimacy-model improvement: R6 is the coupling law for one model, $\tau_t<\sigma_t^2/(2\mu)$. Milli Theorem 5 exhibits one legitimacy-model parameter (temperature) that MLE-$R$ need never get right. [checked / reported]
4. The dynamics themselves — training, a growing agent, a sequence of games — are in none of the sources (OSG §7 and Garber §7 both list the sequential setting as future work). The basin is inexpressible here except as the sequence of static conditions $\Delta_t\ge 0$. [reported]
5. Sorting offered for the author's flagged sentence "Perhaps there has to be a relationship between the quality of the legitimacy-model and the quality of the feedback-model": in the game these are one object — $R$'s model of $\pi^H$ is both its legitimacy model and its feedback model — so the relationship is identity, and the substantive coupling is between $R$'s accuracy $\sigma_t$ and $H$'s reliability $\tau_t$ (R6), i.e. between the agent and the world, not between two models inside the agent. [derived; offered, unvetted]

### I15 — Trust earned via hypotheticals and other agents' shutdowns; second-channel bookkeeping; what settles. Verdict: **partial / inexpressible**

1. In the one-shot game nothing is earned: $B^R$ and the $\pi^H$-model are given. [derived]
2. Milli's repeated game says what learning does to obedience: Theorem 2, $O_n\to P(\text{$H$'s order is optimal})$. Learning $\theta$ *lowers* obedience by theorem; what is earned is knowledge of $\theta$, not trust in $H$. Trust in $H$'s reliability could be learned under a hyperprior over $\tau$ (not in the sources); Theorem 5 says MLE-$R$ does not need it and $R^*$ is sensitive to it. [reported]
3. Redundancy ends deference: Garber Prop. 4.3 — once $H$'s observations are redundant given $A$'s, an optimal pair has $A$ never defer. So what settles is *whether $H$ still knows anything about $\mathrm{sign}(X)$ that $R$ does not*; when that settles to "no", deference rationally ends. "Trust earned" must mean trust that $H$ remains non-redundant on the shutdown domain — Arbital's "attempted rescue by AI unobservability" dilemma restated inside the game. [reported + derived]
4. Feedback about hypotheticals and about other agents' shutdowns is expressible only as further observations in a richer $P_0$; no source models it. The second-channel bookkeeping of 2.13(c) is Carey's point in another form: the channel through which the reports arrive is part of the possibly misspecified model. [derived]

### I16 — Amendment 1a: what the channel must carry. Verdict: **partial** (formalized with a flaw latent)

1. In the game the press carries $\mathrm{sign}(U_a)$, and because $R$'s objective *is* $U_a$, "which plan the humans prefer" and "whether my plan selection is compromised" coincide. The 1a distinction collapses under correct specification. [derived]
2. It reappears under misspecification. Formalisation: add $M\in\{\text{ok},\text{flawed}\}$ to the state, with $B^R$ correct given ok and wrong given flawed; the press is informative about $M$ iff $P(s\mid\text{flawed})>P(s\mid\text{ok})$; the null action is posterior-optimal iff $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s]\le 0$ with the flawed branch included. But $\mathbb E[U_a\mid s,\text{flawed}]$ is computed by the possibly flawed machinery — Carey Fig. 2a. Hence 1a's requirement, that the channel carry information about the agent's *own reliability*, needs a path from press to action that the flawed cognition does not mediate: Carey's desiderata (i)–(iii), a small verified override, no incentive to remove it, a small incentive to keep it. [reported + derived]
3. Milli's $\tilde O_1$ statistic (Remark 4) is the game's one internal attempt to let the order stream carry "your $\Theta$ is wrong"; Carey's conflation example defeats it. [reported]
4. So 1a is right about what the channel must carry, and the formalism adds that carrying it *internally* is exactly what misspecification blocks. The literature's answer is architectural — a verified module — which is a fixed object in small form, the thing 2.3 said would not return. Whether the thesis can do without it is the crux (Open Problems, 1). [derived]

### I17 — Fully updated deference re-expressed. Verdict: **formalized** for the arithmetic; the thesis must **leave the game** for the rest

1. The Arbital argument's decisive option $\pi_6$ — "Avoid being shut down; then gather more evidence $E$ to update $\Delta U$ as far as possible; then optimize the updated $\Delta U\mid E$" — is OSG §7's stated limitation verbatim ("disable its off switch, learn that information, and then decide if $a$ is preferable to $s$"). [reported]
2. Inside the game with a rational $H$, $\pi_6$ has no bite: $V(w(a))=\mathbb E[\max\{U_a,0\}]$ is the full-information value, and any alternative signal $Z$ yields $\mathbb E[\max\{\mathbb E[U_a\mid Z],0\}]\le\mathbb E[\max\{U_a,0\}]$ by Jensen. Deference to a perfect overseer dominates every private investigation. [derived]
3. With a noisy $H$, the gap $\mathbb E[\max\{U_a,0\}]-\mathbb E[U_a\pi^H(U_a)]=\mathbb E[|U_a|\,P(H\text{ errs}\mid U_a)]$ is the expected loss from human error, and a sufficiently informative free $Z$ beats the button: $\pi_6$ bites exactly to the extent the overseer is delegitimized. This and the $\sigma\to 0$ collapse (I13.3) are both inside the game. [derived]
4. The thesis's sentence "a corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught" is, inside the game, the tower property $\mathbb E\big[\mathbb E[U_a\mid H\text{'s move}]\big]=\mathbb E[U_a]$ — true and beside the point. $R$ defers not because it fails to anticipate the update but because the update resolves residual uncertainty $R$ cannot resolve alone; when $\sigma\to 0$ there is nothing to resolve, and *not* deferring is correct if $B^R$ is right. Within the game FUD is not a paradox but arithmetic (Cor. 1, Thm 2, check 6), and the thesis's answer is the oversight condition of I13 — still inside the game. [derived]
5. What requires leaving the game. (a) The case Arbital actually presses — "For any prior $\Delta U$ we currently know how to formalize, the corresponding fully updated $T$ seems likely to be very far from our ideal $V$" — is Carey's misspecification: $B^R$ wrong while internally coherent, and the game's optimality theorems presuppose the correct posterior (Carey's Discussion, S6). Answering it needs $R$ to hold a hypothesis about its own model's flaw — the latent $M$ of I16 — and Arbital's "nontrivially-structured recursive belief about one's own flawedness" is that latent. The thesis's I7 says $M$ is one more coordinate of $P_0$ and the load is $P_0$'s accuracy about $M$; Carey's reply is that $P_0$'s accuracy about $M$ is what a flaw in $P_0$ destroys. (b) The sequential dynamics (I14). (c) The stakes asymmetry needs a catastrophe tail in $B^R$ (I12.2) — expressible, but not Gaussian. [derived + reported]
6. Shah–Yudkowsky record the same line: Shah concedes "a CIRL agent would not let you shut it down" after updated deference and relocates the concern to "misspecification of the reward prior, or misspecification of the P(behavior | reward)"; Soares: corrigibility "is for surviving your civilization's inability to do the job right on the first try". As framed in the position statement, the thesis sits on Shah's side of this line; the formalism's own critical literature (Carey) is the formal version of Soares's side. [reported]

### I18 — The typical-case reframing. Verdict: **formalized**

1. $\Delta$ is a continuous function of $(B^R,\pi^H)$ (R1, R2, Thm 2), and whether $\Delta\ge 0$ is a fact about the pair, not about agency. OSG Figure 3's decision boundary $\Delta=0$ in the $(\sigma,\tau)$ plane is the literal picture of 2.3's claim that anti-naturality arguments are claims about the world. [reported + derived]
2. Two structural residues survive. (i) Corollary 1 / R5: a *certain* agent defers only to a *perfect* overseer, and only the perfect overseer is legitimizing under every prior. (ii) Garber Example 4.1: under information asymmetry there exist games in which optimal play requires non-deference to a perfectly rational human in some states — an existence result, with Prop. 4.3 giving the exact condition for always-deferring to be optimal ($A$'s observations redundant given $H$'s). [reported]
3. The position statement's three-step model (2.8) has the programmers observe $a_1$ and, in effect, $\omega$ — i.e. $R$'s observations are redundant in Garber's sense (Def. 4.2), which is precisely the regime where always-deferring is optimal (Prop. 4.3). Every capability worry in 2.12–2.13 is a departure from redundancy, and Garber's paper is the formalism for that departure. [derived]
4. Verdict of the formalism on anti-naturality: deference is natural *for a value-uncertain agent, facing a legitimizing overseer, whose observations dominate the agent's own*. Each qualifier is a parameter; each incorrigibility result in the sources (Cor. 1, Carey, Ex. 4.1) is the failure of one qualifier. [derived]

## What the formalism adds

Results already in the formalism that bear on the thesis and are absent from the position statement, plus the identities derived here. Register: CLAUDE throughout; each source claim [reported] with location, each derived claim marked.

1. **The incentive condition is Total Trust** (R1, [checked]). OSG Theorems 1–2 and Wängberg Theorem 9 are all instances of $\Delta\ge 0\Leftrightarrow\{\mathbb E[U_a\mid s]\le 0,\ \mathbb E[U_a\mid\neg s]\ge 0\}$. The position statement's 2.9 filler is the incentive theorem of this literature; none of the sources states it in that form, and it is a two-line rearrangement of OSG Eq. 1. Companion: the covariance form R2, a distribution-free Theorem 2 and a graded legitimacy measure; and R5, the rational human as the unique universally legitimizing overseer.
2. **The base-rate inequality is Wängberg et al. (2017) Corollary 10** (R3, [checked]). 2.13(b) is an independent re-derivation; it should cite `wangberg-2017-a-game-theoretic-analysis-of-the-off-switch-game` §3.4. Wängberg's sign-dependent rationality probabilities $p_r^\pm$ are also the published form of 2.10's "for any random variable, it could hold for some values and fail for others".
3. **Fully updated deference is a theorem in the supervision POMDP** ([reported], `milli-2017-should-robots-be-obedient` §3): Theorem 2, obedience $O_n\to P(\text{$H$'s order is optimal})$ as the estimate becomes consistent; Remark 2, the autonomy advantage is *larger* for less rational humans; Theorem 3, blind obedience is optimal iff $H$ is rational. These bound what "trust can be earned" can mean (I15).
4. **Richness and its limits** ([reported], Milli §4–§5): Lemma 1, dominated orders are never followed (opiates/stimulants as a lemma); §5, richer $\Theta$ makes $R$ more obedient (empirical); Theorem 5, MLE-$R$ is invariant to the rationality parameter (a class of harmless legitimacy-model errors); Theorem 4 and Remark 4, decision-rule skews toward obedience that leave the prior alone — the formal home of 2.13(a).
5. **Misspecification breaks everything** ([reported], `carey-2018-incorrigibility-in-the-cirl-framework`): with a sign error in the reward parameterization all five MIRI desiderata fail for a value-learner; hard-coding restores positive value only if the button state cannot be routed around; naive indifference is brittle; Milli's detector is defeatable; and the verbatim diagnosis "if $R$ has the correct posterior over $\theta$ ... With the errors we have considered, however, this assumption is false" is the exact statement of "failures of legitimacy detection". Carey's desiderata (i)–(iii) for a small verified module are the literature's constructive answer, and they conflict with I7.
6. **Information asymmetry** ([reported], `garber-2024-the-partially-observable-off-switch-game`): (a) Ex. 4.1, optimal non-deference to a rational human; (b) Prop. 4.3, always-defer is optimal iff $A$'s observations are redundant given $H$'s — the condition the position statement's three-step model silently assumes; (c) Props. 4.9, 4.11, 5.7, 5.8, deference is non-monotone in information and communication; (d) §4.5, deferral as implicit communication — a legitimizing act by the agent; (e) Prop. 6.3(b), a sensor-tampering incentive against an $A$-unaware human — a delegitimizing act by the agent; (f) Theorem 5.4 and Corollary 5.6: more communication never lowers optimal payoff, and an $A$ that can fully communicate its observation can always defer without loss — the nearest formal object to the thesis's "help repair the shutdown button" repair incentive, valid in the unbounded-communication limit and non-monotone below it; (g) "human feedback is free" is necessary for the OSG results (Freedman and Gleave 2022, reported via Garber §3; not in the local library); (h) App. D, computing optimal policy pairs is NP-hard.
7. **Calibration, not optimism** ([reported], OSG Remark 2 and §5, Figure 4): the accurate $B^R$ maximises value; an over-uncertain $R$ is ineffective, an over-confident one incorrigible. Settles 2.12's calibration-versus-optimism question, inside the game, for the author's second formulation.
8. **The exact oversight boundary** (R6, [checked] boundary, [conjectured] monotonicity): $\tau^*=\sigma^2/(2\mu)$ in the Gaussian–logistic model, with the even-density proof; the collapse of $h/c$ under Gaussian tails (check 6); and the $1/\varepsilon$ growth of the catastrophe magnitude needed to keep deference (check 7).
9. **The debate's own terms** ([reported], `shah-yudkowsky-2022-on-alignment-failures` §20.2): Shah — CIRL is "corrigible-in-a-certain-sense" and the residual concern is misspecification; Soares — corrigibility "is for surviving your civilization's inability to do the job right on the first try"; Yudkowsky — a coherent updating agent is "antithetical in its intrinsic nature to corrigibility". The thesis, as framed, is on Shah's side of this line and must say so or move.

## Open problems and conjectures (ranked by bearing on the thesis)

1. **[conjectured] Can amendment 1a be met internally?** In Carey's Fig. 2a game, give $R$ a non-dogmatic prior over its own parameterization, $P(R^R=R_1)=0.9$, $P(R^R=R_2)=0.1$, and a correct likelihood for the order stream. Conjecture: D1–D3 are restored for the sign-error class whenever the flaw does not touch the module computing $P(o\mid R^R)$ and $\mathbb E[U\mid o,R^R]$; not restored under Carey's conflation example. Settling this decides whether the thesis needs Carey's verified module — a fixed object in small form (I16.4) — or not.
2. **[conjectured] Monotonicity in R6.** $\Delta$ is strictly decreasing in $\tau$ for Gaussian $B^R$ and logistic $\pi^H$, so $\Delta>0$ iff $\tau<\sigma^2/(2\mu)$. Confirmed numerically on five $(\mu,\sigma)$ pairs; a proof (Stein's identity plus a monotone-likelihood-ratio argument) is wanted, and the boundary for other tail shapes (which control $h/c$) should be characterised.
3. **[conjectured] Sequential coupling law.** In a repeated Gaussian–logistic game where $B^R$ sharpens both from capability and from earlier presses, does $\tau_t<\sigma_t^2/(2\mu)$ at every step keep $\Delta_t\ge 0$? The feedback's own contribution to $\sigma_t$ may need to enter the law. This is the nearest formal object to the basin picture (I14).
4. **[open] The selection worry** (position statement §4). If $R$ chooses which channel to consult (button versus a private signal $Z$, or which observation to wait on), does the Total Trust pair for the chosen channel survive the choice? Garber's non-monotonicities show the meaning of a press depends on $A$'s waiting policy; an optional-stopping-type counterexample should be constructible from Prop. 5.7's one-message collapse.
5. **[open] Non-linear aggregation.** R1's proof uses linearity of expectation twice. Under a geometric or quantile certainty equivalent $\mathrm{CE}[\cdot]$ over $U_a$, is $\mathrm{CE}[U_a\mathbf 1_{\neg s}]\ge\max\{\mathrm{CE}[U_a],\mathrm{CE}[0]\}$ still equivalent to a pair of threshold inequalities? This is where "or perhaps some other rule" (I1.4) meets the incentive theorem.
6. **[open] Legitimacy learned.** Put a hyperprior on $\tau$ (or on $p_r^\pm$) and let $R$ update it from presses whose outcomes it later observes. Does deference rise or fall with experience? Conjecture: the sign is that of the realised false-press rate, so "trust can be earned" (2.12) is a claim about the training distribution — matching the author's "if you're in fact in a trustworthy environment".
7. **[open] Redundancy as the capability variable.** Formalise capability growth as the loss of redundancy (Garber Def. 4.2) and test whether "help repair the shutdown button" is Corollary 5.6 — the agent communicating enough to make itself redundant, then deferring — and how far below unbounded communication the incentive survives (Prop. 5.7 says: not monotonically).
