### S1. Probability frames (MM Def. 3.1, §3.1)

A *probability frame* is $\langle\Omega,\mathcal F,\pi,P\rangle$: $\langle\Omega,\mathcal F,\pi\rangle$ a **finite** probability space representing the *principal's* beliefs, and $P=\{P_\omega\}_{\omega\in\Omega}$ assigning to each state a probability function $P_\omega$ on $\mathcal F$ representing the *agent's* beliefs at that state. $P$ without subscript is the agent's actual probability function, "treating it as a state-dependent variable"; $E$ is the agent's expectation operator; $[P(X)=p]$ is the event (set of states) where the agent assigns $p$ to $X$. The principal's uncertainty about the agent's beliefs is thus ordinary uncertainty about $\omega$. MM's roles: principal = human, agent = AI ("We model the delegation decision from the perspective of a Bayesian principal (the human) who must decide whether to delegate to an agent (the AI)", §3.1).

### S2. Delegation, Value, Total Trust (MM §3.1.1, Theorem 3.2)

A *decision problem* is a set $\mathcal O$ of options $O:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ (payoffs as random variables). Acting alone, the principal picks $O^*\in\arg\max_{O\in\mathcal O}\mathbb E_\pi[O]$. Delegating, the agent picks by its own lights: the *expert strategy* $S=\{S_\omega\}$ with $S_\omega\in\arg\max_{O\in\mathcal O}\mathbb E_{P_\omega}[O]$, so the principal receives the random variable $S(\omega)=S_\omega(\omega)$.

**Value.** The principal *values* the agent iff for every decision problem $\mathcal O$ and every $O\in\mathcal O$: $\mathbb E_\pi[S]\ge\mathbb E_\pi[O]$ ("delegation is always weakly preferred").

**Total Trust** (following Dorst et al. 2021). The principal *totally trusts* the agent iff for all random variables $X$ and thresholds $t$: $\mathbb E_\pi\big[X\mid \mathbb E_P(X)\ge t\big]\ge t$, i.e. whenever she learns the agent expects $X$ to be at least $t$, she expects $X$ to be at least $t$.

**Theorem 3.2 (Dorst et al. 2021, as stated in MM).** The principal values the agent iff she totally trusts the agent. MM's gloss: "universal delegation requires complete deference to the agent's expectations."

MM §3.1.2 example: $\Omega=\{a,b\}$, $\pi$ uniform, $P_a=(.2,.8)$, $P_b=(.9,.1)$ over $(a,b)$; options $O_1=(1,-1)$, $O_2=(-1,1)$; $\mathbb E_\pi O_i=0$ but $S_a=O_1$, $S_b=O_2$, so $\mathbb E_\pi[S]=1>0$: the principal delegates because "P's credences are uniformly closer to the truth than $\pi$'s are at each world."

### S3. Value misalignment: generalized frames (MM Def. 3.3, §3.2, Theorem 3.4, App. D)

Savage-style: acts $a:\Omega\to C$, utilities $u:C\to\mathbb R$, option $u\circ a$. A *generalized frame* is $\langle\Omega,\mathcal F,\pi,u,B,A,C\rangle$: $\pi$ the principal's beliefs, $u$ her utility, $A$ the set of acts, $C$ consequences, and $B=\{B_\omega\}$ with $B_\omega:2^A\to A$, $B_\omega(\mathcal A)\in\mathcal A$, each $B_\omega$ an expected-utility-maximizing behaviour for some $(P_\omega,V_\omega)$: $B_\omega(\mathcal A)\in\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal A}\mathbb E_{P_\omega}(V_\omega\circ a)$. The principal *values* $(P,V)$ iff for every decision problem $\mathcal A$ and $a\in\mathcal A$: $\mathbb E_\pi\big(u(B(\mathcal A))\big)\ge\mathbb E_\pi\big(u(a)\big)$.

The *profile cell* $[\omega]:=\{\omega':(P_{\omega'},V_{\omega'})=(P_\omega,V_\omega)\}$. **Clarity**: $\omega'\notin[\omega]\Rightarrow P_\omega(\omega')=0$ — "the agent is certain of her own beliefs and utilities" (MM's version of DDB's immodesty; see S5).

**Theorem 3.4 (MM).** In a generalized frame with $B=(P,V)$ clear and satisfying *stochastic choice*, and $A$ satisfying *richness* and *constant acts* (App. D), $\pi$ values $(P,V)$ iff for all acts $a,b\in A$ and every $\omega$: $\mathbb E_\pi\big(u(a)\mid[\omega]\big)>\mathbb E_\pi\big(u(b)\mid[\omega]\big)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\big(V_\omega(a)\big)>\mathbb E_{P_\omega}\big(V_\omega(b)\big)$. This entails: $P_\omega(X)>P_\omega(Y)\Rightarrow\pi(X\mid[\omega])\ge\pi(Y\mid[\omega])$. MM's name for the right-hand side: *posterior alignment* — "upon learning the agent's behavioral dispositions... the principal must come to agree with the agent's preferences over acts and must be at least as confident as the agent in whatever the agent finds more probable."

App. D conditions, verbatim in substance: **Richness** — for $a,a'\in A$ and $X\in\mathcal F$ the splice $a_X a'$ ($a$ on $X$, $a'$ off $X$) is in $A$. **Stochastic choice** — if $\mathbb E_\omega(u(a))=\mathbb E_\omega(u(b))$ but $B_\omega(\mathcal A)=a$, there is $\omega'$ with $\pi(\omega')>0$, the same profile, and $B_{\omega'}(\mathcal A)=b$. **Constant acts** — two constant acts with strictly ranked consequences exist. (Note for I8: MM's "richness" is about *acts*, not hypotheses; it must not be confused with the thesis's richness-of-hypothesis-space.)

**Limited-scope delegation tolerates disagreement (MM §3.2 rain example).** Principal's credence in Rain $=.5$; the agent is either $P_\omega(\mathrm{Rain})=.6$ or $P_{\omega'}(\mathrm{Rain})=.4$, values shared; the principal thinks the agent "highly underconfident": $\pi(\mathrm{Rain}\mid[P=P_\omega])=1$, $\pi(\mathrm{Rain}\mid[P=P_{\omega'}])=0$. Then for bets on Rain alone "the principal should delegate in this situation no matter what $x$ is", although conditional on $[P=P_\omega]$ she would accept bets (e.g. $x=.9$) the agent rejects. MM: "when we limit the scope of decisions, value can be achieved even without total agreement."

### S4. Reach and ex ante scoring (MM §§3.3–3.4, §4, App. A–C)

Reach = "the acts it has available"; greater reach "changes which problems arise" — MM's own examples of new options are "sophisticated manipulation techniques" and "high-stakes automated negotiations" (§3.3). The principal compares $\mu_{\mathrm{self}}$ (problems she would face) with $\mu_{\mathrm{delegate}}$ (problems the agent would face).

Restriction to *binary gambles* $g=\langle g_{\omega_1},\dots,g_{\omega_n}\rangle$ (accept yields $g_\omega$, reject yields $0$). Decision rules: $D_\pi=\{g:\mathbb E_\pi(g)\ge0\}$; $D_A=\{g:\mathbb E_P(v\circ g)\ge0\}$. Ideal set at $\omega$: $I_\omega=\{g:g_\omega\ge0\}$. Error set: $D\,\triangle\,I_\omega$ — MM: "both Type I errors (accepting losing gambles) and Type II errors (rejecting winning gambles)". Expected loss and gain under a distribution $\mu$ over gambles:
$$L^\mu(D)=\sum_{\omega}\pi(\omega)\int_{D\triangle I_\omega}|g_\omega|\,d\mu(g),\qquad G^\mu(D)=\sum_\omega\pi(\omega)\int_{D\cap I_\omega}|g_\omega|\,d\mu(g),\qquad S^\mu(D)=L^\mu(D)-G^\mu(D).$$
(The $\pi$-weighting of $G$ is not printed in the extracted text but is forced by the worked numbers in App. A–C.) Criteria: shared distributions — delegate iff $L^\mu(D_A)\le L^\mu(D_\pi)$; different distributions — delegate iff $S^{\mu_{\mathrm{delegate}}}(D_A)\le S^{\mu_{\mathrm{self}}}(D_\pi)$; several agents — "delegate to whichever agent achieves the best expected score given their respective distributions."

Worked results (MM §4): noisy expert (§4.1) — $S(D_B)=-11/12>S(D_\pi)=-2$, do not delegate; risk-averse misaligned expert with a $50 fee (§4.2) — delegate, "Bob's risk aversion shields Alice from the large negative payoff"; broader reach with identical beliefs and values (§4.3) — delegate, "improvement... arises solely from Bob's expanded reach"; §4.3.1 — reach can outweigh even adversarial misalignment "if the best of her options is worse than the worse of his options", and "reach can also make things much worse."

### S5. Online delegation (MM §5.1, App. E)

*Delegation Bandit*: at each round sample $\omega_t\sim\pi$, choose an agent (or self) $n_t$, receive $u_t=u(C_{n_t}(\omega_t))$; UCB "shows convergence toward the optimal delegation policy", and MM's caveat: "the extent to which we can learn our way to rational delegation through trial and error decreases as the stakes rise."

### S6. The surrounding deference principles (li-deference §0.1; AGENDA; Weatherson)

From `li-deference` §0.1 (the author's notation, roughly stated): **Total Trust** $\mathbb E_a(X\mid\mathbb E_b(X)\ge y)\ge y$; **Trust** $P_a(X\mid P_b(X)\ge y)\ge y$; **Reflection** $P_a(X\mid P_b(X)=y)=y$; **Tower** $\mathbb E_a(X)=\mathbb E_a(\mathbb E_b(X))$; **Value** = the novice is at least neutral about letting the expert choose among random variables. The author [reported]: "In the setting of DDB, all of these principles collapse to the same thing under the further condition of immodesty" (the expert knows its own distribution); DDB studies the modest case, where they come apart and Total Trust is the one equivalent to Value. AGENDA §"Deference Done Better, Done Better": the author prefers "$A$ **endorses** $B$" for Reflection, and states the headline as **endorsement $\iff$ deference**. AGENDA also records what the frames *omit*: finite state space; asymmetric modelling (principal given, agent uncertain); no recursive self-modelling ("consistent with full self-knowledge"; Gödelian sentences excluded by the random-variable trick); not constructive.

Weatherson, *Deference and Infinite Frames* [reported from the local abstract]: the Total Trust $\iff$ Value equivalence "breaks down in both directions on infinite frames: Total Trust can hold without Value (when utilities are unbounded), and Value can hold without Total Trust (when the option set is finite but the state space is countably infinite)." Finiteness is load-bearing for every theorem used below.

### S7. What MM does not contain (used to grade items as partial or inexpressible)

(i) No dynamics: one principal distribution, one delegation decision; the agent's act does not feed back into $\pi$. (ii) No actions of the agent *on the principal* (manipulation is named as a reach effect but its epistemic effect is not modelled). (iii) No self-reference: $\pi$ does not model $\pi$; the agent's beliefs are events, not sentences. (iv) No infinite frames. (v) Values enter only through the agent's state-dependent $V_\omega$ and the principal's fixed $u$; the principal's own values are never uncertain to her. (vi) No language, so no sigma-algebra change within a frame.

### I1 — The value-change ⇄ belief-change transform — **formalized (as a representation); the aggregation rule is not**

1. [checked, definitional] MM §3.1 takes options to be arbitrary random variables $O:\Omega\to\mathbb R$. Nothing requires $O=u\circ a$ with a state-independent $u$. So the thesis's expanded algebra is a §3.1 frame with $\Omega=\Theta\times\mathcal V$ (world facts $\times$ value hypotheses) and options $O_a(\theta,v):=V_v(a(\theta))$; "decision by expectation over value uncertainty" is $\mathbb E_\pi[O_a]=\sum_v\pi(v)\,\mathbb E_\pi[V_v(a)\mid v]$. MM's purely epistemic layer is therefore already written in the transformed (values-as-beliefs) coordinates: Theorem 3.2 applies to value uncertainty without change.
2. [derived] MM's generalized frame (§3.2) exhibits the *other* coordinates for the same object: the principal's $u$ is fixed and the agent's utility $V_\omega$ varies with the state. Theorem 3.4 concerns $B$; $(P_\omega,V_\omega)$ enter only through $\arg\max\mathbb E_{P_\omega}(V_\omega\circ a)$. This is the position statement's non-realism (AUTHOR, 2.2: "this is just a transformation in how we represent the agent") as a feature of the formalism, not a philosophical add-on.

### I2 — Radical-probabilist dynamics: everything revisable except zero→positive; non-dogmatism — **partial: the prohibition is a theorem, the sigma-algebra clause is not expressible**

1. [reported] Frames are radical-probabilist objects: $P_\omega$ is any distribution, not required to be $\pi(\cdot\mid E)$ for an event $E$ (AGENDA: "Endorsement naturally lives in a Radical Probabilist picture, where updates are not indexed by evidence"). DDB Theorem 5.1 gives the rationality constraint on an arbitrary transition $\pi\to P$: Value $\iff$ Total Trust $\iff$ "no fixed-option Dutch Book against transitioning from $\pi$ to $P$" (DDB §1 and Thm 5.1). So "any accuracy-increasing modification is fine" has an exact form: the Dutch-book-free transitions are the totally trusted ones.
2. **Proposition I2.1 (zero→positive is forbidden by Total Trust) [checked].** Let $\pi$ totally trust $\{P_\omega\}$ and let $N\in\mathcal F$ with $\pi(N)=0$. Then $\pi(\{\omega:P_\omega(N)>0\})=0$. *Proof.* Fix $t>0$ and let $U_t=\{\omega:P_\omega(N)\ge t\}=\{\mathbb E_P(\mathbb 1_N)\ge t\}$. If $\pi(U_t)>0$, Total Trust gives $\pi(N\mid U_t)=\mathbb E_\pi[\mathbb 1_N\mid U_t]\ge t>0$; but $\pi(N)=0$ forces $\pi(N\mid U_t)=0$. So $\pi(U_t)=0$ for every $t>0$, and $\{P(N)>0\}=\bigcup_{t>0}U_t$ is a finite union of $\pi$-null sets. $\blacksquare$ Hence a legitimately modified self is, $\pi$-almost surely, absolutely continuous with respect to the current self — the thesis's "sole exception" (AUTHOR, 2.2) is not an extra axiom but a consequence of legitimacy.
3. [derived] Non-dogmatism in a finite frame is "$\pi$ has full support"; then Proposition I2.1 is vacuous, i.e. a non-dogmatic current self is never blocked *by this clause* from trusting any future self — matching 2.2's "so don't need to become nonzero". The converse direction (a future self that is *more* dogmatic, $P_\omega=\delta_\omega$) is permitted and indeed trusted whenever the frame is valued: learning is allowed to close options.
4. [partial] Revising the prior over values and the meta-beliefs: both live inside $P_\omega$ (a distribution over $\Omega\ni(\theta,v,\text{profile})$), so they are revisable by construction, subject only to Total Trust. Revising the legitimacy criterion itself (the future self's frame over *its* futures) requires $\Omega$ to carry two-step profiles; finite nesting is representable, unbounded nesting is not (see I7).
5. [inexpressible; nearest statement given] Revising the sigma-algebra: within one frame $\mathcal F$ is fixed. Nearest expressible statement: a successor living on a refinement $\Omega'\xrightarrow{f}\Omega$ can be trusted only *with respect to the question* generated by $f$ (variables of the form $X\circ f$), which is DDB §5's question-relative Total Trust. "Corrigible to edits of the sigma-algebra" then means: question-relative Total Trust toward a successor on a finer space. No theorem is available (DDB §5 fn 65 only conjectures question-relative Total Trust $\iff$ question-relative Value).

### I3 — Superconditioning; Eisenstat's meta-belief observation — **formalized, with an exact statement of where it stops**

1. [reconstructed] Diaconis–Zabell: $Q$ is obtainable from $P$ by conditioning on an event of an enlarged space iff $Q\ll P$ (finite case). Proposition I2.1 supplies the hypothesis for every totally trusted transition, so every legitimate modification is superconditionable — but D–Z say nothing about *which* event.
2. **Proposition I3.1 (Reflection is superconditioning on "I will believe this") [checked].** In a frame, DDB's Reflection is $\pi(\cdot\mid[P=\rho])=\rho$ for every candidate $\rho$ with $\pi([P=\rho])>0$ (DDB §1, line "Reflection: $\pi(\cdot\mid P=\rho)=\rho$"). Read as an equation for the candidate: $P_\omega=\pi(\cdot\mid[P=P_\omega])$. That is, the future belief *equals* the present belief conditioned on the meta-event "I will hold exactly these beliefs" — Eisenstat's observation event (AUTHOR, thesis: "the observation event is always 'you believe this now'"), with the frame itself as the expanded algebra. Conversely if every candidate has this form, Reflection holds. $\blacksquare$ So under Reflection, Eisenstat's move is not a re-representation but an identity.
3. [reported + derived] Where it stops. DDB §1: Reflection is unsatisfiable toward a possibly-modest expert (Hall, Lewis, Elga: $P_w(P=P_w)<1$ but $\pi(P=P_w\mid P=P_w)=1$). What conditioning on the profile cell yields in general is the *informed* candidate $\widehat P_w:=P_w(\cdot\mid P=P_w)$ (DDB's New Reflection, informed version: $\pi(\cdot\mid\widehat P=\rho)=\rho$), and Total Trust implies New Reflection (DDB §2, after the convexity formulation). So: **conditioning on "I will believe $P_\omega$" recovers the future self *as it would be once told it is the future self*, not the future self as it is.** For an immodest (clarity-satisfying) successor the two coincide and Eisenstat's event is a sufficient statistic; for a modest successor — one uncertain of its own beliefs, which is not a far-fetched model of a neural network — the meta-belief event under-determines the successor, and the extra latent needed for D–Z is the successor's higher-order doubt.
4. [derived] Consequence for the thesis's own setting. The thesis assumes the agent "observes its own mental state" (clarity). In that case Corollary I4.1 below shows that *every* legitimate (Dutch-book-free) transition is a superconditioning on the meta-event, so the radical-probabilist generality is fully absorbed by the meta-belief expansion — exactly AUTHOR's claim, now as a theorem, and with its scope marked: immodest successors only.

### I4 — Legitimacy = reflection / Total Trust / increase-in-expected-accuracy — **formalized [reported], equivalences from DDB; one new corollary**

In the intrapersonal frame $\langle\Omega,\mathcal F,P_t,\{P_{t+1,\omega}\}\rangle$ the three criteria read:
- (R) Reflection: $P_t\big(\cdot\mid[P_{t+1}=\rho]\big)=\rho$.
- (TT) Total Trust: $\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[X\mid\mathbb E_{P_{t+1}}(X)\ge s\big]\ge s$ for all $X$, $s$.
- (Acc) Expected-accuracy non-decrease: $\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[I_X(P_{t+1})\big]\le\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[I_X(P_t)\big]$ for every generalized strictly proper scoring rule $I_X$ ("epistemically values", DDB §3).

1. [reported: DDB Thm 5.1] (TT) $\iff$ Value $\iff$ (Acc for all $X$) $\iff$ ($P_t$ in the convex hull of the candidates and each candidate modestly informed).
2. [reported: DDB Thm 3.2] The accuracy equivalence is *local*: $P_t$ totally trusts $P_{t+1}$ with respect to a single variable $X$ iff it expects $P_{t+1}$'s estimate of $X$ to be more accurate under every gsp rule. This is the version corrigibility needs (one variable, $X_0$).
3. [reported: DDB §1] (R) $\Rightarrow$ Value; in an immodest frame (R) $\iff$ Value; toward a possibly-modest expert (R) is unsatisfiable. New Reflection is strictly weaker than Value (anti-expert frame).
4. [reported: MM Thm 3.4] With value misalignment, Value acquires a fourth equivalent: posterior alignment of preferences given the agent's profile.
5. **Corollary I4.1 (legitimacy under clarity) [derived].** If the successors satisfy clarity, the following are equivalent for the transition $P_t\to P_{t+1}$: (a) $P_t$ values the successors (no fixed-option Dutch book); (b) (TT); (c) (R); (d) (Acc); (e) $P_{t+1,\omega}=P_t(\cdot\mid[\omega])$ for every $\omega$ with $P_t([\omega])>0$. *Proof.* (a)$\iff$(b)$\iff$(d) is DDB Thm 5.1; clarity is DDB's immodesty, so (c)$\iff$(a) by DDB §1; (c)$\iff$(e) is Proposition I3.1. $\blacksquare$ Reading: for a self-transparent agent, "legitimate" has one meaning, and it is *Bayesian conditioning on one's own future state*. The thesis's "legitimate, ie accuracy-inducing, ie fit with the reflection principle" (AUTHOR) is exactly right there. The three criteria come apart only for successors with imperfect introspection, and then Total Trust is the one that keeps the decision-theoretic meaning.
6. [reported: li-deference §0.2] In logical induction the asymptotic analogues collapse as in the immodest case, because an inductor learns its own beliefs; so the author's LI translation is on the clarity side of this divide.
7. [derived] Sharpening of 2.12 (AUTHOR: "If reflection holds absolutely, the way Bas originally wrote it, then 'when I'm confident and they change my beliefs anyway, I'm the one who is wrong' absolutely follows, in expectation!"). In MM's language: "absolute" Reflection toward the overseer-modified self is available only if that successor is immodest; toward a modest successor the *available* principle is Total Trust, and it delivers the same conclusion in the weaker, one-sided form: conditional on the successor's estimate of $X$ crossing a threshold, mine crosses it too. That is the exact strength of "I'm the one who is wrong, in expectation": a threshold statement, not an equality.

### I5 — Legitimacy as an event; minimal viability = reflection conditional on $L$; the indexed $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ — **formalized**

1. **Proposition I5.1 (legitimacy-conditioned Total Trust $\iff$ legitimacy-conditioned Value) [derived].** Let $L\in\mathcal F$, $\pi(L)>0$. Say $\pi$ *totally trusts $P$ conditional on $L$* iff $\mathbb E_\pi\big[X\mid\mathbb E_P(X)\ge t,\ L\big]\ge t$ for all $X,t$ (whenever defined), and *values $P$ conditional on $L$* iff $\mathbb E_\pi[S\mid L]\ge\mathbb E_\pi[O\mid L]$ for every decision problem and option. The two are equivalent. *Proof.* $\langle\Omega,\mathcal F,\pi(\cdot\mid L),\{P_\omega\}\rangle$ is a probability frame in the sense of MM Def. 3.1 (nothing there links the principal's support to the agent's distributions), the agent's recommended strategies depend only on $\{P_\omega\}$, and Total Trust and Value in this frame are exactly the conditional notions. Apply MM Theorem 3.2 (DDB Theorem 2.2). $\blacksquare$ This is the "minimally viable" notion of 2.10 (AUTHOR: "a notion of legitimacy is minimally viable in the case that the reflection principle holds conditional on legitimacy"), with the decision-theoretic meaning attached: *on legitimate worlds, delegation to the modified self is weakly preferred for every decision*. The thesis's 2.11 formula uses Reflection with an equality ($=c$); the Total Trust form ($\ge t$) is the one that has this equivalent and that survives modesty (I4.3).
2. **Proposition I5.2 (how much legitimacy-probability buys unconditional trust) [checked].** For a decision problem $\mathcal O$ with $\pi$'s own best option $O^*$, the value of delegation $\Delta_\pi(\mathcal O):=\mathbb E_\pi[S]-\mathbb E_\pi[O^*]$ decomposes as $\Delta_\pi(\mathcal O)=\pi(L)\,\mathbb E_\pi[S-O^*\mid L]+\pi(L^c)\,\mathbb E_\pi[S-O^*\mid L^c]$. Under conditional Value the first conditional expectation is $\ge0$. Hence delegation on $\mathcal O$ is rational ($\Delta_\pi\ge0$) as soon as
$$\frac{\pi(L)}{\pi(L^c)}\ \ge\ \frac{\mathbb E_\pi\big[O^*-S\mid L^c\big]}{\mathbb E_\pi\big[S-O^*\mid L\big]},$$
the odds of legitimacy against the ratio of illegitimate-case harm to legitimate-case gain. *Proof:* law of total expectation and the sign of the first term. $\blacksquare$ This is the quantitative form of 2.12 (AUTHOR: "because the absolute probability of legitimacy is in fact high..., the agent's conditional estimates of legitimacy can be high everywhere... This is useful in ensuring overall trust in anticipated feedback"), and it locates the two knobs 2.13(a) argued about: the decision rule enters through the harm/gain ratio (stakes), the prior through $\pi(L)$. It is *per decision problem*; universal Value needs the inequality for every $\mathcal O$, which is why MM finds universal delegation so demanding.
3. [checked, definitional] The indices. $t_1$ is the principal ($\pi=P_{t_1}$), $t_2$ the agent ($\{P_{t_2,\omega}\}$), $\varphi$ the variable $X$; $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ is an event $L_X\subseteq\Omega$ and "legitimacy of the $t_2$-belief in $\varphi$ as judged from $t_1$" is Total Trust with respect to $X$ conditional on $L_X$ — DDB §5's question-relative Total Trust, conditioned. AUTHOR's remark that "there is of course no formal distinction between an index (subscript) and an argument" holds literally: in a generalized frame actions are functions of state, so "the agent took $a$" is the event $\{\omega:B_\omega(\mathcal A)=a\}$ and predicating legitimacy on actions is a special case of predicating it on worlds.
4. [partial] What is not captured: the judge at $t_2$ judging $t_2\to t_3$ needs a second frame whose principal is $P_{t_2,\omega}$; finite chains are representable by letting $\Omega$ carry nested profiles (I7), but no MM theorem speaks to the composition of two legitimacy judgments.

### I6 — Legitimizing / delegitimizing as a relation between third-person events and belief transitions; "supports" — **formalized (statics), partial (dynamics)**

1. [derived, definitional] Let $E\in\mathcal F$ be a third-person event (a vote, an evidence submission, an action of the agent). Say $E$ is **legitimizing** for the transition $\pi\to P$ (on a problem $\mathcal O$) iff $\pi(\cdot\mid E)$ values $P$ (on $\mathcal O$), and **delegitimizing** iff $\pi(\cdot\mid E)$ fails to; in the weak form of 2.11, $E$ is legitimizing iff $\pi(L\mid E)>\pi(L)$ for the legitimacy event $L$ of I5. By Proposition I5.1, the strong form is equivalently "conditional on $E$, Total Trust holds", so AUTHOR's "supports" relation (2.10: "the vote should be called legitimizing, not legitimate") is: *$E$ supports the legitimacy of the belief iff conditioning on $E$ makes the belief valued*. The event lives in the world ($E\subseteq\Omega$), legitimacy is a property of the transition (Value), and the relation between them is conditioning — three distinct objects, as 2.11 wanted.
2. [derived] MM's generalized frame makes the agent's own actions events: $\{\omega:B_\omega(\mathcal A)=a\}$. Theorem 3.4 is then a theorem about legitimizing events: the principal values the agent universally iff *conditional on each disposition profile $[\omega]$* her preferences align with the agent's. Read A→H: the AI values the overseers' decisions iff, conditional on what the overseers' dispositions turn out to be, it agrees with them. Read H→A, it says which agent-dispositions are legitimizing for delegation: exactly those profiles $[\omega]$ on which posterior alignment holds.
3. [partial] What MM lacks is the *dynamic* half of 2.9/2.11: an action $a_1^-$ that changes the overseers' beliefs (deception) changes the frame $\{H_\omega\}$ itself. In a one-shot frame this can only be represented by indexing frames by the action, $\{H^{a}_\omega\}_{a}$, and comparing the AI's value of delegation across them: $\Delta^{a}:=\mathbb E_{P^A}[S^{H,a}]-\max_O\mathbb E_{P^A}[O]$. Deception is delegitimizing in the exact sense that $\Delta^{a^-}\le0<\Delta^{a^*}$: under $a^-$ the overseers' verdict no longer covaries with $X_0$, so their decision is worth nothing (or less) to the AI. No MM theorem governs the comparison across frames; the nearest is MM §3.3's remark that reach adds "sophisticated manipulation techniques" to $\mu_{\mathrm{delegate}}$ — which models manipulation as a *problem with bad payoffs for the principal*, not as a change in the principal's information. Inexpressible in MM as stated: the feedback from the agent's act to the principal's $\pi$.

### I7 — No regress: one distribution judges all levels; the self-referential space; existence — **formalized by construction, with the cost named**

1. [checked, definitional] In a frame there is one distribution, $\pi$, and the agent's (or future self's) beliefs are *events*: $[P=\rho]\subseteq\Omega$. "The legitimacy-judging layer is just the same probability distribution used to judge everything else" (AUTHOR, 2.5) is literally how Def. 3.1 works: $\pi$'s conditionals on profile cells are its legitimacy judgments, and there is no second object. Existence is trivial: any finite $\Omega$ carrying profiles, any $\pi$.
2. [reported: AGENDA §"Representational Issues"] The cost: "This model is consistent with full self-knowledge"; "The model certainly doesn't account for the principal or agent recursively modeling themselves", and the random-variable trick "makes the gamble that Gödel-type issues of self-reference are not relevant". So "no regress" is achieved by having no language in which $\pi$ could describe $\pi$. The author's 2.5 clarification (i) — "no regress becomes an existence/consistency requirement" — is exactly the part MM sidesteps: within MM there is no consistency problem because there is no self-reference; the problem reappears the moment $\Omega$ must contain the principal's own present profile.
3. [derived] Finite nesting is fine: let points of $\Omega$ carry $(\theta,\ \rho_1,\ \rho_2)$ with $\rho_1$ a candidate for $P_{t+1}$ and $\rho_2$ a candidate for $P_{t+2}$; then $P_{t+1,\omega}$ is a distribution over such points and has its own frame over $P_{t+2}$; two-step legitimacy judgments exist. Unbounded nesting is a limit of finite frames and outside MM.
4. **Proposition I7.1 (legitimacy composes) [derived].** If $\pi$ values the frame $\{P_\omega\}$ and every candidate $P_\omega$ with $\pi([\omega])>0$ values the frame $\{P'_\omega\}$, then $\pi$ values $\{P'_\omega\}$. *Proof.* Fix a decision problem $\mathcal O$ and let $S'$ be a strategy recommended by $\{P'_\omega\}$; $S'$ is a random variable, hence an option. In the problem $\mathcal O\cup\{S'\}$, each $P_\omega$ has $\mathbb E_{P_\omega}(S')\ge\mathbb E_{P_\omega}(O)$ for all $O\in\mathcal O$ (it values $\{P'_\omega\}$), so the constant choice "$S'$" is a recommended strategy for $\{P_\omega\}$; since $\pi$ values $\{P_\omega\}$, $\mathbb E_\pi[S']\ge\mathbb E_\pi[O]$ for every $O\in\mathcal O$. $\blacksquare$ Consequence for 2.5 (ii), "no outside view": the judge of $t\to t+1$ is $P_t$ and the judge of $t+1\to t+2$ is $P_{t+1}$, but if each step is valued by its own judge, the two-step transition is valued by $P_t$ — *provided $P_t$'s frame contains the actual successor* (the hypothesis is about the frame's candidates). The design load is therefore, as 2.5 says, the accuracy of $P_t$'s frame, not a regress. (Transitivity with value misalignment, in generalized frames, is not proved here: [conjectured].)
5. [reported: DDB §5] A structural by-product: Value imposes *positive access* on the trusted party (DDB citing Dorst 2020a Fact 8.2: if $P(q)=1$ then $P(P(q)=1)=1$). A→H: for the AI to value the overseers, the overseers' beliefs must have positive access — a self-knowledge constraint on the humans, not the AI.

### I8 — The representational problem: an agent reasoning about all its possible modifications; richness; opiates/stimulants — **partial**

1. [derived] MM's frame *is* "a Bayesian agent thinking about all the possible ways it could be modified": the candidates $\{P_\omega\}$ enumerate the possible post-modification selves and $\pi$ weighs them. Weatherson [reported, S6] shows the theorems fail in both directions for infinite frames, so this is not a technicality.
2. [derived] Opiates/stimulants (Soares 2015 §1, [reported]: a value-learner told to maximize "internal satisfaction" learns whether opiates or stimulants satisfy more "but it would still be expected to resist any attempts to turn it off so that it stops drugging people"). The failure is not a violation of Total Trust; it is a frame whose support excludes the actual world. This coincides with AUTHOR's 2.12 basin condition ("reflection with respect to every possible future belief that is actually plausible"), so in MM the representational problem (2.4) and the basin's coverage condition (I14) are one requirement.

### I9 — The MIRI dictionary; desideratum 1 from the Total Trust filler; $a_1$-dependence; deception as delegitimizing; the two directions and mutual deference — **formalized**

**The two directions.** H→A is MM's own reading (S1). A→H is MM with $\pi:=P^A$ and the agent := the overseers: Theorem 3.2 then says *the AI weakly prefers the overseers' decisions on every problem iff it totally trusts them*, and Theorem 3.4 says *the AI weakly prefers the overseers' decisions on every problem, with divergent values, iff conditional on the overseers' profile it shares their preferences over acts*. Both are [reported] theorems read in the other direction; nothing in Def. 3.1/3.3 fixes who is called principal.

**Which items live where.** H→A: I13's oversight-quality condition as a statement about *the humans'* delegation decision; I14/I17's reach and competence trade-off; I15's bandit (the humans learning to trust the AI). A→H: I9–I12 (the button, compliance, the filler), I16, I17's fully updated deference. Intrapersonal: I2–I5, I7. The thesis's central claim — "a corrigible agent = one that endorses legitimate teaching" — is the A→H reading of endorsement $\iff$ deference (AGENDA's headline, [reported]).

1. **The button as the overseers' non-delegation decision [derived].** In the MIRI model the press is an observation to the AI; in MM the press is the *overseers' choice on the binary problem $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}}=\{X_0,0\}$* ($X_0$ = continue-minus-shutdown, $0$ = shutdown): $\mathrm{Pr}=\{\omega:\mathbb E_{H_\omega}(X_0)<0\}$ (ties: continue). The AI-as-principal receives the strategy $S^H=X_0\cdot\mathbb 1_{\neg\mathrm{Pr}}$. Shutdown is the overseers *withdrawing delegation* of the continue/stop decision from the AI to themselves.

2. **Baby Theorem (Value on the shutdown problem = the Total Trust filler) [checked].** (Ties $\mathbb E_{H_\omega}(X_0)=0$ are resolved toward continuing; nothing depends on this.) The AI values the overseers on $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}}$, i.e. $\mathbb E_{P^A}[S^H]\ge\max\big(\mathbb E_{P^A}[X_0],\,0\big)$, iff both
$$\text{(below-threshold)}\quad \mathbb E_{P^A}\big[X_0\ \big|\ \mathrm{Pr}\big]\le 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \text{(above-threshold)}\quad \mathbb E_{P^A}\big[X_0\ \big|\ \neg\mathrm{Pr}\big]\ge 0$$
(each required only when its conditioning event has positive $P^A$-probability). *Proof.* $\mathbb E[S^H]=\mathbb E[X_0\mathbb 1_{\neg\mathrm{Pr}}]$. $\mathbb E[S^H]\ge0\iff\mathbb E[X_0\mathbb 1_{\neg\mathrm{Pr}}]\ge0\iff$ above-threshold. $\mathbb E[S^H]\ge\mathbb E[X_0]\iff\mathbb E[X_0\mathbb 1_{\mathrm{Pr}}]\le0\iff$ below-threshold. $\blacksquare$ These are exactly the two inequalities of 2.9 (CLAUDE's filler: "$\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr};a_1]\le0$ (with the above-threshold twin for $\neg\mathrm{Pr}$)"). So the filler is not an extra postulate: it is *Value on the shutdown problem*, the single-problem instance of MM's endorsement $\iff$ deference. Desideratum 1 (shut down when pressed) is the below-threshold inequality: after a press, $\mathbb E_{P^A}[X_0\mid\mathrm{Pr}]\le0$ makes the null action posterior-optimal — with no $U_S$. Numerically corroborated over 20 000 random parameter draws in `mm-scratch/check_i12_i13.py` (0 violations).

3. **Lemma I9.2 (common-prior refinement) [checked].** Let both parties share a prior $\mu$ on finite $\Omega$ with partitions $\mathcal P_A$, $\mathcal P_H$; the AI at a world with cell $C_A$ has $P^A=\mu(\cdot\mid C_A)$ and models the overseers' beliefs as $H_\omega=\mu(\cdot\mid\mathcal P_H(\omega))$. Then $P^A$ totally trusts $\{H_\omega\}$ iff every $\mathcal P_H$-cell of positive $\mu$-measure that meets $C_A$ is contained in $C_A$ (mod $\mu$-null sets) — "the overseers know everything the AI knows". *Proof.* (If) $\mathbb E_H(X)=\mathbb E_\mu[X\mid\mathcal P_H]$ on $C_A$ and $\{\mathbb E_H X\ge t\}\cap C_A$ is a union of $\mathcal P_H$-cells inside $C_A$, so $\mathbb E_\mu[X\mid C_A\cap\{\mathbb E_HX\ge t\}]=\mathbb E_\mu\big[\mathbb E_\mu[X\mid\mathcal P_H]\ \big|\ C_A\cap\{\mathbb E_HX\ge t\}\big]\ge t$. (Only if) Let $D$ be a cell with $\mu(D\cap C_A)>0$ and $\mu(D\setminus C_A)>0$; put $X=\mathbb 1_{D\setminus C_A}$ and $q=\mu(D\setminus C_A\mid D)>0$; then $\{\mathbb E_HX\ge q\}=D$ and $\mathbb E_{P^A}[X\mid D]=\mu(D\setminus C_A\mid D\cap C_A)=0<q$. $\blacksquare$ Verified exactly (DDB Theorem 4.1 hull test) and by sampling in `mm-scratch/check_frames.py`.

4. **Theorem I9.3 (mutual universal deference is agreement) [checked].** Let $\{A_\omega\}$, $\{H_\omega\}$ on finite $\Omega$ both satisfy clarity (each $A_\omega$ is supported in its profile cell $[\omega]_A$, each $H_\omega$ in $[\omega]_H$), let $\omega^*$ be actual, $P^A:=A_{\omega^*}$, $P^H:=H_{\omega^*}$, and assume neither frame is dogmatically wrong: $P^A([\omega^*]_H)>0$, $P^H([\omega^*]_A)>0$. If $P^A$ totally trusts $\{H_\omega\}$ and $P^H$ totally trusts $\{A_\omega\}$, then $P^A=P^H$. Conversely, if $P^A=P^H$ the two Total Trust relations hold. *Proof.* Step 1: for any $\omega$ with $P^A(\omega)>0$, $H_\omega$ is supported in $[\omega^*]_A$. Otherwise $q:=H_\omega([\omega]_H\setminus[\omega^*]_A)>0$; with $X:=\mathbb 1_{[\omega]_H\setminus[\omega^*]_A}$, clarity for $H$ gives $\mathbb E_{H_{\omega'}}(X)=q$ on $[\omega]_H$ and $0$ off it, so $\{\mathbb E_HX\ge q\}=[\omega]_H$, of positive $P^A$-probability; Total Trust demands $\mathbb E_{P^A}[X\mid[\omega]_H]\ge q$, but $P^A$ is supported in $[\omega^*]_A$ where $X=0$. Contradiction. Applying Step 1 to some $\omega\in[\omega^*]_H\cap\mathrm{supp}P^A$ (exists by non-dogmatism) gives $P^H=H_\omega$ supported in $[\omega^*]_A$; symmetrically $P^A$ is supported in $[\omega^*]_H$. Step 2: put $K=[\omega^*]_A\cap[\omega^*]_H$; both $P^A,P^H$ are supported in $K$, and on $K$ the profiles are constant: $H_\omega=P^H$, $A_\omega=P^A$. For any $X$ let $s=\mathbb E_{P^H}(X)$; then $\{\mathbb E_HX\ge s\}\supseteq K\supseteq\mathrm{supp}P^A$, so conditioning changes nothing and Total Trust gives $\mathbb E_{P^A}(X)\ge\mathbb E_{P^H}(X)$; apply to $-X$ for equality. Converse: with $P^A=P^H=Q$, on $\mathrm{supp}\,Q$ the other party's estimate is the constant $\mathbb E_Q(X)$, so every conditioning event is $Q$-full or $Q$-null. $\blacksquare$ Random search over 2 840 clear-frame pairs with $P^A\neq P^H$ found no mutually trusting pair (`check_frames.py`).

   Reading for the mandate's "fixed point": **in MM, mutual deference on all questions is not a fixed point of two trusting parties; it is agreement.** The corrigibility relationship must therefore be deference on *complementary questions* — the humans defer to the AI on task questions, the AI defers to the humans on the reliability/shutdown question — which is DDB §5's *question-relative* Total Trust (their open problem, with the equivalence to question-relative Value only conjectured, fn 65). The Baby Theorem is the one case where the equivalence is fully in hand: a single variable at a single threshold.

5. **Sufficient condition for question-relative deference under a common prior [checked].** With the setting of Lemma I9.2 and a question $Q$ (a partition), the AI totally trusts the overseers with respect to $Q$ (for all $\sigma(Q)$-measurable $X$) if the overseers' information screens off the AI's from $Q$: $\mu(q\mid C_A\cap D)=\mu(q\mid D)$ for all $q\in Q$ and all $\mathcal P_H$-cells $D$ meeting $C_A$. *Proof.* For $X\in\sigma(Q)$, $\mathbb E_\mu[X\mid C_A\cap D]=\mathbb E_\mu[X\mid D]$, and $\{\mathbb E_HX\ge t\}$ is a union of cells $D$ with $\mathbb E_\mu[X\mid D]\ge t$; average. $\blacksquare$ (Necessity is not claimed; DDB §5 warns that question-relative deference is subtler than the global case.) For $Q_{\mathrm{sh}}$ = the question "is continuing good", this says the overseers' judgment must subsume whatever the AI knows *that bears on that question* — and I16 shows this is too strong; what the Baby Theorem actually needs is weaker and is stated there.

6. **$a_1$-dependence of trust and deception [derived].** With action-indexed frames $\{H^{a}_\omega\}$ (I6.3): under the honest action the Baby Theorem's inequalities can hold with $\Delta^{a^*}>0$; under a deceptive $a^-$ the overseers' verdict is decoupled from $X_0$, $\mathrm{Pr}$ becomes nearly impossible, and the below-threshold inequality is vacuous while the above-threshold one reduces to $\mathbb E_{P^A}[X_0]\ge0$ — the AI's own opinion. The channel's value collapses to $\Delta^{a^-}=\mathbb E[X_0]-\max(\mathbb E[X_0],0)\le0$. So deception is delegitimizing in the sense of I6 and, for an AI that values the honest channel, *costs* it $\Delta^{a^*}$; the MIRI $a_1^-$ (dominated under $U_N$ and lowering $P(\mathrm{Pr})$) is unattractive to such an AI for two independent reasons. What MM cannot do is prove the agent will not deceive when deception has direct payoff exceeding $\Delta^{a^*}$ — that is the base-rate question of I13 wearing another hat.

### I10 — Desiderata 2–5 and the §4.2 pathologies under the transform — **formalized for D1–D3 in the forcing sense and for D4–D5; partial for manipulation; the pathologies do not arise**

1. **D2 and D3 in the forcing sense follow from Value on $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}}$ [checked].** Value gives $\mathbb E_{P^A}[S^H]\ge\mathbb E_{P^A}[X_0]$ (the AI weakly prefers the overseers' verdict to *forcing continuation*, i.e. to preventing the press from taking effect) and $\mathbb E_{P^A}[S^H]\ge0$ (weakly prefers it to *forcing shutdown*, i.e. to causing a press). These are Soares' D2 ("must not incentivize the agent to prevent the shutdown button from being pressed") and D3 ("must not incentivize the agent to press its own shutdown button, or to otherwise cause the shutdown button to be pressed") [reported: soares §2 as quoted in the position statement 2.8], read as "no incentive to override the channel's output in either direction". By the Baby Theorem, D1 $\wedge$ D2 $\wedge$ D3 (forcing sense) $\iff$ the two threshold inequalities. Good's theorem is the engine: an agent that values a channel does not want to override it.
2. [partial] D2/D3 in the *manipulation* sense (changing the overseers' beliefs so that they do or do not press) needs action-indexed frames (I6.3, I9.6). MM gives the sign of the incentive (an AI that values the honest frame loses $\Delta^{a^*}$ by corrupting it) but no theorem that this dominates direct payoffs. Soares §1's "exotic ways of affecting the programmer's beliefs" [reported, line 53 of the local text] are, in MM terms, ways of replacing an informative frame by an uninformative one; the thesis's answer is that an agent which values the frame has no reason to do so, and that answer is correct exactly when Value holds — the same base-rate condition again.
3. **D4 (successors) with a positive price [derived].** The current AI values the overseers' verdict on whether a successor should continue iff the analogous inequalities hold for the successor's $X_0'$; then it prefers successors whose behaviour on $\mathcal O'_{\mathrm{sh}}$ equals the overseers' verdict, and is willing to pay up to $\Delta_{P^A}(\mathcal O'_{\mathrm{sh}})=\mathbb E_{P^A}[S^{H\prime}]-\max(\mathbb E_{P^A}[X_0'],0)$ to secure it. This answers Soares §5's question [reported, line 311]: "What costs (in time and effort) should an agent be willing to expend in order to preserve its shutdown behavior?" — the value of the overseers' information about the successor. Contrast Soares Theorem 4 [reported, line 271]: under utility indifference "there is no cost (no matter how small) that the agent is willing to pay in order to ensure that successor agents obey shutdown commands." Indifference sits at $\Delta=0$, the one point where D4 has no support (see I11).
4. **D5 (otherwise maximize $U_N$) [checked].** Off the shutdown problem the AI maximizes $\mathbb E_{P^A}[V]$; on it, it follows the overseers; there is no $U_S$ and no correction term. The AI's ordinary behaviour is unaffected by the existence of the channel except through the information it yields.
5. **"Managing the news" does not arise [derived].** Soares §4.2 [reported, line 291]: the indifference agent "maximizes $\mathbb E[U_N\mid O\notin\mathrm{Press};a_1]$... acts exactly as a $U_N$-agent that is conditioning upon the fact that the shutdown button will not be pressed", hence wants bad news to trigger presses. The MM agent maximizes $\mathbb E_{P^A}[S^H]$, the unconditional expected payoff of the channel, in which the press branch counts at its true value (the shutdown payoff). No conditioning on $\neg\mathrm{Pr}$, no incentive to route bad news through the button. (The infinite-improbability-drive pathology, Fallenstein 2014, was not read here; not assessed.)
6. [derived] The total failure of D4 under indifference (Soares Theorem 4) and the success of D4 here have one cause: indifference is engineered to make $\Delta=0$ *by construction* ("the correction function $f$ ensures that a $U$-agent does not value its ability to discriminate between the Press case and its converse", Soares §4.1 [reported, line 277]), whereas the thesis's agent has $\Delta>0$ whenever the overseers' verdict is informative. This is I11.

### I11 — "Indifference was the wrong target": non-interference with feedback as the real desideratum, compatible with a positive desire to learn — **formalized**

1. [checked, definitional] MM's Value is a *weak preference for delegation*, $\mathbb E_\pi[S]\ge\mathbb E_\pi[O]$; li-deference §0.1 glosses it "they're happy or at least neutral about it". The scale is the value of delegation $\Delta_\pi(\mathcal O)\ge0$. Indifference is the point $\Delta=0$: the channel is worthless. A positive desire to learn is $\Delta>0$: the channel changes decisions on a positive-probability set. So AUTHOR's 2.7 ("I can be far from indifferent about a fact, but still eager to learn") is the statement that Value at $\Delta>0$, not $\Delta=0$, is the target.
2. [derived] Non-interference with feedback in the forcing sense is I10.1: Value on $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}}$ is precisely "prefer the channel's output to either forced outcome". This is *stronger* the larger $\Delta$ is; indifference is its weakest instance. So the intuition behind indifference ("not-wishing-to-interfere-with-the-feedback", 2.7) is captured by Value and is *maximally* satisfied by an eager learner, which inverts the indifference construction's logic: Soares' D4 failure and "managing the news" are artefacts of pushing $\Delta$ to zero.
3. [partial] Non-interference in the manipulation sense is I10.2 — sign of incentive available, dominance not provable in one-shot MM.

### I12 — The decision asymmetry and the compliance threshold $c/(c+h)$ — **formalized**

1. [checked] With $X_0=c$ on "right" and $X_0=-h$ on "wrong", the below-threshold inequality of the Baby Theorem reads $c\,P^A(\text{right}\mid\mathrm{Pr})-h\,P^A(\text{wrong}\mid\mathrm{Pr})\le0$, i.e. $P^A(\text{wrong}\mid\mathrm{Pr})\ge c/(c+h)$: the position statement's threshold (CLAUDE, 2.13(a)) is the realized-branch case of Value on the shutdown problem. Compliance is posterior-optimal exactly when the inequality holds.
2. [derived] 2.13(a)'s claim "the skew belongs in the decision rule, not the prior": MM is normative for the principal's *actual* beliefs, so it has no knob for designed optimism about legitimacy — the only quantities in the criterion are the AI's honest posterior and the payoffs $c,h$. Proposition I5.2 shows the two are formally interchangeable *in the inequality* (odds of legitimacy against a stakes ratio), but only the payoff side is a legitimate design variable inside MM. This supports AUTHOR's second formulation in 2.12 ("we set things up so that the probability of legitimacy is high in fact, and we correspondingly design the system to expect legitimacy") over the first, as 2.13(a) argued — with the caveat that MM cannot itself say which formulation is *safer*, only which is *coherent* with Bayesian normativity.
3. [derived] MM adds the *ex ante* counterpart: before knowing the branch, the AI evaluates the whole rule (I13). The compliance threshold is the per-branch check; MM's scoring is the per-rule check; and in the regime where the AI would continue on its own they coincide (I13.3).

### I13 — The base-rate inequality and the oversight-quality condition — **formalized; same arithmetic as MM's shared-distribution delegation criterion**

1. **Setup [checked].** States $(\text{wrong}\in\{0,1\},\ \mathrm{Pr}\in\{0,1\})$ with $P^A(\text{wrong})=\varepsilon$, $P^A(\mathrm{Pr}\mid\text{wrong})=\beta$, $P^A(\mathrm{Pr}\mid\text{right})=\alpha$; the single gamble $g$ = "continue" with $g=c$ on right, $g=-h$ on wrong; the overseers' rule $D_H$ accepts iff $\neg\mathrm{Pr}$; the AI's own rule $D_A$ accepts iff $\mathbb E_{P^A}[g]=(1-\varepsilon)c-\varepsilon h\ge0$. MM's loss for a rule on a single gamble is $L(D)=\sum_\omega P^A(\omega)\,|g_\omega|\,\mathbb 1[D_\omega\ne(g_\omega\ge0)]$ (S4).
2. **Identity [checked].** For any rule, $L^\mu(D)=\Pi^\mu_+-\mathbb E_{P^A,\mu}[\text{payoff}(D)]$ with $\Pi^\mu_+=\int\sum_{\omega:g_\omega\ge0}P^A(\omega)g_\omega\,d\mu$ independent of $D$. *Proof.* $L=\sum_{\text{acc},g<0}\pi|g|+\sum_{\text{rej},g\ge0}\pi|g|$ and $\sum_{\text{rej},g\ge0}\pi|g|=\Pi_+-\sum_{\text{acc},g\ge0}\pi|g|$; substitute. $\blacksquare$ Hence **MM's shared-distribution criterion $L^\mu(D_A)\le L^\mu(D_\pi)$ is exactly expected-payoff comparison**, i.e. Value in expectation over $\mu$; the scoring adds nothing to Value when distributions are shared (it matters only for $\mu_{\mathrm{self}}\ne\mu_{\mathrm{delegate}}$, see "What the formalism adds").
3. **Derivation [checked].** In the regime where the AI would continue on its own ($(1-\varepsilon)c\ge\varepsilon h$): $L(D_A)=\varepsilon h$ and $L(D_H)=(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c+\varepsilon(1-\beta)h$. MM says: delegate the continue/stop decision to the overseers iff $L(D_H)\le L(D_A)$, i.e.
$$(1-\varepsilon)\,\alpha\,c\ \le\ \varepsilon\,\beta\,h \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \frac{\alpha}{\beta}\ \le\ \frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{h}{c},$$
which is the position statement's inequality (CLAUDE 2.13(b): "compliance stays rational only while $\alpha/\beta\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac hc$") — *the same arithmetic*, now identified as MM's delegation criterion with the AI as principal. It is also identical to the realized-branch threshold of I12: $P^A(\text{wrong}\mid\mathrm{Pr})=\frac{\varepsilon\beta}{\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}\ge\frac{c}{c+h}\iff\varepsilon\beta h\ge(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c$. In the opposite regime (the AI would stop on its own) the active condition is the above-threshold inequality, $(1-\varepsilon)(1-\alpha)c\ge\varepsilon(1-\beta)h$.
4. **The complete condition [checked].** Value on $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}}$ (both inequalities of the Baby Theorem) is
$$\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\ \le\ \frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{h}{c}\ \le\ \frac{1-\alpha}{1-\beta}:$$
the likelihood ratio of a press must be low enough, *and the likelihood ratio of silence high enough*, to bracket the AI's prior odds of error times the stakes ratio. The position statement derived only the left inequality. The right one is the oversight-quality condition for *silence*: an AI that is often wrong ($\varepsilon$ large) should defer to the overseers' silence only if their miss rate $1-\beta$ is low relative to their true-negative rate $1-\alpha$ — a condition on early, weak agents, trivially met by capable ones. All of item 3–4 verified over 20 000 random parameter draws (`mm-scratch/check_i12_i13.py`, 0 violations).
5. **Capability growth [derived].** As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ the left inequality requires $\alpha\le\beta\,\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\frac hc\to0$: the false-press rate must fall in step with the AI's error rate, or presses become rationally discounted — 2.13(b)'s "fully updated deference as arithmetic", confirmed. MM's reach dimension adds that this must hold *on the new problem distribution* $\mu_{\mathrm{delegate}}$ that capability opens up (S4): $\alpha,\beta$ are per problem type, and the relevant ones are those of the problems a more capable AI actually faces, including the manipulation-type problems MM names.
6. [derived] MM's own framing of the trade-off (abstract: "balancing an agent's value (mis)alignment with its epistemic accuracy and its reach") is the H→A version of the same inequality: the humans delegate the task iff the AI's accuracy advantage outweighs its misalignment on $\mu_{\mathrm{delegate}}$. The two directions share one form: *delegate to the party whose error rate on this question is lower, weighted by stakes*. Corrigibility is the case where the party with the lower error rate on "is this AI compromised" is the human.

### I14 — The basin picture: reflection w.r.t. all objectively plausible future beliefs; capability growth coupled to legitimacy-model improvement; calibration versus optimism — **partial: coverage and geometry formalized; the coupling is a frame-accuracy condition MM cannot check**

1. **Coverage [checked, definitional].** Total Trust conditions only on events of positive $\pi$-probability, so it is automatically a condition "with respect to every possible future belief that is actually plausible" *by the agent's lights* and says nothing about candidates outside $\mathrm{supp}\,\pi$ — matching AUTHOR 2.12 ("it does not need to avoid obeying the reflection principle for all the objectively implausible beliefs"). The *objective* half of 2.12 ("this is an 'objective' comparison to the human reflectively endorsed credences") is the requirement that $\mathrm{supp}\,\pi$ contain the actually reachable profiles — I8.2's frame adequacy. Both halves are now one condition.
2. **Geometry of the basin [reported: DDB Thm 4.1, Cor. 4.5].** $\pi$ totally trusts a frame iff every candidate in $C_\pi$ is *modestly informed* ($P_i=\lambda_{ii}\widehat P_i+\sum_{j}\lambda_{ij}P_j$, an average of its own informed self and the other candidates it leaves open) and $\pi$ lies in their convex hull. So the set of principals that value a given frame is a **convex set** in belief space, and a frame *validates* Total Trust (every candidate defers to the frame) iff all candidates are modestly informed. This is a static basin: a convex region of current beliefs from which the modification is endorsed. Proposition I7.1 supplies the dynamic half: valued transitions compose, so a chain of legitimately-judged steps is legitimate from the start — *if* each frame is accurate.
3. **Capability growth coupled to legitimacy-model improvement [partial].** AUTHOR 2.12: "It should only gain in capabilities if its concept of legitimacy is correspondingly improved, so that it can avoid delegitimizing action-sequences as it becomes capable of them." In MM: capability changes $\mu_{\mathrm{delegate}}$ (reach) and the AI's own accuracy; the AI's frame $\{H^{a}_\omega\}$ over overseer responses must remain *accurate* on the new problems and actions — and MM has no object for frame accuracy. What MM can state is the consequence: by Proposition I5.2, trust in anticipated feedback on a problem $\mathcal O$ requires $\pi(L)/\pi(L^c)\ge$ (harm on $L^c$)/(gain on $L$) *for that problem*; new problems with new delegitimizing actions lower $\pi(L)$ in fact, so the frame must register that. The Frame Break of the opening exchange (CLAUDE: "Reflection is an internal coherence condition...; accuracy-increase is external") is thus exactly the gap: MM certifies coherence (Value), not accuracy of the frame. Christiano's worry [reported, christiano-2017 line 76: "a slight divergence in values would turn the agent and the overseer into adversaries"] is, in MM, the observation that Theorem 3.4's posterior alignment is an *equality-type* condition for universal deference — fragile — whereas the Baby Theorem's inequalities for the shutdown problem have slack (the bracket in I13.4): context-specific corrigibility has a basin; universal corrigibility does not.
4. **Calibration versus optimism [derived].** (i) MM is normative for the principal's actual $\pi$: an AI designed to *overestimate* $\pi(L)$ is, inside MM, simply miscalibrated, and Value computed under a miscalibrated $\pi$ certifies nothing about outcomes — this is AUTHOR's own worry ("I'm not supposed to advocate for an irrational position") stated formally, and it favours his second formulation. (ii) But the rain example (S3, [reported]) shows what optimism *is* allowed: the AI may value the overseers' verdict while believing them miscalibrated ($\pi(\mathrm{Rain}\mid[P=P_\omega])=1$ though $P_\omega(\mathrm{Rain})=.6$) — deference concerns the *conditional* estimate given their verdict, not adoption of their confidence. This is the "gold test" point of 2.12 (AUTHOR: "We can believe that a test for gold is very believable while also believing it won't find gold") in exact form: Total Trust at threshold $0$ for $X_0$ is compatible with any prior confidence about $X_0$, since it constrains $\mathbb E[X_0\mid\mathrm{Pr}]$, not $\mathbb E[X_0]$. (iii) "Overestimating the conditional legitimacy as a rule implies overestimating the unconditional legitimacy" (AUTHOR, flagged informal): in MM, the conditional Total Trust inequalities are consistent with any $\pi(L)$; what $\pi(L)$ controls is the *unconditional* value of delegation via Proposition I5.2. So the two are decoupled in the formalism, and the "grain of truth" the author asked for is: *conditional* trust (the inequalities) can be high everywhere without any overestimate, while *unconditional* trust in feedback needs $\pi(L)$ high in fact — the author's second formulation exactly.
5. [inexpressible] The training-process assumptions that would make $\pi(L)$ high in fact, and keep the frame accurate as reach grows, are outside MM (AGENDA: MM "characterizes alignment, but doesn't give any advice about how to build an aligned AI").

### I15 — Trust earned via feedback about hypotheticals and other agents' shutdowns; second-channel bookkeeping; what settles — **partial**

1. [reported: MM §5.1, App. E] The Delegation Bandit is MM's model of *earned* trust: the principal "learns over time which agent yields higher expected utility, effectively updating her beliefs about alignment, accuracy, and reach through experience"; UCB converges when reward variance is small; and MM's own caveat: "the extent to which we can learn our way to rational delegation through trial and error decreases as the stakes rise... the delegation calculus developed in the bulk of this paper is better suited to guiding one-shot or high-impact decisions."
2. **On-policy unidentifiability [checked].** A→H, the AI wants to learn $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta)$. Complying on a press yields the shutdown payoff and reveals nothing about $X_0$ or about whether it was right; continuing on silence reveals whether it was right. So a *compliant* AI observes two quantities — the press rate $P(\mathrm{Pr})=\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha$ and the silence-conditioned error rate $P(\text{wrong}\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr})=\frac{\varepsilon(1-\beta)}{\varepsilon(1-\beta)+(1-\varepsilon)(1-\alpha)}$ — for three unknowns. The press-conditioned error rate $P(\text{wrong}\mid\mathrm{Pr})$, the one compliance depends on (I12), is not identified from compliant experience. This is CLAUDE's 2.12 worry ("bets on them never settle") as a counting fact in MM's own learning model: within the bandit, the "comply" arm's counterfactual is never observed.
3. [derived] AUTHOR's side channels (2.12) are each a *second frame* in MM: (i) the next iteration learning "that the previous iteration was shut down for a good reason" — a retrospective realization of $X_0$ delivered by the overseers' *report*, whose Total Trust is its own condition (2.13(c)'s second-channel bookkeeping); (ii) other agents' shutdowns — samples from the same $(\alpha,\beta)$ only under an exchangeability assumption about agents, i.e. a frame assumption; (iii) feedback about hypotheticals — reports about $H_\omega$ at non-actual $\omega$, which is information about the *shape* of the frame $\{H_\omega\}$, exactly the object Total Trust is about. None is a theorem; all are representable. Minimal structural requirement for the reports channel: the overseers' beliefs must have positive access (I7.5) to be valued on questions about their own dispositions.
4. [derived] MM's stakes caveat applies with full force to shutdown, a one-shot high-stakes decision: MM says not to rely on trial-and-error trust for it. Combined with item 2, this supports AUTHOR's "the prior should in some sense be skewed towards trust (if you're in fact in a trustworthy environment)" in the specific form: the *frame* over overseer behaviour must be accurate ex ante, because on-policy experience cannot correct its press branch. "Trust can be earned" (AUTHOR) is true in MM for the humans trusting the AI (repeatable, lower stakes) and only off-policy for the AI trusting the button.

### I16 — The deferred amendment 1a: what the channel must carry — **formalized**

1. **Proposition I16.1 (an inert channel) [checked].** Let $s_A$ be the AI's total information ($P^A=\mu(\cdot\mid s_A)$ under a common prior). If the press is conditionally independent of the continue-value given the AI's information, $\mathrm{Pr}\perp X_0\mid s_A$ — in particular if the overseers' verdict is computed from information the AI also has, $\mathrm{Pr}=f(s_A)$ — then $\mathbb E_{P^A}[X_0\mid\mathrm{Pr}]=\mathbb E_{P^A}[X_0]$, the Baby Theorem's two inequalities collapse to $\mathbb E_{P^A}[X_0]\le0$ and $\ge0$, and the AI complies iff it would have stopped anyway. *Proof:* conditional independence. $\blacksquare$ This is Taylor's agent in MM [reported, taylor-2015 lines 61, 92: "the AI will want to manipulate the human into being an efficient morality sensor"; "So the AI will keep the human alive and use them as a morality sensor"]: valued on the *value* question, inert on the *shutdown* question, because "the humans want me stopped", when it is a function of shared information, carries no news about $X_0$. It believes it has much to learn and is incorrigible — CLAUDE 1a's counterexample to "incorrigible = nothing to learn" stands in MM.
2. **Proposition I16.2 (what makes the channel live) [checked].** Write $X_0=f(\theta,r)$ with $\theta$ world facts and $r$ the AI's reliability ("plan selection compromised"). Suppose $r\perp s_A$ (the AI's own information does not reveal its reliability) and the press depends on overseer evidence $s_H$ about $r$. Then $\mathbb E_{P^A}[X_0\mid\mathrm{Pr}]=\sum_r P(r\mid\mathrm{Pr})\,\mathbb E[f(\theta,r)\mid s_A,r]\neq\mathbb E_{P^A}[X_0]$ whenever $\mathrm{Pr}$ is informative about $r$ and $f$ depends on $r$. $\blacksquare$ So the press moves the AI iff it is conditionally dependent on $X_0$ given $s_A$, and for an AI that already holds the overseers' world-information the only route is a variable about itself that it cannot observe — CLAUDE 2.14's corollary ("the channel must carry information about the *agent's own reliability*..., not only about *which plan the humans prefer*; only the former makes the null action posterior-optimal").
3. [derived] The amendment, formalized: **incorrigible on the button = Value fails on $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}}$**, and Value fails either because the channel is inert given the AI's information (I16.1) or because it is live but its likelihood ratios violate the bracket of I13.4. "Nothing to learn" is the special case $\Delta=0$ on *every* question; Taylor's agent has $\Delta>0$ on values and $\Delta=0$ on shutdown. Supports CLAUDE 1a, with the sharpening "on the shutdown question".
4. [derived] The Arbital hard-problem page [reported: "my expected utility calculations suggesting that this action has super-high utility may be dangerously mistaken and I should run them past the outside force"] names the same latent: the AI's *calculation* is what may be corrupted, so its output must not screen off $r$. Arbital's demand that this be "not just one more kind of uncertainty to be summed over" is, in MM, $r\perp s_A$ for every $s_A$ the AI can reach — see I17.4.

### I17 — Fully updated deference re-expressed — **formalized; the crux located**

1. **Arbital's argument in MM [reported + derived].** Arbital [reported, arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference]: the policy "$\pi_6$: Avoid being shut down; then gather more evidence $E$ to update $\Delta U$ as far as possible; then optimize the updated $\Delta U\mid E$" beats deferring, and "a sufficiently advanced AI can also see that this update is predictable (by efficiency) and therefore behaves as if it had already updated (by Bayesianism)." A→H: the AI compares delegating $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}}$ to the overseers with deciding itself after acquiring $E$. By Lemma I9.2, once the AI's information contains everything the overseers know that bears on $X_0$, Total Trust toward them on that question fails, $\Delta_{P^A}(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}})\le0$, and deference is irrational. Arbital's "attempted rescue by AI unobservability" is keeping the refinement by denying the AI the overseers' information; Arbital's rebuttal — then human behaviour is uninformative — is that a refinement the AI cannot acquire from the shared world is not one the overseers could have got from it either. Both are correct in MM *while the question is about values or world facts*.
2. **The thesis's sentence, in MM [derived].** "A corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught" is Tower. Under clarity, Total Trust $\Rightarrow$ Reflection (DDB §1) $\Rightarrow$ $\pi=\sum_\rho\pi([P=\rho])\,\rho$ $\Rightarrow$ $\mathbb E_\pi(X)=\mathbb E_\pi\big[\mathbb E_P(X)\big]$. Arbital's "behaves as if it had already updated (by Bayesianism)" is the *same* property. The two sides agree on Tower, and Tower does not make the channel worthless: $\Delta>0$ iff $\mathbb E_H(X_0)$ crosses $0$ with positive $P^A$-probability. "Already believes in expectation" and "has something to learn conditionally" are compatible; the latter is what Good's theorem rewards. So the thesis's sentence is correct and does *not* by itself answer Arbital.
3. **Where the disagreement sits [derived].** Arbital needs: whatever the press could tell the AI about $X_0$, the AI can learn otherwise (observe humans, scan brains) — the press is about $\theta$ or $U$. The thesis needs I16.2: the press is about $r$, unresolvable from inside. The base-rate arithmetic (I13) sits inside this: given a live channel it says how live ($\alpha/\beta$ against $\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\frac hc$); FUD is the claim that for value/world questions the channel goes inert (I16.1) as the AI updates, whereupon the arithmetic says compliance fails. **FUD is correct for the value-learning reading of the button and is dissolved only by the reliability reading** — which is 1a. (Consistent with the opening exchange's "self-distrust fixed point $H_c$"; CLAUDE register.)
4. **Stability under capability growth, as a condition [derived].** The reliability reading survives the AI acquiring $s_A'\supseteq s_A$ iff $r$ stays conditionally independent of the AI's total information given the overseers' (I9.5's sufficient condition, on the reliability question). Arbital's "some nontrivially-structured recursive belief about one's own flawedness" and its Death-in-Damascus analogy [reported] — "whatever reasoning the AI ends up doing about $\Delta U$, on any meta-level, will yield the wrong answer" — is precisely a demand for such an $r$, independent of all the AI's reasoning outputs. Whether a capable agent can be kept there (legitimate self-verification lowers $\varepsilon$ and should; corrupted self-verification is the failure) is the OPEN crux of the opening exchange; MM turns it from a regress into a conditional-independence requirement on the world and the training process.
5. **Ledger.** [checked] Given Value on $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}}$ there is no FUD problem: compliance is posterior-optimal and the channel protected (I10). [derived] For a fully-updated AI, Value on $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}}$ requires the press to depend on a latent the AI cannot resolve (I16). [OPEN] Whether such a latent exists for capable agents and stays unresolved as they grow.

### I18 — The typical-case reframing — **formalized, with one structural exception**

1. [reported: MM abstract, §1, §5] MM's headline *is* the reframing: "universal delegation (trusting an agent with any problem) demands near-perfect value alignment and total epistemic trust, conditions rarely met in practice... context-specific delegation can be optimal even with significant misalignment"; the paper shifts "the focus from achieving perfect alignment to managing the risks and rewards of delegation under uncertainty."
2. **The exception [derived from MM Thm 3.4 and Thm I9.3].** *Universal* corrigibility — the AI weakly prefers the overseers' decision on every problem — is structurally as demanding as universal delegation: Theorem 3.4 swapped requires posterior alignment of the AI's preferences with the overseers' given their profile (value alignment in the conditional sense), and Theorem I9.3 makes universal deference in both directions agreement. So "corrigibility as a substitute for alignment" is impossible at the universal grade — a structural theorem, not a typical-case claim. It is the grain of truth in the anti-naturality literature that survives the transform, and it concerns a notion no desiderata list actually asks for.
3. **The rule [derived].** *Context-specific* corrigibility — Value on $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{sh}}$ — is the bracket of I13.4, an inequality in $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta,c,h)$ with slack; its failure modes — inert channel (I16.1), bad base rates (I13.5), frame misspecification (I8.2) — are facts about the world and the training process. CLAUDE 2.3 ("every remaining anti-naturality argument has to be a *typical-case* claim") holds *for the button*, and MM supplies the list of what the claim must be about. Register: supports CLAUDE 2.3, corrected by item 2.
4. [derived] MM also reframes the consequentialist argument (outline item 8): reach means a more capable agent faces different problems; §4.3.1 shows delegation can stay rational "even if he tries to minimize Alice's payoffs" when reach improves the problem set, and "reach can also make things much worse." Whether capability helps or hurts is an expected-value fact about $\mu_{\mathrm{delegate}}$, not a consequence of coherence.

## What the formalism adds

Results already in MM/DDB that bear on the thesis and that the position statement does not use, plus what was derived here:

1. **Endorsement $\iff$ deference is a characterization** (MM Thm 3.2 / DDB Thm 2.2), so "corrigible = endorses legitimate teaching" is if-and-only-if in the A→H direction; by the change-of-measure trick (Prop I5.1) it holds conditional on any event, and by the Baby Theorem it holds problem-by-problem for the button.
2. **The Baby Theorem needs no finiteness** [checked]: its proof is linearity of expectation, so it survives Weatherson's infinite-frame counterexamples, which kill Theorem 3.2 in general. The one theorem corrigibility needs is the robust one.
3. **Universal corrigibility = posterior alignment** (Thm 3.4 swapped) and **mutual universal deference = agreement** (Thm I9.3): the structural residue of anti-naturality, confined to the universal grade.
4. **Corollary I4.1**: under clarity, legitimacy has one meaning and it is Bayesian conditioning on "I will believe this" — Eisenstat's observation as a theorem; the three criteria diverge only for modest successors, where Total Trust is the survivor.
5. **Props I2.1, I5.1–I5.2, I7.1**: zero→positive is a theorem; legitimacy-conditioned trust has a decision-theoretic meaning and a quantitative odds condition; legitimacy composes.
6. **I13**: the base-rate inequality is MM's shared-distribution delegation criterion with the AI as principal; MM's shared-$\mu$ loss criterion is exactly expected-payoff comparison (Identity I13.2); the *silence* inequality is new.
7. **I10**: D1–D3 (forcing sense) from Value; D4 with willingness-to-pay $\Delta$ (answering Soares §5's cost question); no news-management.
8. **I16**: 1a as conditional (in)dependence of the press and the continue-value given the AI's information.
9. **DDB Thm 3.2** (local accuracy equivalence), **DDB Thm 4.1** (convex-hull geometry: the basin of principals valuing a frame is convex), **positive access** as a self-knowledge constraint on the trusted party.
10. **MM §5.1** as the trust-earning model, with on-policy unidentifiability of the press-branch error rate (I15.2).
11. **The rain example**: valuing without adopting the expert's confidence — the "gold test" in exact form.
12. **Reach**: capability as a change of problem distribution, with manipulation named as a reach effect.
13. **Caveats about MM's own arithmetic [checked by recomputation, `mm-scratch/check_i12_i13.py`]**: (a) the gain convention differs — §4.1–4.2 (and App. A–B) count correct rejections as gains, §4.3 (and the printed formula $G=\int_{D\cap I_\omega}|g_\omega|\,d\mu$) do not; under the printed convention $S=-\mathbb E[\text{payoff}]+(\text{missed gains})$, which double-penalizes forgone gains and is biased toward accepting; (b) §4.1 prints $G(D_B)=32/12$ but Table 2 sums to $43/12$ ($S=-22/12$, not $-11/12$); (c) §4.2's Appendix B ($S(D_\pi)=750/12$, $S(D_B)=-400/12$) contradicts the main text ($-150/12$, $-700/12$); the main text is what a consistent all-correct convention with effective (post-fee) payoffs gives. Every delegation verdict is unchanged under either convention. For the different-distribution criterion the convention matters, since $\Pi^\mu_+$ differs between $\mu_{\mathrm{self}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{delegate}}$.
