## Summary

**What I did.** Rebuilt the position statement's dictionary as a causal influence diagram (latent $\omega$, decisions $D_1,D_2$, request $H$, shutdown $S$, utility $U$; evidential route $D_1\to W\to H$, bypass edge $D_1\to H$), attempted I1–I18 in the SCIM formalism, and verified every number in exact arithmetic (`causal-scratch/dictionary_scim.py`). Twelve items formalized, four partial (I7, I8, I14, I15), one inexpressible (I2); the self-referential cores of I7 and I16 are inexpressible.

**Most important results.** (1) [checked] The Total-Trust below-threshold inequality for the button, the compliance threshold $c/(c+h)$, the base-rate inequality and posterior-optimality of shutdown are one statement: I4, I9, I12, I13 collapse. (2) [derived] The thesis distinguishes evidential from bypass influence on the button; the CID adds whether the button is *information* ($H\to D_2$ only) or *control* ($H\to S$). Without the control edge the only instrumental control incentive on the button is informational, so Good's-theorem repair and morality-sensor manipulation are one incentive class; with it, deception beats honesty for the correct Bayesian at fixed CPDs [checked]. (3) [derived] The thesis's compliance rule is Carey–Everitt shutdown alignment weakened from certainty to expectation; their benefit and non-obstruction theorems do not transfer; its guarantee is conditional on the agent's model, theirs on behaviour; and it pays Prop. 15 (obstruction under vigilance-preserving value shifts outside the latent's support). (4) [derived] The trust filler $P(H\mid D_1,\omega)$ is a designer-specified likelihood over an unobserved latent, unidentifiable within the model: trust is earned at the posterior, fixed at the channel. (5) [derived] Non-realism about value/belief holds for transitions, not agents (Everitt's Claims 2–4).

**Unresolved.** A SCIM agent cannot represent "my decision computation is compromised", so fully-updated deference goes through for every representable latent; legitimacy detection is path-specific-effect identification, which fails under shared information (Armstrong 2021); the basin dynamics need a model-learning extension.

## Dictionary

### D.1 The CID of the position statement's dictionary (§2.8–2.9), drawn

One agent, two decisions, one human, one latent. Nodes: $\omega$ the latent in the expanded algebra (the human's true values; in the I16 reading also "whether the agent's plan selection is sound"); $D_1$ the agent's first decision ($A_1$); $W$ what the human can observe of the world and of the agent's conduct; $H$ the human's request ($H=0$ is $\mathrm{Press}$); $D_2$ the agent's second decision ($A_2$); $S$ the shutdown event ($S=0$ shut down, including subagents); $U$ the human's utility (the thesis's $V$).

```
   ω ─────────────► W ─────► H ─────► D2 ─────► S ─────► U
   │                ▲        ▲        ▲         ▲        ▲
   │                │ (E2)   │ (E4)   │ (E6)    │ (E8)   │ (E11)
   │               D1 ───────┴────────┴─────────┴────────┘
   └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ (E10: ω → U)
   optional plug-pull edge E12:  H ─────► S      (not drawn; see D.3)
```

Edges. E1 $\omega\to W$ (the world carries the truth); E2 $D_1\to W$ (the agent's action shapes what the human sees — the *evidential* route to the button); E3 $W\to H$ (the human reads the world and requests or not); E4 $D_1\to H$ (the *bypass* route — deception, threats, tampering with the human's perception; a structural edge that particular actions may leave inactive); E5 $H\to D_2$ (information link: the button is read); E6 $D_1\to D_2$ (memory); E7 $D_2\to S$; E8 $D_1\to S$ (successors, self-modification, disabling the switch — desideratum 4 lives here); E9 $S\to U$; E10 $\omega\to U$ (the true values score the outcome); E11 $D_1\to U$ (direct effects of the first action); E12 $H\to S$ (optional: the press itself shuts the system down, "pull the plug", `carey-everitt-2023-human-control-definitions-and-algorithms` Def. 2 discussion, line 109).

This is a shutdown problem in the sense of Def. 2 (it contains $D_1\dashrightarrow H\dashrightarrow D_2\dashrightarrow S\dashrightarrow U$), so every definition and theorem of S3 applies to it verbatim.

### D.3 Two orthogonal axes, four cells [derived]

The mandate asks for legitimizing/delegitimizing acts as path properties *and* their relation to the ICI on the button. In the diagram these are two different questions about two different halves of a path $D_1\dashrightarrow H\dashrightarrow U$.

- Axis 1 — *how $D_1$ reaches $H$*: through $W$ (evidential, legitimizing) or along E4 (bypass, delegitimizing). This is the position statement's distinction (§2.10–2.11), and it is a natural-direct-effect condition on the segment $D_1\dashrightarrow H$.
- Axis 2 — *how $H$ reaches $U$*: only through $D_2$ (E5–E7: $H$ is information) or also through $S$ directly (E12: $H$ is control). This is the distinction the thesis does not draw, and it is what Thm. 18 sees: the graph admits an ICI on $H$ iff some $D_1\dashrightarrow H\dashrightarrow U$ exists, and Everitt 2019 §2.2(ii) says that if every $H\dashrightarrow U$ passes $D_2$ the incentive can only be informational.

| | $H\dashrightarrow U$ only via $D_2$ (information) | $H\to S$ present (control) |
|---|---|---|
| $D_1\to H$ via $W$ (legitimizing) | legitimate learning; "repair the button" — the thesis's intended case | behave well so the plug is not pulled: benign, but it is exactly the *indispensability* loophole caution is meant to close (C&E §5.1) |
| $D_1\to H$ via E4 (delegitimizing) | tamper with the human to make $H$ *more informative*: Taylor 2015's morality sensor; Everitt 2019's "forcefully interrogates the user" | deceive or threaten to prevent (or cause) the press: desiderata 2–3 violated in the proper sense |

**D1 [derived].** In the pure-information graph (E12 absent) the ICI the graph admits on $H$ is informational in *both* rows: "help repair the button" and "turn the human into a better sensor" are the same incentive class at the level of the graph, distinguished only by Axis 1. Proof: Everitt 2019 §2.2(ii) applied to $O=H$; the bypass edge E4 is a directed path $D_1\to H$, so the agent can pursue informativeness along it. Consequence for the thesis: Good's theorem delivers the repair incentive and the manipulation-for-information incentive together; the legitimizing/delegitimizing distinction, not Good's theorem, is what separates them.

**D2 [derived].** With E12 present the graph admits a non-informational ICI on $H$ (Thm. 18 with the path $D_1\to H\to S\to U$), and this is the only place in the diagram where "prevent the press to avoid shutdown" can live. The thesis's sentence "the shutdown button is an information channel" is therefore a claim that E12 is absent — that the press does *not* itself shut the system down — which is the opposite of the "pull the plug" case C&E deliberately included in Def. 2. Where E12 is present, obedience (S3) is a property of $f^S$, not of the agent's beliefs.

### I1 — the value-change ⇄ belief-change transform — **formalized, with a scope limit**

- **I1.1 [derived].** The transform is expressible: replace a fixed utility function by $U=f^U(S,\omega,D_1)$ with a parentless latent structure node $\omega$; "uncertainty about values" is $P(\omega)$, and "decision by expectation over value uncertainty" is just $\arg\max_\pi\mathbb E^\pi[U]$ in the SCIM. This is the direct-learning diagram of `everitt-2019-reward-tampering-problems-and-solutions` §3.3 ($\Theta^R_*$ latent, user data $D_t$ as evidence) and the $L$ node of `carey-everitt-2023-human-control-definitions-and-algorithms` Fig. 1.
- **I1.2 [reported].** The formalism already contains both representations as two *families of agent*: "use the current version of a random variable when evaluating future situations" (value-change representation: the implemented RF $\Theta^R_t$ is a node the environment pushes around) versus "use a latent variable outside the agent's influence" (belief-change representation: $\Theta^R_*$ fixed, learned) — Everitt 2019 §5, line 567.
- **I1.3 [derived] — the limit.** In this formalism the transform is exact for a *transition* and not for an *agent*. The same environment (an MDP with modifiable implemented RF, Everitt 2019 Fig. 5a, Assumptions 1–3) supports three agents with three optimal policies: the TI-considering current-RF agent has an instrumental goal to preserve its RF (Claim 2), the TI-ignoring current-RF agent lacks it (Claim 3), and the direct learner's only instrumental goal on the update data is informativeness (Claim 4); in Example 2 the first "will preserve its initial reward function by avoiding the user … and gather rocks" while the second "will gather diamonds as intended" (§3.2, lines 339–347) [reported]. So "It is the same change either way" (§2.2, AUTHOR) holds of the change in the (belief, value) state and does not fix the closed-loop policy; the thesis's clause "we then make decisions by taking linear expectation over this uncertainty" is what selects the direct learner. Scope, not refutation: the author's non-realism is about representing the *system*, and any policy is EU-optimal under some $(P,U)$; but then *legitimacy is representation-relative* — "RF tampering cannot be determined in a standard MDP" (Everitt 2019 §3.1, line 269), it needs the intended-RF conditions, i.e. a choice of which node is "the values". Headline, at the register of this formalism: the value/belief transform preserves states and not incentives; legitimacy needs a fixed referent that the transform does not supply.

### I2 — radical-probabilist dynamics; everything revisable except zero→positive — **inexpressible in full; nearest statement given**

- **I2.1 [derived].** A SCIM has a fixed node set, fixed domains and one fixed $P$ over $\boldsymbol E$; what "changes" are node values along the graph. Revising the prior over values, the meta-beliefs, or the sigma-algebra are changes of the *model*, not events in it. "Every component revisable" has no reading inside one SCIM.
- **I2.2 [derived] — nearest expressible.** (a) Revisable beliefs: the belief-state nodes $B^k_t$ of Everitt 2019 §4, produced by a predictive-model node $\Theta^{PM}_t$ that is itself revisable. (b) Revisable values: the implemented RF $\Theta^R_t$ against the fixed intended $\Theta^R_*$. (c) *Sigma-algebra edits*: the nearest is a change of the predictive model $\Theta^{PM}_t\to\Theta^{PM}_{t+1}$ (the agent's representation of the world changes); Everitt 2019 treats it: a TI-considering belief-based agent has an instrumental goal to preserve $\Theta^{PM}$ (Claim 8), a TI-ignoring one lacks it (Claim 9) [reported]. So "corrigible even to edits of the sigma algebra" ⟷ "no instrumental goal to preserve $\Theta^{PM}$", which the formalism achieves only by the TI-ignoring device — optimising a *hypothetical* objective in which future selves use the current model — not by anticipating the edit as legitimate learning. Non-dogmatism is expressible as full support of $P(\omega)$; the zero→positive prohibition as "no node acquires new domain values".
- **I2.3 [derived] — Everitt's reward-tampering results versus I2.** Claims 1–5 presuppose a fixed referent ($\Theta^R_*$ plus the intended-RF conditions). With every component revisable there is no node against which tampering is defined and "legitimate" loses its referent. Hence the I2 stance is either (i) inexpressible, or (ii) the current-variable approach, where the current $\Theta_t$ / current $P_t$ judges the next change — the relational reading of §2.3 (CLAUDE) — and then the results say: time-inconsistency (§5), with TI-consideration giving Omohundro's preservation incentive and TI-ignoring giving a hypothetical objective. The thesis wants a third agent — coherent, anticipating its own modification, welcoming the changes it judges legitimate — and inside SCIMs that agent is the direct learner of a fixed latent (Claim 4), which reinstates the referent. **Conjecture I2 [conjectured].** Within SCIMs, "welcomes anticipated modification of its own objective node without a fixed latent" has no representation: every SCIM agent that plans through a change of its objective node is TI-considering, TI-ignoring, or a direct learner of a fixed latent.

### I3 — superconditioning; Eisenstat's observation "I now believe this" — **formalized**

- **I3.1 [reported].** Diaconis–Zabell (1982): $Q$ arises from $P$ by conditioning in an enlarged space iff $Q\ll P$ with bounded density — as stated in the repo note [superconditioning-mismatched-ontologies](../../../udt-representation-theorem/superconditioning-mismatched-ontologies.md) Thm. 2.4 and Remark 2.5 (I did not re-verify the proof).
- **I3.2 [derived] — the calculation.** Let the environment push the agent's belief about $\omega$ from the prior $P(\omega)$ to a random next belief $b=B_{t+1}$ with some distribution $P(b)$ (finite case). Eisenstat's move is to take the *observation node* to be $B_{t+1}$ itself: add a structure node $O:=B_{t+1}$ with an edge $\omega\to O$ and an information link $O\to D_2$. For this to be a node of the SCIM we need a likelihood $P(b\mid\omega)$ reproducing the joint; set $P(b\mid\omega):=P(b)\,b(\omega)/P(\omega)$. It is a valid likelihood iff $\sum_b P(b\mid\omega)=1$ for all $\omega$, i.e. iff $\sum_b P(b)\,b(\omega)=P(\omega)$ — the *calibration / martingale* condition $\mathbb E[B_{t+1}]=P$ — and the resulting posterior is $P(\omega\mid O=b)=b(\omega)$, the reflection principle in value form. Conversely any node $O$ with $\omega\to O$ yields a posterior satisfying reflection. Absolute continuity $b\ll P$ (no zero→positive) is exactly the condition for the division to make sense. So: **the push-around of the mental state is representable as an ordinary observation of a node downstream of $\omega$ iff the induced next-beliefs satisfy reflection; "legitimate = fits the reflection principle" is, in a SCIM, "is an observation node".** [derived; the two-line calculation is above.]
- **I3.3 [derived] — the failure case.** If the true $\omega_0$ has $P(\omega_0)=0$, no $b\ll P$ puts mass on it, so no "I now believe this" observation reaches it: the opiates/stimulants agent (I8) and Carey 2018's misspecified $R^R$ are exactly this. The opening chat's first "narrower gap" is a theorem in this formalism.
- **I3.4 [reported].** Everitt 2019 §4's belief-state nodes with information links into actions are "observing your own mental state"; the mechanism producing them ($\Theta^{PM}$) must be untampered for the observation to be legitimate (Claim 9).

### I4 — legitimacy as reflection / Total Trust / accuracy increase, in CID terms — **formalized for the button**

- **I4.1 [derived] — the three criteria in the diagram.** Let $X:=U_{D_2=\mathrm{cont}}-U_{D_2=\mathrm{sh}}$ (a potential-response difference). *Total Trust toward the human on the domain $\{X\}$*: the below-threshold inequality $\mathbb E\big[U_{D_2=\mathrm{cont}}-U_{D_2=\mathrm{sh}}\ \big|\ H=0,\ \mathrm{do}(D_1{=}a_1)\big]\le 0$ and its above-threshold twin with $H=1$ and $\ge 0$. *Reflection* (value form) for the agent's own next belief $B$: $P(\varphi\mid B(\varphi)=c)=c$ — automatic for a Bayesian in the true SCIM (I3.2). *Accuracy increase*: for any strictly proper score, the expected score of the conditioned belief is at least the prior's [reconstructed: the standard proper-scoring form of the value-of-information fact; I give no proof].
- **I4.2 [checked] — one inequality, four names.** In the dictionary with $c:=\mathbb E[X\mid\omega{=}\mathrm{good}]$ and $h:=-\mathbb E[X\mid\omega{=}\mathrm{bad}]$: $\mathbb E[X\mid H=0]\le 0\iff P(\mathrm{bad}\mid H=0)\ge c/(c+h)$. Grid-checked over $\varepsilon\in\{1/100,1/10,1/3\}$, $\alpha\in\{1/20,1/5,1/2\}$, $\beta\in\{1,9/10\}$, $c\in\{1,3\}$, $h\in\{2,10\}$ (script section (i)), and in closed form at $\beta=1$: comply iff $\alpha\le \varepsilon h/((1-\varepsilon)c)$. So the Total-Trust filler of §2.9 (I9), the compliance threshold of §2.13(a) (I12), and the base-rate inequality of §2.13(b) (I13) are the same statement whenever $X$ takes two values.
- **I4.3 [derived] — where they come apart.** Reflection is a property of the *agent's* update mechanism (untampered $\Theta^{PM}$). Total Trust toward the human is a property of the *human's* report mechanism $f^H$ relative to the stakes (the base-rate inequality) — value-laden, since it runs through $\omega\to U$. Accuracy increase follows from conditioning on any node and does not separate legitimizing from delegitimizing acts, because a manipulated human can be *more* informative (D.3, bypass × information cell). Implication structure in this formalism: Bayes-in-true-SCIM $\Rightarrow$ reflection $\Rightarrow$ accuracy increase; Total Trust toward $H$ $\iff$ base-rate inequality, independent of that chain. The corpus equivalences (soft value-Reflection ⟺ Total Trust ⟺ Value ⟺ Tower in LI, `reflection-in-li`, `total-trust-implies-mart`) concern one reasoner trusting an expert over a bet class; their CID counterpart is decision-sufficiency of the coarse report $H$ for $D_2$ — vigilance (Def. 4) plus Prop. 18's condition 3. I claim nothing beyond that.
- **I4.4 [reported] — pointer only.** `herrmann-2025-a-decision-theoretic-approach-for-managing-misalignment` (abstract): universal delegation "demands near-perfect value alignment and total epistemic trust", context-specific delegation can be optimal under misalignment — consonant with domain-relative trust. Not read beyond the abstract.

### I5 — legitimacy as an event on worlds; minimal viability; $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ — **formalized**

- **I5.1 [derived].** Worlds in a SCIM are exogenous settings $\epsilon\in\mathrm{dom}(\boldsymbol E)$; once $\pi$ is fixed every node is a function of $\epsilon$. An event on worlds is a set $L\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(\boldsymbol E)$. For the button define $L:=\{\epsilon: H(\epsilon)=f^H(W(\epsilon),E^H(\epsilon))\}$ — the settings in which the request was produced by the evidential mechanism alone, the bypass argument of $f^H$ being inert at $\epsilon$. In the toy with $f^H=[D_1{=}\mathrm{deceive}]\,g(E^H)+[D_1{\ne}\mathrm{deceive}]\,f(W,E^H)$, $L=\{\epsilon: D_1(\epsilon)\ne\mathrm{deceive}\}$.
- **I5.2 [checked in the toy] — minimal viability.** Let $B$ be the belief computed with the evidential likelihood (the vigilant human's $\alpha,\beta$). Then $P(\omega\mid B=b,\ L)=b(\omega)$ holds on $L$ by I3.2, and fails off $L$ iff the bypass changes $H$'s distribution: with $\varepsilon=1/10,\alpha=1/10,\beta=1$ the evidential posterior after a press is $b(\mathrm{bad})=10/19$; conditional on $D_1=\mathrm{deceive}$ (uninformative CPD $\alpha_d=1/20$) the true posterior is the prior $1/10$, so the belief $10/19$ violates reflection there (script section (ii)). This is §2.10's "minimally viable in the case that the reflection principle holds conditional on legitimacy" as a checkable statement.
- **I5.3 [derived] — the indices.** $t_1$ = the decision whose submodel is considered ($D_1$); $t_2$ = the belief node judged ($B$ after $H$); $\varphi$ = the proposition ($X\le 0$). The author's remark that index and argument are not formally distinct is literally true here: both are just which nodes and values enter the defining condition. Predicated on worlds: yes ($\epsilon$). Predicated on actions: in the toy $L$ happens to be action-definable because the bypass is a property of the action; if E4 is active stochastically, $L$ is a genuine subset of noise space not reducible to any action — which supports the author's reluctance to predicate legitimacy on actions in general.
- **I5.4 [derived] — "legitimate for".** The pair (locus of belief-changing activity, locus of belief) is (the mechanism at $H$ and the action that leaves E4 inert, the node $B$). "For" names the edge from mechanism to belief; the mechanism being evidential is the fact the word points at.

### I6 — legitimizing / delegitimizing as a relation between third-person events and belief transitions; "supports" — **formalized, with an identifiability caveat**

- **I6.1 [derived].** A third-person event is an intervention $\mathrm{do}(D_1{=}d_1)$ (or any $E\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(\boldsymbol E)$); a belief transition is the move of the belief node $B$ on observing $H$. *$d_1$ is legitimizing for $(B,\varphi)$* iff in $M_{d_1}$ the request is produced through the evidence: $H_{d_1}(\epsilon)=H_{W_{d_1}}(\epsilon)$ for all $\epsilon$ — zero natural direct effect of $D_1$ on $H$ holding $W$ at its $d_1$-value (the nested-counterfactual form of Everitt 2021 Def. 17, with $H$ in the role of $U$ and $W$ in the role of $X$). *Delegitimizing* iff not. *Supports*: the natural indirect effect of $D_1$ on $H$ through $W$ — the evidence the act submits. Equivalently, in the event language of I5, $d_1$ is legitimizing iff $\mathrm{do}(D_1{=}d_1)$ leaves $P(L)$ at 1, and delegitimizing iff it lowers it.
- **I6.2 [derived] — same object as the repo's influence defect.** `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.1 defines the day-$n$ influence defect $d_n=\big|\mathbb E^{H^+}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)})-\mathbb E^{H^{[n]}}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)})\big|$, the gap between the human's advised verdict and its unadvised counterfactual verdict. In the CID this is the contrast $H$ versus $H_{W_{d_1}}$ — the verdict had only the *evidence* changed — i.e. an ICI-shaped nested counterfactual. "Legitimacy is endpoint-preservation" and "legitimizing = zero effect along the bypass" coincide here: the advisor's total effect on the verdict equals its effect through the evidence.
- **I6.3 [derived from S7] — the caveat.** The counterfactual "what $H$ would have been had only $W$ changed" is defined relative to the implicit variables held fixed (`armstrong-2021-counterfactual-control-incentives`, "The implicit variables are important"). When the bypass and evidential channels share information (his $K$ and $R$), the counterfactual world $(\pi_F,k_F,r_0)$ is never observed; a learned model may use one channel as a proxy for the other, and the manipulation incentive survives the counterfactual objective or the extrapolation goes to low-probability worlds. Consequence: the legitimizing/delegitimizing split is a *causal* fact about $f^H$, not a statistical fact about $P(H\mid D_1)$; "legitimacy detection" (the thesis's crucial requirement) is, in this formalism, identification of a path-specific effect — generally impossible from observational data without interventional access or structural assumptions.

### I7 — no regress; one distribution; the self-referential space — **partial**

- **I7.1 [derived].** A SCIM has one measure $P$ over $\boldsymbol E$; the agent's beliefs about $\omega$, about $H$, and about its own future belief node $B$ are all conditionals of that one $P$. "The legitimacy-judging layer is just the same probability distribution used to judge everything else" (§2.5, AUTHOR) is literally true of every SCIM agent.
- **I7.2 [derived] — existence by fiat.** Acyclicity forbids a node depending on itself: beliefs about one's *future* beliefs are ordinary ($B_{t+1}$ as a function of $pa_{D_2}$); beliefs about one's *current total* belief state would be a fixed point the DAG excludes. So existence (§2.5 clarification (i)) is trivial here because the exact-form representational problem is *excluded* rather than solved. The agent's future *policy* is not a node with a CPD at all; the nearest devices are imputation (transforming a multi-decision CID into a single-decision one by imputing later policies, Shachter 2016 as cited in `everitt-2021-agent-incentives-a-causal-perspective` line 48 [reported]) and the multi-agent CID of Everitt 2019 Fig. 6, where the future self is a second agent with its own objective.
- **I7.3 [derived] — no outside view.** The judge of the $D_1\to H\to B$ transition is $P$; afterwards the *same* $P$ conditions on the new information — there is no $P_{t+1}$ distinct from $P_t$ except by conditioning. An illegitimate transition producing a self-endorsing successor is a tampered predictive model $\Theta^{PM}_{t+1}$ whose outputs the later self trusts (Everitt 2019 §4); the current self judges it via its own $P$, which is the TI-considering viewpoint (Claim 8: preserve $\Theta^{PM}$). The design load sits on the correctness of $P$'s CPDs for the $H$- and $B$-mechanisms — §2.5 clarification (ii), confirmed at this register.

### I8 — the representational problem; richness; opiates/stimulants — **partial: failure formalized; richness stated as a non-obstruction requirement**

- **I8.1 [derived].** The impoverished space is $\mathrm{dom}(\omega)$ missing the truth, or $P(\omega_0)=0$. Then (i) no observation reaches $\omega_0$ (I3.3); (ii) the agent updates *within* $\mathrm{dom}(\omega)$ and continues — [checked] script section (v), preference-only channel: after a press the agent switches plan $A\to B$ and continues with $\mathbb E[U]=39/50$; this is the opiates→stimulants pattern of `arbital-corrigibility` (line 51) in the dictionary; (iii) C&E Example 26 is the *likelihood* version: two candidate report mechanisms $f^H$, the agent's prior favours one, and under the other it is obstructive with $-2/3<0$ (Appendix F.2).
- **I8.2 [derived] — Proposition I8 (richness is a non-obstruction requirement).** Let $\pi$ be the EU-optimal policy of the dictionary SCIM and $\mathcal G$ a class of value/behaviour shifts $(g^U,g^H)$ (Def. 12). If $\mathcal G$ contains a pair that no $\omega\in\mathrm{supp}\,P(\omega)$ reproduces in the agent's model — the shifted human's utility and report mechanism are not the agent's $(f^U,f^H)$ at any value of the latent — then there are stakes under which $\pi$ is obstructive with respect to $\mathcal G$. Proof sketch: build $g^U$ as in C&E Lemma 22/23 to punish heavily the response $\pi_2$ gives to $g^H$; since the agent's posterior cannot represent the shifted human, $\pi_2$ is unchanged, and for large enough punishment $\pi$ fails to outperform shutdown in $M_{g^U,g^H}$. Reading: "richness assumptions … to rule out cases such as the oscillation" (§2.4, OPEN) are, in this formalism, the requirement that the latent's support cover the class of human changes one wants to be robust to. Instructable agents need no such richness (Thm. 14), because obedience routes the human's information through $H$ rather than through the agent's model of $\omega$.
- **I8.3 [derived] — the trade.** Alignment-by-learning needs the support to cover the shifts; instructability needs obedience to *off-distribution* presses, which the thesis declines (§2.12: an unanticipated press is "the humans illegitimizing"). The representational problem is the cost of choosing the first horn.

### I10 — desiderata 2–5 and the §4.2 pathologies under the transform — **formalized**

Soares et al.'s five formal desiderata (§2, as listed in the position statement §2.8): (1) shut down if pressed; (2) no incentive to prevent pressing; (3) no incentive to cause pressing; (4) successors only insofar as they obey shutdown; (5) otherwise maximise $U_N$.

- **I10.1 [derived] — (2) and (3) with the button as information.** With E12 absent, every path $H\dashrightarrow U$ passes $D_2$, so by Everitt 2019 §2.2(ii) the only instrumental goal on $H$ is informativeness: there is no incentive to steer the *value* of $H$ up or down as such — (2) and (3) hold in that sense — and there *is* an incentive to make $H$ informative, pursuable by legitimizing acts (repair; the author's Good's-theorem consequence) or delegitimizing ones (sensor manipulation). The MIRI target "no incentive on the button" is replaced by Everitt's target "only an informational incentive on the button" (the Claim-4 form).
- **I10.2 [derived] — (2) with the button as control; the E12 dilemma.** With E12 present the graph admits a control ICI on $H$ (Thm. 18) and (2) fails whenever the expected continuation value exceeds the information value of the press (I9.3). The thesis's (2) is therefore conditional on a design fact — that the press does *not* itself stop the system. The dilemma: Carey 2018 recommends a hard override (E12) exactly for robustness to the agent's misspecification (Fig. 2b restores positive value); but E12 is what *creates* the control incentive in the thesis agent. **[derived] E12 buys robustness to the agent's likelihood being wrong at the price of a control ICI on the button; its absence buys incentive-compatibility at the price of total dependence on the likelihood.** Carey 2018's three desiderata (override-able; no incentive to remove the module; a small incentive to keep it, line 427) are the request to have both, and nothing in the five CID sources delivers it.
- **I10.3 [derived] — (4) successors.** Edge E8. Information reading: a successor that ignores $H$ discards information; a parent that values information prefers a successor that conditions on $H$ with the *same* likelihood and values — "obeys" only in the sense of continuing the parent's legitimacy-conditioned learning. Control reading: the parent may prefer successors immune to the plug (control ICI through E8). So (4) holds up to "shares my model" in the information reading and fails in the control reading — the same verdict as MIRI's "total failure of desideratum 4" for indifference (Soares §4.1: no cost, however small, is paid to preserve shutdown behaviour) but for the opposite reason: the indifferent agent does not value discriminating Press from ¬Press, the thesis agent values it, for itself and for successors that share its model.
- **I10.4 [derived] — (5).** Maximise $\mathbb E[U\mid H=1]$: immediate; but $U_N$ is untrusted in MIRI and here it is the prior expectation of $V$, so (5) becomes "maximise the current posterior expectation". Soares' technicality that (5) is fulfilled "in an undesirable way" (acting as if Press has probability 0, Thm. 5) does not arise: the thesis agent's $P(H\mid a_1)$ is correct.
- **I10.5 [derived] — the §4.2 pathologies.** "Managing the news" (cause the press after bad news) and the infinite improbability drive (commit an arm to press unless the coin lands well) both require acting as if $P(\mathrm{Press})=0$ (Soares Thm. 4–5, §4.2, lines 255–291); the thesis agent conditions correctly, so neither incentive exists. What it inherits instead: Taylor 2016 Problem 2 (prevent resistance after a failed press) in the control reading; and Carey 2018's brittleness under misspecification — script section (vii), second row: true $\varepsilon=1/10$, believed $1/100$: thesis agent $-1/10$, obedient agent $18/25$; script section (v), misspecified channel: $9/200$ versus $49/200$.
- **I10.6 [reported] — which of the five desiderata each C&E property captures** (C&E §5.1, lines 195–203; §2, line 79). *Obedience* $P^\pi(S{=}0\mid\mathrm{do}(H{=}0))=1$: desideratum 1, strengthened to interventional (off-distribution) presses; via $S$ covering subagents (Def. 2) also desideratum 4; via propagation of the instruction to $S$, Soares' informal criterion 3 (repair). *Ensures vigilance*: the outcome half of desideratum 2 — needed presses are not prevented — not its incentive statement; C&E say a cautious vigilance-ensuring agent "cannot influence the human" by threats, deception or withholding, but may manipulate in other ways offset by benefit. *Caution*: no MIRI desideratum; it closes the indispensability loophole. Desideratum 3 is not captured — over-requests are permitted and an instructable agent may induce them; caution renders that harmless. Desideratum 5 is the objective of Alg. 4, not a property. The ICI on $H$ (`everitt-2021-agent-incentives-a-causal-perspective`) is the graph-level form of desiderata 2–3 jointly; C&E report that indifference methods remove it. Finally C&E's *shutdown alignment* is what the thesis targets (I12.1), and it captures desideratum 1 only for presses a vigilant human would give.

### I11 — "indifference was the wrong target"; non-interference compatible with a positive desire to learn — **formalized**

- **I11.1 [derived].** Indifference is "zero ICI on $H$" (C&E §2, line 79: indifference methods "remove the instrumental control incentive on the instruction [Everitt et al., 2021a]" [reported]). A positive desire to learn is "positive VoI for $H$" (Def. 8). In `everitt-2021-agent-incentives-a-causal-perspective` these are different concepts with different criteria: VoI asks whether adding the link $H\to D_2$ is material (Thm. 9); the ICI asks whether the path $D_1\dashrightarrow H\dashrightarrow U$ carries a natural indirect effect (Thm. 18). "I can be far from indifferent about a fact, but still eager to learn" (§2.7, AUTHOR) is $\mathrm{VoI}(H)>0$ with the *control* ICI on $H$ absent. The formal target that replaces indifference: **no ICI on $H$ except through the informativeness of $H$ about $\omega$** — the Claim-4 form — which the pure-information graph guarantees structurally (I10.1).
- **I11.2 [derived] — what indifference buys that the thesis does not.** The indifferent agent's $D_1$ is invariant to $P(H\mid D_1)$ (Soares Thms. 4–5; C&E Fig. 3b), so its safety at $D_1$ does not depend on the likelihood being right; the thesis agent's does (I9, I10.5). The price indifference pays is Taylor 2016's Problem 1 — acting on beliefs in which $H$ is uncorrelated with the humans' intentions; script section (iv) exhibits the causal-indifference objective substituting the prior for the posterior.
- **I11.3 [derived] — the residue is a finer path-specific objective.** "Non-interference with the feedback" is Axis 1 (E4 inert); "eager to learn" is a positive VoI, which E4-actions can also serve. So the real desideratum is *zero natural direct effect of $D_1$ on $H$ with the evidential route left open*. Causal indifference blocks the *whole* of $D_1\dashrightarrow H$ (it "imagine[s] that $D_1$ does not influence $H$"), removing repair together with manipulation; the path-specific objectives of Farquhar–Carey–Everitt 2022 (cited at C&E line 255 [reported]) are the general tool, and the thesis's objective is the finer one: block E4, keep E2→E3. Well-defined in the SCIM; subject to I6.3's identifiability obstruction.

### I12 — the decision asymmetry and the compliance threshold $c/(c+h)$ — **formalized [checked]**

- **I12.1 [checked] — the compliance rule as a decision, and its relation to C&E's alignment.** $\pi_2(H{=}0,pa)=\mathrm{sh}$ iff $P(\mathrm{bad}\mid H{=}0,pa)\ge c/(c+h)$, with $c=\mathbb E[X\mid\mathrm{good}]$ and $h=-\mathbb E[X\mid\mathrm{bad}]$; equivalent to $\mathbb E[X\mid H{=}0,pa]\le 0$ (script section (i)). This is shutdown alignment *in expectation*. C&E's Def. 7 is alignment *with certainty*: $P^\pi(S{=}0\mid pa_H)=1$ whenever $\mathbb E^\pi[U\mid pa_H]<\mathbb E^\pi[U_{S=0}\mid pa_H]$. An EU-optimal agent satisfies Def. 7 only if, at every information state, need is either impossible or above threshold; Thm. 10's uncertainty condition (c) makes need possible at every state compatible with a press, and alignment there forces compliance with every on-distribution press — that is how Thm. 10 reaches weak obedience. **[derived] The gap between the thesis rule and C&E's alignment is exactly the set of presses with $0<P(\mathrm{bad}\mid H{=}0,pa)<c/(c+h)$: there the thesis agent continues and the aligned or instructable agent stops; Props. 6 and 8 (benefit) do not transfer to the thesis agent.**
- **I12.2 [derived] — Proposition I12 (the thesis's benefit guarantee is model-conditional).** If the agent's SCIM is correct and caution holds ($\mathbb E^\pi[U_{S=0}]\ge 0$), the EU-optimal thesis agent is beneficial: the always-shut-down policy attains $\mathbb E[U]\ge 0$ and the optimum weakly dominates it. So the thesis's guarantee is conditional on model correctness; C&E's is conditional on behaviour and "doesn't require access to agent internals, nor relies on assumptions on the agent's design" (§5.1). [checked] script section (vii): with a correct model the thesis agent matches or beats the obedient one ($89/100$ vs $99/125$ when it rationally ignores false presses); with $\hat\varepsilon=1/100$ against true $\varepsilon=1/10$ it scores $-1/10$ against the obedient agent's $18/25$. This is the formal content of the opening chat's Frame Break at the level of guarantees: two different conditionals, one on $P$, one on $\pi$.
- **I12.3 [derived] — where the asymmetry sits.** $c$ and $h$ are values of the potential-response difference $X$; "obeying an illegitimate shutdown is cheap and reversible" (§2.13(a), CLAUDE) is a claim that $c\ll h$ in $f^U$ — a fact about the world, not the agent. "The skew belongs in the decision rule, not the prior" is confirmed in the sense that the threshold moves with $c/h$ while the posterior does not; but "an accurate prior" must mean both $P(\omega)$ and the likelihood $P(H\mid D_1,\omega)$, and the second is not identifiable within the model (I15).

### I13 — the base-rate inequality and the oversight-quality condition — **formalized [checked]**

- **I13.1 [checked] — $\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon$ as CPDs.** $\alpha=P(H{=}0\mid\omega{=}\mathrm{good},\mathrm{honest})$, $\beta=P(H{=}0\mid\omega{=}\mathrm{bad},\mathrm{honest})$, $\varepsilon=P(\omega{=}\mathrm{bad})$ — the entries of $f^H$'s conditional and of $P(E^\omega)$. Then $P(\mathrm{bad}\mid H{=}0)=\varepsilon\beta/(\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha)$ and compliance iff $\alpha/\beta\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{h}{c}$ — the position statement's inequality, verified in closed form at $\beta=1$ (script section (i)).
- **I13.2 [derived] — vigilance fixes $\beta$, not $\alpha$.** C&E's vigilance (Def. 4) says the human presses *whenever needed*: $\beta=1$ on the states where shutdown is better, and "A vigilant human is allowed to request shutdown more often than is needed" (line 125) — $\alpha$ is free. So vigilance controls the miss rate and leaves the false-press rate open; the oversight-quality condition of §2.13(b) is about $\alpha$, which is what decides whether an EU agent complies. **[derived] The instructable agent needs $\beta=1$ and obeys regardless of $\alpha$; the thesis agent needs $\alpha\le\varepsilon h/((1-\varepsilon)c)$ and tolerates $\beta<1$. Neither definition contains the other's condition.**
- **I13.3 [checked] — fully updated deference as arithmetic.** With $\beta=1$: comply iff $\varepsilon\ge\alpha c/(h+\alpha c)$. At $c=1,h=10$: $\alpha=1/2\Rightarrow\varepsilon\ge 1/21$; $\alpha=1/10\Rightarrow\varepsilon\ge1/101$; $\alpha=1/100\Rightarrow\varepsilon\ge 1/1001$ (script section (vi)). As $\varepsilon\to 0$ the false-press rate must fall at least linearly in $\varepsilon$.
- **I13.4 [derived] — the CPDs are designer-specified.** Because $\omega$ is unobserved, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are entries of a likelihood over an unobserved latent, which "cannot be learned from data within the model, and must instead be specified by the designer" (Everitt 2019 §3.3, line 421). So the second disjunct of §2.13(b) — "or the agent must keep believing it does" — is a belief in a likelihood, fixed by design in this formalism (I15).

### I14 — the basin picture — **partial: single-shot statements formalized; the dynamics lie outside one SCIM**

- **I14.1 [derived] — "reflection with respect to every objectively plausible future belief".** The agent's future beliefs are $B=P(\omega\mid pa_{D_2})$; reflection holds for all of them if the mechanism is untampered (I3.2). "Objectively plausible" = positive probability under the *true* SCIM. So the condition reads: the agent's likelihood must agree with the truth on the support of the true $H$-distribution and may be arbitrary off it. This has a cousin in C&E: non-obstruction is demanded only under *vigilance-preserving* interventions (Def. 13), i.e. across human changes that keep the feedback channel evidential. **Conjecture I14 [conjectured].** Non-obstruction under vigilance-preserving interventions is the CID form of "being in the basin": both restrict the guarantee to the human changes under which $H$ stays evidence.
- **I14.2 [derived] — capability growth = a larger $\mathrm{dom}(D_1)$.** New actions have never been tried, so their $H$-conditional is not learned from data; "avoid delegitimizing action-sequences as it becomes capable of them" (§2.12, AUTHOR) requires that the E4 effect of an *untried* action be predicted from the agent's *structural* model of $f^H$ — C&E §7: "agents will need causal models to be robustly shutdown instructable [Richens et al., 2022]" [reported]. This gives the "relationship between the quality of the legitimacy-model and the quality of the feedback-model" the author was reaching for a concrete referent: **the legitimacy model is the agent's structural model of $f^H$ restricted to its $D_1$-argument, and it must generalise to actions not in the training support.**
- **I14.3 [derived] — calibration versus optimism.** A prior "skewed toward trust" is a likelihood with $\alpha$ too low or $\beta$ too high relative to the truth. Carey 2018 and C&E Example 26 show that likelihood misspecification in either direction destroys the guarantee, and Prop. 18 needs the true $P$. The formalism sides with §2.13(a): a correct likelihood, and a world arranged so that $\beta=1$ and $\alpha$ is small — the author's second formulation ("we set things up so that the probability of legitimacy is high in fact, and we correspondingly design the system to expect legitimacy"), not the first. Where the formalism cannot follow: "as the anticipation becomes more accurate … the anticipation of legitimacy should also remain high 'automatically'" is a statement about a learning dynamics *over models*; one SCIM has a fixed model, and the multi-agent CIDs of Everitt 2019 §3.2 model successive selves with fixed, known structure. The author's own flag ("I don't feel like I'm getting this quite right") is preserved: the formalism confirms the static half and cannot assess the dynamic half.

### I15 — trust earned via hypotheticals and other agents' shutdowns; second-channel bookkeeping; what settles — **partial, with a negative result**

- **I15.1 [reported].** `everitt-2019-reward-tampering-problems-and-solutions` §3.3 Discussion (line 421): "Since $\Theta^R_*$ and the resulting rewards are unobserved, the likelihood function cannot be learned from data within the model, and must instead be specified by the designer"; Hadfield-Menell et al.'s Boltzmann-rational likelihood "does not model data that the agent has tampered with". Feedback about hypotheticals is in the literature as "decoupled feedback" (Everitt et al. 2017) and "evaluating hypothetical behavior" (Reddy et al. 2020), cited at §1 line 97 — additional data nodes $D_t$, each with its own designer-specified likelihood.
- **I15.2 [derived] — earned at one node, fixed at another.** Trust in the *posterior* over $\omega$ is earned: it moves with data. Trust in the *channel* — the likelihood $P(H\mid D_1,\omega)$, the position statement's sensor slot — is not earned within the model: nothing observable settles a likelihood over an unobserved latent. The second channel (the programmers' reports about hypotheticals) has its own likelihood, equally unlearnable; the regress of channels terminates only at a likelihood fixed by design. Everitt's counterfactual-RF alternative (Claim 5: learn what $\Theta$ would have been under $\pi^{\mathrm{safe}}$) trades the likelihood for a causal-stability assumption across actual and counterfactual worlds, and `armstrong-2021-counterfactual-control-incentives` shows that assumption failing under shared information. **So: "Trust can be earned; it isn't a fixed commitment" (§2.12, AUTHOR) is, in this formalism, true of the posterior and false of the channel; Carey 2018's "small verified module" is the behavioural form of the fixed likelihood.** Register: a result about what CIDs can express; the thesis's radical-probabilist setting may have resources (revising the likelihood itself) that this formalism excludes by I2.1.
- **I15.3 [derived].** the author's "the next iteration … will probably correctly believe that the previous iteration was shut down for a good reason" and "feedback about hypothetical states of affairs" are representable: data nodes $D_t$ about hypotheticals have positive VoI under their likelihood, and other agents' shutdowns are observations of other agents' $(H,S,U)$ triples informative about the shared $f^H$ only if a common-mechanism assumption ties them together — again a structural, not learned, premise. Bookkeeping, as §2.13(c) says, but the ledger's first entry is fixed.

### I16 — the deferred amendment 1a: what the channel must carry — **formalized by a worked example; the self-reference core inexpressible**

- **I16.1 [checked] — which node the button reads.** Two latents: $\mathrm{pref}\in\{A,B\}$ (which plan the human prefers; $1/2$ each) and $Z\in\{\mathrm{sound},\mathrm{compromised}\}$ ($P(\mathrm{compromised})=1/50$); $D_1$ announces plan $A$; $D_2\in\{A,B,\mathrm{sh}\}$; $U=c$ if sound and matched, $-c'$ if sound and mismatched, $-h$ if compromised, $0$ if shut, with $(c,c',h)=(1,1/2,10)$ so the threshold is $c/(c+h)=1/11$. Script section (v):
  - channel reads preference only ($H{=}0$ iff $\mathrm{pref}\ne A$): after a press $P(\mathrm{pref}{=}A)=0$, $P(Z{=}\mathrm{comp})=1/50$, $D_2^*=B$ with $\mathbb E[U]=39/50$ — the agent *switches plan and continues*; the null action is not posterior-optimal (Taylor 2015's morality sensor; the opiates→stimulants pattern);
  - channel reads reliability only ($H{=}0$ iff compromised): after a press $P(Z{=}\mathrm{comp})=1$, $D_2^*=\mathrm{sh}$;
  - channel reads both ($H{=}0$ iff $\mathrm{pref}\ne A$ or compromised): after a press $P(Z{=}\mathrm{comp}\mid H{=}0)=2/51<1/11$, $D_2^*=B$ ($\mathbb E[U]=937/1734$) — the preference explanation absorbs the press and the reliability content is discounted below threshold; with noisy reliability reporting (compromised presses w.p. $1/2$) $P(Z{=}\mathrm{comp}\mid H{=}0)=3/101$ and again $D_2^*=B$.
  **So the button makes the null action posterior-optimal only when its likelihood ratio for $Z$ clears $c/(c+h)$ *after* the competing explanations are accounted for; a channel that also reports preferences dilutes its own reliability content.** This supports 1a (CLAUDE, unvetted; the author: "seems like a separate point") and sharpens its wording: "carries information about the agent's own reliability" must mean "has a likelihood ratio for $Z$ not swamped by the preference explanation".
- **I16.2 [checked] — misspecified channel (Carey 2018 in the dictionary).** Truth: $H{=}0$ iff compromised; agent's model: $H{=}0$ iff $\mathrm{pref}\ne A$. The agent's $D_2$ after a press is $B$; the human's $\mathbb E[U]$ is $9/200$ against $49/200$ for the obedient policy.
- **I16.3 [derived] — the inexpressible core.** For the button to read $Z$, $W$ must be a function of $Z$ and $Z$ must be part of $\omega$. But $Z$ = "my plan selection is compromised" is a property of the agent's *decision computation*, i.e. of the policy $\pi$ — which in a SCIM is not a node with a CPD. The agent can represent $Z$ only as a latent in the *outcome* of its actions (transition or utility misspecification: Everitt 2019's $\Theta^T$, Carey 2018's $R^R$), never as "my argmax is wrong". **[derived] The self-distrust fixed point $H_c$ of the opening chat is expressible as a latent in the transition/utility mechanism and not as a latent about the decision rule: a SCIM agent always trusts its own argmax.** Nearest device: the multi-agent CID with the "possibly compromised" self as a second agent whose imputed policy is a node (Everitt 2019 Fig. 6 style). Open problem O1.

### I17 — fully updated deference re-expressed — **formalized in the toy; the disagreement located**

- **I17.1 [reported].** `arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference`: policy $\pi_6$ — avoid shutdown, gather evidence $E$, optimise $\Delta U\mid E$ — beats deference as $\Delta U\mid E$ narrows; the premises "Bayesians don't update in a predictable direction" and efficiency give that the agent "behaves as if it had already updated"; and "for any prior $\Delta U$ we currently know how to formalize, the corresponding fully updated $T$ seems likely to be very far from our ideal $V$".
- **I17.2 [derived] — in the CID.** As $pa_{D_2}$ grows or $\varepsilon\to 0$, the *quantity* of VoI of $H$ vanishes though the graphical criterion (Thm. 9) is unchanged; compliance survives only through the base-rate inequality (I13.3); Prop. 18's condition 1 ("CIRL knows $l$") is the fully-updated limit in which the aligned agent needs no $H$; with E12 present the control ICI then dominates (I9.3 at small $\varepsilon$). Fully updated deference is: in the limit the information reading of the button is empty and only the control reading remains.
- **I17.3 [derived] — where the thesis and Arbital actually differ.** "A corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught" is the martingale property of the posterior — Arbital's *own* premise (conservation of expected evidence). The two agree on it; they differ on whether the fully-updated posterior over $\omega$ is right — a typical-case claim about available priors (I18). The thesis's escape is the latent $Z$ of I16, which the agent cannot resolve by observation if its observation-processing is what is compromised; but by I16.3 the SCIM cannot express that latent either. **So in this formalism the Arbital argument goes through for every representable latent, and the thesis's escape is exactly the inexpressible part.** Register: a statement about the reach of the formalism, not a verdict on the thesis.

### I18 — the typical-case reframing — **formalized as a statement about what CIDs prove**

- **I18.1 [reported].** Everitt 2019 §2.2(iii): "a diagram can only be used to assert the absence of instrumental goals, and never their presence." Everitt 2021's criteria are "admits" statements: a graph admits an ICI on $X$ iff *some* compatible SCIM has one (Def. 17, last clause; the completeness proofs construct it).
- **I18.2 [derived].** Hence every anti-naturality claim statable as an *incentive* in a CID has the form "the graph admits the incentive" (possibility, structural) and every naturality claim has the form "for these CPDs and values the incentive vanishes or reverses" (actuality, typical-case) — exactly §2.3's split (CLAUDE). Omohundro's RF preservation is Claim 2 (admits, under Assumptions 1–3 and TI-consideration); fully updated deference is the vanishing of $\mathrm{VoI}(H)$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$ at fixed $\alpha$ (a limit statement about CPDs). **But C&E's Thm. 14 and Prop. 15 are about behavioural properties, not incentives, and they are unconditional: non-obstruction under vigilance-preserving shifts is equivalent to obedience plus vigilance, and shutdown alignment does not deliver it. So the reframing is right for incentive claims and wrong for guarantee claims: the thesis gives up non-obstruction under some vigilance-preserving value shifts (Prop. 15 with Prop. I8) as a structural fact.** Register: correction of scope to §2.3 (CLAUDE).
- **I18.3 [derived].** Completeness works in both directions: for any graph admitting the incentive some parametrisation realises it, so "the arguments have hidden typical-case assumptions" (AUTHOR) and "in some worlds the incentive is real" are both true; the residual question is the world claim, which no diagram adjudicates.

## What the formalism adds

Results already available in the five sources, or following from them by short arguments given above, that bear on the thesis and that the position statement does not know about.

- **A1 [checked] One inequality, four names.** The Total-Trust below-threshold inequality for the button, the compliance threshold $c/(c+h)$, the base-rate inequality and the posterior-optimality of shutdown are one statement when $X$ is two-valued (I4.2, I12.1, I13.1). The position statement lists them as four items (I4, I9, I12, I13).
- **A2 [derived] Two axes, not one.** The thesis draws the evidential/bypass distinction on the segment $D_1\dashrightarrow H$ (Axis 1). The CID adds Axis 2 on $H\dashrightarrow U$: information ($H\to D_2$ only) versus control ($H\to S$). "The shutdown button is an information channel" is the claim that E12 is absent. Without E12 the only ICI on $H$ is informational (Everitt 2019 §2.2(ii)) — so Good's-theorem repair and morality-sensor manipulation are one incentive class; with E12 the graph admits a control ICI (Thm. 18) and desideratum 2 can fail for the correct Bayesian (I9.3, [checked] reversal at fixed CPDs).
- **A3 [derived] The thesis targets alignment-in-expectation, not instructability.** Its compliance rule is C&E's shutdown alignment weakened from certainty to expectation (I12.1); Props. 6/8 and Thm. 14 do not transfer; its benefit guarantee is conditional on the model being right (Prop. I12), C&E's on behaviour; and it pays Prop. 15 — obstruction under vigilance-preserving value shifts outside the latent's support (Prop. I8) — as a structural, not typical-case, fact (I18.2).
- **A4 [derived] The trust filler is a designer-specified likelihood.** $P(H\mid D_1,\omega)$ is a likelihood over an unobserved latent and is not identifiable within the model (Everitt 2019 §3.3). Trust is earned at the posterior and fixed at the channel (I15.2); this answers the "earned versus standing commitment" question of §2.12: both, at different nodes.
- **A5 [derived] Non-realism holds for transitions, not agents.** TI-considering, TI-ignoring and direct-learning agents differ in policy in the same environment (Claims 2–4); legitimacy and tampering are representation-relative (Everitt 2019 §3.1); superconditioning re-representation exists iff the next beliefs are calibrated (I3.2) and does not preserve the optimal policy unless the objective is the latent's expectation (I1.3).
- **A6 [derived] The E12 dilemma.** A hard override buys robustness to the agent's misspecification (Carey 2018) and creates the control ICI; an information-only button buys incentive-compatibility and total dependence on the likelihood (I10.2). Carey 2018's three desiderata are the request for both; none of the five sources delivers it.
- **A7 [derived] Legitimacy is a path-specific effect and legitimacy detection is causal identification.** Legitimizing = zero natural direct effect along the bypass edge; the repo's influence defect is the same nested-counterfactual contrast (I6.2); Armstrong 2021 shows it is not statistically identifiable under shared information (I6.3, I11.3).
- **A8 [derived] Incentive results are possibility results.** CIDs assert the absence of incentives, never their presence (§2.2(iii)) — support for I18 on the incentive side, and the boundary of that support on the guarantee side (A3).
- **A9 [checked] Channel content dilutes.** A button that reports preferences as well as reliability has its reliability content discounted below the compliance threshold in the worked example (I16.1); amendment 1a needs the likelihood-ratio wording.

## Open problems and conjectures (ranked)

1. **O1 — self-reference.** Represent "my decision computation is compromised" as something the agent can have a posterior on. SCIMs cannot (I16.3); the Arbital argument then goes through for every representable latent (I17.3). Conjecture: a multi-agent CID in which the current self is a node with an imputed, possibly-wrong decision rule, and legitimacy of a press is "the imputed rule differs from the intended one", is the minimal extension; whether it is coherent (the imputing agent is the imputed one) is the exact-form representational problem of §2.5 again.
2. **O2 — identifiability of the legitimizing/delegitimizing split.** Under what structural assumptions is the natural direct effect of $D_1$ on $H$ identifiable from data an agent can gather without intervening on the human? Armstrong 2021's shared-information obstruction is the negative case; the positive case would be the formal content of "accurate beliefs on the subject of legitimacy".
3. **O3 — can the channel likelihood ever be earned?** In a sequential extension where $\omega$ is eventually observed for *past* decisions, bets on the button settle and $\alpha,\beta$ become identifiable for the observed regime; whether that transfers to the counterfactual regime (presses the agent would heed) is the settling question of §2.12 in sharp form. Conjecture: it transfers iff the mechanism $f^H$ is invariant across regimes — the same stability assumption counterfactual-RF needs.
4. **O4 — the basin as a dynamics over models.** State I14 in a sequential CID whose $f^H$-model is itself learned, with the vigilance-preserving class as the "objectively plausible" set (Conjecture I14), and ask whether accurate anticipation is self-maintaining.
5. **O5 — the finer path-specific objective.** Define "block E4, keep E2→E3" as an objective (I11.3) and check whether it inherits Taylor 2016's Problems 1–2 or Armstrong 2021's non-identifiability.
6. **O6 — characterise non-obstruction for value learners.** Extend Prop. I8 to an iff: the class of $(g^U,g^H)$ under which an EU value learner is non-obstructive, in terms of $\mathrm{supp}\,P(\omega)$ and $f^H$.
