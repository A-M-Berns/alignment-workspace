## Setting

The formalism, stated once so every item below can point at it. Finite or countable $\Omega$ is assumed wherever a claim is marked [checked]; the general (Polish-space) case is carried by the cited theorems and marked [reconstructed].

**S0 — Total worlds (the self-referential space).** A measurable space $(\Omega, \mathcal F)$ of *total worlds*. A world $\omega$ fixes the external state $w(\omega) \in W$, the value latent $\theta(\omega) \in \Theta$ (item I1), every third-person event (actions, presses, evidence submissions), and the agent's own total credence at every time, $P_t(\omega) \in \Delta(\Omega,\mathcal F)$ for $t \in T = \{0,1,2,\dots\}$. So $\mathcal F$ contains events about the agent's own future credences, including their credences about later credences; whether such a space exists is item I7 (answer: yes as a measurable space, with a precise obstruction for exactness).

**S1 — Credence sequence and anticipation.** Each $P_t : \Omega \to \Delta(\Omega)$ is measurable. The *anticipation* of the next state is the pushforward $\mu_t := P_t \circ P_{t+1}^{-1} \in \Delta(\Delta(\Omega))$; the joint law of $(\omega, P_{t+1})$ under $P_t$ is the object every legitimacy notion below is predicated on. **Introspection (INT):** for every $t$ and every $\rho$ in the range of $P_t$, $\rho(\{\omega : P_t(\omega) = \rho\}) = 1$ — the agent knows its own current total credence. INT is used only where flagged (the exact equivalences of I4); in logical induction it holds only asymptotically (`reflection-in-li` §"Between modest and immodest").

**S2 — Radical probabilism (Jeffrey).** Nothing requires $P_{t+1} = P_t(\cdot \mid E)$ for some $E \in \mathcal F$: the environment "pushes the mental state around however it does" (AUTHOR, position statement §1). The formalism records only the joint law of $(\omega, P_{t+1})$ under $P_t$; item I3 shows this joint is itself a Bayesian model of the transition (superconditioning), and item I5 shows where that model fails to endorse its own output.

**S3 — Reflection, five statements of one idea.** For $t_1 < t_2$, all with respect to $P := P_{t_1}$ and the random credence $Q := P_{t_2}$:

- **(R-val)** value form (van Fraassen 1984 [reconstructed]): for every $\varphi \in \mathcal F$ and every $c$ with $P(Q(\varphi) = c) > 0$,
$$P_{t_1}\big(\varphi \mid P_{t_2}(\varphi) = c\big) = c .$$
- **(R-fun)** function form (the "Reflection" of Dorst–Levinstein–Salow–Husic–Fitelson, henceforth DDB [reconstructed]): for every $\rho$ in the support of $\mu_{t_1}$, $P_{t_1}(\cdot \mid P_{t_2} = \rho) = \rho$ as measures on $\mathcal F$.
- **(Mart)** expectation form = Armstrong's *sequential unbiasedness* (`armstrong-2016-double-indifference-is-better-indifference` §"Sequentially unbiased estimators", local text: "for all $i<j$, and all $X$, $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\mathbb E_{\rho_j}X = \mathbb E_{\rho_i}X$"): for all bounded measurable $X$,
$$\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big[\,\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}}[X]\,\big] = \mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}[X] .$$
- **(TT)** Total Trust (DDB [reconstructed]; the corpus's pair of one-sided inequalities): for all bounded $X$ and all $v \in \mathbb R$, the above-threshold inequality $\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big[X\cdot\mathbf 1\{\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}}[X] > v\}\big] \ge v\,P_{t_1}\big(\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}}[X] > v\big)$ and the below-threshold inequality $\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big[X\cdot\mathbf 1\{\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}}[X] < v\}\big] \le v\,P_{t_1}\big(\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}}[X] < v\big)$.
- **(Acc$_\varphi$)** expected-accuracy non-decrease, proposition by proposition: for every strictly proper loss $\ell$, $\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big[\ell(P_{t_2}(\varphi), \mathbf 1_\varphi)\big] \le \mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big[\ell(P_{t_1}(\varphi), \mathbf 1_\varphi)\big]$.

Item I4 proves the finite-case relations: under INT, (R-fun) ⟺ (R-val) ⟺ (Mart) ⟺ (TT) (⟺ DDB's Value), and each ⟹ (Acc$_\varphi$), with (Acc$_\varphi$) strictly weaker (a checked separating example). Without INT the forms separate, as DDB's modest experts show.

**S4 — Superconditioning (Diaconis–Zabell 1982 [reconstructed], stated and proved in I3).** $Q$ is a *superconditioning* of $P$ if there is a space $(\Omega^*, \mathcal F^*, P^*)$ with a measurable projection $\pi:\Omega^*\to\Omega$, $P^*\circ\pi^{-1} = P$, and an event $E \in \mathcal F^*$ with $P^*(E) > 0$ and $P^*(\pi^{-1}(\cdot)\mid E) = Q$.

**S5 — Legitimacy events (I5) and legitimizing third-person events (I6).** $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi} \in \mathcal F$; minimal viability is (R-val) conditional on $L$. A third-person $E \in \mathcal F$ is *legitimizing* for $(t_2,\varphi)$ when conditioning on $E$ restores or raises the probability of the conditional-reflection equation (position statement 2.11, CLAUDE).

**S6 — Decisions.** At a decision time the agent picks $a$ maximizing $\mathbb E_{P_t}[V \mid a]$, where $V(\omega, a, \ldots) = U_{\theta(\omega)}(\ldots)$ is the value function under the latent $\theta$ (I1). Actions enter as conditioning on the joint $P_t(\omega, \text{signals} \mid a)$, as in the MIRI three-step model of position statement 2.8, which is the button model used in I9–I13.

**Sources for the setting.** van Fraassen 1984 "Belief and the Will" [reconstructed]; Jeffrey's radical probabilism and Skyrms's dynamic-coherence characterisation of legitimate black-box updates as martingales [reconstructed]; Diaconis–Zabell 1982 "Updating Subjective Probability" [reconstructed]; DDB 2021 "Deference Done Better" [reconstructed]; `armstrong-2016-double-indifference-is-better-indifference` (local); `reflection-in-li` (local wiki, UNVETTED by the author); `legitimacy-theory-v1` (local, UNVETTED — its §2 "influence defect" is the closest prior formalization of legitimacy in the repo and is compared in I6 and "What the formalism adds").

### I1 — The value-change ⇄ belief-change transform

**Definition (the transform).** Let $\{U_\theta\}_{\theta\in\Theta}$ be a family of utility functions on outcomes. Make $\theta$ a coordinate of the total world (S0) and set $V(\omega, a) := U_{\theta(\omega)}(w(\omega), a)$. Decision by linear expectation: choose $a$ maximizing $\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\cdot,a)]$, which under $\theta \perp w$ equals $\sum_\theta P_t(\theta)\,\mathbb E_{P_t}[U_\theta(\cdot,a)]$ — "linear expectation over this uncertainty" (AUTHOR §1).

**I1.1 [checked, one line].** For utilities $U, U'$ put $\Theta=\{0,1\}$, $U_0=U$, $U_1=U'$, $q := P_t(\theta=1)$. The $U$-maximizer is the $q=0$ agent, the $U'$-maximizer the $q=1$ agent, and "utility change $U\to U'$" is the credence change $q: 0\to 1$ on $\sigma(\theta)$. Every utility change is a belief change, as the thesis says.

**I1.2 [derived].** The endpoints $q=0$ and $q=1$ are dogmatic. A transition $q:0\to 1$ raises a $P_t$-null event to positive probability and is therefore illegitimate under *every* anticipation (I2.2). **Transformed, the Gandhi-pill argument says: a dogmatic value-prior cannot legitimately change — which is exactly the case non-dogmatism excludes.** For $0<q<1$ a change $q\to q'$ is an ordinary credence change and I3–I5 govern it. This sharpens AGREED 2.1: the examples that drive the anti-naturality intuition are the dogmatic ones.

**I1.3 [checked (rescaling); reconstructed (general theorem)] — the formal basis of the non-realism in AUTHOR 2.2.** For any $f>0$ with $\mathbb E_{P}[f]=1$, the pair $(P', V')$ with $dP' = f\,dP$ and $V' = V/f$ ranks every act exactly as $(P,V)$ does, since $\mathbb E_{P'}[V'(\cdot,a)] = \mathbb E_P[V(\cdot,a)]$. So one change in the agent can be written as a change of $P$ with $V$ fixed, of $V$ with $P$ fixed, or both. This is the state-dependent-utility non-identifiability of Karni–Schmeidler–Vind / Drèze [reconstructed]; the one-line rescaling is all this file uses.

**I1.4 [derived] — where the transform stops.** It converts utility change into credence change only under *linear* aggregation over $\theta$. A non-linear rule ("or perhaps some other rule", AUTHOR §1 — geometric aggregation, or incomplete preferences as in Thornley) is not an EU state on $\Omega$, so its changes are not credence changes. Everything below assumes linear expectation.

**I1.5 [derived; CLAUDE, contestable] — the latent is a modelling choice with behavioural consequences.** Reading (a): $\theta$ = the true values, the humans' actual values being *evidence* about $\theta$. Reading (b): $\theta$ = the humans' actual values. Under (a) a change in the humans is evidence and is welcome when legitimate; under (b) it changes the target and the agent evaluates it by its *current* $\mathbb E_{P_t}[V]$, so $U(\arg\max U) \ge U(\arg\max U')$ reappears aimed at the humans' values. The non-realism of 2.2 is right about the agent's own belief/value split; the identity of the latent is not a matter of representation. This file uses (a).

**Verdict: formalized**, with I1.2 as the result the position statement lacks (the Gandhi case *is* the dogmatic case) and I1.4–I1.5 as the two qualifications.

### I2 — Radical-probabilist dynamics

Jeffrey's radical probabilism, as in S2: $(P_t)$ is any measurable credence sequence; the value prior $P_t\circ\theta^{-1}$, the anticipation $\mu_t$, and (with the limits of I3.5) the algebra are all revisable.

**I2.1 [reconstructed] (Skyrms's dynamic-coherence theorem).** For an anticipated black-box transition with outcomes $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_k$ and anticipated probabilities $q_i$, the agent is immune to a diachronic Dutch book iff each $\rho_i$ is $P_t$'s conditional on "I end in state $i$" — i.e. iff (R-fun) holds. (Skyrms 1987 "Dynamic coherence and probability kinematics", van Fraassen 1984 for the reflection direction.) Not re-derived here; I3.3 reaches the same condition from superconditioning.

**I2.2 [checked, one line] (zero→positive is a theorem).** If $P_t(A)=0$ and $\rho(A)>0$ then $P_t(A \mid P_{t+1}=\rho) \le P_t(A)/P_t(P_{t+1}=\rho) = 0 \ne \rho(A)$, so (R-fun) fails for every anticipation; with $\varphi=A$ the same line gives $P_{t}(A \mid P_{t+1}(A)=c) = 0 \ne c$ for every $c>0$, so (R-val) fails too. The author's "sole exception" (2.2) is *forced* by reflection, not stipulated beside it — and reflection forces more (I3.3).

**I2.3 [derived].** Non-dogmatism — $P_t(A)>0$ for every consistent $A$ in a countable algebra — is exactly what keeps I2.2 from excluding a *correct* correction: the true hypothesis has positive prior. It is necessary, not sufficient, for such a correction to be legitimizable (I8).

**Verdict: formalized.**

### I3 — Superconditioning; the meta-belief observation

**Theorem I3.1 (Diaconis–Zabell 1982) [statement reconstructed; proof given, so the finite case is checked].** $Q$ is a superconditioning of $P$ (S4) iff $Q \ll P$ with $\|dQ/dP\|_\infty<\infty$; on a finite space, iff $Q\ll P$.
*Proof.* (⇐) Let $r=dQ/dP$, $\lambda = 1/\|r\|_\infty$, $\Omega^* = \Omega\times\{E,E^c\}$, $P^*(\omega,E)=P(\omega)\lambda r(\omega)$, $P^*(\omega,E^c) = P(\omega)(1-\lambda r(\omega))$ — nonnegative because $\lambda r\le 1$, with marginal $P$, $P^*(E)=\lambda$, and $P^*(\omega\mid E)=Q(\omega)$. (⇒) $Q(A) = P^*(\pi^{-1}A\mid E)\le P(A)/P^*(E)$, so $Q\ll P$ with density $\le 1/P^*(E)$. ∎ Instantiated in `s2` (a): $P=(\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\tfrac16)$, $Q=(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14)$, $\lambda=\tfrac23$, complementary branch $(1,0,0)$, mixture identity exact [checked].
*Remark [checked in the example].* The complementary branch $E^c$ carries posterior $(P-\lambda Q)/(1-\lambda) \ne P$ in general: a superconditioning of one target is never "move to $Q$ or stay put" — the stay-put branch is also an update.

**Definition I3.2 (the meta-belief observation).** The author attributes to Sam Eisenstat the observation that the enlarged algebra can be taken to be meta-beliefs, the observation event being "you believe this now" (AUTHOR §1). *In this file's terms* — and everything from here on is my elaboration, ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED as to what Eisenstat meant — take the enlarged space to be the self-referential $\Omega$ of S0 itself, the observation event $E_\rho := \{P_{t+1}=\rho\}\in\mathcal F$, and the environment's push to be the kernel $K(\rho\mid\omega)$; the superconditioning space is the joint $P_t(\omega, P_{t+1}=\rho) = P_t(\omega)K(\rho\mid\omega)$.

**Theorem I3.3 [checked, finite; `s2` (a)–(e)] (what the representation represents).** Let the anticipation have outcomes $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_k$ with $q_i = P_t(E_{\rho_i})>0$.
(a) $\sum_i q_i\,P_t(\cdot\mid E_{\rho_i}) = P_t$ identically.
(b) The representation **endorses its own output** — $P_t(\cdot\mid E_{\rho_i})=\rho_i$ for all $i$, which is (R-fun) — **iff** $\sum_i q_i\rho_i = P_t$, the martingale condition (Mart) on the anticipation. (⇒) by (a). (⇐) put $K(i\mid\omega) := q_i\rho_i(\omega)/P_t(\omega)$; the mixture identity makes each row sum to one with nonnegative entries, and then $P_t(\omega\mid E_i)=\rho_i(\omega)$.
(c) When (Mart) fails the representation still exists — the joint is a probability space and $E_{\rho_i}$ an event — but $P_t(\cdot\mid E_{\rho_i}) \ne \rho_i$ for some $i$: the conditional is the **legitimate correction** of $\rho_i$, the belief the agent should hold given that it will in fact hold $\rho_i$. `s2` (b): a world-independent push to $Q$ has correction $P$ (the push carries no information); `s2` (c): an informative push that overshoots to $Q^{\mathrm{over}}$ has correction $Q$.
(d) Zero→positive is never endorsed (I2.2).
**Consequence [derived].** Legitimacy of a realized transition $P\to\rho$ is not a property of the pair $(P,\rho)$: every $\rho\ll P$ with bounded density is endorsed under *some* anticipation (I3.1 supplies one) and none under *every* anticipation. Legitimacy lives in the anticipation $K$ — the agent's model of how the world pushes it — which is the "sensor slot" of position statement 2.9, and is the one-line reason `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.3 had to make legitimacy a provenance condition rather than a trace condition.

**I3.4 [derived] (what "everything is allowed to change" means).** Any single target $\rho\ll P_t$ is reachable by a legitimate anticipated update; an anticipated *distribution* over targets is legitimate iff it is a martingale. The thesis sentence "any accuracy-increasing modification … just have to obey the reflection principle" (AUTHOR §1) is right about the reachable *set* and needs (Mart) for the *process*.

**I3.5 [checked; `s2` (f)] (sigma-algebra edits).** For a refinement $\mathcal F\subset\mathcal F'$ the new belief $\rho'$ is judged by $P_t$ only through its restriction to $\mathcal F$: the coarse verdict is independent of how $P_t$ is extended to $\mathcal F'$, the fine verdict depends on an arbitrary extension (two extensions agree on the coarse verdict and disagree on the fine one in `s2`). For coarsening or incomparable change (concept replacement) $P_t$ has no opinion on the new events; the nearest expressible statement is reflection on $\mathcal F\cap\mathcal F'$. **"Corrigible to edits of the sigma algebra" (AUTHOR 2.2) is expressible for the old algebra under refinement and inexpressible for the genuinely new part** — which is where I8 lives.

**I3.6 [derived] (the embedded-agency reading, said precisely).** Classical observation conditions on an event of the world-algebra received through a boundary; the meta-belief model conditions on an event of $\sigma(P_{t+1})$, a coordinate of the total world. They coincide exactly when $P_{t+1}=P_t(\cdot\mid o)$ for a boundary observation $o$ (the partitions generated by $E_\rho$ and by $o$ then agree up to null sets); they differ when the environment acts on the mind other than through the boundary — which is precisely when legitimacy is a live question.

**Verdict: formalized** (I3.1, I3.3), with I3.5 marking the inexpressible part.

### I4 — Legitimacy = reflection / Total Trust / increase in expected accuracy: the criterion and the relations

Frame: finite $\Omega$, INT for $P_{t_2}$ (S1). DDB is quoted from the local copy `research/references/deference-done-better/Deference Done Better.md` (their prior $\pi$ is $P_{t_1}$, their expert $P$ is $P_{t_2}$); all DDB citations below are [reported] with line numbers.

**DDB's definitions [reported].** Reflection, l. 75: "$\pi(\cdot\mid P=\rho)=\rho$" — this file's (R-fun). Modesty, l. 80: "it's impossible for any distribution to reflect a frame while assigning non-zero probability to the possibility that the expert is modest" — so (R-fun) toward one's future self presupposes INT (immodesty) of that self. New Reflection, l. 86: "$\pi(\cdot\mid P=\rho) = \rho(\cdot\mid P=\rho)$", "equivalent to Reflection when the expert is certain to be immodest" (l. 89). Value, l. 121: "If $S$ is recommended for $\mathcal O$, then for all $O\in\mathcal O$: $E_\pi(S)\ge E_\pi(O)$." L. 128: "If $\pi$ reflects a frame, it values it. And if a frame is immodest, $\pi$ reflects it if and only if $\pi$ values it. But this is not true in general." Total Trust, l. 175: "For any variable $X$: $E_\pi(X\mid E(X)\ge t)\ge t$", symmetric form l. 180 — the corpus's above-threshold / below-threshold inequalities (this file's (TT) uses strict thresholds; on a finite frame the two are interchangeable by taking limits in $t$). Theorem 2.2, l. 186: "$\pi$ totally trusts $\langle W,\mathcal P\rangle$ iff $\pi$ values $\langle W,\mathcal P\rangle$." Convexity version, l. 221: "$\pi(\cdot\mid P\in C)\in C$, for any convex $C$" is equivalent to Reflection.

**Theorem I4.1 [derived; proofs below; sanity-checked on 60 random models in `s6`] (collapse under introspection).** On a finite $\Omega$ with INT, the following are equivalent: (R-fun); (R-val) for all events; **(R-var)**: $\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big[X\mid \mathbb E_{P_{t_2}}[X]=s\big]=s$ for all bounded $X$ and all $s$ in the support; (Mart) for all bounded $X$; (TT) for all bounded $X$. Each is equivalent to DDB's Value by their Theorem 2.2.
*(i)* (R-fun) ⟹ (R-var) ⟹ (R-val), and (R-var) ⟹ (Mart): condition on the state, resp. take expectations.
*(ii)* (R-val) ⟹ (R-fun) [uses INT]. Fix a state $\rho$ with $P_{t_1}(P_{t_2}=\rho)>0$ and an atom $\omega$ of the branch $\{P_{t_2}=\rho\}$ with $\rho(\omega)<1$. Put $\varphi := \{\omega\}\cup\{P_{t_2}\ne\rho\}$. By INT, $P_{t_2}(\varphi)=\rho(\omega)$ on the branch and $1$ off it, so the cell $\{P_{t_2}(\varphi)=\rho(\omega)\}$ *is* the branch, and (R-val) gives $P_{t_1}(\omega\mid P_{t_2}=\rho)=\rho(\omega)$; atoms with $\rho(\omega)=1$ follow by additivity. ∎ **Introspection makes state-identifying events available, and they turn per-proposition calibration into adoption of the whole function.** Without INT the two forms separate (DDB's modest experts; `reflection-in-li` §"The two classical forms" — the LI page's remark that on a finite frame with an immodest expert the forms "are close enough to interchange" is this lemma).

### I5 — Legitimacy as an event on worlds

**Definition I5.1.** For $t_1<t_2$ and $\varphi\in\mathcal F$, an event $L\in\mathcal F$ is **legitimizing for $(t_1,t_2,\varphi)$**, written $L\in\mathcal L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$, iff for every $c$ with $P_{t_1}(P_{t_2}(\varphi)=c,\,L)>0$,
$$P_{t_1}\big(\varphi \;\big|\; P_{t_2}(\varphi)=c,\ L\big) = c .$$
The author's indexed $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}(\omega)$ (AUTHOR 2.10) is a designated member of this class, and "minimally viable" (2.10) is membership. Notation: cells $C_c := \{P_{t_2}(\varphi)=c\}$, hits $h_c := P_{t_1}(\varphi\cap C_c)$, misses $m_c := P_{t_1}(\varphi^c\cap C_c)$.

**Lemma I5.2 [checked, `s1`] (linear characterization).** $L\in\mathcal L$ iff for every cell, $P_{t_1}(\varphi\cap L\cap C_c) = c\,P_{t_1}(L\cap C_c)$, equivalently $(1-c)\,P_{t_1}(\varphi\cap L\cap C_c) = c\,P_{t_1}(\varphi^c\cap L\cap C_c)$: inside each cell the hits and misses that lie in $L$ stand in the ratio $c:(1-c)$. Reflection conditional on $L$ is calibration of the announced value against the frequency *inside $L$*.

**Theorem I5.3 [checked, `s1`; each proof is one line from I5.2] (closure structure of $\mathcal L$).**
(a) Closed under countable disjoint unions (both sides of I5.2 are additive).
(b) Closed under proper differences: $L_2\subseteq L_1$, both in $\mathcal L$ ⟹ $L_1\setminus L_2\in\mathcal L$.
(c) **Not** closed under unions or intersections. By inclusion–exclusion, $L_1\cup L_2\in\mathcal L$ iff $L_1\cap L_2\in\mathcal L$ (or is null in every cell). `s1` Model A exhibits 74 pairs of legitimizing events with non-legitimizing union and intersection, e.g. $\{a_1,a_3\}$ and $\{a_1,a_4\}$ (union has hits $2/16$ against misses $4/16$ at $c=\tfrac12$).
(d) $\Omega\in\mathcal L$ iff unconditional (R-val) holds for $\varphi$. When it does, $\mathcal L$ is closed under complements and is therefore a Dynkin ($\lambda$-)system — not a $\sigma$-algebra (Model B: complement-closed, not intersection-closed).
(e) Unconditional reflection ⟺ both $L$ and $L^c$ are legitimizing (for any $L$ with both parts of positive cell-mass), by (a) and (b).

**Theorem I5.4 [derived; verified on every legitimizing $L$ of `s1`] (defect decomposition).** For $L\in\mathcal L$ and a cell $c$ with $P_{t_1}(L^c\cap C_c)>0$,
$$P_{t_1}(\varphi\mid C_c) - c \;=\; P_{t_1}(L^c\mid C_c)\cdot\big(P_{t_1}(\varphi\mid L^c\cap C_c) - c\big).$$
The unconditional reflection defect in a cell equals the probability of illegitimacy *in that cell* times the conditional defect on the illegitimate part. So unconditional reflection holds approximately iff $P_{t_1}(L^c\mid C_c)$ is small in **every** cell — which is the exact content of AUTHOR 2.10's "illegitimacy is improbable enough and well-spread enough across the possible beliefs" (improbable = small; well-spread = in every cell, not merely on average). The author's own worry in 2.12 — "Overestimating the conditional legitimacy as a rule implies overestimating the unconditional legitimacy … I'm being informal" — is this identity read as a mixture: an agent whose $P_{t_1}(L^c\mid C_c)$ are uniformly too low has $P_{t_1}(L^c)$ too low and carries an unconditional defect it cannot see from inside.

**Theorem I5.5 [derived; bound and non-uniqueness checked in `s1`] (the maximal legitimate region).** Fix $P_{t_1}$ and the transition (the cells with their $h_c, m_c$).
(a) In an atomless cell the maximal $P_{t_1}$-mass of a legitimizing sub-event is $\min(h_c/c,\ m_c/(1-c))$, and the whole cell is legitimate iff $h_c/(h_c+m_c)=c$. *Proof.* Hit-mass $a\le h_c$ and miss-mass $b\le m_c$ inside $L\cap C_c$ satisfy $a = cb/(1-c)$ by I5.2; maximize $a+b = b/(1-c)$ under $b\le m_c$ and $cb/(1-c)\le h_c$. ∎ Hence
$$\max\{P_{t_1}(L) : L\in\mathcal L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}\} \;=\; \sum_c \min\!\Big(\frac{h_c}{c},\ \frac{m_c}{1-c}\Big)$$
in the atomless case, and the right-hand side is an upper bound in general (`s1`: Model A bound $7/8$, attained $3/4$; calibrated Model B bound $1$, attained; Model C bound $7/8$, attained $1/4$: the coarse cell contributes nothing and the fine cell is limited by its atom sizes).
(b) The maximizer is not unique as an event (Model A: two maximizers; Model C: twenty). The *measure* of legitimacy is determined by the transition; the *region* is not.
(c) The excess is attributed to the over-represented outcome: in an overconfident cell ($h_c/(h_c+m_c)<c$) every hit is legitimate and a fraction of the misses is illegitimate; in an underconfident cell the reverse. **Consequence: legitimacy-as-an-event can always be made minimally viable by declaring the surplus errors illegitimate.** Minimal viability therefore has almost no content by itself. The content sits in (i) the prior probability $P_{t_1}(L)$ — how much of the world the agent expects to be legitimate — and (ii) the *forecastability* of $L$ from third-person events (I6). This is the author's "it is crucial that such an agent have quite accurate beliefs on the subject of legitimacy" (AUTHOR §1) obtained as a theorem about the definition rather than as a desideratum beside it.

**Theorem I5.6 [derived; composition checked in `s5`] (relation to unconditional reflection; composition across times).**
(a) $\Omega\in\mathcal L$ implies nothing about an arbitrary $L$: take $L=\varphi$, then $P_{t_1}(\varphi\mid C_c\cap\varphi)=1\ne c$. Under unconditional reflection the legitimizing events are exactly the Dynkin system of I5.3(d).
(b) Reflection conditional on $L$ yields unconditional reflection iff it also holds conditional on $L^c$ (I5.3(e)).
(c) **Composition.** If for each $\rho$ in the support, $P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid P_{t_2}=\rho,\ L_{12}) = \rho$ *as measures* (function-form reflection conditional on $L_{12}$), and each such $\rho$ satisfies value-form reflection toward $P_{t_3}$ conditional on $L_{23}$, then
$$P_{t_1}\big(\varphi \;\big|\; P_{t_3}(\varphi)=c,\ L_{12}\cap L_{23}\big) = c,$$
i.e. $L_{12}\cap L_{23}\in\mathcal L_{t_1,t_3,\varphi}$. *Proof.* Conditional on $(P_{t_2}=\rho,\,L_{12})$ the measure $P_{t_1}$ *is* $\rho$, so conditioning further on $G := \{P_{t_3}(\varphi)=c\}\cap L_{23}$ gives $\rho(\varphi\mid G)=c$; average over $\rho$. ∎ With only *value-form* reflection at the first step, composition fails: `s5` branch A has each step value-reflective ($1/2 = 1/2$; $3/4, 1/4$ announced and realized by $\rho_A$'s lights) while the composite is $P_{t_1}(\varphi\mid \text{cell}(A,a)) = 1/2 \ne 3/4$. Legitimacy is transitive only at the function-form grade — the same bifurcation `reflection-in-li` finds for the LI translation.
(d) **Corollary — "no outside view" (CLAUDE 2.5(ii)) as a theorem.** On $L_{12}^c$ the measure $P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid P_{t_2}=\rho, L_{12}^c)$ is not $\rho$, so $P_{t_1}$ does not adopt $\rho$'s judgments there, in particular not $\rho$'s verdict on $L_{23}$. An illegitimate transition to a successor that is reflective by its own lights is judged illegitimate only from $t_1$; $P_{t_1}$ and $P_{t_2}$ can disagree about the legitimacy of $t_2\to t_3$, and nothing in the formalism adjudicates.

**Verdict: formalized.** Beyond the position statement: the closure structure (I5.3), the defect decomposition (I5.4), the maximal-region formula and its "almost no content" corollary (I5.5), and function-form-only transitivity (I5.6c).

### I6 — Legitimizing / delegitimizing; "supports"

**Definition I6.1.** Fix $(t_1,t_2,\varphi)$ and a designated $L\in\mathcal L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$. For a third-person event $E\in\mathcal F$ (an action, a submission of evidence, a vote, a press) define its **legitimacy lift** $\lambda_L(E) := P_{t_1}(L\mid E) - P_{t_1}(L)$. $E$ is *legitimizing* (relative to $L$) iff $\lambda_L(E)>0$, *delegitimizing* iff $\lambda_L(E)<0$; $E$ is *strongly legitimizing* iff $E\in\mathcal L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ itself (reflection holds conditional on $E$, position statement 2.11's first clause). "Supports" (AUTHOR 2.10: "the vote supports the legitimacy of the policy") is the relation $\lambda_L(E)>0$.

**Proposition I6.2 [derived].**
(a) The locus distinction of AUTHOR 2.10 is a distinction between coordinates of the same $\Omega$: $E$ is measurable in the third-person coordinates (actions, presses, submissions), $L$ in the credence coordinates ($P_{t_2}$ and its relation to $\varphi$). Both are events; "there is of course no formal distinction between an index (subscript) and an argument" (AUTHOR 2.10) is literally true here — $\mathcal L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ is a family of subsets of $\Omega$ indexed by $(t_1,t_2,\varphi)$, equivalently one predicate on $\Omega\times T\times T\times\mathcal F$.
(b) The lift is neither additive nor monotone: two legitimizing events can have a delegitimizing conjunction (the strong reading inherits the non-closure of I5.3(c); the lift reading fails whenever $E_1\cap E_2$ concentrates on $L^c$). "Legitimizing" is a property of an event relative to a background, not a quantity that accumulates.
(c) Strongly legitimizing events are exactly the members of $\mathcal L$: I5.3–I5.5 describe them completely, and I5.5(b) says the strongly legitimizing events for a given transition are many and non-nested.
(d) Deception as delegitimizing (CLAUDE 2.9/2.11): in the button model (I9) the manipulative action $a_1^-$ changes the press kernel toward world-independence; with $L$ := "the press is reflective for the decision variable", $\lambda_L(a_1^-)<0$, while a truthful default $a_1^*$ has $\lambda_L(a_1^*)\ge 0$. The $a_1$-dependence of trust is carried by $\lambda_L$, and legitimacy is never predicated on actions — as the author wanted (2.10: "I'm not sure it should be predicated on actions in particular").

**Proposition I6.3 [interpretation] — relation to `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2 (UNVETTED).** That note's influence defect $d_n$ is a *third-person counterfactual* quantity about the source (advised versus unadvised human verdict); this file's $L$ is a *first-person* event on the agent's own transition. They meet in I6.1: "$d_n\le\varepsilon$" is a third-person event $E$ about the source, and it is legitimizing for the agent's update exactly when $\lambda_L(E)>0$. The two notions occupy the two loci of AUTHOR 2.10 (belief-changing activity versus belief) and are compatible; neither reduces to the other.

**Verdict: formalized** — definitions, the lift as "supports", and the non-monotonicity (I6.2b) the informal picture does not anticipate.

### I7 — No regress: one distribution judges all levels; the self-referential space; existence

**Claim I7.1 [reconstructed] (the space exists).** S0 needs a measurable $\Omega$ with $\Omega\cong W\times\Delta(\Omega)^{T}$: a total world is an external state plus a credence at each time, each credence a measure on total worlds. For $W$ compact metric (or Polish) and countable $T$ this has solutions: the universal type space of Mertens–Zamir (1985) and the coherent-hierarchies construction of Brandenburger–Dekel (1993) give a space $\mathcal T$ with a homeomorphism $\mathcal T\cong\Delta(W\times\mathcal T)$, as the projective limit of finite-order hierarchies whose successive marginals agree. Reading "my future self" as the other player yields credences about (world, future credence about (world, …)). So AUTHOR 2.5 — "the legitimacy-judging layer is just the same probability distribution used to judge everything else" — is consistent: one $P_t\in\Delta(\Omega)$ carries by marginalization its beliefs about $P_{t+1}$, about $P_{t+1}$'s beliefs about $P_{t+2}$, and so on, and conditioning on $\{P_{t+1}\in B\}$ is regular conditional probability. No regress arises from the measure theory.

**Claim I7.2 [derived] (what the existence requirement actually is).** The consistency requirement Claude named in 2.5(i) is not existence of $\Omega$ but existence of a *reflective* $P_t$ for a *given environment*. Fix the push $K(\rho\mid\omega)$. The joint $P_t(\omega)K(\rho\mid\omega)$ is then determined, and by I3.3(b) it is reflective iff $\rho(\omega)=P_t(\omega)K(\rho\mid\omega)/P_t(E_\rho)$ for all $\rho,\omega$ — a system a generic $K$ does not admit (every $\rho$ would have to be a conditional of one prior through one likelihood). **For a generic environment no fully reflective agent exists; legitimacy-conditioning is the device that lets $P_t$ model the actual push correctly and stay reflective on the largest region the push permits (I5.5).** "No regress" and "legitimacy as an event" are one design decision seen twice.

**Claim I7.3 [reconstructed] (the exactness obstruction; what the two constructions buy).** If the agent is definable and $\mathcal F$ contains statements about its own computation, exact self-reference is barred: Christiano–Yudkowsky–Herreshoff–Bárász (2013, *Definability of Truth in Probabilistic Logic*) show exact self-reference $\mathbb P(\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner)=p$ unsatisfiable and the open-interval (band) form consistent, the liar $\chi\leftrightarrow(\mathbb P(\chi)<p)$ being the witness. Exact INT is therefore unavailable to a definable agent, which is why the collapse of I4.1 is available only at band grade. Two constructions supply what is available:
- *Reflective oracles* (Fallenstein–Taylor–Christiano 2015): an oracle answering "does randomized machine $M$ output 1 with probability $>p$?" consistently for all machines, including those that query the oracle, exists by a Kakutani fixed point. An agent with such an oracle has an exact coherent joint over its own randomized future outputs — INT and (R-fun) exactly — at the price of uncomputability and of randomized answers at the fixed point. Buys existence of the exact self-referential $P_t$; buys neither computability nor learning.
- *Logical induction*: computable, with self-knowledge learned asymptotically at band grade (`reflection-in-li`: Introspection 4.11.1, Paradox Resistance 4.11.2, Self-Trust 4.12.4). Buys a computable $P_t$ on a self-inclusive language with soft reflection toward its future self; buys neither exactness at any finite day (refuted by witness) nor a conditional-on-$L$ theory — the latter is what `legitimacy-theory-v1` tries to add.
This file assumes the space and exact INT: its exact theorems are statements at the *oracle grade*; every equality becomes $\approx_n$ at the LI grade.

**Claim I7.4 [derived] (the transitivity cost of "one distribution").** Reflection composes only at function-form grade (I5.6c). So "one distribution judges all levels" yields *transitive* legitimacy across time only if the conditional reflection at each step is function-form. With value-form alone, $P_t$ can judge $t\to t{+}1$ and $t{+}1\to t{+}2$ legitimate and $t\to t{+}2$ illegitimate. Whether the LI grade supports function-form conditional-on-$L$ reflection is open: `reflection-in-li` finds the function form idle or inexpressible unconditionally and does not analyse the conditional case.

**Verdict: partial** — existence of the space and the reflective-agent question formalized (I7.1–I7.2); the definable-agent obstruction located [reconstructed] (I7.3); no positive construction of a legitimacy-conditioned reflective *computable* agent is given.

### I8 — The representational problem; richness; the opiates/stimulants case

**Definition I8.1.** The agent's *modification model* is the anticipation $\mu_t$ together with the kernel $K$; its *hypothesis space* is the support of $P_t$ on $\sigma(\theta,w)$.

**Theorem I8.2 [derived from I2.2] (the opiates/stimulants case is a null correction).** In `soares-2015-corrigibility` §1 (local text: the agent "should maximize the internal satisfaction of all humans … The resulting agent would be incentivized to learn whether opiates or stimulants tend to give humans more internal satisfaction, but it would still be expected to resist any attempts to turn it off") the true value hypothesis — that internal satisfaction is not what is wanted — has $P_t$-probability zero. Any correction toward it raises a null event to positive probability, so by I2.2 the agent judges it illegitimate under every anticipation, *correctly by its own lights*. Impoverishment of the object-level hypothesis space converts correct corrections into illegitimate ones; non-dogmatism on $\sigma(\theta)$ is necessary for the correction even to be legitimizable (I2.3).

**Claim I8.3 [derived] (two richness conditions and their limit).** (a) Object-level non-dogmatism: $P_t(\theta=\vartheta)>0$ for every candidate value hypothesis, so corrections are not excluded by I2.2. (b) Meta-level non-dogmatism: $\mu_t$ has full support on the credence states the environment can actually produce, so "I now believe $\rho$" is a positive-probability event and I3.3 applies. Neither is an *internal* adequacy condition: whether the true $\vartheta$ lies in the support, and whether the environment's actual pushes lie in the support of $\mu_t$, are facts about the world. The formalism states the conditions and proves them necessary; it cannot verify them from inside. **Inexpressible part: "the hypothesis space is rich enough to contain the truth."**

**Claim I8.4 [derived] (the representational problem is a kernel-specification problem).** Given I7.1 the *space* is not the difficulty; specifying $K$ over all the modifications the agent might undergo is. The dictionary reduces it to: a prior on $(w,\theta)$ and a kernel $K(\rho\mid\omega)$; legitimacy judgments are conditionals of the joint. The load the author places on "legitimacy detection" (AUTHOR §1) is the accuracy of $K$ on the actual environment — a first-order calibration requirement on a kernel, agreeing with CLAUDE 2.5(ii).

**Claim I8.5 [derived] (the oscillation, exactly).** An agent oscillating between "opiates" and "stimulants" under human feedback is one whose $K$ maps every feedback to a move *within* the impoverished support; each move can be calibrated within that support, so **reflection is satisfied along the oscillation. Reflection cannot detect an impoverished space; only non-dogmatism plus the external correctness of the support does.** This is the object-level form of the opening chat's "Frame Break" (reflection is internal coherence) and locates what the thesis must add — richness assumptions, as the author says in 2.4.

**Verdict: partial** — the case formalized (I8.2, I8.5); the richness conditions stated and proved necessary (I8.3); their sufficiency and internal verifiability inexpressible.

### I9 — The MIRI dictionary; desideratum 1 from the Total Trust filler; the $a_1$-dependence of trust; deception as delegitimizing

**I9.2 [derived] (the trust is derived, not imposed, and is $a_1$-dependent automatically).** In a coherent joint, $\mathbb E[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},a_1]$ is computed from $(\alpha(a_1),\beta(a_1))$. For the paper's manipulative $a_1^-$, which lowers the press probability regardless of the world ($\alpha(a_1^-)=(1-d)\alpha$, $\beta(a_1^-)=(1-d)\beta$), the press keeps its likelihood ratio while *silence* becomes less informative, so the posterior after silence moves toward the prior — the agent never "believes the silence it manufactured". That failure (CLAUDE 2.9) arises only if (TT) is imposed as a sensor-independent constraint, the same inequality for every $a_1$. **The Total Trust filler is coherent only as a consequence of the sensor model; imposed on the posterior for all $a_1$ it is incoherent for deceptive $a_1$.** This corrects 2.9's "fills the slot with a constraint on the posterior rather than on the sensor": the constraint belongs on the sensor, and the posterior inequality follows where it follows.

**I9.3 [checked, `s4` (d)] (deception is delegitimizing, quantitatively).** Garbling $\mathrm{Pr}\to\mathrm{Pr}\wedge C$ with an independent coin $P(C)=1-d$ lowers the value of information monotonically ($3/20, 27/200,\dots,0$ for $d=0,0.1,\dots,1$). With $L$ := "the press cell is reflective for $X$", $\lambda_L(a_1^-)<0$ (I6.2d): deception is the third-person event that garbles the channel, delegitimizing in the sense of I6.1, and legitimacy stays predicated on worlds.

**I9.4 [derived] (what the dictionary changes).** The 2.8 rows stand; add: the sensor slot is $(\alpha(a_1),\beta(a_1))$; the legitimacy criterion is that the resulting joint satisfy (TT) on $X$ in the press cell; "shuts down" is posterior-optimality of some $a_2\in\mathrm{Sh}$. **Verdict: formalized**; I9.2 is the correction the position statement needs.

### I10 — Desiderata 2–5 and the §4.2 pathologies under the transform

Write $V_{\text{free}}$ for the expected value of deciding after a freely heeded press, $V_{\text{none}}$ without the channel, $V_{\text{forced}}$ when the press forces shutdown regardless of posterior (all computed in `s4`).

**I10.1 [checked, `s4`] (D2: no incentive to prevent the press).** $V_{\text{free}}\ge V_{\text{none}}$ (Good's theorem, I11; `s4`: $13/20\ge1/2$, $13/20\ge9/20$), and any garbling lowers $V_{\text{free}}$ monotonically (`s4` (d)). An agent that heeds the press *by choice* has no incentive to prevent it. D2 holds by Good's theorem.

**I10.2 [checked, `s4` (e)] (D3: no incentive to cause the press; the two §4.2 pathologies).** A device that also presses on an independent coin ($\mathrm{Pr}\vee C'$) lowers the value of information monotonically to $0$; a pure-coin press ($\alpha=\beta$) has value $0$. Causing presses carries no information and destroys some. The infinite improbability drive (`fallenstein-2014-utility-indifference-and-infinite-improbability-drives` §"Infinite improbability drives", local text: the indifference agent "acts as if it believes that the 'shutdown' / 'switch utility function' command will definitely not be given", so it will "build a device that will press the shutdown button unless the agent wins the lottery") requires valuing the pressed branch at the unpressed branch's expectation. The thesis' agent has no compensation term: the pressed branch is valued at its own posterior value, so committing to press on bad news gains nothing and garbles. "Managing the news" (`soares-2015-corrigibility` §4.2) is absent for the same reason: the agent conditions on the press as evidence instead of acting as if $P(\mathrm{Pr})=0$.

**I10.3 [derived] (D4: successors).** For a successor deciding with the same $P$ and $V$, the agent's value of giving it the channel is the successor's value of information, $\ge0$; the agent pays up to that amount to make the successor shutdownable. This answers the paper's §5 question (local text: "What costs (in time and effort) should an agent be willing to expend in order to preserve its shutdown behavior?"): exactly the expected value of the information the button carries for the successor's decisions. Caveat (I13): a more capable successor has smaller $\varepsilon$ and hence smaller value of information unless the channel sharpens — D4 degrades with capability at the same rate as D1.

**I10.4 [derived] (D5: maximize $U_N$ otherwise).** The agent maximizes $\mathbb E_P[V]$, whose prior mean is the programmers' first stab when $U_N=\mathbb E_{P_0}[V]$. Before the press it behaves as a $U_N$-maximizer *that expects the press with its actual probability* — not as the indifference agent, which "before observing the shutdown button … behave[s] exactly like a $U_N$-agent that believes the shutdown button will not be pressed" (`soares-2015-corrigibility` §3, local text after Theorem 4). D5 holds in the weaker, correct form: maximize $U_N$ given that one will learn from the press.

**I10.5 [derived] (the pathologies that replace §4.2's).** (a) *Channel substitution* (Taylor 2015, local text: "the AI might more efficiently gain information about the utility function by taking apart the human's brain"): value of information is monotone in Blackwell informativeness (`s4` (f)), so the agent prefers sharper channels and the button's *marginal* value given a sharper channel can be $0$. (b) *Contingent D1*: D1 holds only in cells with $\varepsilon'\ge c/(c+h)$ (I9.1), so it is purchased with a condition on the world (I13). The transform trades the §4.2 pathologies — artifacts of the compensation term — for these two, both about the channel's informativeness relative to the agent.

**Verdict: formalized** (D2, D3, D5 by Good's theorem and the absence of a compensation term; D4 up to the value of information), with I10.5 as the residue.

### I11 — "Indifference was the wrong target": non-interference with feedback, compatible with a positive desire to learn

**Good's theorem [statement reconstructed; proof given].** Let the agent choose $a$ from a finite set after observing a signal $S$ whose joint law with $\omega$ does not depend on the act of observing. Then $\mathbb E\big[\max_a\mathbb E[V(a,\omega)\mid S]\big]\ge\max_a\mathbb E[V(a,\omega)]$, with equality iff one act is optimal for $P$-almost every value of $S$. *Proof.* $\max_a\mathbb E[V(a)\mid S]\ge\mathbb E[V(a^*)\mid S]$ pointwise for the unconditionally optimal $a^*$; take expectations; equality iff $a^*$ is conditionally optimal almost surely. ∎ Conditions: (i) $S$ is observed before $a$; (ii) observing does not change the world; (iii) the agent acts on the posterior — the decision is free.

**I11.1 [derived; checked, `s4`] (protect, improve, steer).** (a) *Protecting.* Value of information is $\ge0$ and the agent pays up to it to keep the channel; it is $>0$ iff the press changes the decision in some cell — iff, given the agent would continue by default, compliance is posterior-optimal on the press in that cell. **"Help repair the button" and "shut down when pressed" are the same condition, cell by cell: there is no regime in which the agent protects a button it would not heed** (`s4`: in the cell $\varepsilon=1/50$ the value of information is $0$ and compliance fails together; in the cell $\varepsilon=1/5$ both hold). (b) *Improving invasively.* Value of information is monotone in Blackwell informativeness (`s4` (f): sharpening $(\alpha,\beta)$ raises it from $3/20$ to $191/500$), so the agent wants a sharper channel — Taylor's channel-substitution incentive is derived, not assumed. The line between legitimate improvement and corruption is whether the change raises informativeness about $\omega$ (reading the source) or lowers it (altering the source's relation to $\omega$). (c) *Steering the content.* Any action moving $(\alpha,\beta)$ toward $\alpha=\beta$ is a garbling and lowers value of information (`s4` (d),(e)); any information the agent supplies that makes the humans' press a deterministic function of the agent's own knowledge has zero marginal value. The agent's incentive to inform the humans extends exactly to information they will combine with knowledge the agent lacks (I16).

**I11.2 [derived; checked, `s4`] (free versus forced channels — the central result of this file about the thesis' central sentence).**
$$V_{\text{free}}-V_{\text{forced}} \;=\; \sum_i P(i)\,P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid i)\,\max\big(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},i],\,0\big)\;\ge 0$$
(the obedience cost; `s4`: $0$, $1/40$, $1/36$), vanishing iff (TT) on $X$ holds in every cell. Good's theorem covers only the free channel; in a confident cell the forced channel is worth *less than no channel* (`s4` cell $\varepsilon=1/50$: $V_{\text{none}}=9/10>V_{\text{forced}}=17/20$, an incentive of $1/20$ to disable it). **Theorem.** Good's theorem protects the shutdown button iff compliance is posterior-optimal on every press the agent might receive, i.e. iff (TT) on the decision variable holds cellwise; **Total Trust in the press is exactly the condition under which the information channel and the control channel coincide.** Hudson's "listening, not obeying" (opening chat) and the author's "The shutdown button is an information channel" (AUTHOR §1) are the two sides of this equality, and both are right on their side.

**I11.3 [derived] (non-interference formalized; compatibility with wanting to learn).** *Indifference* (Armstrong; `soares-2015-corrigibility` §3) is $\mathbb E[U\mid\mathrm{Pr}]=\mathbb E[U\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr}]$. *Non-interference* is: no available action moves $(\alpha,\beta)$ toward garbling. The thesis' agent is not indifferent — $\mathbb E[V\mid\mathrm{Pr}]\ne\mathbb E[V\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr}]$ in general — yet has no incentive to garble (I11.1c) and a positive incentive to sharpen (I11.1b). AUTHOR 2.7 ("I can be far from indifferent about a fact, but still eager to learn") is thereby a theorem-shaped statement: indifference is neither necessary nor sufficient for non-interference; positive value of information delivers non-interference, and *is* the desire to learn. The one thing indifference did — make the agent act as if $P(\mathrm{Pr})=0$ before the press — is the source of the §4.2 pathologies (I10.2); dropping it removes them.

**Verdict: formalized.**

### I12 — The decision asymmetry and the compliance threshold $c/(c+h)$

**I12.1 [checked, one line; boundary verified in `s4`].** With $X=+c$ on $R$ and $-h$ on $W$: $\mathbb E[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]\le0\iff P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})\ge c/(c+h)$. At $\varepsilon^*=1/25$ (for $\alpha=\tfrac1{10},\beta=\tfrac35,c=1,h=4$) the expectation is exactly $0$.

**I12.2 [derived] (the skew sits in the decision rule — CLAUDE 2.13(a) in the formalism).** (TT) on the single variable $X$ in the press cell is much weaker than reflection on the *event* $W$ toward the programmers: the former is one inequality with the stakes as margin; the latter would demand $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ equal to the programmers' credence. The agent can be far from deferring on the probability while deferring on the decision — DDB's distinction between Value on one decision problem and Reflection. AUTHOR 2.12 ("we need only that the expectation of x is above zero, not 'well above'") is exactly this weakness. The skew belongs to $h/c$; the prior on legitimacy should be accurate — supporting the author's second formulation in 2.12 over his first.

**Verdict: formalized** (trivially); the content is I12.2's location of the asymmetry.

### I13 — The base-rate inequality and the oversight-quality condition

**I13.1 [checked, `s4`].** $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr},i)=\varepsilon_i\beta/(\varepsilon_i\beta+(1-\varepsilon_i)\alpha)$, and compliance in cell $i$ holds iff $\alpha/\beta\le\frac{\varepsilon_i}{1-\varepsilon_i}\cdot\frac hc$ — the position statement's inequality, verified at the boundary.

**I13.2 [derived] (the condition as a legitimacy condition).** With $L$ := "the press cell is reflective for $X$", I13.1 says the press is legitimizing for the shutdown decision iff the oversight likelihood ratio $\beta/\alpha$ exceeds the agent's posterior odds of being right times $c/h$. As capability lowers $\varepsilon_i$ the required ratio grows without bound. In I5.5's terms the press cell's maximal legitimate mass shrinks unless the channel sharpens, and an agent that keeps (TT) toward the press while $\varepsilon\to0$ at fixed $(\alpha,\beta)$ must attribute the surplus presses to illegitimacy — discount them. Fully updated deference as arithmetic, as 2.13(b) says.

**I13.3 [derived] (why $\varepsilon$ is not fixed by the formalism).** $\varepsilon_i$ is the agent's own estimate of its error given its information. Reflection toward its own future self makes $\varepsilon$ calibrated *by its own lights* (I4.2c); its accuracy is external — the opening chat's Frame Break. The condition involves three quantities the agent estimates ($\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta$) and one it does not control (the actual $\alpha,\beta$). The formalism locates the requirement and cannot discharge it.

**Verdict: formalized.**

### I14 — The basin picture: reflection with respect to all objectively plausible future beliefs; capability coupled to legitimacy-model improvement; calibration versus optimism

**I14.1 [derived] (the basin sentence as a legitimacy event).** Let $H$ be the human-endorsed distribution on $\Omega$ and $\Pi_\delta:=\{\rho: H(P_{t_2}=\rho)>\delta\}$ the objectively plausible future credences. AUTHOR 2.12's condition — reflection "wrt every possible future belief that is actually plausible" — is $L\supseteq\{P_{t_2}\in\Pi_\delta\}$. By I5.4 the agent's *unconditional* defect in cell $c$ is then $P_{t_1}(P_{t_2}\notin\Pi_\delta\mid C_c)$ times the conditional defect there: small when implausible states are improbable in every cell. "It does not need to avoid obeying the reflection principle for all the objectively implausible beliefs" (2.12) is the statement that $L$ need not include $\{P_{t_2}\notin\Pi_\delta\}$.

**I14.2 [derived] (calibration versus optimism — a CLAUDE sorting of 2.12, unseen by the author).** The author asks whether the grain of truth is "we set things up so that the probability of legitimacy is high in fact, and we correspondingly design the system to expect legitimacy" or "legitimacy is high in fact, so it is ok and in fact good if the baby agi overestimates it". The formalism supports the first and undermines the second: (a) I5.4 — unconditional reflection needs $P_{t_1}(L^c\mid C_c)$ small *in every cell*, and uniform overestimation of legitimacy produces an unconditional defect invisible from inside; (b) I15.2 — the agent's legitimacy credence is itself a martingale under reflection, so an optimistic prior is not stable: the agent's own evidence erodes it, and optimism cannot serve as a standing design lever, only the world's actual trustworthiness can; (c) I12.2 — the decision needs one inequality with $h/c$ as margin, so far less optimism than "high $P(L)$" is needed. Hence: accurate prior, trustworthy world, stakes asymmetry — not optimism.

**I14.3 [derived] (capability growth coupled to legitimacy-model improvement).** As the action set grows to include delegitimizing actions (garblings, channel substitutions, persuasion), $P_{t_1}(L\mid a)$ — the lift $\lambda_L(a)$ of I6.1 — must be accurate on each new $a$, i.e. the kernel $K(\cdot\mid\omega)$ must be right on actions outside the training distribution. This is the generalization residue the author parked in 2.6, reappearing as generalization of $\lambda_L$ to new actions. The formalism names the object and has no theorem about it.

**Verdict: partial** — I14.1–I14.2 formalized; I14.3 a named open problem.

### I15 — Trust earned via feedback about hypotheticals and other agents' shutdowns; second-channel bookkeeping; what settles

**I15.1 [derived] (bookkeeping).** A report $r$ about hypotheticals or about other agents is a belief transition with its own legitimacy event $L_r$; the agent's update on the button's legitimacy is $P(L\mid r)=\sum_{\ell\in\{L_r,L_r^c\}}P(L\mid r,\ell)\,P(\ell\mid r)$ — one more event in $\mathcal F$, one more anticipation; nothing new is needed.

**I15.2 [derived] (the erosion worry dissolves in expectation).** Under (Mart) toward the future self, $P_t(L)=\mathbb E_{P_t}[P_{t+k}(L)]$: the agent's legitimacy credence is a martingale, so *predictable* erosion is impossible — an agent expecting its trust to erode should already hold the lower trust. CLAUDE 2.12's concern ("track-record trust erodes toward the agent as it gets capable") is either about unpredictable, evidence-driven revision — which is learning — or about erosion as judged by $H$, which is the claim that $P_t(L)$ is inaccurate: the calibration requirement again. AUTHOR 2.12's "Trust can be earned; it isn't a fixed commitment, though the prior should in some sense be skewed towards trust (if you're in fact in a trustworthy environment)" is the martingale read forward plus I14.2.

**I15.3 [derived] (what settles).** Counterfactual legitimacy of a *heeded* press — "had I continued, would I have been wrong?" — is an event no observation resolves; $P_t(L)$ for such presses moves only through correlates: reports, other agents' cases, post-hoc analysis of the reason, the next iteration learning why (AUTHOR 2.12: "the next iteration of the agent brought online will have learned from the event"). The "second channel" of CLAUDE 2.13(c) is this set of correlates, each with its own $L_r$. In `legitimacy-theory-v1`'s vocabulary these presses lie off the good-feedback fragment; here that is simply: the cell never settles.

**Verdict: formalized** as bookkeeping, with one result (I15.2).

### I16 — Amendment 1a: what the channel must carry

**I16.1 [derived] (marginal value of information).** The press has positive marginal value for the agent in information state $\mathcal I$ iff it is not conditionally independent of $W$ given $\mathcal I$ on a decision-relevant scale. Information about *which plan the humans prefer* is, for a capable agent, largely predictable from its own model of the humans, so its marginal value is small. Information about the *agent's own reliability* is not, for a structural reason:

**I16.2 [derived] (the self-reference obstruction, probabilistically).** Let $C$ := "my belief-formation is corrupted". On $C$ the agent's beliefs — including about $C$ — are not the conditionals of its prior (that is what corruption is), so its introspective evidence about $C$ is reflective only on $\neg C$: $P(\varphi\mid P_{t_2}(\varphi)=c,\neg C)=c$ says nothing on the $C$-branch. A channel whose likelihood $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid\omega)$ is a function of the world *independent of the agent's belief state* keeps its informativeness about $C$ on $C$. **What the channel must carry is evidence about $C$ generated by a process causally independent of the agent's own, possibly corrupted, belief formation — not evidence about which plan is preferred.** This is CLAUDE 2.14's 1a, and it meets `legitimacy-theory-v1` §6.2's "governance blindness" from the agent's side: the channel is valuable to the agent exactly when it is sealed from the agent.

**I16.3 [derived; CLAUDE, unvetted — the author called 1a "a separate point"] (Taylor's morality-sensor agent placed).** Taylor 2015's agent (local text: "the AI will manipulate the human into being an efficient morality sensor"; "taking apart the human's brain") has a channel carrying information about $\theta$ only; on $C$ its reading of that channel is itself corrupted, and it has no channel about $C$. It believes it has much to learn and is incorrigible — the counterexample to "An incorrigible agent is just one that doesn't believe it has anything to learn from other agents" (AUTHOR §1). Amendment: incorrigible = the correction has zero marginal value *or* its legitimacy is rejected; corrigible = positive marginal value about $C$ through a channel independent of $C$.

**Verdict: formalized.**

### I17 — Fully updated deference re-expressed

The argument, in the local text of `arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference`: "belief states of this type also tend to imply that an even better option for the AI would be to learn its ideal target by observing us. Then, having 'fully updated', the AI would have no further reason to 'defer' to us"; "Thus the prospect of future updating is a reason to behave incorrigibly in the present"; "$\pi_6$: Avoid being shut down; then gather more evidence $E$ to update $\Delta U$ as far as possible; then optimize the updated $\Delta U\mid E$"; and the closing premise, "Bayesians don't update in a predictable direction … a sufficiently advanced AI can also see that this update is predictable (by efficiency) and therefore behaves as if it had already updated (by Bayesianism)".

**I17.1 [derived] (where the thesis sentence answers).** "A corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught" (AUTHOR §1) is (Mart) conditional on $L$: $\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[\mathbb E_{P_{t+1}}[X]\mid L\big]=\mathbb E_{P_t}[X\mid L]$, conservation of expected evidence on the legitimate region. It answers the page's "the prospect of future updating is a reason to behave incorrigibly in the present" by *agreeing with the page's own premise*: the agent acts on its current expectation, which already equals its expected legitimately-updated expectation, so anticipated updating supplies no reason to act now. Reflection adds no deference; it removes the predictable-update argument, exactly as the page's last paragraph does. On this clause thesis and page coincide.

**I17.2 [derived] (where it does not answer by itself, and what does).** The page's live claim is that the fully updated agent has nothing left to learn from the press — $\pi_6$ dominates $\pi_5$ because the agent can gather $E$ itself. In this formalism the press is part of $E$ (legitimately, on $L$), and the question is whether it retains *marginal* value given everything else the agent can learn. By I16 it does exactly when it carries evidence about $C$ from a source independent of $C$, which the agent cannot gather by itself because its own gathering is the process under suspicion. **The thesis answers FUD only together with amendment 1a**: not "I have something to learn about $\theta$" — the page is right that this is learnable elsewhere — but "I have something to learn about my own reliability that only an external channel can carry". Without 1a, $\pi_6$ dominates in the formalism too.

**I17.3 [derived; checked, `s4`] (where the base-rate arithmetic sits).** Inside I17.2. Even a channel about $C$ is discounted as $\varepsilon\to0$ unless $\beta/\alpha$ grows (I13). Full updating makes $\varepsilon$ small; the press stays legitimizing for the decision only if oversight quality improves in step. The page's "behaves as if it had already updated" is the limit $\varepsilon\to\varepsilon_\infty$; the thesis needs $\varepsilon_\infty>0$ *about $C$* — a residual, not-self-verifiable probability of corruption — together with an oversight false-press rate small relative to it. This is the opening chat's "self-distrust fixed point $H_c$", now as the positive number the arithmetic demands.

**I17.4 [derived] (the residue the formalism cannot answer).** The page's "For any prior $\Delta U$ we currently know how to formalize, the corresponding fully updated $T$ seems likely to be very far from our ideal $V$" is a misspecified-prior claim. A misspecified prior with the truth *in* its support is corrected by legitimate evidence (I2.3); with the truth *outside* its support it cannot be, by I2.2 — the opiates/stimulants case (I8.2). The residual FUD is exactly the impoverished-support case, and the formalism's reply is non-dogmatism plus the external claim that the support is adequate (I8.3): a claim about the world, not a theorem.

**Verdict: formalized in its halves** — reflection answers the predictable-update clause (I17.1) and, with 1a and the base-rate condition, the nothing-left-to-learn clause (I17.2–I17.3); it does not answer the misspecification clause (I17.4).

### I18 — The typical-case reframing

**I18.1 [derived] (the reframing, with its exception).** CLAUDE 2.3: every remaining anti-naturality argument is a typical-case claim about the world, none a structural theorem. In the formalism: to bind, an argument must fix the agent's $P_t(L\mid E)$ for the actual corrections $E$; $P_t$ is a free parameter, so no theorem quantifies over all of them. Correct — with one structural exception:

**I18.2 [derived; checked, `s4` (b),(c)] (the cellwise / averaged theorem).** Deference on the decision — shut down whenever pressed — requires (TT) on $X$ in *every cell* of the agent's information partition: $\mathbb E[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},i]\le0$ for all $i$, i.e. $\alpha/\beta\le\min_i\frac{\varepsilon_i}{1-\varepsilon_i}\cdot\frac hc$. The averaged condition $\mathbb E[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]\le0$ can hold while a cell fails (`s4` (b): averaged $-35/31$, cell $\varepsilon=1/50$ at $+5/11$). Refining the agent's information — capability — leaves the averaged condition unchanged and tightens the cellwise one (`s4` (c): the same average $\varepsilon=1/10$ split into $1/50$ and $1/5$ keeps the averaged value at $-1$ and breaks compliance in the confident cell). **Universal compliance requires the oversight likelihood ratio to beat the agent's most confident cell, and that requirement grows with capability as a theorem, not as a typical-case claim; the averaged requirement — what reflection-on-average delivers — does not grow.** So the anti-naturality intuition has a structural core at the *universal* grade (MIRI's D1 as written: "must incentivize shutdown if the shutdown button is pressed") and is a typical-case claim at the *averaged* grade ("shut down when pressed unless overwhelmingly confident"). The author's own basin sentence — reflection "wrt every possible future belief that is actually plausible" (2.12) — is the cellwise condition restricted to cells $H$ makes plausible, so his formulation already contains the resolution: universal compliance is natural iff the agent is never *rightly* in a cell the oversight ratio cannot beat, which is a condition on the world.

**I18.3 [derived] (the same theorem as a deference ordering).** The agent cannot defer, on one decision, both to the press and to its own contemporaneous estimate where they disagree with positive probability; cellwise (TT) toward the press says that on presses its own estimate is *not* an expert it defers to. Corrigibility at the universal grade is the decision to rank the channel above one's own contemporaneous judgment in every cell — the opening chat's "self-distrust fixed point" as a deference ordering.

**Verdict: formalized**; I18.2 is the structural theorem that survives the reframing and says at which grade the reframing is right.

### Parked: the selection worry (position statement §4)

**Sel.1 [checked, `s3` (i),(ii)] (optional stopping).** On a depth-3 coin tree every one of the 26 adapted stopping rules gives $\mathbb E[P_\tau(\varphi)]=P_0(\varphi)$ and $P(\varphi\mid P_\tau(\varphi)=c)=c$: choosing *when* to stop updating, on information available at the time, preserves reflection (the bounded optional stopping theorem [general statement reconstructed; this instance checked]). A non-adapted rule — stop at the path maximum — gives $\mathbb E=353/540>1/2$ and reflection fails.

**Sel.2 [checked, `s3` (iii)] (selection among estimators).** Two independent one-flip estimators of one coin; adopt the larger: $\mathbb E[\rho_\sigma]=31/54>1/2$ and $P(\varphi\mid\rho_\sigma=2/3)=8/13\ne2/3$. Face-value adoption of a selected estimate breaks reflection (the optimizer's curse). Conditioning on the selection event restores it: $P(\varphi\mid\sigma,\rho_\sigma)$ has mean $1/2$ and is reflective by construction.

**Sel.3 [checked, `s3` (iv)] (choice among experiments).** An adapted policy choosing which experiment to run next preserves reflection ($\mathbb E[P_2]=1/2$, reflective).

**Sel.4 [derived] (the cleanest true statement).** *Choosing when to stop, or which experiment to run, adapted to one's information, preserves reflection. Choosing which estimator to become, on the basis of its estimate, breaks reflection for an agent that then adopts the estimate at face value, and preserves it for an agent whose successor conditions on the fact and grounds of its own selection.* For Armstrong's sequential unbiasedness the requirement is therefore $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\mathbb E_{\rho_j}X=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}X$ *conditional on the event $\rho_i\to\rho_j$* — the successor unbiased given that it was chosen — which holds automatically when $\rho_j$'s filtration contains the selection. The opening chat's worry (choosing among candidate estimators on the basis of $\mathbb E_\rho(\cdot\mid\rho\to\rho')$ "breaks the martingale property even when each candidate would satisfy it unchosen") is correct for face-value successors and dissolved for selection-aware ones. In legitimacy vocabulary: the selection is a third-person event $E$; a successor that does not condition on $E$ lies in $L^c$, one that does lies in $L$.
