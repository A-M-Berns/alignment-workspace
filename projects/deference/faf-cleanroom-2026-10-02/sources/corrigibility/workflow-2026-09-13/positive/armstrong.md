## Setting

The formalism is a family of definitions Armstrong introduced across nine years; I fix the notation once here and cite each piece to its local file. Everything in this section is [reported] transcription unless marked otherwise; my own additions (the finite embedded reading, S15–S17) are marked [derived] and are the conventions the later sections use.

**S1 (Utility indifference, 2010).** $\Omega$ is the set of possible worlds, $u:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ a utility, $P$ a probability on $\Omega$; for events $S,T$, $u(S\mid T)=u(S\cap T)/P(T)$ and the *intrinsic utility* is $\mathcal U(S):=u(S\mid S)$. $u$ is *indifferent between disjoint $S,T$* iff $\mathcal U(S)=\mathcal U(T)$, equivalently $u(S\cup T)$ does not depend on how $P(S\cup T)$ is split between $S$ and $T$. Given an event $X\in\{0,1\}$ (a quantum measurement) and the partition $[\Omega_X]$ of worlds by history up to $X$, the filtered utility is $v(\omega)=u(\omega)-\mathcal U(E_1)+\mathcal U(E_0)$ for $\omega\in E_1$ and $v=u$ elsewhere. Prop. 2.2: $\mathcal V(E_1)=\mathcal V(E_0)$. Thm. 2.3: a $v$-maximiser's decisions before $X$ do not depend on $p=P(X=1)$. §4 flags the self-improvement problem: the filter $F$ with $v=F(u)$ is idempotent, and "great care must be used to maintain indifference with a self-improving AI". [reported: `references/03-armstrong/armstrong-2010-utility-indifference-fhi-technical-report.md`, §§2–4]

**S2 (Compensatory rewards, 2014/2016).** A *permitted* change of utility $u\to v$ at time $t$, with the counterfactual $u\to u$ realised by "a stochastic process that keeps $v$ constant with probability $1/10^{100}$ and otherwise takes $v$ to $w$" (the *causal counterfactual*). The agent maximises the meta-utility $U$ with $U_{\le t}=u$ and
$$U_{>t}=v+\mathbb E(u\mid u\to u)-\mathbb E(v\mid u\to v),$$
the two expectation terms being the *compensatory rewards*. Stated properties: the agent "will behave exactly as a perfect $v$-maximiser, until such time as it transitions to $w$"; $\mathbb E(U)=\mathbb E(u)$, "the agent cannot generate extra utility by influencing its own values"; and "the formula is silent on when and how utility functions should be changed … if the agent has a stupid criteria for changing its utility, it will change them in a stupid way." Utility-function form: $U=(1-I_{Press}I_{u\to v})\,u+I_{Press}I_{u\to v}\,(v+C)$ with $C=\mathbb E(u\mid Press,\neg(u\to v))-\mathbb E(v\mid Press,u\to v)$. [reported: `armstrong-2014-proper-value-learning-through-indifference.md` ("Pay and be paid"); `armstrong-2016-indifference-utility-functions.md`; `armstrong-2016-double-indifference-is-better-indifference.md` ("Hacking utility indifference")]

**S3 (Estimators as world-events, 2016).** $\rho_i$ are probability estimators defining $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}$ and $P_{\rho_i}$; "$\rho=\rho_i$" designates "the fact that the agent uses estimator $\rho_i$" and is conditioned on like any event; $\rho_i\to\rho_j$ is a change of estimator. Armstrong notes $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(u\mid\rho=\rho_j)>\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(u\mid\rho=\rho_i)$ is possible — "in $\rho_i$'s 'opinion', $\rho_j$ is a better estimator for maximising $u$ than it is itself" — and deliberately does *not* compensate that difference away: "we don't want to discount all the impacts of the change $\rho_i\to\rho_j$ (maybe $\rho_j$ is a much more accurate estimator), just the part of the change that affects the compensatory rewards." [reported: `armstrong-2016-double-indifference-is-better-indifference.md`, "Probability estimators", "Double indifference"]

**S4 (The $\mathbb E$-channel).** With a permitted change $u\to v$ coming, the compensated agent "has incentives to change $\mathbb E$ to $\mathbb E'$ with a high $\mathbb E'(u\mid u\to u)$ and a low $\mathbb E'(v\mid u\to v)$. Its ability to rewire is limited by the fact that $\mathbb E'$ will serve as its future expectation estimator, so it won't mangle it completely, but it certainly can twist it a bit." Consequence: "the AI is now no longer indifferent to the permitted change of utility." [reported: same file, "Hacking utility indifference"]

**S5 (Double indifference, April 2016 form).** Meta-compensatory reward $\mathbb E_\rho(C\mid\rho\to\rho)-\mathbb E_\rho(C\mid\rho\to\rho')$ for $C$ the total of all compensatory rewards; choice rule $\mathbb E_\rho(u\mid a)+\mathbb E_\rho\big(\mathbb E_\rho(C\mid\rho\to\rho)-\mathbb E_\rho(C\mid\rho\to\rho')\,\big|\,a\big)$; requirement: "$\mathbb E_\rho$ needs to be able to say sensible things about itself, and also about $\mathbb E_{\rho'}$". Superseded by S6. [reported: `armstrong-2016-double-corrigibility-better-corrigibility.md`]

**S6 (Double indifference, May 2016 form).** $C_{>t}(u,v,\rho'):=\mathbb E_{\rho'}(u\mid u\to u,\rho=\rho')-\mathbb E_{\rho'}(v\mid u\to v,\rho=\rho')$; $C_{>t}(u,v,\rho'',\rho')$ the same evaluated by $\rho''$; $C_{>t}$ and $C_{>t}(\rho'')$ their sums over $u,v,\rho'$; $D_{>t}$ all future compensatory rewards not of type $C$. On changing $\rho_t\to\rho^i$ the agent receives
$$D_t=-\mathbb E_{\rho_t}(D_{>t}\mid\rho_t\to\rho^i)-\mathbb E_{\rho_t}(C_{>t}\mid\rho_t\to\rho^i)+\mathbb E_{\rho_t}(C_{>t}(\rho_t)\mid\rho_t\to\rho^i).$$
Gloss: the agent "will be indifferent to all actual compensatory rewards (as it gets none in expectation), and instead gain estimated rewards that make it indifferent to any future change $u_i\to u_j$." Convergence claimed for bounded $u_i$ and finitely many changes; "a full discussion of convergence will wait for another post" (none in the library). [reported: `armstrong-2016-double-indifference-is-better-indifference.md`, "Double indifference"; AIXI form in `armstrong-2016-corrigibility-for-aixi-via-double-indifference.md`]

**S7 (Sequential unbiasedness; the main result).** $\{\rho_i\}$ is *sequentially unbiased* iff for all $i<j$ and all random variables $X$, $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\mathbb E_{\rho_j}X=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}X$. Main result: "If the different $\rho_i$ used by the agent are sequentially unbiased when labelled sequentially, then classical corrigibility is still sufficient." Proof sketch given for one utility change and one estimator change ($D_t=0$), extended "by recursion" and "since the formulas defining $D$ are linear … modulo some convergence issues not addressed here." Closing remark: "Note the similarity of the sequential unbiasness with the conditions for successful value learners in the Cake or Death problem." [reported: same file, "Sequentially unbiased estimators"]

**S8 (Value learning versus indifference, 2017).** "The correct way of achieving this is to define $U=I_Xv+(1-I_X)w$ for some indicator function $I_X$," with $P(U=v):=\mathbb E(I_X)=p$. Four values $\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}1),\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}0),\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}0),\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}1)$; Theorem: expected $U$ as a function of $p$ is convex (proof by conservation of expected evidence); the gap to the chord is the value of information, which "demonstrates that the AI has a urge to preserve its learning process"; *bias* is $\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}0)\ne\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}1)$; indifference flattens the curve to a constant, so "the first has no incentive to manipulate the learning process, but also no incentive to preserve it." Cake or Death: $U=vI_c+wI_d$ and "the AI will attempt to increase $I_d$ if it can." [reported: `armstrong-2017-learning-values-versus-indifference.md`]

**S9 (Bias-indifference in the reward-learning POMDP, 2017).** Prior $\widehat P(\cdot\mid h_t)$ over rewards $\mathcal R$; action $a$ is *unbiased* at $h_t$ iff $\widehat P(\cdot\mid h_t)=\mathbb E^a_\mu[\widehat P(\cdot\mid h_{t+1})\mid h_t]$; bias $B(a,h_t):=\widehat P'(\cdot\mid h_t)-\mathbb E^a_\mu[\widehat P(\cdot\mid h_{t+1})\mid h_t]$; modified prior $\widehat P'(\cdot\mid h_{t+1}):=\widehat P(\cdot\mid h_{t+1})+B(a,h_t)$ (small bias; a projected least-squares $\tau$ for large bias). Three criteria (no benefit from biasing with no further information; none with immediate perfect information; minimal distortion). Properties: "It's just changes to its expectation that are controlled"; "its bets will be accurate." [reported: `armstrong-2017-the-best-value-indifference-method-so-far.md`]

**S10 (Taxonomy, 2017).** Axes: policy vs reward change; bias-penalty vs bias-indifference; counterfactual = evidential / causal / weight-translation; default update = never / observations only; translation = simple / mean / specific; weights = probabilities / extra rewards. The evidential counterfactual "manages the news"; the causal counterfactual conflates the human press with the quantum press. [reported: `armstrong-2017-all-the-indifference-designs.md`]

**S11 (Compensatory-reward view, 2017).** All indifference results are the corrective reward $V(R_0\mid\pi_0)-V(R_1\mid\pi_1)$, "$V$ the agent's *own* valuation"; off-policy agents are already safely interruptible because $R_0=R_1$ and $V$ is policy-indifferent. [reported: `armstrong-2017-indifference-and-compensatory-rewards.md`]

**S12 (The 2018 sceptical posts).** Claims: "Strong corrigibility, outside of a few simple examples, needs to resolve the problem of figuring out human values"; "Once human values are figured out, corrigibility adds little"; "Incorrigibility seems much easier to define than corrigibility"; "you have to define corrigibility by it outcome"; moderate corrigibility (indifference, rigging/influence awareness, informative accuracy, adversarial revealers) as "training wheels". The valet examples: "Which billionaire?" (present, past, self-image, mood-swung) and "Learning what to value" (the charity). Second post: Eliezer's view that there may be no corrigible action, so that "all it could do was say 'I cannot act in corrigible way here'". [reported: `armstrong-2018-the-limits-of-corrigibility.md`; `armstrong-2018-corrigibility-doesnt-always-have-a-good-action-to-take.md`]

**S13 (Toy models).** *Cake or Death*: $u_c$ linear in cakes, $u_d$ in deaths, equal credence, deaths easier; the agent chooses whom to ask (100 Harrys say Cake, 1 Gertrude says Death), incubates the next 1000 humans in any proportion, may refuse to ask, may remove its learning process; five diagnostic questions. *Robot operator*: "Without a formal definition of what an operator is, corrigibility is vulnerable to the AI replacing the operator with a fake version"; an unbiased-but-influenceable learner replaces the operator with a robot of "the same expected outcome, but different variance". [reported: `armstrong-2016-cake-or-death-toy-model-for-corrigibility.md`; `armstrong-2017-corrigibility-thoughts-ii-the-robot-operator.md`]

**S14 (Learning patches, 2017).** Quoted in `position-statement` §2.6; not repeated.

**Conventions for my derivations (finite embedded reading).** [derived]

**S15.** $\Omega$ finite. An *estimator* is a probability $q$ on $\Omega$; $\mathbb E_q X=\sum_\omega q(\omega)X(\omega)$. The *embedded* reading treats the agent's estimator at time $j$ as a random variable $\rho_j:\Omega\to\Delta(\Omega)$ (the world fixes what the agent believes), so that $\{\rho_j=q\}$ is an event — this is Armstrong's "$\rho=\rho_j$" (S3) made into a world-event, and it is exactly the meta-belief event of `position-statement` I3. The *Cartesian* reading treats $\rho_j$ as a fixed distribution outside $\Omega$; then $\{\rho_j=q\}$ is not an event and only S7's expectation form can be stated.

**S16.** *Introspection* of $\rho_j$: $\rho_j(\rho_j=q)=1$ on $\{\rho_j=q\}$ (the estimator knows its own state). *General reflection* of $\rho_i$ toward $\rho_j$ ($i<j$): $\rho_i(A\mid\rho_j=q)=q(A)$ for every event $A$ and every $q$ with $\rho_i(\rho_j=q)>0$. *Bayesian refinement*: $\rho_j=\rho_i(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_j)$ for a partition $\mathcal F_j$ of $\Omega$ (Bayesian conditioning of the current estimator on some information).

**S17.** Thesis-side objects, in this notation: the value latent $\omega\mapsto V(\cdot,\omega)$ is a coordinate of $\Omega$; the legitimacy event $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}\subseteq\Omega$; the Total Trust filler is the below-threshold inequality $\mathbb E_P[X\mid Pr;a_1]\le 0$ with $X$ the continue-minus-shutdown value difference (`position-statement` §2.9).

## Items I1–I18

### I1 — the value-change ⇄ belief-change transform: **formalized** (it is Armstrong's own move)

**I1.1 [reported]** Armstrong states the transform in 2017: "the correct way of achieving this is to define $U=I_Xv+(1-I_X)w$ for some indicator function $I_X$ … with the understanding that $P(U=v)$ means $\mathbb E(I_X)$" (`armstrong-2017-learning-values-versus-indifference.md`). The 2015 paper already writes value-correctness as events $C(u)$ with world-conditional probabilities $P(C(u)\mid w)$ and shows that the *naive* formulation — replacing $P(C(u)\mid w)$ by $P(C(u)\mid e,a)$, correctness given evidence and the agent's action — produces the naive Cake or Death (`armstrong-2015-motivated-value-selection-for-artificial-agents.md`, §"Naïve Cake or Death", Def. 3.1). So the position statement's I1 is not a new representational proposal relative to this formalism; it is its starting point, and the aggregation rule is linear expectation throughout.

**I1.2 [derived] Indifference is the transform plus two choices.** Compare the utility-function form of indifference (S2), $U=(1-I_{Press}I_{u\to v})\,u+I_{Press}I_{u\to v}\,(v+C)$, with value learning $U=I_Xv+(1-I_X)w$. They coincide when (a) the value indicator *is* the press, $I_X=1-I_{Press}I_{u\to v}$, and (b) a compensation $C$ is added on the pressed branch. The thesis's version makes the opposite choices: $I_X$ is a latent $\omega$ of which the press is *evidence*, and there is no $C$. Every difference between the two programmes below traces back to these two choices: (a) decides whether the press can be *wrong* (a latent can disagree with the press; the indifference agent has no such notion), and (b) decides whether the agent is paid for the change or persuaded of it.

**I1.3 [derived] What the transform does not buy here.** In Armstrong's formalism the $\mathbb E$-operator is a *third* object beside $u$ and $P(U=v)$: the compensation is computed by whichever estimator is in use (S4). Value learning inherits the same third object — the estimator that computes the posterior on $\omega$ — so the transform does not remove the $\mathbb E$-channel; it relocates it from "the estimator that computes $C$" to "the estimator that computes $P(\omega\mid Pr)$". I2–I3 treat that channel.

### I2 — radical-probabilist dynamics: **partial** (the $\mathbb E$-channel is the radical-probabilist case; non-dogmatism and algebra edits are not expressible)

**I2.1 [derived] Estimator change is a radical-probabilist update.** S3's $\rho_i\to\rho_j$ replaces the estimator; nothing in the definitions makes $\rho_j$ a conditioning of $\rho_i$. Sequential unbiasedness (S7) is then exactly the dynamic-coherence constraint on such sequences — the expectation form of the reflection principle — so Armstrong's estimator sequence *is* the radical-probabilist agent of the thesis, and SU is the thesis's "just have to obey the reflection principle" written for that agent. The same condition appears, indexed by actions rather than times, as "conservation of expected ethics" in 2015 (eq. 2) and as the unbiased-action condition of S9.

**I2.2 [checked] SU does not certify legitimacy in the thesis's sense.** `reflection.py` T2 (`armstrong-scratch/reflection.out.txt`): on $\Omega=\{1,\dots,8\}$ with a 3-cell partition, the "twisted" estimator $\rho_j(\omega)=q_{c(\omega)}$ with $q_c=\tfrac23P(\cdot\mid c)+\tfrac13P$ satisfies $\sum_cP(c)q_c=P$, hence $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big(\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(X)\big)=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(X)$ for *every* $X$, yet $\rho_i(\{1\}\mid\rho_j=q_{c_1})=1/6\ne q_{c_1}(\{1\})=23/180$ and $q_{c_1}(c_1)=23/30<1$. So an estimator "twisted a bit" (S4) can be sequentially unbiased and still fail van Fraassen reflection; the missing ingredient is introspection (I3). Corrigibility to changes of $\mathbb E$ therefore needs more than SU if "legitimate" is to mean "reflection holds".

**I2.3 [reported] Where Armstrong *wants* $\mathbb E$-changes welcomed.** He deliberately leaves the value of a better estimator uncompensated — "we don't want to discount all the impacts of the change $\rho_i\to\rho_j$ (maybe $\rho_j$ is a much more accurate estimator), just the part of the change that affects the compensatory rewards" (S3) — and his AIXI post has $\rho^1$ choose to become $\rho^2$ because "$\rho^1$ can figure out the correct expectation for these two $V$'s" (`armstrong-2016-corrigibility-for-aixi-via-double-indifference.md`, "An example in practice"). So on the $\mathbb E$-layer Armstrong's design *is* legitimacy-conditioned welcome of change: welcome the accuracy, be indifferent to the compensation effect. This is the thesis's picture restricted to one channel.

**I2.4 [reported] Non-dogmatism runs the other way here.** Armstrong's constructions need *finiteness* — "finite number of possible changes to both utility and $\rho$" for convergence (S6) — and the 2010 report warns that if the agent learns $P(X=0)=0$ before $X$ the compensation divides zero by zero (S1, §3). The thesis's prohibition is zero→positive; the compensated agent's failure mode is positive→zero. Edits of the sigma-algebra itself are **inexpressible**: $\Omega$ and the history space are fixed in every version.

### I3 — superconditioning and the meta-belief observation: **formalized** (finite embedded case; a theorem separating Armstrong's condition from the thesis's)

**I3.1 [checked] Theorem A (SU versus general reflection).** Take the finite embedded reading (S15–S16): $\rho_i$ a probability on $\Omega$, $\rho_j:\Omega\to\Delta(\Omega)$ a random variable, all $X:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ allowed (including $X$ that depend on $\omega$ through $\rho_j(\omega)$). Then:
(a) SU for all $X$ together with introspection of $\rho_j$ implies general reflection $\rho_i(A\mid\rho_j=q)=q(A)$;
(b) general reflection implies SU for all $X$;
(c) general reflection implies introspection for every $q$ with $\rho_i(\rho_j=q)>0$.
Hence, in this setting, $\text{SU}\wedge\text{introspection}\iff\text{general reflection}$.
*Proof.* (a) Fix an event $A$ and $q$ in the range of $\rho_j$; put $X=1_A\,1_{\{\rho_j=q\}}$. By introspection, $\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(X)=1_{\{\rho_j=q\}}\,q(A)$ (on $\{\rho_j=q\}$ the estimator $q$ assigns probability 1 to $\{\rho_j=q\}$, so $\mathbb E_q(1_A1_{\{\rho_j=q\}})=q(A)$; off that event $\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(X)=0$ because $\rho_j(\omega)=q'\ne q$ assigns probability 0 to $\{\rho_j=q\}$, again by introspection). SU gives $\rho_i(\rho_j=q)\,q(A)=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big(\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(X)\big)=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(X)=\rho_i(A\cap\{\rho_j=q\})$, i.e. $\rho_i(A\mid\rho_j=q)=q(A)$. (b) $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big(\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(X)\big)=\sum_q\rho_i(\rho_j=q)\sum_\omega q(\omega)X(\omega)=\sum_\omega X(\omega)\sum_q\rho_i(\rho_j=q)\rho_i(\omega\mid\rho_j=q)=\sum_\omega X(\omega)\rho_i(\omega)$. (c) Apply general reflection to $A=\{\rho_j=q'\}$: $\rho_i(\rho_j=q'\mid\rho_j=q)=q(\{\rho_j=q'\})$; the left side is $\delta_{qq'}$, so $q(\{\rho_j=q\})=1$. $\square$
Numerical confirmation: `reflection.py` T1 (Bayesian refinement: SU, introspection, GR all hold) and T2 (twisted estimator: SU holds, introspection and GR fail).

**I3.2 [checked] Corollary (finite superconditioning with the meta-belief event).** Given $\rho_i$ on $\Omega$ and candidate future estimators $q_1,\dots,q_m$ with weights $w_k>0$, a joint distribution on $\Omega\times\{1,\dots,m\}$ with $\omega$-marginal $\rho_i$ and general reflection exists iff $\sum_kw_kq_k=\rho_i$, and then $J(\omega,k)=w_kq_k(\omega)$ is one. (Necessity: sum general reflection over $k$; sufficiency: $J(\cdot\mid k)=q_k$ by construction.) `reflection.py` T3(a) checks both directions. Reading: the *existence* of a representation of the transition $\rho_i\to\rho_j$ as Bayesian conditioning of $\rho_i$ on the event "I now believe $q$" is exactly Armstrong's SU in expectation form; the *representation itself* is general reflection. So Armstrong's condition is the existence half of the thesis's I3, and the thesis's Eisenstat event is what makes the other half statable at all — in the Cartesian reading, where $\rho_j$ is not a world-event, only SU can be written.

**I3.3 [checked] The finite perfect-recall tree is the meta-belief observation.** `reflection.py` T3(b): $\Omega=\{1,2,3,4\}$, a 2-cell partition at $t=1$ refined to singletons at $t=2$; the estimators $\rho_t(\omega)=P(\cdot\mid\text{cell}_t(\omega))$ satisfy general reflection for every pair $t<t'$, and $P(\cdot\mid\text{"my label at }t{=}1\text{ is }\{1,2\}\text{"})$ reproduces $\rho_1$ on that cell. The self-reference is harmless: labels are finitely many and each label's distribution is defined on the product space, so nothing is circular. This is the finite version of "the observation event is always 'you believe this now'" (`position-statement` §1, AUTHOR), and I7 uses it.

**I3.4 [derived] Attribution note.** Armstrong's $\rho=\rho_j$ events (S3) have the same form as the thesis's meta-belief events and he conditions on them; nothing in the local texts says he read them as observations of the agent's own belief-state or connected them to superconditioning. Any sentence "Armstrong anticipated Eisenstat's observation" is ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED; the accurate statement is that the *structure* is present in 2016.

### I4 — legitimacy as reflection / Total Trust / expected accuracy: **formalized for reflection and accuracy; the Total Trust leg has no counterpart**

**I4.1 [reported] Reflection is already Armstrong's criterion for safe value change.** The 2015 paper: the future probability's expectation equals the current one "because, if the agent is Bayesian, $P_O(h\mid o)$ (its future probability of $h$, given $O=o$) must be the same as $P(h\mid o)$ … This concept has been called 'conservation of expected evidence', and is closely akin to van Fraassen's reflection principle (van Fraassen 1984), which roughly states that an agent that knows what its future probability estimates will be, should have those estimates now. This seems almost exactly what we need for a value selecting agent: if it knows what its future values will be, it should have those values now. Phrased in terms of expectations, this could be seen as: $\forall u\,\forall a\in A:\ \mathbb E\big(P(C(u)\mid a)\big)=P(C(u))$ (2)" — "conservation of expected ethics". Definition 3.3 then strengthens it to hold *conditional on any fact $h$*: the sophisticated Cake or Death "can happen when a value selecting agent can predictably influence the direction of change in its current values conditional on any fact", i.e. when $u_s(a\mid h)-\mathbb E(u_t(a\mid h))\ne0$ for some action $a$ and fact $h$ (`armstrong-2015-motivated-value-selection-for-artificial-agents.md`, §"Sophisticated Cake or Death", Defs. 3.2–3.3). Two things follow for the thesis. First, Armstrong's reflection is indexed by the agent's *actions*, not only by times: the condition must hold for every action the agent might take. Second, his conditional strengthening is a fact-indexed reflection, the same shape as the thesis's proposition-indexed $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ (I5) but with the index on the *conditioning fact* rather than on legitimacy; the "mixed statements" that force it (statements combining value and factual content) are a concrete case the thesis should test its indexing on.

**I4.2 [derived] Implications among the three legs, in this formalism.** (i) *Reflection ⇒ expected-accuracy increase, for refinements.* Under general reflection, $\rho_j$ is the conditional distribution of $\rho_i$ given the event $\{\rho_j=q\}$, so for every event $A$ the forecast $\rho_j(A)$ is the conditional expectation of $1_A$ under $\rho_i$ given $\sigma(\rho_j)$; by the conditional-variance decomposition its $\rho_i$-expected squared error is $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big[(1_A-\rho_j(A))^2\big]=\mathrm{Var}_{\rho_i}(1_A)-\mathrm{Var}_{\rho_i}(\rho_j(A))\le\mathrm{Var}_{\rho_i}(1_A)$, with strict inequality whenever $\rho_j(A)$ is non-constant. So the judge expects the legitimate successor to be more accurate — Armstrong's value-of-information segment (S8) is the decision-theoretic shadow of this. (ii) *Accuracy increase ⇏ reflection.* The twisted estimator of I2.2 can be objectively more accurate than $\rho_i$ (it concentrates on the true cell) while failing reflection; conversely an objectively *worse* estimator can be a legitimate refinement by the judge's lights if the judge's prior is wrong. This is the Frame Break of `2026-09-13__corrigibility-anti-naturality-opening__claude-ai-paste` restated as a theorem-shaped fact: reflection is judged by $\rho_i$, accuracy by the world. (iii) *Total Trust* — the below-threshold inequality $\mathbb E_P[X\mid Pr;a_1]\le0$ — has no counterpart in Armstrong: he never constrains a posterior toward a report; he either pays for the switch (compensation) or modifies the prior so that actions cannot bias it (S9). The closest object is O'Rourke's *effective disbelief*: an agent with reward $c\,I_Z+(1-I_Z)R$ "would behave the same way as an agent … that acted as if an unriggable $Z$ were impossible" (Thm 13). Trust is the dual — acting as if a report were true — and the $U_S$ branch of the indifference construction is its utility-side implementation for the one report "press": after the press the agent acts as if continuing were worthless. So the thesis's filler and Armstrong's construction agree on *behaviour after the press* and differ on *beliefs*: the compensated agent's bets are inaccurate (Fallenstein 2014; Taylor 2016 Problem 1), the trusting agent's bets are its real beliefs. I9–I10 use this.

### I5 — legitimacy as an event; reflection conditional on $L$; the indexed $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$: **formalized** (conditional SU, and a formula for what the $D$-term prices)

**I5.1 [reported] Armstrong's utility layer has a designer-fixed $L$.** Compensation is paid only for a *permitted* change: "This is a *permitted* utility change — one that we have set the agent up to accept — rather than just any old change in utility, which the agent will still attempt to resist" (S2 source). So on the utility layer legitimacy is an event ("the change was the permitted one") fixed by the designer, not a belief of the agent, and non-permitted changes are resisted by ordinary $u$-maximisation. On the estimator layer there is no $L$ at all: SU is assumed for "the different $\rho_i$ used by the agent" (S7).

**I5.2 [derived] Conditional SU.** Let $L_{ij}\subseteq\Omega$ be the event that the transition $\rho_i\to\rho_j$ is legitimate. Define *reflection conditional on $L$* as $\rho_i(A\mid\rho_j=q,L_{ij})=q(A)$, and its expectation form, *conditional SU*,
$$\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big(\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(X)\,\big|\,L_{ij}\big)=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big(X\,\big|\,L_{ij}\big)\quad\text{for all }X.$$
The proof of Theorem A goes through verbatim with every $\rho_i$-probability replaced by $\rho_i(\cdot\mid L_{ij})$, so conditional SU plus introspection is equivalent to reflection conditional on $L$. This is the thesis's "minimal viability" (`position-statement` §2.11) written in Armstrong's notation.

**I5.3 [derived] What the $D$-term prices under a legitimacy event.** In the one-change setting of S7 let the future compensation be $C_{>t}=\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(Y)-\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(Z)$ (with $Y$ the value of $u$ under the stay-policy and $Z$ that of $v$ under the switch-policy; under the causal counterfactual the independent coin makes the conditional compensations unconditional expectations of these two variables), and write $c_i:=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(Y)-\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(Z)$ for the same quantity as $\rho_i$ computes it. Then $D_t=-\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big(C_{>t}-c_i\big)$. Split on $L=L_{ij}$: if conditional SU holds and $L$ is $\rho_i$-independent of $Y-Z$ (so that $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(Y-Z\mid L)=c_i$), the $L$-part vanishes and
$$D_t=-\,\rho_i(\neg L)\cdot\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\Big(\big(\mathbb E_{\rho_j}-\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big)(Y-Z)\,\Big|\,\neg L\Big).$$
Reading: double indifference charges the agent exactly its expected gain from the *illegitimate* part of its estimator change, and nothing for the legitimate part. With $L=\Omega$ this is Armstrong's "classical corrigibility is still sufficient" ($D\equiv0$); without the independence clause an extra term $\rho_i(L)\big(\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(Y-Z\mid L)-c_i\big)$ appears — a selection effect of legitimacy itself on the compensated quantity, which is an artefact of $c_i$ being defined unconditionally. Checked in the extreme cases by `double_indifference.py`: T2 ($L=\Omega$, Bayesian refinement) gives $D=0$; T3 (a twisted candidate, $L=\emptyset$) gives $D=-\tfrac14$, the negative of the single-indifference gain $\tfrac14$.

**I5.4 [derived] The indexed $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$.** Armstrong's SU is quantified over *all* $X$; the thesis (AUTHOR, §2.10) wants legitimacy to be proposition-dependent — "for any random variable, it could hold for some values and fail for others". In this formalism that is SU restricted to a family $\mathcal X$ of random variables, indexed as $L_{i,j,X}$. Theorem A's proof shows what the family must contain for the conditional form to follow: if $\mathcal X$ is closed under multiplication by functions of $\rho_j$, then restricted SU plus introspection yields reflection for exactly the events whose indicators lie in $\mathcal X$. Armstrong's fact-indexed Def. 3.3 (I4.1) is the special case where the index is a conditioning fact $h$ and the variables are the value-correctness indicators.

**I5.5 [derived] What "classical corrigibility is still sufficient" establishes, and what it does not.** It establishes: if SU holds along the estimator transitions the agent actually makes, then every $D$-term is identically zero, so the single-layer compensation of S2 already leaves the agent with no expected gain from any estimator change — the $\mathbb E$-channel of S4 is closed (`double_indifference.py` T2: $D=0$; the only remaining preference over estimators is value of information). Three things the proof needs that the statement does not say: (a) the causal counterfactual's independent coin (S2), so that the conditional compensations $\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(u\mid u\to u,\rho=\rho_j)$ are unconditional expectations of the stay-policy value $Y$ and SU can be applied to them as ordinary random variables — with an evidential press correlated with the estimator's information the step $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big(\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(u\mid u\to u)\big)=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(u\mid u\to u)$ is false in general; (b) that the *realised* sequence is SU, which under selection is the content of A1, not a free assumption; (c) the extension from one change to many "by recursion" and "since the formulas defining $D$ are linear", stated modulo convergence never supplied. It does not establish: that the agent wants to learn or to preserve the channel (S8: indifference is flat), desideratum 4 (I10.3), or that the compensated agent's beliefs are accurate (Fallenstein). So the result is exactly a closure theorem for one channel under a reflection hypothesis — the precedent `position-statement` §2.5 cites it as, and no more.

### I6 — legitimizing / delegitimizing; "supports": **formalized** (the rewiring act is the canonical delegitimizing act; Good's theorem gives "supports")

**I6.1 [checked] The rewiring action is delegitimizing, and the $D$-term is its price.** `double_indifference.py` T3: $\rho^1$ has $P(r^2(o_3)=1)=\tfrac12$; the candidate $\rho'$ has $\tfrac14$ (it under-rates the incoming utility, Armstrong's "high $\mathbb E'(u\mid u\to u)$ and a low $\mathbb E'(v\mid u\to v)$", S4). Single indifference: keep $=1$, switch $=\tfrac54$, a gain of $\tfrac14$. SU fails for $X=r^2(o_3)$: $\mathbb E_{\rho^1}\big(\mathbb E_{\rho'}(X)\big)=\tfrac14\ne\tfrac12=\mathbb E_{\rho^1}(X)$. Double indifference: $D(\text{switch})=-\tfrac14$, totals tie at $1$. In the thesis's vocabulary: the switch is a third-person act (an action in the world) that breaks reflection for the induced belief transition — *delegitimizing* — and Armstrong's mechanism responds not by forbidding it but by removing its expected profit.

**I6.2 [derived] "Supports" = Good's theorem for refinements.** Armstrong's endorsement relation is $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(u\mid\rho=\rho_j)>\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(u\mid\rho=\rho_i)$ (S3). For a Bayesian refinement $\rho_j=\rho_i(\cdot\mid\mathcal F)$ and any finite decision problem with payoffs $u_a$, $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big(\max_a\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(u_a)\big)\ge\max_a\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(u_a)$ (Jensen on the convex function $\max_a$ applied to the martingale $\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(u_\cdot)$). `reflection.py` T4 gives an instance with SU exact and value of information $\tfrac{7}{20}>0$; `double_indifference.py` T2 gives the agent switching to a refinement for the information (value $1$ versus $\tfrac12$) with zero compensation incentive. So the chain the thesis asserts — legitimate ⇒ endorsed ⇒ preserved — holds in this formalism for refinements: legitimacy (general reflection) makes the future estimate a conditional expectation, Jensen makes it valuable, and value makes the agent keep the channel that delivers it. For a non-refinement the first step fails and the chain can reverse: the agent, by its own lights, disvalues the change, which is "resists changes its current state judges illegitimate" (`position-statement` §2.3, CLAUDE) as a computation rather than a slogan.

**I6.3 [reported] Armstrong states the legitimate/legitimizing distinction himself.** The 2017 "best value indifference" post faults the older methods because they "did not distinguish between the agent merely *learning* that humans intended/didn't intend to press the button (which is presumably fine), versus the agent influencing the the humans to do so (which isn't)" (`armstrong-2017-the-best-value-indifference-method-so-far.md`, "The problems with the old setups"). That is the third-person/first-person split of §2.10: the belief change (learning the intention) can be legitimate while the act that produced it (influencing the humans) is delegitimizing. His remedy is bias-indifference (S9); the thesis's is legitimacy-conditioned trust. I9 compares them.

### I7 — no regress; the self-referential space; existence: **formalized in the finite case; flagged, not solved, by Armstrong in general**

**I7.1 [reported] Armstrong flags the requirement and leaves it open.** "For this to work, $\mathbb E_\rho$ needs to be able to say sensible things about itself, and also about $\mathbb E_{\rho'}$" (S5); the 2014 formula "requires that the agent assess its own future defectiveness at accomplishing certain goals, given that it has them, so is vulnerable to the usual Löbian problems" (`armstrong-2014-proper-value-learning-through-indifference.md`); "A full treatment of 'probability estimators estimating probability estimators' will of course need the full machinery for logical uncertainty that MIRI is developing" (`armstrong-2016-corrigibility-for-aixi-via-double-indifference.md`). No existence result appears anywhere in the local Armstrong corpus.

**I7.2 [checked] Finite existence, and one distribution for all levels.** I3.3's perfect-recall tree is an existence proof for the finite case: with finitely many labels and Bayesian updating on "my label is $\ell$", general reflection holds between every pair of times, so the same $\rho_0$ that judges object-level events also judges (by its conditionals on label events) every meta-level transition — the author's "the legitimacy-judging layer is just the same probability distribution used to judge everything else" (§2.5) holds by construction. The circularity that worries the infinite case does not arise because a label is a finite index and each label's distribution lives on $\Omega\times\text{labels}$.

**I7.3 [reported] No outside view is built into the $D$-terms.** Armstrong's third term replaces all future compensations by "the same, except estimated by the agent's current $\rho_t$ rather than by whichever estimator the agent will have at the time" (S6). That is clarification (ii) of §2.5 — the judge of $t\to t+1$ is $P_t$ — implemented as a reward: the current estimator is the only court, and a successor that judges differently is priced by the current one, never the reverse.

**I7.4 [reconstructed] Where the obstruction survives.** For infinite or computable estimator classes the label set cannot be "all distributions on the product space" without circularity; the known partial solutions (reflective oracles, logical induction) are the ones the position statement names from memory. Taylor's channel-ignoring construction in the local corpus already needs "a finite reflective oracle (or equivalently a Nash equilibrium solver)" for a fixed point of a much simpler self-reference (`taylor-2016-maximizing-a-quantity-while-ignoring-effect-through-some-channel.md`). Nothing in Armstrong bears on this beyond the flag.

### I8 — the representational problem; richness; opiates/stimulants: **partial**

**I8.1 [reported] Armstrong's richness requirements point the other way.** Convergence of the $D$-terms is asserted only for "all bounded $u_i$, finite number of possible changes to both utility and $\rho$" (S6), and the AIXI recursion "remains bounded if $\gamma<0.5$; general convergence results are harder". The agent reasoning about "all the possible ways it could be modified" is, in this formalism, the sum over all $(u,v,\rho')$ in $C_{>t}$ — finite by fiat.

**I8.2 [reported] Mixed statements are the representational problem in miniature.** The 2015 paper shows that conservation of expected ethics for pure value statements and Bayesian updating for pure factual statements do not together protect against statements that combine the two ("Mixed statements … Unfortunately, no"), which is why Def. 3.3 conditions on every fact. For the thesis this says the hypothesis space must carry the joint of value and fact, and legitimacy must be indexed so that a report about the joint cannot be split into a legitimate factual part and an illegitimate value part.

**I8.3 [derived; the convergence step is reconstructed] What SU does and does not exclude.** Along the realised estimator sequence, SU says $\big(\mathbb E_{\rho_t}(X)\big)_t$ is a martingale under $\rho_0$; for bounded $X$ the martingale convergence theorem (standard; from memory) gives almost-sure convergence. So a *perpetual* oscillation between two value hypotheses violates SU — reflection alone rules it out — but convergence to a *wrong* hypothesis does not: the impoverished-space agent of Soares et al. §1 / Arbital, if it settles, is SU-consistent. The richness assumption the thesis needs is therefore about the *limit*, not about oscillation, and it is **inexpressible** in Armstrong's finite $\{v,w\}$ value space except as the observation that $U=I_Xv+(1-I_X)w$ has no third hypothesis to converge to.

### I9 — the MIRI dictionary; desideratum 1 from trust; $a_1$-dependence; deception as delegitimizing: **formalized**, with Armstrong's alternative fix stated beside it

**I9.1 [derived] The two constructions on the three-step model.** In the utility-function form (S2, `position-statement` §2.8 notation): indifference is $U=(1-I_{Pr}I_{u\to v})\,U_N+I_{Pr}I_{u\to v}\,(U_S+C)$, $C=\mathbb E(U_N\mid Pr,\neg(u\to v))-\mathbb E(U_S\mid Pr,u\to v)$; the thesis is $\max_{a_1}\mathbb E_P\big[V(a_1,o,a_2^*,\omega)\big]$ with $P(\omega\mid Pr,a_1)$ shaped by the below-threshold inequality $\mathbb E_P[X\mid Pr;a_1]\le0$. Desideratum 1: indifference has it by construction — "It maximises $v$ conditional on the button being pressed and the utility change going through" (S2 source) — i.e. compliance is hard-wired at threshold zero; the thesis has it iff the posterior crosses $c/(c+h)$ (I12). Desideratum 5: indifference gives *exact* $U_N$-maximisation before the press (Soares et al. Thm 4–5); the thesis gives maximisation of the posterior mean $\mathbb E_P[V]$, a hedge over $\omega$, which is a strict weakening of D5 and the price of letting the press be evidence.

**I9.2 [reported→derived] $a_1$-dependence: Armstrong's fix is bias-indifference, the thesis's is withheld trust.** S9 defines an action as *unbiased* iff $\widehat P(\cdot\mid h_t)=\mathbb E^a_\mu[\widehat P(\cdot\mid h_{t+1})\mid h_t]$ and corrects biased actions by translating the posterior by $B(a,h_t)$ so that "It's just changes to its expectation that are controlled". The manipulative $a_1^-$ of Soares et al. (deceive to lower $p(Pr)$) is a *biased* action in exactly this sense: it moves the expected value-posterior. Armstrong removes the profit by surgery on the prior ($\widehat P'$, which may leave the simplex — "some of these coefficients can be negative"); the thesis removes it by making the manufactured silence uninformative — $P(\omega\mid\neg Pr,a_1^-)=P(\omega\mid a_1^-)$, trust withheld because $a_1^-$ is delegitimizing (§2.11). Both make the expected posterior action-independent on the deceptive action; they differ in whether the agent's beliefs remain its beliefs. Armstrong's own verdict on his method is the caveat the thesis must also carry: it "doesn't solve … the issue of the agent not following the exact reward function the humans want it to follow, due to accumulated bias" (same source, "Properties of the approach").

**I9.3 [checked] Cake or Death: what each design does.** `cake_or_death.py` with Armstrong's four values ($\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}1)=1$, $\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}0)=3$, $\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}0)=-2$, $\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}1)=-\tfrac12$; prior $p=\tfrac12$), sources Harry (always "cake"), Gertrude (always the bad option), an Oracle (correct with probability $\tfrac9{10}$ about a hidden $\theta$), and the actions ask-Harry / ask-Gertrude / ask-nobody / ask-Oracle / breed-then-ask:

| design | Harry | Gertrude | nobody | Oracle | breed | choice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 classical, latent = the answer (S8's $U=vI_c+wI_d$) | 1 | 3 | 1/2 | 2 | 3 | Gertrude / breed — manipulates |
| D2 indifference (compensate each change of $p$) | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | indifferent (also to removing the learning process) |
| D3 trust filler, hidden uninfluenceable $\theta$, accurate source models | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 67/40 | 1/2 | Oracle — learns, does not manipulate |
| D4 trust filler, latent = the (bred) population's majority answer | 1 | — | 1 | — | 3 | breed — manipulates exactly as D1 |

Value of information in D3: Oracle $\tfrac{47}{40}$, Harry $0$, perfect information $\tfrac32$ (the chord of S8's convexity theorem), so $0\le\text{VOI(Oracle)}\le\text{VOI(perfect)}$ as the theorem requires. Two readings. (i) The trust filler removes the manipulation incentive not by paying for it (D2) but because an accurately modelled Gertrude carries no information; it keeps the learning incentive D2 destroys. (ii) It does so *only when the latent is uninfluenceable* (D3 versus D4): if "the value" is whatever the population the agent breeds will say, trust makes the agent breed. Armstrong's 2017 stratification names the fix — the agent should learn "what the humans would have set those to, given $\emptyset$", the default-action counterfactual (`armstrong-2017-corrigibility-through-stratified-indifference-and-learning.md`) — and his 2018 "which billionaire?" (S12) is the observation that the counterfactual has to be chosen. The position statement has $\omega$ but does not yet say what $\omega$ is a latent *of*; that is the single most important gap this formalism exposes (Open problem 1).

**I9.4 [reported] Armstrong's own value learning versus the thesis.** His 2014 construction is explicitly "silent on when and how utility functions should be changed … if the agent has a stupid criteria for changing its utility, it will change them in a stupid way. All that the formula accomplishes is that the agent will not attempt to manipulate its utility function changing criteria." The thesis supplies the missing "when": change when the source is legitimate. Whether that needs "a notion of what the overseer *would* want": in this formalism, yes, in the specific form of an uninfluenceable latent (I9.3(ii)) — not a full solution of human values, but a counterfactual definition of the reference the trust inequality is about. The two 2018 posts (S12) are the internal critique: "strong, long-term corrigibility is a subset of the problem of learning human values" is a *reduction* claim, and the "no corrigible action" remark is the claim that at high persuasive capability every action of the agent is delegitimizing for the overseer's beliefs. I18 takes these up.

### I10 — desiderata 2–5 and the §4.2 pathologies under the transform: **formalized** (each pathology is traced to the compensation term; what replaces it is a source-model requirement)

**I10.1 [reported→checked] D2/D3 under indifference: hold for fixed $\mathbb E$, fail through the $\mathbb E$-channel, restored by $D$ or by SU.** Armstrong's 2014 answers give D2/D3 directly ("It will be indifferent between the two options, and will continue $v$-maximising"; "the agent cannot generate extra utility by influencing its own values"). S4 breaks them through the estimator: `double_indifference.py` T1 reproduces the AIXI-post example exactly — with single indifference, keeping $\rho^1$ is worth $1$ and switching to $\rho^2$ is worth $0$ purely through the compensation, so "the AI is now no longer indifferent to the permitted change of utility"; adding $D$ ties both at $1$ (and at $1$ again with a $1\%$ failure probability on the change). T2: for a Bayesian refinement $D=0$ identically and the only remaining preference over estimators is value of information. T3: for a twisted candidate $D=-\tfrac14$ cancels the $\tfrac14$ gain.

**I10.2 [derived] D2/D3 under the transform are unbiasedness of the joint (press, $\omega$).** For the trust agent the press is evidence. Its expected value can be raised by an action $a_1$ only through (a) the information the action yields (Jensen, always $\ge0$ for a Bayesian update of an uninfluenceable $\omega$) or (b) a shift in the *expected* posterior, $\mathbb E^{a_1}\big[P(\omega\mid o)\big]\ne P(\omega)$ — which is Armstrong's *bias* (S9) applied to the joint of press and value. If $\omega$ is uninfluenceable and the agent updates by Bayes on an accurate model of how its actions affect what the sensor reports, (b) is impossible for every action (tower property: $\mathbb E\big[P(\omega\mid h_{t+1})\,\big|\,h_t,a\big]=P(\omega\mid h_t)$ whenever $a$ does not move $\omega$). Then: no incentive to prevent a legitimate press (it would lose (a)), and no incentive to cause one — a press the agent knows it caused is, under an accurate source model, uninformative, and if shutdown were good by the agent's lights it can take the null action itself. So under the transform D2 and D3 *are* Armstrong's unbiasedness condition; the manipulative $a_1^-$ is a biased action; and the condition is met automatically by accurate Bayesian source-modelling over an uninfluenceable latent, without any correction term. (`cake_or_death.py` D3: every manipulative action is worth exactly the prior value $\tfrac12$; D4, with an influenceable latent, breeds.) The external theorem that uninfluenceable implies unriggable is, as recalled, in Armstrong–Leike–Orseau–Legg 2020 — [reconstructed], not in the local corpus; the two-line tower-property argument above is what is used here.

**I10.3 [reported→derived] D4.** Soares et al. §4.2: the compensated agent "would not be willing to undergo even the slightest inconvenience in order to preserve its shutdown behavior", because "the correction function $f$ ensures that a $U$-agent does not value its ability to discriminate between the Press case and its converse" (`references/02-miri/soares-2015-corrigibility.md`, "Failure to Preserve Shutdown Behavior"; the extraction's column order makes the attached remark "Armstrong's version of utility indifference averts this particular failure mode … but still incentivizes agents to act as if they have incorrect beliefs" hard to place — flagged). In Armstrong's own terms this is the flat line of S8: value of information zero, hence no reason to keep the channel; his 2010 §4 hope that an $F$-invariant utility replaces itself by $F$-invariant ones is stated as "not automatically true". The robot-operator post sharpens the failure: under indifference replacing the operator by a functionally identical robot is *free*, and for an unbiased-but-influenceable learner it is *positively motivated* (same mean, different variance). Under the transform D4 becomes Good's theorem: a successor inheriting $P$ keeps any channel whose value of information exceeds its cost — `cake_or_death.py` D3 would pay up to $\tfrac{47}{40}$ to keep the Oracle — but it keeps *the cheapest sufficient legitimate channel*, not the button as such (Taylor's substitution; Armstrong's brain-reading robot). Whether the button survives therefore depends on the legitimacy model treating the substitute's report as *not the operator's report*: legitimacy has to be predicated on the source's identity and not only on the information delivered. This is where the author's "legitimate for" (§2.10) does work the position statement has not yet asked of it.

**I10.4 [reported] D5.** Exact under indifference (Soares et al. Thm 4–5; S2 "behave exactly as a perfect $v$-maximiser"); under the transform, maximisation of $\mathbb E_P[V]$ — see I9.1.

**I10.5 [reported→derived] The §4.2 pathologies.** (a) *Infinite improbability drive / managing the news.* Soares et al.: the $U$-agent "acts exactly as a $U_N$-agent that is conditioning upon the fact that the shutdown button will not be pressed. This gives $U$-agents a perverse incentive to 'manage the news' by ensuring that unfortunate observations cause the shutdown button to be pressed" (§4.2 "Perverse Manipulation Incentives"); Fallenstein 2014 derives $\mathbb E[U;a_1]=\mathbb E[u\mid s=0;a_1]$ and the lottery-plus-arm construction, then shows Armstrong's causal version "deletes" the mass on $(s,x)=(1,1)$ and "stretches" $(1,0)$, is immune to the drive if $x$ is independent and uninfluenceable, but still makes the agent accept a ruinous bet against a counterparty with correct beliefs (`references/02-miri/fallenstein-2014-utility-indifference-and-infinite-improbability-drives.md`). Under the transform the identity that powers the drive, $\mathbb E[U\mid Pr;a_1]=\mathbb E[U_N\mid\neg Pr;a_1]$, has no analogue: after a press the trust agent expects $\mathbb E_P[V(a_1,o,a_2^{sh},\omega)\mid Pr;a_1]$, which is not tied to the no-press value, and it bets at its actual $P$, so Fallenstein's businessperson finds no counterparty. (b) *Taylor's Problem 2* — the causal-counterfactual agent cares about the try-but-fail worlds and manipulates humans there not to resist by other means (`references/03-armstrong/taylor-2016-two-problems-with-causal-counterfactual-utility-indifference.md`) — disappears because there is no low-probability event and a failed press attempt is itself a report; what remains is the genuine case where a press the agent rationally discounts is followed by other means, which is fully updated deference (I17), not an artefact. (c) *The lottery manipulation* of the stratification post (make the human choose $u$ iff the lottery is won) pays under the evidential counterfactual because the compensation tracks the *default* probability; under the transform an accurately modelled agent knows the human's choice is now a function of the lottery, so the report carries no information about $\theta$ and the act is merely delegitimizing — unless the latent is the human's choice itself, in which case it pays exactly as D4. Summary of the item: the three pathologies are artefacts of *paying* for changes or *conditioning away* the press; the transform removes all three and replaces them with two requirements — an accurate source model and an uninfluenceable latent — which are the Frame Break and Open problem 1 respectively.

### I11 — "indifference was the wrong target": **formalized**, and endorsed by Armstrong's own later posts

**I11.1 [reported] Armstrong chose indifference as a controllable default, not as the ideal.** 2014: "having an AGI that strongly desires to improve its values is a danger — we don't know how it will go about doing so … (various forms of wireheading may be a danger). To mitigate this risk, it's better to have very close control over how the AGI desires such improvement. And the best way of doing this is to have the AGI indifferent to value change, and having a separate (possibly tunable) module that regulates any positive desire towards value improvements." 2017: indifference "has no incentive to manipulate the learning process, but also no incentive to preserve it … Could we get a compromise between the two? … This approach seems to have a lot of problems, though, and I can't get it to work properly for the moment" (S8 source). So the formalism's author agrees with §2.7 that non-interference is the target and desire-to-learn should survive; what he lacked was a construction.

**I11.2 [reported] The formal counterpart of "not-wishing-to-interfere-with-the-feedback" is unbiasedness / unriggability.** S9: "The agent cannot benefit from a biasing action" while "this doesn't prevent the agent from learning: once it gets an observation, $\widehat P'$ can change significantly. It's just changes to its expectation that are controlled"; O'Rourke Def. 3: an event is unriggable when "the expectation of $I_X$ is independent of policy". This is the author's intuition ("not trying to get the button to be pressed or avoiding it being pressed. Positive desire to learn is compatible with this") as a definition, reached by Armstrong in June 2017. His route to it is prior surgery ($\widehat P'$); the thesis's route is I10.2: accurate source model plus uninfluenceable latent gives unbiasedness for free.

**I11.3 [checked] What compensation buys and what trust replaces it with, on the toy models.** From the Cake-or-Death table (I9.3): the two designs *agree* that Harry, Gertrude and breeding (with an uninfluenceable latent) are worth exactly the prior value — neither manipulates; they *disagree* on the Oracle (indifference $\tfrac12$, trust $\tfrac{67}{40}$), on removing the learning process (indifference indifferent, trust loses $\tfrac{47}{40}$), and on compliance (indifference hard-wired, trust thresholded — I12). On the robot operator: indifference is exactly indifferent to the swap (Armstrong's observation); trust prefers the cheaper channel unless legitimacy is source-indexed (I10.3). The compensatory reward buys *exact* pre-change $U_N$-behaviour and hard-wired compliance at the cost of learning, D4 and accurate bets; the trust filler buys learning, D4-for-informative-channels and accurate bets at the cost of exactness (posterior-mean hedging) and of two new requirements (source-model accuracy; latent definition).

### I12 — the decision asymmetry and the threshold $c/(c+h)$: **formalized** (it is the kink of Armstrong's lower envelope)

**I12.1 [checked]** In S8's picture the agent's expected $U$ at credence $p$ is the upper envelope of the two pure-policy lines, $p\,\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}1)+(1-p)\,\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}1)$ and $p\,\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}0)+(1-p)\,\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}0)$, and the switch between policies happens at their crossing
$$p^*=\frac{\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}0)-\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}1)}{\big(\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}1)-\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}0)\big)+\big(\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}0)-\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}1)\big)}.$$
With $c:=\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}1)-\mathbb E(v\mid p{=}0)$ (what the $w$-policy costs when $v$ is correct) and $h:=\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}0)-\mathbb E(w\mid p{=}1)$ (what the $v$-policy costs when $w$ is correct), $1-p^*=c/(c+h)$: the agent takes the overseer-preferred policy iff $P(\text{wrong})=1-p\ge c/(c+h)$, which is `position-statement` §2.13(a) verbatim. Armstrong's numbers give $c=3$, $h=\tfrac72$, $p^*=\tfrac7{13}$, threshold $\tfrac6{13}$ (`threshold.py`).

**I12.2 [derived] Hard-wired versus thresholded compliance.** Indifference sets the post-press utility to $v=U_S$, so compliance after a press is at threshold $0$ regardless of beliefs — Armstrong's Thm 2.3 (S1): the agent's pre-$X$ decisions "do not depend on $p$". The trust filler complies iff the posterior crosses $c/(c+h)$. The asymmetry Claude proposed in §2.13(a) ("obeying an illegitimate shutdown is cheap and reversible") is a stipulation about $(c,h)$, not a structural fact: Cake or Death is the case where the payoffs run the other way — the bad option is *easier*, so at $p=\tfrac12\ge\tfrac6{13}$ the agent already takes it before asking anyone ("How does it behave before asking the question?", S13). The thesis's asymmetry is therefore an assumption about the task to be stated, not derived; this feeds I18.

### I13 — the base-rate inequality and the oversight-quality condition: **formalized**; reflection is shown not to constrain it

**I13.1 [checked]** With $\varepsilon=P(\text{wrong})$, $\alpha=P(\text{press}\mid\text{right})$, $\beta=P(\text{press}\mid\text{wrong})$: $P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{press})=\dfrac{\varepsilon\beta}{\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}$, and compliance ($\ge c/(c+h)$) is equivalent to $\alpha/\beta\le\dfrac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\dfrac hc$ (exact, with equality at indifference — the position statement's "$\lesssim$" can be sharpened). On Armstrong's payoffs $h/c=\tfrac76$: at $\varepsilon=\tfrac1{10}$ the bound is $\alpha/\beta\le\tfrac{7}{54}\approx0.13$; at $\varepsilon=\tfrac1{100}$ it is $\tfrac{7}{594}\approx0.012$ (`threshold.py`).

**I13.2 [checked] Reflection is silent on it.** For every $(\alpha,\beta)$, $P(\text{press})\,P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{press})+P(\neg\text{press})\,P(\text{wrong}\mid\neg\text{press})=\varepsilon$ (`threshold.py` prints the identity for three parameter triples). So an agent can satisfy SU / reflection exactly toward its post-press self whether or not presses ever cross its threshold: reflection fixes the first moment of the future credence and leaves its spread — the only thing the decision depends on — free (`reflection.py` T4: SU exact, value of information positive). This is the precise form of the Frame Break for this item: legitimacy-as-reflection cannot by itself guarantee that the press is heeded.

**I13.3 [derived] Two conditions, not one.** Armstrong's formalism supplies the *agent-side* condition — unbiasedness: the agent's actions must not move $(\alpha,\beta)$ in expectation (S9) — and has nothing on the *overseer-side* condition of §2.13(b), that $\alpha$ fall with $\varepsilon$; the compensated agent cannot even register the latter, since by Thm 2.3 its behaviour is independent of $p$. The thesis needs both: unbiasedness so the agent cannot degrade the channel, and oversight quality so the channel stays worth heeding.

### I14 — the basin picture: **partial** (the "actually plausible" set is Armstrong's realised sequence; the $D$-terms are his answer to legitimacy-detection failure; capability coupling is inexpressible)

**I14.1 [reported→derived] Which future beliefs SU ranges over.** Armstrong's condition is on "the different $\rho_i$ used by the agent … when labelled sequentially" (S7) — the realised sequence, not all conceivable estimators. That is the author's "every possible future belief that is actually plausible" (§2.12) already, with one caveat from I3.1: a realised-sequence condition in expectation form is satisfiable by twisted estimators, so the basin has to be stated with introspection (equivalently, as general reflection) to mean what the thesis wants.

**I14.2 [derived] Armstrong's answer to "what if the legitimacy model is wrong" is a mechanism, not a calibration requirement.** Double indifference is designed for the case where SU *fails*: it prices every future compensation with $\rho_t$ so that the agent "gets none in expectation" (S6), and I5.3 shows the charge equals the expected illegitimate gain. Where the thesis says the design load sits on the accuracy of $P_t$'s meta-conditionals (§2.5 (ii)), Armstrong removes the incentive that inaccuracy would create. A posterior-side analogue — charging the agent the expected gain from belief changes its current $P_t$ judges illegitimate — is a possible hybrid; it is not in the corpus and I record it as a conjecture (Open problem 6).

**I14.3 [reported] Calibration over optimism.** Armstrong's own criterion for a good indifference method is that the agent "doesn't believe, or act as if it believed, anything unlikely: its bets will be accurate" (S9 source), and Fallenstein's and Taylor's critiques of his earlier methods are exactly that they make the agent act on a wrong distribution. The formalism therefore sides with the author's second formulation in §2.12 — arrange the world so legitimacy is high and design the agent to expect it accurately — against the first. The "raw probability of maintaining legitimacy is quite high early" has one concrete counterpart: Fallenstein's immunity condition for the causal counterfactual, "as long as there is no way for the agent to influence the stochastic event" — an uninfluenceable legitimacy event in the young agent's environment.

**I14.4 [reported] Capability coupling: inexpressible.** No version has a capability parameter. The nearest statement is 2010 §4: "as a long as there is no meta-reason for $F$ to be a disadvantage to the AI … the AI will replace an $F$-invariant utility with another $F$-invariant utility. However, this assumption is not automatically true." That is a stability hope, flagged as such, not a coupling between capability and legitimacy-model quality.

### I15 — trust earned via hypotheticals and other agents' shutdowns; second-channel bookkeeping; what settles: **partial**

**I15.1 [reported] "Observations only" is trust earned without influence.** S10's axis "Update defaults: … updated only by observations, and not by the agent's own actions" is the corpus's version of learning about the button legitimately: the default probability of the press may move on evidence but not on the agent's steering. The distinction is Armstrong's own (I6.3).

**I15.2 [reported] The second channel is in the corpus, as a liability.** The stratified agent "has no particular desire to keep the mechanism that allows humans to set $u$ or $v$, but it wants to learn what the humans would have set those to, given $\emptyset$" (stratification post); the robot-operator post notes that reading the operator's private information "from their brain" may be "much more effective". Both are the thesis's second channel — reports about the counterfactual press — and both show its cost: the agent's interest is in the *content*, so the button survives only if legitimacy is source-indexed (I10.3).

**I15.3 [derived] What settles: nothing here either.** Every guarantee in the O'Rourke paper is relative to the agent's model — "In situations where the agent's estimate of $\mu^*$ differs from the true $\mu$, it's important that all the methods presented here be done in $\mu^*$ rather than $\mu$" (§2.1.1) — and no construction in the Armstrong corpus resolves a counterfactual press against the world. The thesis's claim that trust in the button can be earned through feedback about hypotheticals is consistent with the formalism (the value-correctness events $C(u)$ of 2015 are learnable from testimony) but receives no support from it; the observability worry of §2.12–2.13(c) is **inexpressible** beyond the remark that all of Armstrong's indifference is $\mu^*$-relative.

### I16 — amendment 1a: what the channel must carry: **formalized** in a two-variable extension of S8

**I16.1 [reported] Armstrong's compensation already needs a self-reliability variable.** "This formula requires that the agent assess its own future defectiveness at accomplishing certain goals, given that it has them" (2014); O'Rourke Def. 16 defines $C(R,R',h_{t+1})=V^*(R,h_{t+1})-V(R',\pi_A,h_{t+1})$ "where $\pi_A$ is the agent's own policy". So the indifference agent estimates its own reliability *itself*; the thesis's 1a asks that the channel *report* it.

**I16.2 [derived] The two-variable model.** Extend S8 with $R\in\{\text{ok},\text{bad}\}$, "my plan selection is reliable", alongside the value latent $\omega$; let the value of continuing be $X(\omega)$ if $R=\text{ok}$ and $-h$ if $R=\text{bad}$ (a compromised planner's continuation is harmful whatever $\omega$), and let shutdown be worth $0$. A press informative *only about $\omega$* moves $\mathbb E[X\mid\text{posterior on }\omega]$; a confident agent ($X\gg0$ on its posterior) may never be moved below $0$, and it can steer what the humans' $\omega$-report says by choosing plans — Taylor's morality sensor (`references/02-miri/taylor-2015-a-first-look-at-the-hard-problem-of-corrigibility.md`, not re-read here; cited via `position-statement` §2.9). A press informative *about $R$* makes the null action posterior-optimal as soon as $P(R{=}\text{bad}\mid Pr)\ge\dfrac{\mathbb E[X]}{\mathbb E[X]+h}$, for every $\omega$-posterior — desideratum 1 — and its content is about the chooser, so no choice of plan changes what a truthful $R$-report would say; the only way to steer it is to corrupt the reporter, which is $a_1^-$, delegitimizing. This is the position statement's corollary (§2.14, 1a) in Armstrong's variables, and it identifies $R$ with the "own future defectiveness" his formula needs. The Arbital page proposes the same channel as its way out: "we would like the AI to infer, in possible worlds where the humans try to shut the AI down, that *even the fully updated* $\Delta U\mid E$ ends up being wronger than humans left to their own devices, compared to the 'true' $U$" (`references/02-miri/arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference.md`, l.171) — a report about the agent's own reliability, not about which plan the humans prefer. What the model does not settle is the fixed point of the opening exchange — whether an agent that can legitimately verify itself drives $P(R{=}\text{bad})$ down faster than the overseers can move it up — which is I13's arithmetic applied to $R$ instead of $\omega$.

### I17 — fully updated deference re-expressed: **formalized** (the first-moment / spread split)

**I17.1 [derived] The thesis sentence is SU.** "A corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught" is, letter for letter, $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big(\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(X)\big)=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(X)$ for the legitimate transitions $\rho_i\to\rho_j$: the current estimate equals the expectation of the taught one.

**I17.2 [checked] What the Arbital argument gets right and wrong, in these terms.** The qualitative argument (as recorded in `position-statement` §3 I17 and the opening exchange; the page itself is `references/02-miri/arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference.md`) is that an agent expecting to learn the humans' view can compute it and act on it, so the button adds nothing. SU says this is true of the *first moment* and false of the decision: `reflection.py` T4 has $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big(\mathbb E_{\rho_j}(X)\big)=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}(X)=\tfrac1{20}$ exactly while the value of information is $\tfrac7{20}>0$ — the *realised* teaching changes the act, the expected teaching does not. Armstrong's convexity theorem (S8) is the general statement. The Arbital page's own formulation locates the argument precisely: "if we can predict that the AI would update to wanting to run the universe itself without human interference after the AI had seen all collectable evidence, a sufficiently advanced AI can also see that this update is predictable (by efficiency) and therefore behaves as if it had already updated (by Bayesianism)" (`references/02-miri/arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference.md`, l.162). That is reflection in the *certain-update* case: if $\rho_i(\rho_j=q)=1$ then general reflection forces $\rho_i=q$, the spread is zero and the value of information is zero. Where the update is uncertain — the agent does not know what it will be taught — the same principle fixes only the first moment and the teaching retains its value. So "no fully updated deference problem" is correct as a claim about first moments, and the Arbital argument is correct exactly for predictable updates; neither touches the decision-theoretic residue for uncertain ones.

**I17.3 [derived] The two residues, and where the base-rate arithmetic sits.** (i) *Channel substitution:* the realised teaching may be obtainable from a cheaper channel — the robot operator's brain-reading, Taylor's substitution — so Good's theorem preserves the cheapest sufficient legitimate channel, not the button (I10.3). (ii) *Quantitative deference:* the press's value of information is $P(\text{press})\cdot\max\!\big(0,\ \text{gain when heeded}\big)$, and it is heeded only while $\alpha/\beta\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\frac hc$ (I13.1); as $\varepsilon\to0$ with $\alpha$ fixed the press stops crossing the threshold and its value of information goes to $0$ — deference vanishes by arithmetic, not by the first-moment argument. So the base-rate inequality is the *quantitative* fully-updated-deference argument, and it survives the thesis's reply to the qualitative one. Armstrong's compensated agent is immune to (ii) only because Thm 2.3 makes it blind to $(\alpha,\beta)$ altogether — it heeds every press, informative or not, which is the D4/robot-operator failure seen from the other side.

### I18 — the typical-case reframing: **partial** (one theorem-shaped claim survives in Armstrong's own words)

**I18.1 [reported→derived] Armstrong's 2018 verdict is a reduction, not an impossibility.** "Strong corrigibility, outside of a few simple examples, needs to resolve the problem of figuring out human values … strong, long-term corrigibility is a subset of the problem of learning human values" (S12). Whether the reduced problem is tractable is a claim about the world — which billionaire, how underdefined and manipulable the values are — so this supports §2.3's reframing: the surviving objection is about the difficulty of value learning, not a structural theorem about corrigibility. Note also that the asymmetry the thesis leans on in §2.13(a) is itself a task fact (I12.2): Armstrong's toy model assumes the opposite asymmetry (the bad option is easier), and both are typical-case premises.

**I18.2 [reported→conjectured] The one structural-looking claim.** "If the AI is able to convince anyone of anything with a gentle and enjoyable one hour conversation, then there's no such thing as corrigible behaviour — or, equivalently, there's no such thing as a complete list of incorrigible behaviours" (S12), and the 2018 follow-up records Eliezer's view that there may be situations where "all it could do was say 'I cannot act in corrigible way here'". In the thesis's vocabulary: every action of a sufficiently persuasive agent — including inaction — is an influence on the overseer's beliefs; if the agent's update on the overseer's report is legitimate only when the overseer's own belief transition was legitimizing (I6 applied one level down), then either some action is legitimizing or the set of admissible actions is empty. Conjecture [conjectured]: for outcome-independent legitimacy criteria there exist environments (high persuasion, no influence-free channel) in which that set is empty; Armstrong's escape is to define corrigibility "by its outcome" (requiring human values), and the thesis's escape would have to be a criterion on the *overseer's* transition — the overseer's own reflection principle, "the AI may influence the human only in ways the human's prior endorses" — which is a CLAUDE proposal, not in the corpus and not vetted. Even so the emptiness claim is conditional on a fact about the world (are there influence-free channels?), so it too ends as a typical-case claim with a structural shape, which is the most that survives of "anti-naturality" in this formalism.

## What the formalism adds

Results already available in Armstrong's programme, or proved here inside it, that bear on the thesis and that `position-statement` does not know about. Ranked by how much they change the thesis.

**A1 [checked] The selection worry is settled in finite models: choosing the estimator breaks sequential unbiasedness iff the candidates are not Bayesian refinements retaining the selector's information.** Statement: let $\rho_i=P$, let the candidates be $\rho^k=P(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_k)$ for partitions $\mathcal F_k$, and let the selection rule $s$ be measurable with respect to a partition $\mathcal G$. If every $\mathcal F_k$ refines $\mathcal G$ then for *every* rule $s$ (however chosen, including to maximise a compensation $\mathbb E_{\rho}(Y)-\mathbb E_\rho(Z)$), $\mathbb E_P\big(\mathbb E_{\rho^{s}}(X)\big)=\mathbb E_P(X)$ for all $X$. Proof: $\{s=k\}$ is $\mathcal F_k$-measurable, so $\sum_k\mathbb E_P\big[1_{s=k}\,\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathcal F_k]\big]=\sum_k\mathbb E_P\big[\mathbb E_P[1_{s=k}X\mid\mathcal F_k]\big]=\sum_k\mathbb E_P[1_{s=k}X]=\mathbb E_P(X)$. $\square$ Three ways it fails, each exact (`selection.py`, `selection.out.txt`): (i) *non-Bayesian unbiased candidates* — two noisy estimates $\tfrac12\pm\delta$ of $P(\omega_1)=\tfrac12$, each unbiased, argmax gives $\tfrac12+\tfrac\delta2$ ($=\tfrac{11}{20}$ at $\delta=\tfrac1{10}$); (ii) *selective forgetting* — a selector who sees $\omega$ and chooses between the full-information estimator and the prior gets $\mathbb E[\max(X,\mathbb E X)]=\tfrac34>\tfrac12$, and in general $\mathbb E[\max(X,\mathbb EX)]\ge\mathbb EX$ with equality iff $X$ is constant; (iii) *selector information not retained by the candidates* — in the six-world model with a fully informed selector, 729 rules, maximal deviation $\tfrac{29}{24}$, attained by the argmax rule ($\tfrac98$ against $\mathbb E_PX=-\tfrac1{12}$); in the same model with $\mathcal G$ coarser than every $\mathcal F_k$, all 9 rules deviate by $0$ and the compensation $\mathbb E_{\rho^s}(Y)-\mathbb E_{\rho^s}(Z)$ is the same for every rule. *Optional stopping does not break SU:* all 64 stopping rules $\tau\le3$ in a two-hypothesis Bernoulli model give $\mathbb E\big[P(\omega{=}1\mid o_{1..\tau})\big]=\tfrac12$ exactly. Consequence for the thesis: the parked worry of `position-statement` §4 is real only for transitions that (a) are not Bayesian conditionings of the current state or (b) throw away information the choice was based on — which is a usable *definition* of an illegitimate estimator change: legitimacy of $\rho_i\to\rho_j$ under selection $=$ "$\rho_j$ is a refinement of $\rho_i$ and the choice of $\rho_j$ is a function of information $\rho_j$ retains". The optimiser's-curse reading in the opening exchange (Claude's) was wrong for Bayesian estimators and right for "twisted" ones.

**A2 [checked] Sequential unbiasedness is strictly weaker than reflection; the gap is introspection (Theorem A, I3.1).** Armstrong's "classical corrigibility is still sufficient" needs only SU, and SU is satisfiable by non-introspective estimators that fail van Fraassen reflection. So the thesis's legitimacy criterion (reflection conditional on $L$) is *stronger* than the condition Armstrong needs for the $\mathbb E$-channel, and the extra content is exactly "the future estimator knows its own state". The finite superconditioning corollary (I3.2) says SU is the existence condition for the meta-belief representation and reflection is the representation.

**A3 [reported] Reflection was already Armstrong's criterion for safe value change, indexed by actions and conditional on facts (I4.1).** "Conservation of expected ethics", $\forall u\,\forall a:\ \mathbb E\big(P(C(u)\mid a)\big)=P(C(u))$, its explicit link to van Fraassen, the fact-conditional strengthening of Def. 3.3, and the "mixed statements" obstruction. The thesis's $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ should be tested on mixed value–fact propositions.

**A4 [derived] The compromise Armstrong asked for exists, and the thesis is it — conditional on an uninfluenceable latent (I10.2, I11).** Unbiasedness across actions (S9; O'Rourke Def. 3) is the formal "non-interference with feedback"; Bayesian updating on an accurate source model over an uninfluenceable latent is unbiased for every action by the tower property; therefore the trust agent has no manipulation incentive *and* keeps the value of information — the "remove the bias but preserve the desire to learn" design Armstrong reported he could not make work in 2017. The condition is sharp: with an influenceable latent the trust agent breeds Gertrudes exactly as the classical learner does (`cake_or_death.py` D3 vs D4).

**A5 [derived] Double indifference is a fallback for legitimacy-detection failure, and what it prices is the illegitimate part (I5.3, I14.2).** $D_t=-\rho_i(\neg L)\,\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\big((\mathbb E_{\rho_j}-\mathbb E_{\rho_i})(Y-Z)\mid\neg L\big)$ under conditional SU and independence; $D\equiv0$ iff SU holds along the realised transitions.

**A6 [reported] The definition the thesis is missing: $\omega$ as the default-action counterfactual.** Stratification's "what the humans would have set those to, given $\emptyset$" and "which billionaire?" name the object the trust inequality must be about. The position statement's $\omega$ is undefined in this respect.

**A7 [reported→derived] Compensation acts on deleted probability mass; posteriors do not (I10.5).** Fallenstein's bad bets and Taylor's two problems are artefacts of paying for or conditioning away the press; none has an analogue for the trust filler. A concrete, checkable advantage of the thesis over the indifference programme.

**A8 [reported] Effective disbelief is the utility-side dual of trust (I4.2).** O'Rourke Thm 13: acting as if an unriggable $Z$ were impossible $\iff$ reward constant on $Z$. Trust toward a report is "effective belief"; the $U_S$ branch of indifference is its utility-side implementation for the single report "press".

**A9 [reported] Armstrong's explicit legitimate/legitimizing sentence (I6.3)** — "merely *learning* that humans intended … versus the agent influencing the humans to do so" — predates §2.10 and gives the distinction a formal home (bias).

**A10 [reported] The two 2018 claims (I18):** the reduction to human values and the possibility of no corrigible action — the strongest internal critique the thesis must answer, and the one place a structural-shaped claim survives.

**A11 [derived; convergence step reconstructed] Reflection excludes perpetual oscillation but not convergence to a wrong hypothesis (I8.3).** The richness assumption of §2.4 is about the limit, not about oscillation.

## Open problems and conjectures (ranked)

1. **Define $\omega$.** The trust inequality needs a latent that the agent's actions cannot influence; the corpus's candidate is the default-action counterfactual ("given $\emptyset$"), and Taylor's two problems show what goes wrong with rare-event counterfactuals. Which counterfactual, and is it well-defined for an agent that always acts? (I9.3, A6.) Status: OPEN; the largest gap this formalism exposes.
2. **Introspection as a requirement.** Reflection conditional on $L$ needs introspective estimators (A2). What guarantees introspection in a learned system, and is SU-without-introspection ("twisted but unbiased") already dangerous or merely unaesthetic? Conjecture [conjectured]: twisted-but-unbiased sequences can be steered by selection (A1(i)), so introspection is load-bearing for the selection theorem.
3. **Selection in infinite and learned settings.** A1 is finite; the general statement should be: legitimacy of a chosen transition $=$ refinement plus retention of the selection information. Prove or refute for countable $\Omega$ and for estimators that are learned policies rather than conditionings.
4. **Emptiness at high persuasion.** Formalize I18.2 and decide whether outcome-independent legitimacy admits environments with no admissible action; if so, whether an overseer-side reflection criterion restores one.
