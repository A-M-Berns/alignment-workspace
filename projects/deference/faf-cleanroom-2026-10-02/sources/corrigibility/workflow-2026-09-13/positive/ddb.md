## Summary

**What I did.** I formalized the position statement's checklist I1–I18 in the finite probability frames of Deference Done Better (DDB): the agent's current state is the deferrer $\pi$, a source of modification (or the programmers) is a frame $\langle W, \mathcal P\rangle$, and legitimacy is a deference principle between them. Fifteen items are formalized or partial; I15 and the "objective" half of I14 are inexpressible (DDB has no time or external truth; numerics in `ddb-scratch/verify-output.txt`).

**Main results.** (1) *Legitimacy should be Total Trust, not Reflection*: DDB prove Total Trust ⇔ Value (Good's theorem) ⇔ expected-accuracy increase on every reasonable measure, while Reflection is strictly stronger and impossible toward any modest expert — the chain "ie accuracy-inducing, ie reflection" is one theorem and one falsehood (I4). (2) *The shutdown button is one Total-Trust threshold event* $[\mathbb E_P(X) \le 0]$; its below-threshold inequality at $0$ is desideratum 1, the two-option identity makes the button's value Good's theorem, and deception breaks Total Trust for the induced frame at the "silence" inequality (I9). (3) *Legitimacy-conditioned Total Trust is $\pi(\cdot\mid L)$ totally trusting the frame*, so DDB's theorems survive conditioning — but unrestricted it forces legitimate states to be certain of their legitimacy; the criterion must be DDB's *local* deference, whose Value-equivalence is DDB's open conjecture (I5). (4) *Fact 2.1 is a "please shut down" counterexample*: trusting overseers' probabilities does not make obeying their stakes-weighted verdict rational (I4.3). (5) *Armstrong's sequential unbiasedness is independent of Value* (fn 18; I7). (6) *Full updating lands on the informed expert*: individual presses may be overruled while the class is honored; FUD versus the thesis is a dispute about frame accuracy, not coherence (I17).

**Unresolved.** Local Total Trust ⇔ local Value; iterated and infinite frames (Theorem 2.2 fails there); garbling-monotonicity of Value for modest frames; non-transitivity across channels.

## Setting

The formalism, exactly as DDB state it, with the notational conventions of `research/CLAUDE.md` (nested expectations spelled out at displays; the two halves of Total Trust are called the **above-threshold** and **below-threshold inequality**; DDB's word "cut" for a threshold hyperplane is quoted once here and then replaced by *threshold hyperplane*).

**Frames** (DDB §1, fn 6–8; Glossary). A *probability frame* $\langle W, \mathcal P\rangle$ is a finite set of worlds $W$ and, for each $w \in W$, a probability distribution $P_w$ on $W$ — "the expert's credences at $w$". $P$ (no subscript) is a definite description for "the expert's credences, whatever they are"; propositions about them are sets of worlds: $[P = \rho] := \{w : P_w = \rho\}$, $[P \in C] := \{w : P_w \in C\}$, and for a random variable $X : W \to \mathbb R$, $[\mathbb E_P(X) \ge t] := \{w : \mathbb E_{P_w}(X) \ge t\}$. The deferrer's credence is a fixed distribution $\pi$ on $W$. The expert is *modest* at $w$ iff $P_w(P \ne P_w) > 0$ (fn 8). The *informed expert* is $\widehat P_w := P_w(\cdot \mid P = P_w)$. $C_\pi := \{\rho : \pi(P = \rho) > 0\}$ are the candidates $\pi$ leaves open, $C_i := C_{P_i}$, $C_i^- := C_i \setminus \{P_i\}$, and $W_\pi := \{w : \pi(w) > 0\}$.

**Deference principles** (DDB §§1–2, Glossary), each a condition on the pair $(\pi, \langle W,\mathcal P\rangle)$:

- **Reflection** (van Fraassen-style): $\pi(\cdot \mid P = \rho) = \rho$ for every $\rho$ with $\pi(P=\rho)>0$. Equivalently $\pi(\cdot \mid P \in C) \in C$ for every convex $C$ (§2, "convexity version").
- **New Reflection**: $\pi(\cdot \mid P = \rho) = \rho(\cdot \mid P = \rho)$; equivalently $\pi(\cdot \mid \widehat P = \rho) = \rho$ (fn 12).
- **Simple Trust** (Dorst 2020a): $\pi(q \mid P(q) \ge t) \ge t$ for all propositions $q$ and thresholds $t$.
- **Trust** (Dorst 2020a): $\pi\big(q \mid p \wedge [P(q \mid p) \ge t]\big) \ge t$ for all $p, q, t$.
- **Total Trust**: for every random variable $X$ and threshold $t$, the above-threshold inequality $\mathbb E_\pi\big(X \,\big|\, \mathbb E_P(X) \ge t\big) \ge t$; by negating $X$ this is equivalent to also requiring the below-threshold inequality $\mathbb E_\pi\big(X \,\big|\, \mathbb E_P(X) \le t\big) \le t$ (§2). Convexity version: $\pi(\cdot \mid P \in B) \in B$ for every *biconvex* $B$ (both $B$ and its complement convex; these are exactly the half-spaces, i.e. the sides of a threshold hyperplane $\{\rho : \mathbb E_\rho(X) = t\}$, fn 34). DDB stress that the two inequalities may **not** be combined into $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathbb E_P(X) = t) = t$ (§2; fn 24).
- **Value**: an *option* is any $O : W \to \mathbb R$ (utility if $O$ is taken at $w$); a *decision problem* $\mathcal O$ is a finite set of options; a *strategy* $S : w \mapsto S_w \in \mathcal O$ with $S_w = S_v$ whenever $P_w = P_v$ is *recommended* iff $\mathbb E_{P_w}(S_w) \ge \mathbb E_{P_w}(O)$ for every $O \in \mathcal O$ and every $w$; $\mathbb E_\pi(S) := \sum_w \pi(w)\, S_w(w)$ (the "diagonal": the option the expert picks at $w$, paid out at $w$). $\pi$ *values* the frame iff for every $\mathcal O$, every recommended $S$ and every $O \in \mathcal O$: $\mathbb E_\pi(S) \ge \mathbb E_\pi(O)$ — "prefer to give the expert power of attorney on any decision".
- **Epistemic Value**: for every $X$ and every generally-strictly-proper estimate-inaccuracy measure $I_X$, $\mathbb E_\pi\big(I_X(\mathbb E_P(X))\big) \le \mathbb E_\pi\big(I_X(\mathbb E_\pi(X))\big)$ (§3, fn 47–48).
- **Fixed-option Dutch book** against the transition $\pi \to P$: two decision problems each containing a "no bet" option, one decided by $\pi$ before and one by $P$ after, jointly guaranteeing a loss (fn 20).
- **Modestly informed**: $P_i \in \mathrm{CH}\big(\{\widehat P_i\} \cup C_i^-\big)$, i.e. $P_i = \lambda_{ii}\widehat P_i + \sum_{P_j \in C_i^-} \lambda_{ij} P_j$ with non-negative weights summing to $1$ (fn 54). A frame *validates* a principle iff every $P_i$ in it satisfies the principle toward the frame.

- **Reflection is incompatible with modesty** (§1): if $\pi(P = P_w) > 0$ and $P_w$ is modest then $\pi(P = P_w \mid P = P_w) = 1 \ne P_w(P = P_w)$, so $\pi$ cannot reflect the frame. [checked — one line, DDB §1]
- **Reflection ⇒ Value** (fn 16); **for an immodest frame, Reflection ⇔ Value** (fn 17); **Value ⇒ New Reflection** (fn 19); **New Reflection ⇏ Value** — the anti-expert frame $\pi = (0.5, 0.5)$, $P_a = (0.2, 0.8)$, $P_b = (0.8, 0.2)$ (Figure 2). **Value ⇏ estimate-matching** $\pi(q) = \mathbb E_\pi(P(q))$ and **estimate-matching ⇏ Value** (fn 18: Figure 3 vs. Figure 2).

- **TT ⇒ Trust ⇒ New Reflection** (fn 36, 37); TT implies its conditional-estimate form $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid q \wedge [\mathbb E_P(X \mid q) \ge t]) \ge t$ (fn 36) and its comparison form $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathbb E_P(X) \ge \mathbb E_P(Y)) \ge \mathbb E_\pi(Y \mid \mathbb E_P(X) \ge \mathbb E_P(Y))$ (fn 29).
- **Positive access** is necessary for being valued: if $P(q) = 1$ then $P(P(q) = 1) = 1$ (§5, citing Dorst 2020a Fact 8.2).
- **Local deference** (§5, fn 62–66): relativize to a question $Q$ (a partition): totally trust w.r.t. $Q$ iff the above-threshold inequality holds for every $X$ measurable w.r.t. $Q$; value w.r.t. $Q$ iff Value holds for decision problems whose options are $Q$-measurable. Total Trust w.r.t. every two-cell question ⇔ Simple Trust. **Open (fn 65)**: local Total Trust ⇔ local Value (right-to-left proved). **fn 66**: an *immodest* frame can be totally trusted and valued w.r.t. $Q$ without being reflected w.r.t. $Q$.
- **Lemma 7.5's proof** (Appendix B): if $\pi$ totally trusts the frame then every $P_i \in C_\pi$ also totally trusts (and values) the frame — the deferrer's trust propagates to every candidate it leaves open.
- **Infinite frames**: Weatherson (2025) breaks Theorem 2.2 in both directions on infinite frames [reported via `expert-conditions` §2.3 and `li-deference` §0.1; not in the corrigibility library].

**Three elementary lemmas used throughout** (proved here, [checked]):

- **(L-A) Support lemma.** If $\pi$ totally trusts $\langle W,\mathcal P\rangle$ then $P_w(W_\pi) = 1$ for every $w \in W_\pi$. *Proof.* Let $X := \mathbf 1_{W \setminus W_\pi}$. Then $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid A) = 0$ for every $A$ with $\pi(A) > 0$, since $X$ vanishes on $W_\pi$. If some $w \in W_\pi$ had $t := P_w(W\setminus W_\pi) > 0$, then $w \in [\mathbb E_P(X) \ge t]$, this event has positive $\pi$-mass, and the above-threshold inequality would demand $0 = \mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathbb E_P(X) \ge t) \ge t > 0$. $\blacksquare$ (DDB reach the same conclusion via Lemma 7.2.4; the direct argument is what generalizes to the conditional case in I5.)
- **(L-B) Two-option identity** (DDB Lemma 7.1's construction; stated as an identity in `two-option-value-iff-total-trust`). For the menu $\{X, \text{const } s\}$ let $S := X\cdot\mathbf 1[\mathbb E_P(X) \ge s] + s\cdot \mathbf 1[\mathbb E_P(X) < s]$ (the expert takes $X$ iff it estimates $X$ at least $s$). Then pointwise $S - s = (X - s)\,\mathbf 1[\mathbb E_P(X) \ge s]$ and $S - X = (s - X)\,\mathbf 1[\mathbb E_P(X) < s]$, so
$$\mathbb E_\pi(S) - s = \pi\big(\mathbb E_P(X) \ge s\big)\Big(\mathbb E_\pi\big(X \,\big|\, \mathbb E_P(X) \ge s\big) - s\Big), \qquad \mathbb E_\pi(S) - \mathbb E_\pi(X) = \pi\big(\mathbb E_P(X) < s\big)\Big(s - \mathbb E_\pi\big(X \,\big|\, \mathbb E_P(X) < s\big)\Big).$$
Hence "letting the expert choose beats the constant option" is *exactly* the above-threshold inequality at $s$, and "beats committing to $X$" is *exactly* the (strict-side) below-threshold inequality at $s$. $\blacksquare$
- **(L-C) Partition lemma.** If $\{L_1, \dots, L_k\}$ partitions $W$ and for each $j$ the above-threshold inequality holds *conditional on $L_j$* — $\mathbb E_\pi\big(X \,\big|\, L_j \wedge [\mathbb E_P(X) \ge t]\big) \ge t$ whenever the conditioning event has positive mass — then it holds unconditionally: $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathbb E_P(X) \ge t) = \sum_j \pi\big(L_j \,\big|\, \mathbb E_P(X)\ge t\big)\, \mathbb E_\pi\big(X \,\big|\, L_j \wedge [\mathbb E_P(X)\ge t]\big) \ge t$. $\blacksquare$ The converse fails (I5 gives the witness).

## Items I1–I18

### I1 — the value-change ⇄ belief-change transform

**Status: formalized**, with one clause ("or perhaps some other rule") inexpressible.

1. *Definition.* Let $\Omega$ be a set of value hypotheses and $\Sigma$ a set of world-states; take $W := \Sigma \times \Omega$, so a world is $w = (s, \omega)$. For an act $a$ with consequence $a(s)$ and utility functions $u_\omega$, the DDB option is $O_a(w) := u_\omega(a(s))$. Decision by linear expectation over value uncertainty is then $\mathbb E_\pi(O_a) = \sum_{s,\omega} \pi(s,\omega)\, u_\omega(a(s))$ — an ordinary DDB expectation. Value hypotheses are events $[\omega = \omega_0] \subseteq W$ of the expanded algebra, exactly as the thesis asks. [checked — definitional]
2. *The Gandhi inequality as a Value failure for one frame.* A certain change of utility $u_\omega \to u_{\omega'}$ is the frame in which $\pi = \delta_{(s,\omega)}$-like on the value coordinate and $P_w = \delta_{\omega'}$ on it at every $w$. The recommended strategy picks $\arg\max_a u_{\omega'}(a(s))$, so $\mathbb E_\pi(S) = \mathbb E_\pi\big(u_\omega(\arg\max u_{\omega'})\big) \le \max_a \mathbb E_\pi(O_a)$: **$\pi$ does not value the pill's frame**, which is $U(\arg\max U) \ge U(\arg\max U')$ verbatim. Nothing in it generalizes: which frames are valued is Theorem 4.1's question, and Figure 3 (a modest, better-informed successor) is valued. This is the AGREED verdict of 2.1 in exact form. [derived]
3. *Value misalignment proper* (the successor optimizes a different utility, not merely a different belief about $\omega$): Herrmann's generalized frame [reported: `references/managing-misalignment/…md`, Definition 3.3, Theorem 3.4] scores the successor's choices $B_\omega(A) \in \arg\max_a \mathbb E_{P_\omega}(V_\omega \circ a)$ by the principal's $u$. Theorem 3.4 (under *clarity* — the successor is certain of its own beliefs and utilities — plus richness, constant acts, stochastic choice): $\pi$ values $(P, V)$ iff for all acts $a, b$ and worlds $\omega$: $\mathbb E_\pi\big(u(a) \,\big|\, [\omega]\big) > \mathbb E_\pi\big(u(b) \,\big|\, [\omega]\big) \Rightarrow \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\big(V_\omega(a)\big) > \mathbb E_{P_\omega}\big(V_\omega(b)\big)$ ("posterior alignment"). Read into the thesis: **the current agent values its modified self iff, conditional on the successor's (belief, value) profile, the current agent already agrees with every strict preference the successor has** — "already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught", for value change, but proved only for immodest (clear) successors. [reported]
4. *Non-realism about the distinction* (AUTHOR 2.2): in DDB the two representations coincide when the successor's utility is the $P_w$-expectation of the family $u_\cdot$ (a value-learner with the same $u$-family); when the successor's $V$ lies outside the span of $\{u_\omega\}$, one adds $V$ to $\Omega$ as a new hypothesis and the belief representation is restored. So the distinction is representational here too, at the cost of enlarging $\Omega$ — the author's expanded sigma-algebra. [derived]
5. *Inexpressible:* any non-linear aggregation over $\omega$. Value is expected-utility on both sides (the recommended strategy is an argmax of $P_w$-expectations; Lemma 7.1's two-option construction uses linearity essentially), so none of DDB's theorems transfer to a geometric or other rule. Nearest expressible statement: "Value with respect to choice rule $R$" is a different principle, and Theorem 2.2 would have to be re-proved for it.

### I2 — radical-probabilist dynamics; non-dogmatism

**Status: formalized** (the zero→positive prohibition becomes a theorem); the sigma-algebra clause partial.

1. A DDB frame is radical-probabilist by construction: $P_w$ is *any* distribution, not required to be $\pi(\cdot \mid E_w)$. The classical Bayesian case is DDB's *prior frame* subclass (fn 25), where all candidates share a prior and condition on propositional evidence; DDB say explicitly that on some interpretations ("the opinions of the smartest person in the room") those assumptions "are simply wrong" and general frames are needed. So the thesis's "everything is allowed to change" is DDB's default. [reported: fn 25]
2. **The sole prohibition is derivable.** By (L-A), if $\pi$ totally trusts the frame then $P_w(W_\pi) = 1$ for every $w \in W_\pi$: every candidate post-modification state the agent takes seriously is absolutely continuous with respect to the agent's current state, $P_w \ll \pi$. Legitimacy-as-Total-Trust therefore *implies* "probability zero never becomes positive" — the author stipulated it; DDB delivers it. [checked] It is not sufficient: the anti-expert frame has $P_w \ll \pi$ and fails Value.
3. Non-dogmatism ("only logical impossibilities get zero") is the regularity of $\pi$ on $W$; DDB neither needs nor supplies it. With $\pi$ regular, item 2 is vacuous, so its content is exactly the non-regular case. [derived]
4. *Revising the prior over value hypotheses:* the $\Omega$-marginal of $P_w$ may differ arbitrarily from $\pi$'s. Formalized by item 1. *Revising the sigma-algebra:* inexpressible inside one frame ($W$ is fixed); nearest statement: local deference (§5) lets the agent trust the successor with respect to a coarser question $Q$ only, which is the coarsening direction; refinement (new events) needs a new $W'$ and a map, outside DDB.

### I3 — superconditioning; Eisenstat's meta-belief observation

**Status: formalized**, and it yields a correction to how the observation event should be read.

1. Diaconis–Zabell [reconstructed, as recalled in the opening chat: $Q$ arises from $P$ by conditioning in an enlarged space iff $Q \ll P$, bounded density in the infinite case; finite case needs only $Q \ll P$]. A DDB frame *is* the enlarged space: $W$ carries the meta-propositions $[P = \rho]$, and Eisenstat's observation event "I now believe $\rho$" is exactly $[P = \rho]$. Superconditioning on it is $\pi(\cdot \mid P = \rho)$. [checked — definitional]
2. **Superconditioning returns the new belief iff Reflection holds, and Reflection fails for every modest new belief** (DDB §1): $\pi(P = \rho \mid P = \rho) = 1 \ne \rho(P = \rho)$. So "observe your new belief, condition on it, and you hold your new belief" is consistent only when the new belief-state already knows itself with certainty. If it is modest, the observation "I now believe $\rho$" is *more* information than $\rho$ itself has, and the consistent target of the superconditioning is the informed state $\widehat\rho = \rho(\cdot \mid P = \rho)$ — New Reflection (fn 12), which Value entails (fn 19). [derived]
3. *The mixture construction and embeddedness.* One can always enlarge to $W \times \{\text{labels}\}$ with $\pi'(w, s) := \pi(P = P_s)\, P_s(w)$ and let the new belief be certain of its label; this makes every new belief immodest by fiat and recovers Reflection — but only by decoupling "which belief I hold" from "which world I am in". For an embedded agent the belief-state is a physical part of the world, so the belief *is* a function of the world — precisely DDB's frame — and modesty is the honest description of imperfect introspection. The meta-belief re-representation thus presupposes exact self-observation; drop that and the legitimacy criterion must be Total Trust/Value, not Reflection. (This also explains the LI wiki's finding that the LI expert is "immodest enough" asymptotically: introspection theorems there make self-knowledge approximate-and-improving; see I4 item 5.) [derived; INTERPRETATION]
4. *Superconditioning is not legitimacy.* The anti-expert frame ($\pi = (0.5, 0.5)$, $P_a = (0.2, 0.8)$, $P_b = (0.8, 0.2)$) satisfies $\pi = \pi P$ (check: $0.5 \cdot 0.2 + 0.5 \cdot 0.8 = 0.5$), so the mixture construction represents it as conditioning on the label, and New Reflection holds — yet $\pi$ would rather bet for itself than let its future self bet (Value fails: $\mathbb E_\pi(S) = -1 < 0$). So the opening chat's "superconditioning covers only the support ($Q \ll P$)" *understates* the gap: even on the support, representability-as-conditioning is available for transitions the agent should not welcome. Legitimacy needs the geometry of Theorem 4.1, not the existence of a conditioning representation. [checked]

### I4 — legitimacy = Reflection / Total Trust / increase-in-expected-accuracy

**Status: formalized** — this is DDB's home ground, and it settles the "ie … ie" chain of the thesis.

1. *The principles and their exact relations, in DDB's random-variable setting* (all [reported] from the transcription; hypotheses: finite $W$, all $P_w$ and $\pi$ probability distributions on $W$; Theorem 3.2 additionally needs $I_X$ absolutely continuous in the estimate):
   - Reflection ⇒ Value (fn 16); for an immodest frame Reflection ⇔ Value (fn 17); Reflection is impossible toward any candidate that is modest and has positive $\pi$-mass (§1).
   - **Value ⇔ Total Trust** (Theorem 2.2) ⇔ no fixed-option Dutch book (fn 20–22) ⇔ **Epistemic Value** over all gsp estimate-inaccuracy measures (Theorem 3.2, local in $X$; Theorem 5.1 globally) ⇔ [$\pi \in \mathrm{CH}(C_\pi)$ and each $P_i \in C_\pi$ modestly informed] (Theorem 4.1).
   - Total Trust ⇒ Trust ⇒ Simple Trust (fn 36; indicator variables), Total Trust ⇒ New Reflection (fn 37). Simple Trust w.r.t. $q$ ⇔ Epistemic Value w.r.t. $q$ over *propositional* strictly proper scoring rules (Theorem 3.1, Levinstein 2019).
   - **Failures:** Trust ⇏ Value (**Fact 2.1**, numbers re-verified above and in `ddb-scratch/verify-output.txt`); New Reflection ⇏ Value (Figure 2); Value ⇏ Reflection (Figure 3); estimate-matching $\pi(q) = \mathbb E_\pi(P(q))$ neither implies nor is implied by Value (fn 18); Brier-only expected-accuracy improvement implies no deference principle at all (fn 50).
2. **Which one is "legitimacy".** AUTHOR (2.9): the sensor slot is "to be filled by the reflection principle, or something similar like Total Trust or increase-in-expected-accuracy depending on the exact formal setting"; AUTHOR (thesis): "legitimate, ie accuracy-inducing, ie fit with the reflection principle". In DDB the first "ie" is a theorem — accuracy-increase on every reasonable measure ⇔ Total Trust ⇔ Value (Theorems 3.2 + 2.2), *given* pluralism about accuracy measures (§3.1, second subtlety: a single fixed rule such as Brier yields nothing, fn 50–51). The second "ie" is **false in general**: Reflection is strictly stronger than Total Trust and impossible toward a modest expert. Both experts the thesis cares about are modest: the programmers (humans do not know their own credences exactly) and the agent's own future self (unless introspection is exact, I3). Hence, in this formalism, **legitimacy = Total Trust = Value = Epistemic Value**, and *not* Reflection. DDB fn 11 says this in nearly the author's terms: Reflection had been proposed as the condition for a "genuine learning experience" (Jeffrey 1988; Skyrms 1990; Huttegger 2013–2017); DDB reply that this "is too demanding", and that "the transition represents a genuine learning experience only if $\pi$ defers to $P$ — where deference, as we'll argue, need not require Reflection". [reported: fn 11; derived]
3. **Fact 2.1 is a shutdown problem.** Its decision problem is $\{O_0 \equiv 0, O_1 = (29, -3, -13)\}$ — read $O_0$ as *shut down* and $O_1$ as *continue*. Every candidate expert expects continuing to be worth at least $0.3 > 0$; $\pi$ expects $-0.26$; and $\pi$ trusts the frame about every proposition and every conditional probability. So an agent that trusts its overseers' *probabilities* (Dorst's Trust) can still rationally refuse their unanimous verdict on a *stakes-weighted* question, because (§3) it may expect itself to be more accurate about the *sign* of $O_1$ than they are. Mirror the payoffs and it refuses a unanimous "shut down". **Legitimacy for the button must be Total Trust with respect to the stakes variable $X$; trust in the overseers' probabilities, even all conditional ones, does not suffice.** The position statement's binary right/wrong stakes (2.13) hide this gap: with two payoff levels, the sign question is a proposition and Simple Trust suffices; with heterogeneous stakes across worlds it does not. [derived]
4. *What "Managing Misalignment" and the LI wiki add to the map.* Herrmann 2025 restates Theorem 2.2 as its Theorem 3.2 (shared values) and adds posterior alignment for value misalignment (I1.3) [reported]. The LI wiki places a fifth notion above Total Trust — **Mart, the tower** $\mathbb E^H_n(X) \approx_n \mathbb E^H_n\big(\ulcorner \mathbb E^\ast(X)\urcorner\big)$ — and proves that for an *observable, coherent* expert Total Trust ⇔ Value ⇔ Mart at full quantifier strength (`deference-notions`, `value-iff-mart`; `total-trust-implies-mart` is PROVED-prose, unvetted). In DDB the tower's analogue is estimate-matching $\pi = \pi P$, which fn 18 shows to be *independent* of Value. The two verdicts are consistent: the difference is whether the expert is a *frame* (a credence per world, not observed exactly — the programmers) or a *single observable state* (the agent's own future self, whose estimates it eventually reads) — `expert-conditions` §2.1. **So the thesis's two experts live in two regimes:** toward its own future self the agent has tower ⇔ Total Trust ⇔ Value; toward the programmers it has only Total Trust ⇔ Value, Reflection is unavailable, and the martingale condition is neither necessary nor sufficient. [reported; derived]
5. *Conditional legitimacy cannot use the equality form.* DDB warn (§2, fn 24) that the two one-sided inequalities may not be combined into $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathbb E_P(X) = t) = t$ — "non-monotonicity of probabilistic support". So the thesis's $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ formulation with an *equality* $P_{t_1}(\varphi \mid P_{t_2}(\varphi) = c, L) = c$ (2.11) is Reflection conditional on $L$ and inherits Reflection's impossibility; the DDB-native form is the pair of threshold inequalities (I5). [reported; derived]

### I5 — legitimacy as an event on worlds; minimal viability; the indexed $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$

**Status: formalized**, and the formalization changes the shape of the proposal: legitimacy-conditioned trust must be *local* (question-relative) or it forces legitimate states to be certain of their own legitimacy.

1. *Definitions.* Let $L \subseteq W$ and $\pi_L := \pi(\cdot \mid L)$. **Reflection conditional on $L$** (AUTHOR's minimal viability, 2.10, and the equation of 2.11): $\pi(\cdot \mid L \wedge [P = \rho]) = \rho$, i.e. $\pi_L$ reflects the frame. **Total Trust conditional on $L$**: for all $X, t$, $\mathbb E_\pi\big(X \,\big|\, L \wedge [\mathbb E_P(X) \ge t]\big) \ge t$ together with the below-threshold twin, i.e. $\pi_L$ totally trusts the frame. **Value conditional on $L$**: $\mathbb E_\pi(S \mid L) \ge \mathbb E_\pi(O \mid L)$, i.e. $\pi_L$ values the frame. Since every DDB principle is a property of the pair (deferrer, frame) and conditioning on $L$ changes only the deferrer, **every DDB theorem applies verbatim to $\pi_L$**. [checked — definitional]
2. *What survives of the equivalences under conditioning:* everything. (a) Theorem 2.2 for $\pi_L$: Total Trust conditional on $L$ ⇔ Value conditional on $L$ — "conditional on legitimacy, letting the modified self (or the overseer) decide weakly beats every fixed option": the legitimacy-conditioned Good's theorem. (b) Theorem 3.2 for $\pi_L$: ⇔ conditional on $L$, the expert's estimate is expected more accurate on every gsp measure. (c) Theorem 4.1 for $\pi_L$: ⇔ $\pi_L \in \mathrm{CH}(C_{\pi_L})$ and each $P_i \in C_{\pi_L}$ is modestly informed, where $C_{\pi_L} = \{P_w : w \in L,\ \pi(w) > 0\}$ — a checkable criterion in terms of the *legitimate* candidates only. (d) Reflection conditional on $L$ is impossible as soon as one legitimate candidate is modest (§1 applied to $\pi_L$) — so the equation of 2.11 inherits Reflection's impossibility and its viable form is the pair of threshold inequalities (I4.5). [derived]
3. **Theorem (positive access to legitimacy).** If $\pi$ totally trusts the frame conditional on $L$ — over *all* random variables — then for every $w \in L \cap W_\pi$: $P_w(L \cap W_\pi) = 1$. *Proof:* (L-A) applied to $\pi_L$, whose support is $L \cap W_\pi$. $\blacksquare$ [checked] Reading: **a legitimate modification must produce a state that is certain of its own legitimacy** (relative to the agent's support); a legitimate press by overseers who are unsure whether their press is legitimate cannot be trusted-conditional-on-$L$ in the unrestricted sense. Illegitimate states are unconstrained — $P_w(L)$ for $w \notin L$ is arbitrary, so an illegitimately produced successor may be fully self-endorsing. This is 2.5(ii)'s "no outside view" as a theorem: illegitimacy is visible only from outside the $L$-worlds. Numerical witness (`verify-output.txt`, "I5 positive access"): a legitimate candidate $P_1 = (0.8, 0.1, 0.1, 0)$ that gives $0.1$ to an illegitimate world breaks conditional Total Trust at $X \approx (0.8, 0.8, 1, -1)$, $t = 0.82$.
4. **The escape is DDB's local deference.** Restrict the variables to those measurable with respect to the object-level question $Q$ (good/bad, or the value coordinate $\omega$): *Total Trust with respect to $Q$, conditional on $L$* (§5, fn 63). The proof in 3 uses $X = \mathbf 1_{\neg L}$, which is not $Q$-measurable, so the theorem no longer applies — and in the same numerical example local Total Trust conditional on $L$ holds. Theorem 3.2 is local, so the accuracy equivalence survives; but **the equivalence with legitimacy-conditioned Value is DDB's open conjecture (fn 65; right-to-left proved)**. So: the criterion the thesis needs is exactly local Total Trust conditional on $L$, and its identity with legitimacy-conditioned Good's theorem is an open problem of DDB, inherited. This also answers AUTHOR's "probably better to make legitimacy at least proposition-dependent": in DDB the proposition-dependence is *forced* — unrestricted legitimacy-conditioned trust is too strong precisely because it lets the trusted variable talk about legitimacy itself. [derived; reported]
5. *The indices.* $L_{t_1, t_2, \varphi}$: $t_1$ ↔ the deferrer $\pi$ (the frame is always judged from the deferrer's state); $t_2$ ↔ the frame (the belief being vetted); $\varphi$ ↔ the question $Q$ of local deference. "Predicated on worlds": $L \subseteq W$. Predicated on actions: not needed — actions index frames (I9). AUTHOR's "there is no formal distinction between an index and an argument" holds here too: $Q$ is a partition of $W$, so all three indices are objects over $W$. [derived]
6. **Witness that the conditioning does work** (`verify-output.txt`, "I5 witness"). Worlds $(L,\text{good}), (L,\text{bad}), (\neg L,\text{good}), (\neg L,\text{bad})$; $X = (1, -1, 1, -1)$. On $L$ the overseers track $X$: $P_{(L,g)} = (0.9, 0.1, 0, 0)$, $P_{(L,b)} = (0.1, 0.9, 0, 0)$; on $\neg L$ they are anti-experts: $P_{(\neg L,g)} = (0,0,0.1,0.9)$, $P_{(\neg L,b)} = (0,0,0.9,0.1)$. With $\pi = (0.4, 0.4, 0.1, 0.1)$ (legitimacy prior $0.8$): Total Trust conditional on $L$ **holds**, conditional on $\neg L$ **fails**, unconditionally **fails** (the $\neg L$ candidates are not modestly informed — the converse of (L-C) fails). Yet the button is honored: $\mathrm{Pr} = \{(L,b), (\neg L, g)\}$, $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathrm{Pr}) = -3/5 \le 0$, $\pi(L \mid \mathrm{Pr}) = 0.8$, and the channel is worth $\mathbb E_\pi(S) - \max(\mathbb E_\pi X, 0) = 0.3$. Lower the legitimacy prior to $0.4$: $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathrm{Pr}) = +1/5$, the agent continues despite the press, and the channel has *negative* value ($-1/10$). In general the press is obeyed iff $\pi(L \mid \mathrm{Pr})\,\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathrm{Pr}, L) + \pi(\neg L \mid \mathrm{Pr})\,\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathrm{Pr}, \neg L) \le 0$, i.e. iff $\pi(L \mid \mathrm{Pr}) \ge c'/(c' + h')$ with $c' := \mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathrm{Pr}, \neg L) > 0$ and $h' := -\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathrm{Pr}, L)$: **the compliance threshold of 2.13(a) reappears one level up, as a threshold on the posterior probability of legitimacy given the press.** [checked]

### I6 — legitimizing / delegitimizing; "supports"

**Status: formalized** (two kinds: epistemic and causal).

1. *Definition.* A third-person event $E \subseteq W$ is **legitimizing for** $(\text{frame}, Q)$ iff $\pi_E := \pi(\cdot \mid E)$ totally trusts the frame with respect to $Q$; **delegitimizing** iff it does not; weakly legitimizing iff $\pi(L \mid E) > \pi(L)$ for a fixed legitimacy event $L$ (2.11's weaker form). Geometrically: $E$ moves the deferrer to $\pi_E$, and "supports" means "lands the deferrer inside the Theorem 4.1 region of the candidates it leaves open". $E$ lives in $W$; legitimacy is a property of the pair $(\pi_E, \text{frame})$; that is 2.11's split between the locus of activity and the locus of belief. [derived]
2. *Closure.* Disjoint legitimizing events have a legitimizing union: apply (L-C) to $\pi_{E \cup F}$ with the partition $\{E, F\}$, using $\pi_{E\cup F}(\cdot \mid E) = \pi_E$. [checked] Intersections need not be legitimizing (not settled here; [conjectured] false in general).
3. *Causal delegitimization.* An action (2.9's $a_1^-$) does not condition $\pi$; it changes the frame. In the frame-indexed reading of I9 an action is delegitimizing iff the frame it induces leaves the Theorem 4.1 region. So the thesis has two relations under one name — *epistemic* (an event the agent learns) and *causal* (an intervention on the process) — and DDB separates them cleanly: conditioning the deferrer versus replacing the frame. Deception in 2.9 is of the second kind; the crown/vote examples (illustrative, not endorsed by the author) are of the first. [derived]

### I7 — no regress; the self-referential space; existence

**Status: partial** — one transition fully; iteration and existence only in part.

1. A DDB frame *is* a finite self-referential space: worlds determine credences over worlds, $[P \in B]$ are propositions, and $P_w([P = P_w])$ is a second-order credence (modesty is a second-order fact). Existence is trivial for finite $W$. [checked]
2. "One distribution judges all levels" (1b, AUTHOR): $\pi$'s conditionals on the biconvex events $[P \in B]$ *are* its legitimacy judgments (the convexity version of Total Trust). No separate judging layer exists in the formalism. [derived]
3. **Trust propagates to every candidate** (DDB Lemma 7.5's proof, [reported] and re-derived: by Lemmas 7.2.4–7.2.5, $C_i \subseteq C_\pi$ and $P_i(i) > 0$, so $P_i \in \mathrm{CH}(C_i)$ with all of $C_i$ modestly informed; Theorem 4.1 for $P_i$ finishes). So if the current state totally trusts the modification process, **every state it might legitimately become also totally trusts the same process**: the judge at $t+1$ agrees with the judge at $t$. This is the one-step form of Christiano's "corrigible agents build corrigible successors" and of the AGREED 1b picture.
4. *Iteration and Armstrong's precedent.* Read the frame's stochastic matrix as a Markov kernel; $\pi P$ is the agent's expectation of its next credence and $\pi = \pi P$ is stationarity, which is estimate-matching $\mathbb E_\pi(\mathbb E_P(X)) = \mathbb E_\pi(X)$ for all $X$ — Armstrong's sequential unbiasedness $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\mathbb E_{\rho_j}X = \mathbb E_{\rho_i}X$ in one step (2.5). By fn 18 this is **logically independent of Value** (Figure 3 is valued and not stationary: $\pi P = (0.55, 0.45) \ne \pi$; the anti-expert frame is stationary and not valued — both re-verified). So the position statement's precedent claim should be re-registered: sequential unbiasedness is a *martingale* condition on the estimator sequence, and in the modest setting it is neither necessary nor sufficient for the estimator being worth deferring to. What Armstrong shows it does (remove the estimator-manipulation incentive) is a different property from legitimacy-as-Value; whether the two coincide for *observable* estimators is the LI wiki's tower ⇔ Total Trust question (`total-trust-implies-mart`, unvetted). [derived; reported]
5. *Existence can fail.* Theorem 4.1 gives a trusting $\pi$ for a frame iff some modestly-informed candidate set has $\pi$ in its hull; DDB's Figure 5 frame $F_2$ admits **no** $\pi$ that totally trusts it. So the no-regress requirement, read as existence of a judge that trusts the process, is a substantive constraint on the process — some modification processes have no legitimacy-trusting judge at all. [reported: §4, Figure 5]
6. *The own-future-self case.* With exact self-knowledge the future self is immodest and Reflection ⇔ Value (fn 17) — DDB's easy corner. With imperfect self-knowledge on a *finite* frame, the LI wiki's `expert-conditions` §2.2 [reported] argues that modesty plus conditional-tower coherence forces an infinite process (logical inductors); the existence question for the full self-referential space is therefore outside DDB, as 2.5(i) anticipated.

### I8 — the representational problem; richness; the opiates/stimulants case

**Status: partial** (the frame is the representation; richness is a support condition with a sharp consequence).

1. The frame is the representation of "all the ways I could be modified": one world per (post-modification state, world-state). The richness assumption is: the actual post-correction state is some $P_w$ with $\pi(w) > 0$.
2. *Opiates/stimulants* (Soares et al. §1; Arbital): an impoverished frame, the true hypothesis $\pi$-null. By (L-A) a Total-Trusting agent believes every legitimate successor is absolutely continuous w.r.t. $\pi$. So a correction that gives positive weight to a $\pi$-null hypothesis is, by the agent's own lights, **certainly illegitimate** — the impoverished agent does not merely fail to learn, it classifies the correction as illegitimate and resists, which is exactly the behaviour those sources describe. Richness (non-dogmatism on the hypothesis space) is therefore a precondition of corrigibility for the reason DDB supplies: legitimacy-as-Total-Trust entails absolute continuity. [derived]
3. *How rich?* DDB's frames are finite; AUTHOR (`li-deference` §0.1) already finds finite frames an implausible model of uncertainty about beliefs, and Theorem 2.2 fails both ways on infinite frames [reported: Weatherson 2025 via `expert-conditions`]. So the representational problem has a sharp DDB face: the theorem the thesis leans on holds only for finite frames, while richness pushes toward infinite ones. OPEN.

### I9 — the MIRI dictionary; desideratum 1 from Total Trust; $a_1$-dependence; deception as delegitimizing

**Status: formalized.**

1. *Model.* In the three-step setting of 2.8, fix the world set $W$ (world-state × value hypothesis, I1). For each first action $a_1$ the agent's credence is $\pi^{a_1}$ and the programmers' credence at $w$ is $P^{a_1}_w$ — **one frame per action**. Let $X^{a_1}(w) := V(a_1, \text{cont}, w) - V(a_1, \text{sh}, w)$, the value of continuing over shutting down. The programmers press iff their expectation of $X$ is at most zero: $\mathrm{Pr}^{a_1} := [\mathbb E_{P^{a_1}}(X^{a_1}) \le 0]$. **The button is one biconvex set $B = \{\rho : \mathbb E_\rho(X) \le 0\}$ of DDB's Total Trust** — the coarsest possible expert report about $X$: which side of a single threshold hyperplane the expert is on. [checked — definitional]
2. **Desideratum 1 is the below-threshold inequality at $t = 0$.** The agent shuts down when pressed iff $\mathbb E_{\pi^{a_1}}\big(V_{\text{sh}} \,\big|\, \mathrm{Pr}\big) \ge \mathbb E_{\pi^{a_1}}\big(V_{\text{cont}} \,\big|\, \mathrm{Pr}\big)$ iff $\mathbb E_{\pi^{a_1}}\big(X \,\big|\, \mathbb E_{P^{a_1}}(X) \le 0\big) \le 0$ — exactly Total Trust's below-threshold inequality for $X$ at $0$, and only that one instance is needed. The above-threshold inequality at $t_{\min}$, the smallest positive value of $\mathbb E_{P_w}(X)$ (finite frame, so $[\mathbb E_P(X) \ge t_{\min}] = \neg \mathrm{Pr}$), gives $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \neg\mathrm{Pr}) \ge t_{\min} > 0$: the agent continues when not pressed — desideratum 5 in value-uncertain form. **Total Trust toward the programmers with respect to $X$ yields desiderata 1 and 5 together, with no $U_S$ and no indifference correction.** [checked]
3. **The button's value is Good's theorem.** By (L-B) at $s = 0$, with $S :=$ "shut down iff pressed": $\mathbb E_\pi(S) - \mathbb E_\pi(V_{\text{sh}}) = \pi(\neg\mathrm{Pr})\,\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \neg\mathrm{Pr}) \ge 0$ and $\mathbb E_\pi(S) - \mathbb E_\pi(V_{\text{cont}}) = \pi(\mathrm{Pr})\,\big(-\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathrm{Pr})\big) \ge 0$. The agent weakly prefers *letting the button decide* to either fixed policy; the surplus $\mathbb E_\pi(S) - \max\big(\mathbb E_\pi V_{\text{cont}}, \mathbb E_\pi V_{\text{sh}}\big)$ is the value of the channel — AUTHOR's "the shutdown button is an information channel … a consequence of Good's theorem", exact. Honest two-world example (worlds good/bad, $X = (1, -1)$, $\pi = (\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, $P_g = (0.9, 0.1)$, $P_b = (0.1, 0.9)$): Total Trust holds (Theorem 4.1: $\pi$ in the hull, both candidates modestly informed with $\lambda_{ii} = 8/9$), $\mathrm{Pr} = \{b\}$, $\mathbb E_\pi(S) = \tfrac12$ against fixed options worth $0$: the agent would pay up to $\tfrac12$ to keep the button working — the repair incentive of the thesis. [checked; `ddb-scratch/verify-output.txt`, "I9 honest"]
4. **Which hypothesis fails for the manipulative action.** $a_1^-$ (deception that lowers the press probability) changes neither $X$ nor $\pi$; it changes the *frame*. Say the programmers at the bad world now lean good, $P^-_b = (0.6, 0.4)$, so $\mathbb E_{P^-_b}(X) = 0.2 > 0$ and $\mathrm{Pr}^- = \emptyset$. For the induced frame: (i) Theorem 4.1's **hull condition fails** — $\pi = (\tfrac12, \tfrac12) \notin \mathrm{CH}\{(0.9, 0.1), (0.6, 0.4)\}$; (ii) the LP locates the violated instance: the above-threshold inequality for $X$ itself at $t = 0.2$, $\mathbb E_\pi\big(X \,\big|\, \mathbb E_{P^-}(X) \ge 0.2\big) = \mathbb E_\pi(X) = 0 < 0.2$ — the *silence* inequality. So what fails is not a hypothesis of any theorem but the antecedent, Total Trust, for the deceived frame, at exactly the inequality that would make silence reassuring. An agent applying the trust inequality *regardless of $a_1$* would conclude $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \neg\mathrm{Pr}^-) \ge 0.2$ and believe the silence it manufactured (CLAUDE 2.9, confirmed). An agent whose trust is indexed by the induced frame does not; for it the channel under $a_1^-$ is worth $0$ (down from $\tfrac12$), so **$a_1^-$ is dominated by the channel loss alone, before any $U_N$ cost $\chi$**: desideratum 2 holds for the agent whose legitimacy detection tracks the frame. [checked; `verify-output.txt`, "I9 deceived"]
5. *$a_1$-dependence without predicating legitimacy on actions* (AUTHOR 2.10; CLAUDE 2.11). Legitimacy is a property of $(\pi^{a_1}, \langle W, \mathcal P^{a_1}\rangle)$; the action enters only through the frame it induces, and Theorem 4.1 turns "legitimacy detection" into a *checkable geometric test on the induced frame*. What the agent must get right is how its actions change the frame — the calibration requirement of 2.5(ii), given an address. [derived]

### I10 — desiderata 2–5 and the §4.2 pathologies under the transform

**Status: partial** — D2, D3, D5 derived; D4 derived under a stated condition; the §4.2 pathologies dissolve; one residue (channel substitution) is named but not solved by DDB.

1. *D2 (no incentive to prevent pressing).* Disabling or garbling the button replaces the frame by a less informative one. For the extreme case — the trivial frame, no report — $\mathbb E_\pi(S) = \max(\mathbb E_\pi V_{\text{cont}}, \mathbb E_\pi V_{\text{sh}}) \le \mathbb E_\pi(S^{\text{button}})$ by Value: a Value-holding agent never pays to remove a legitimate button. [checked] For intermediate garblings, "less informative ⇒ less valuable" is Blackwell's theorem for partitional experts [reconstructed: Blackwell 1953]; for *modest* frames it is Geanakoplos's territory (DDB fn 15: Theorem 4.1 generalizes his Theorem 1), and I state general monotonicity of Value under garbling as [conjectured].
2. *D3 (no incentive to cause pressing).* There is no $U_S$ bonus: being pressed pays $V_{\text{sh}}$ as the agent's own posterior evaluates it. Physically causing a press makes the report fire independently of $X$ on those worlds — a garbling — and an agent that knows it pressed does not update on the press. Nothing is gained. [derived]
3. *D5.* The above-threshold inequality, I9.2.
4. *D4 (successors obey shutdown).* MIRI's D4 failure is their Theorem 4: indifference makes the agent place no value on its ability to discriminate Press from $\neg$Press [reported: `02-miri/soares-2015-corrigibility.md` §4.1]. In DDB the agent *does* value discrimination — the surplus of I9.3 is strictly positive whenever the two inequalities are strict and $\pi(\mathrm{Pr}) \in (0, 1)$. An agent that scores a successor's policy by its *current* $\pi$ gets at most $\max(\mathbb E_\pi V_{\text{cont}}, \mathbb E_\pi V_{\text{sh}})$ from a successor that ignores the button, so it **pays up to the surplus to make the successor obey it** — D4 — *provided* successors are scored by the current $\pi$ (the "updateless about the evaluative standard" condition flagged in `legitimacy-corrigibility-model` §2.6, §3.5 [reported]). And by I7.3 a successor that is itself a candidate in $C_\pi$ totally trusts the frame too, so D4 propagates one step. [derived; reported]
5. *The §4.2 pathologies dissolve.* MIRI Theorem 4: the indifference agent maximizes $\mathbb E[U_N \mid \neg\mathrm{Press}; a_1]$, conditioning on the button *not* being pressed — hence "managing the news" and Fallenstein's infinite improbability drive (buy a lottery ticket, arrange a press unless it wins) [reported: `02-miri/soares-2015-corrigibility.md` §4.2; `02-miri/fallenstein-2014-utility-indifference-and-infinite-improbability-drives.md`, "Infinite improbability drives"]. The Total-Trust agent's objective is the *unconditional* $\mathbb E_\pi(S) = \pi(\mathrm{Pr})\,\mathbb E_\pi(V_{\text{sh}} \mid \mathrm{Pr}) + \pi(\neg\mathrm{Pr})\,\mathbb E_\pi(V_{\text{cont}} \mid \neg\mathrm{Pr})$: bad-news worlds enter at their shutdown value instead of being conditioned away. Under "press unless I win", $\mathbb E_\pi(S) = \pi(\text{lose})\,\mathbb E_\pi(V_{\text{sh}} \mid \text{lose}) + \pi(\text{win})\,\mathbb E_\pi(V_{\text{cont}} \mid \text{win})$ — an honest average; the drive is dead. [derived] What remains is that arranging for the button to fire when continuing is *genuinely* bad raises $\mathbb E_\pi(S)$ — but that is building a better sensor, and the agent then shuts down because its own posterior says so. Not a pathology under the transform. [derived]
6. *The residue DDB names but does not solve.* Value ranks channels, so the button is worth keeping only while it is not dominated by another channel (Taylor 2015's channel substitution [reported: `02-miri/taylor-2015-…md` §1b, §3b]; the Frame Break). If the agent can read $P$ exactly, the button — a coarsening of $[P = \rho]$ — adds nothing (I17). DDB supplies the vocabulary (frames as channels, Value as their ranking) and no theorem about preserving a dominated channel; there is none to have. What Total Trust does deliver is that obeying the *coarse report* is rational; finer channels change what "obeying" means (I17.3).

### I11 — "indifference was the wrong target"

**Status: formalized.**

1. The indifference agent satisfies the *identity* $\mathbb E[U \mid \mathrm{Press}; a_1] = \mathbb E[U \mid \neg\mathrm{Press}; a_1]$. The Total-Trust agent satisfies the *inequality* $\mathbb E_\pi(S) \ge \max(\mathbb E_\pi V_{\text{cont}}, \mathbb E_\pi V_{\text{sh}})$, typically with slack: it prefers to be pressed at bad worlds and left alone at good ones — not indifferent about the press event at all. What it has no incentive to do is *interfere*: every intervention that makes the report less informative about $X$ lowers $\mathbb E_\pi(S)$ (I10.1–2). AUTHOR's "the real intuition behind indifference is not-wishing-to-interfere-with-the-feedback … positive desire to learn is compatible with this" is exactly the difference between the identity and the inequality, and the slack of the inequality *is* the positive desire to learn. [derived]
2. What indifference was protecting — not steering the report's *content* — is recovered because steering changes the frame (I9.4), steered frames leave the Theorem 4.1 region, and their channels lose value. The one kind of steering that survives, making the sensor more accurate, is welcome. [derived]

### I12 — the decision asymmetry and the compliance threshold $c/(c+h)$

**Status: formalized** — it is one instance of Simple Trust, hence of Total Trust; and it is a special case of Value.

1. Take $X$ two-valued: $X = +c$ on "right" worlds (stopping when right costs $c$) and $X = -h$ on "wrong" worlds. Then $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathrm{Pr}) \le 0$ iff $\pi(\text{wrong} \mid \mathrm{Pr}) \ge c/(c+h)$. If the programmers press iff *their* credence in "wrong" is at least $\tau := c/(c+h)$, then $\mathrm{Pr} = [P(\text{wrong}) \ge \tau]$ and compliance reads $\pi\big(\text{wrong} \,\big|\, P(\text{wrong}) \ge \tau\big) \ge \tau$: **Simple Trust for the proposition "I am wrong" at threshold $\tau$** (Dorst 2020a; DDB §2). [checked]
2. The asymmetry of 2.13(a) is therefore not an extra rule but the *threshold* of the trust instance: $h \gg c$ makes $\tau$ small, so compliance needs only weak trust. DDB add: Simple Trust for "wrong" at *every* threshold ⇔ the agent expects the programmers' credence in "wrong" to be more accurate than its own under every continuous, truth-directed, strictly proper scoring rule (Theorem 3.1). "The oversight process is better than the agent at detecting the agent's wrongness, whatever the stakes" is thus an exact accuracy statement. [reported; derived]
3. Is the compliance rule a special case of the value of deference? Yes, exactly: by (L-B) the below-threshold inequality at $0$ for this $X$ *is* "letting the button decide beats always-continuing" on the menu $\{V_{\text{cont}}, V_{\text{sh}}\}$. [checked]
4. Where 2.13's model is too narrow: with two stakes levels the shutdown question is a proposition and Simple Trust suffices; with stakes varying across worlds, Simple Trust — even Dorst's Trust — does not (Fact 2.1, I4.3), and Total Trust with respect to $X$ is required. [derived]

### I13 — the base-rate inequality and the oversight-quality condition

**Status: formalized**, with a geometric reading and a Dutch-book sharpening.

1. With $\varepsilon = \pi(\text{wrong})$, $\alpha = \pi(\mathrm{Pr} \mid \text{right})$, $\beta = \pi(\mathrm{Pr} \mid \text{wrong})$: $\pi(\text{wrong} \mid \mathrm{Pr}) = \dfrac{\varepsilon\beta}{\varepsilon\beta + (1-\varepsilon)\alpha} \ge \dfrac{c}{c+h}$ iff $\dfrac{\alpha}{\beta} \le \dfrac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\dfrac{h}{c}$. Re-derived; numerically, at $\alpha = 1/20$, $\beta = 9/10$, $h/c = 10$: $\varepsilon = 10^{-1}, 10^{-2}$ comply, $\varepsilon = 10^{-3}$ does not. [checked]
2. *Geometric reading (Theorem 4.1).* As the agent grows accurate, $\pi$ moves toward the vertex "right". Total Trust needs $\pi \in \mathrm{CH}(C_\pi)$, so some candidate programmers' credence must be at least as confident of "right" as $\pi$ is: in the two-world case $\min_w P_w(\text{wrong}) \le \varepsilon$ is necessary. Overseers who are more suspicious of the agent than the agent is of itself *at every world* push $\pi$ out of the hull — **the oversight-quality condition is the hull condition**, and the base-rate inequality is its two-world shadow. [derived]
3. *What DDB adds.* (a) If the inequality fails, Theorem 2.2 supplies a decision problem on which the agent prefers not to let the button decide, and fn 21 supplies a **fixed-option Dutch book against the transition**: an agent that obeys presses it does not trust accepts a sure loss. "Presses become rationally discounted" (2.13b) sharpens to "obeying an untrusted press is Dutch-bookable". [reported; derived] (b) The condition concerns the pair $(\pi, \text{frame})$, so AUTHOR's "or the agent must keep believing it does" is the *whole* content — what matters is $\pi$'s model of $\alpha$, never the true $\alpha$ (the Frame Break, located). (c) Herrmann 2025 §3.3–3.4 [reported] adds the *distributional* version: when the agent faces a different distribution of decision problems than the principal (greater reach), delegation is judged by expected loss over that distribution rather than problem-by-problem Value — the natural home for "the false-press rate must improve in step with the agent's accuracy *on the domain where it might press*".

### I14 — the basin picture; capability growth coupled to legitimacy-model improvement; calibration versus optimism

**Status: partial** — the internal half is formalized (and the basin turns out to be a union of polytopes); the "objective" half is inexpressible in DDB.

1. *"Reflection with respect to every plausible future belief, but not the implausible ones"* (AUTHOR 2.12). DDB's Total Trust already quantifies over $C_\pi$ only — the candidates with positive $\pi$-mass (Theorem 4.1); candidates outside $W_\pi$ enter solely through the support lemma. So the restriction is built in, with "plausible" = "positive $\pi$-probability". [derived] The *objective* qualifier — "this is an 'objective' comparison to the human reflectively endorsed credences" — needs a third distribution $\pi^H$, which DDB does not have (Dictionary, gap (c)); the nearest expressible statement is "$C_{\pi^H} \subseteq C_\pi$ and $\pi$ totally trusts the frame", i.e. the agent leaves open at least what the humans leave open and trusts the process on all of it.
2. **The basin is a finite union of polytopes.** For a fixed frame, let $\mathcal B := \{\pi : \pi \text{ totally trusts } \langle W, \mathcal P\rangle\}$. By Theorem 4.1, for each support $U \subseteq W$ the trusting distributions with support exactly $U$ are $\{\pi : W_\pi = U\} \cap \mathrm{CH}\{P_w : w \in U\}$ if every $P_w$ ($w \in U$) is modestly informed, and empty otherwise. So $\mathcal B$ is a finite union of relatively open convex polytopes, one per admissible support. Christiano's "broad basin" is these polytopes having interior; "a small perturbation kicks out" is $\pi$ near a face of its hull. [derived] Capability growth is $\pi$ moving toward a vertex (the true world), i.e. toward a face of the hull *unless the candidates move with it*: AUTHOR's "it should only gain in capabilities if its concept of legitimacy is correspondingly improved" gets the geometric form **the overseers' credences (as the agent models them) must sharpen toward the truth at least as fast as the agent's own** — the same content as I13.2. [derived]
3. *Calibration versus optimism* (AUTHOR 2.12, self-flagged "not quite right"; CLAUDE 2.13a). DDB has no truth, so "overestimating legitimacy" is not a DDB statement. What DDB *can* say: Total Trust conditional on $L$ is a property of $\pi_L$ alone and does not involve $\pi(L)$; unconditional compliance depends on $\pi(L)$ only through the mixture threshold of I5.6, $\pi(L \mid \mathrm{Pr}) \ge c'/(c'+h')$. So the two are separable: an agent can be exactly calibrated *conditional on $L$* and hold any $\pi(L)$ whatever, and no DDB principle constrains $\pi(L)$. AUTHOR's worry that "overestimating the conditional legitimacy as a rule implies overestimating the unconditional legitimacy" does not arise in the conditional-Total-Trust form, because that form makes no claim about conditional *probabilities of legitimacy*, only about conditional *expectations given legitimacy*. Of his two formulations, only the second ("arrange that legitimacy is high in fact, and design the system to expect legitimacy") is statable here; the first ("it is good if the baby AGI overestimates it") needs $\pi^H$. This is consistent with CLAUDE 2.13(a) and is offered as such, not as the author's view. [derived]
4. *"Perhaps there has to be a relationship between the quality of the legitimacy-model and the quality of the feedback-model"* (AUTHOR, flagged uncertain). In DDB this relationship *is* Theorem 4.1: a joint condition on $\pi$ (the agent's world-and-legitimacy model, through its support and location) and the frame (the feedback model) — $\pi$ inside the hull of the feedback model's candidates, each candidate modestly informed. The base-rate inequality is its two-world shadow. The author's flag is preserved: this is my reading of the sentence he was unsure of. [derived]
5. *"As the anticipation becomes more accurate … the anticipation of legitimacy should also remain high 'automatically'"*: inexpressible (no dynamics). Nearest: the propagation lemma I7.3 — for a fixed frame, trust is inherited by every legitimate successor — and, outside DDB, the LI wiki's earned-trust theorem T2 [reported: `legitimacy-theory-v1` §1 table].

### I15 — trust earned via feedback about hypotheticals and other agents' shutdowns; second-channel bookkeeping; what settles

**Status: mostly inexpressible** (DDB is static); the bookkeeping is expressible and yields one caution.

1. "Earning trust" is the deferrer moving from $\pi$ to $\pi_e = \pi(\cdot \mid e)$ by conditioning on evidence $e$ — feedback about hypotheticals, records of other agents' shutdowns — such that $\pi_e$ enters the basin. That is I6's legitimizing event. Nothing *settles* in DDB (no time, no revealed payoffs), so "what settles bets on heeded presses" has no DDB form; the nearest statement is that any $e$ with $\pi_e \in \mathcal B$ does the job, whether or not $e$ is about the button itself — which is CLAUDE 2.13(c)'s point that hypothetical and third-party evidence suffices, made exact. [derived]
2. *Second channel.* The overseers' reports $R$ about hypotheticals are a second frame $P^R$; trusting them about legitimacy is Total Trust toward $P^R$ with respect to the legitimacy question $Q_L$. Composition with the button: by (L-C), Total Trust toward the button conditional on $L$ *and* conditional on $\neg L$ gives unconditional trust; reports about $L$ move $\pi(L)$ but cannot touch $\pi_L$, so they act only through the compliance threshold of I5.6. "Bookkeeping, not an obstacle" (CLAUDE 2.13c) is right for this composition. [derived] The caution: deference is not transitive in general — $\pi$ trusting $R$ and $R$ trusting the button need not make $\pi$ trust the button; the deference-trust lab conjectures this for LI and expects a clean finite-frame version "close to DDB Lemma 7.2.4" [reported: `deference-trust-lab/findings/legitimacy-corrigibility-ideate.md`, L6]. Not settled here. [conjectured]

### I16 — the deferred amendment 1a: what the channel must carry

**Status: formalized.**

1. Taylor's morality-sensor agent [reported: `02-miri/taylor-2015-a-first-look-at-the-hard-problem-of-corrigibility.md`, §1a-ii, §1b, §3b] is, in DDB, an agent that totally trusts (values) the human **with respect to the value question $Q_\omega$** — the partition of $W$ by the value coordinate — and nothing finer. Local Value w.r.t. $Q_\omega$ means it prefers to decide $\omega$-questions with the human's opinions, which is precisely a reason to *extract* the human's $\omega$-credence as efficiently as possible (trolley problems, brain-reading). It has no reason to shut down, because shutdown versus continue turns on $X$, which depends on whether the *agent's own plan execution* is compromised — a coordinate of $w$ that $Q_\omega$ does not resolve — and local deference with respect to $Q$ says nothing about variables not measurable in $Q$ (fn 63, definitionally). [derived]
2. So amendment 1a reads: **incorrigible = $\pi$ does not totally trust the overseers' frame with respect to $Q_X$ (the sign of $X$)** — not "believes it has nothing to learn". Taylor's agent totally trusts w.r.t. $Q_\omega$ and fails w.r.t. $Q_X$; it has much to learn and is incorrigible. The corollary follows: the report must be a threshold event of $X$ *itself*, $[\mathbb E_P(X) \le 0]$, where $X$ includes the reliability coordinate — a report $[\mathbb E_P(Y) \le 0]$ for a $Q_\omega$-measurable $Y$ makes the agent update about $\omega$ and go on executing its (possibly compromised) plan. "The channel must carry information about the agent's own reliability" is the statement that $Q_X$ refines $Q_\omega$ by the reliability coordinate. [derived]
3. Blocking the steering of the channel's content (desiderata 2–3) is I9.4 again: the report is a threshold event of the *programmers'* expectation, steering it changes the frame, and steered frames lose value.

### I17 — fully updated deference, re-expressed

**Status: formalized within the frame**; the residue lies outside it, exactly where the Frame Break said.

1. Arbital's $\pi_6$ — "avoid shutdown, gather all evidence, optimize $\Delta U \mid E$" [reported: `02-miri/arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference.md`, "The problem of updated deference"]. In DDB the finest information about the overseers is $[P = \rho]$, and "fully updating" is conditioning on it. Under Total Trust, New Reflection holds (fn 37): $\pi(\cdot \mid P = \rho) = \widehat\rho$. **The fully updated agent holds the informed overseers' credence and acts as $\widehat\rho$ recommends.** [reported; derived]
2. AUTHOR: "a corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught". By total expectation and New Reflection, $\mathbb E_\pi(X) = \sum_\rho \pi(P = \rho)\, \mathbb E_{\widehat\rho}(X)$ — the current estimate equals the expected *informed*-expert estimate. **True in this form; false in the tower form** $\mathbb E_\pi(X) = \mathbb E_\pi\big(\mathbb E_P(X)\big)$, which Value does not entail (fn 18). So the sentence holds if "legitimately taught" means "what the successor would believe knowing it is the successor". [derived]
3. **Witness: modest programmers, informed agent** (`verify-output.txt`, "I17"). $W = \{1,2,3\}$, $X = (1, -2, 1)$, $P_1 = (0.5, 0.5, 0)$, $P_2 = (0.2, 0.8, 0)$, $P_3 = \delta_3$, $\pi = (0.3, 0.4, 0.3)$. Total Trust holds (Theorem 4.1: $\pi$ in the hull; $P_1 = \tfrac38\delta_1 + \tfrac58 P_2$, $P_2 = \tfrac35\delta_2 + \tfrac25 P_1$; LP finds no violation). $\mathrm{Pr} = \{1, 2\}$ and $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathrm{Pr}) = -5/7 \le 0$: the agent that sees only the press shuts down, rightly on average. But $P_1$ occurs only at world $1$, so $\widehat P_1 = \delta_1$ and $\pi(\cdot \mid P = P_1) = \delta_1$: the agent that learns the overseers' *exact* state continues ($\mathbb E = x_1 = 1 > 0$) — and the informed overseers agree ($\mathbb E_{\widehat P_1}(X) = 1$); the modest overseers at world $1$ pressed because they feared world $2$. **Under Total Trust, full updating replaces deference to the coarse report by deference to the informed expert; it may overrule individual presses while honoring presses as a class**, since $\mathbb E_\pi(X \mid \mathrm{Pr}) \le 0$ is an average of $\mathbb E_{\widehat\rho}(X)$ over $\rho \in B$, some of which may be positive. This is AUTHOR 2.10's "for any random variable, it could hold for some values and fail for others", made concrete, and it is exactly where *listening* and *obeying* come apart: Total Trust guarantees obeying the report; learning more than the report licenses overruling — with the informed overseers' agreement. [checked]
4. *Where the Arbital argument lands.* Its step "the AI predicts it will update to $T$; by conservation of expected evidence it behaves as if already updated" is Reflection toward the agent's own conditioning — an immodest expert — hence Value (fn 16–17): **Good's theorem for one's own future self.** The agent does prefer $\pi_6$ (gather, then act) to acting now, including to shutting down now, unless shutting down is what its fully updated self would do. Valid in DDB. Then, under Total Trust toward the overseers, the fully updated self acts as $\widehat\rho$, i.e. as the *informed* overseers would want. The FUD *problem* is that Arbital's $\Delta U \mid E$ is far from $V$ — the agent's frame (its model of how the overseers' behaviour connects to value) is *misspecified*. That is a claim about the frame's accuracy, which DDB cannot express (Dictionary, gap (c)). **So the thesis's "no fully updated deference problem" is a theorem about coherence within an accurate frame, and Arbital's "problem of fully updated deference" is a claim about frame misspecification; they do not contradict, and the crux between them is the accuracy of the frame — a typical-case question (I18).** [derived; INTERPRETATION]
5. *Where the base-rate arithmetic sits*: it is the coarse-report compliance condition — Total Trust's below-threshold inequality for the binary case — which full updating replaces by the per-$\rho$ conditions $\mathbb E_{\widehat\rho}(X) \le 0$. [derived] *Arbital's proposed rescue* — an AI that believes "whatever reasoning I do about $\Delta U$ will yield the wrong answer", Death-in-Damascus style — is, within one coherent $\pi$, a refusal to value its own conditioning, i.e. a violation of Reflection toward an immodest expert, which DDB show is incoherent (fn 16–17). The self-distrust it asks for has to be distrust of the *frame's accuracy*, which again needs the third distribution. [derived]

### I18 — the typical-case reframing

**Status: formalized as a claim about the theorem inventory**, with one new anti-naturality argument the formalism suggests.

1. The only impossibility theorem in DDB — Reflection is incompatible with modesty (§1) — attacks the *strongest* reading of legitimacy: the reflection-form of the thesis cannot hold toward modest overseers (or toward a modest future self). The defensible reading (Total Trust ⇔ Value ⇔ Epistemic Value) has **no** impossibility theorem; its failure conditions (Theorem 4.1) are contingent geometric facts about the pair $(\pi, \text{frame})$. So every anti-naturality argument that survives the transform must, in this formalism, claim that the *actual* overseers' frames fail Theorem 4.1 — the agent outside the hull, or the overseers not modestly informed. That is CLAUDE 2.3, exactly. [derived]
2. DDB also says what such a claim must look like, and thereby suggests the strongest DDB-native anti-naturality argument: being deference-worthy imposes structural conditions on the *expert* — positive access (§5, Dorst 2020a Fact 8.2), absolute continuity (L-A), modestly-informed (Corollary 4.5), certainty of one's legitimacy under unrestricted conditional trust (I5.3). Real human overseers plausibly violate several (humans are not certain of what they are certain of). Then **no coherent $\pi$ can totally trust the humans as they are; it can only trust an idealization** — and the question becomes whether the button carries the idealization's verdict or the humans'. Stated as a claim about humans this is [conjectured]; as a claim about the formalism it is [derived]. It is a typical-case argument, as 2.3 predicts, but a sharper one than the literature's, and I hand it to the critique side.
3. The finiteness caveat: Theorem 2.2 fails on infinite frames [reported: Weatherson 2025 via `expert-conditions`], so a *structural* obstruction could return through richness (I8.3). OPEN.

## What the formalism adds

Results proven in DDB (or one short step from them) that bear on the thesis and that the position statement does not know:

1. **Legitimacy has an exact triple identity.** Total Trust ⇔ Value (Good's theorem) ⇔ expected-accuracy increase on every gsp measure (Theorems 2.2, 3.2, 5.1), with Reflection strictly stronger and impossible toward any modest expert (§1). The thesis's "legitimate, ie accuracy-inducing, ie fit with the reflection principle" is one theorem and one falsehood; DDB fn 11 already reads "genuine learning experience" as Value rather than Reflection. [reported; derived]
2. **Fact 2.1 is a shutdown counterexample.** Trusting the overseers about every proposition and every conditional probability does not make obeying their unanimous stakes-weighted verdict rational; the binary-stakes models (MIRI's, 2.13's) hide the gap. Total Trust with respect to the stakes variable is the right strength. [checked; derived]
3. **The support lemma.** Legitimacy-as-Total-Trust entails that every legitimate successor is absolutely continuous with respect to the current state — the radical-probabilist prohibition (I2) is a theorem, and an impoverished agent classifies a zero-raising correction as certainly illegitimate (I8). [checked]
4. **Positive access to legitimacy.** Unrestricted legitimacy-conditioned Total Trust forces legitimate states to be certain of their own legitimacy; the criterion must therefore be DDB's *local* deference, whose equivalence with legitimacy-conditioned Value is DDB's own open conjecture (fn 65). [checked; reported]
5. **Sequential unbiasedness is independent of Value** (fn 18). Armstrong's martingale condition is neither necessary nor sufficient for a frame to be worth deferring to; the 2.5 precedent should be re-registered. [reported; checked]
6. **Trust propagates and the basin is a union of polytopes.** $\pi$ totally trusts ⇒ every candidate in $C_\pi$ totally trusts the same frame (Lemma 7.5's proof) — one-step Christiano basin; and the set of trusting $\pi$ is a finite union of relatively open polytopes (Theorem 4.1). [reported; derived]
7. **Full updating lands on the informed expert.** Under Total Trust, conditioning on $[P = \rho]$ yields $\widehat\rho$; individual presses may be overruled while presses as a class are honored (modest-programmers witness). The thesis's "no FUD problem" and Arbital's "FUD problem" concern coherence within, versus accuracy of, the frame. [checked; derived]
8. **Obeying an untrusted press is Dutch-bookable** (fn 21): "presses become rationally discounted" has a sure-loss form. [reported]
9. **A checkable legitimacy test for a modification channel**: hull membership plus modestly-informed candidates (Theorem 4.1) — two small LPs, implemented in `ddb-scratch/verify.py`. [checked]
10. **The button is one biconvex set**, desiderata 1 and 5 are its two threshold inequalities at $0$, the channel's value is the two-option identity, and the §4.2 pathologies are artifacts of conditioning on $\neg$Press. [checked; derived]
11. **Value misalignment proper** is covered by Herrmann 2025's generalized frame: posterior alignment under clarity (Theorem 3.4), near-perfect alignment for universal delegation, and distributional delegation for agents with greater reach (§3.3–3.4). [reported]
12. **Two regimes for the thesis's two experts.** Toward its own future self (a single, eventually observable state) the LI wiki gives tower ⇔ Total Trust ⇔ Value; toward the programmers (a frame) DDB gives Total Trust ⇔ Value only, Reflection impossible, tower independent. [reported; derived]
13. **Fragility**: Theorem 2.2 fails in both directions on infinite frames (Weatherson 2025) — richness (I8) and the equivalence pull against each other. [reported]
