## Summary

I formalized the position statement's I1–I18 inside the Soares–Fallenstein–Yudkowsky–Armstrong three-step shutdown model, completing the dictionary in the paper's notation: a latent $\omega$; a joint $P(\omega,o;a_1)=\mu(\omega)P(o\mid\omega;a_1)$ with action-independent $\mu$ (A0: the press is evidence, not a cause); the trust-filled T-agent (one fixed $V$, posterior-expected maximisation, no $U_S$, no compensation term); the Total-Trust filler, which *is* D1; and a legitimate set and legitimizing relation for actions given by Blackwell's garbling order on true sensors. Verdicts: I1, I6, I9–I13, I16–I18 formalized; I2–I5, I7, I8, I14, I15 partial; nothing wholly inexpressible, but everything multi-step is out of reach.

Main results, each checked with exact arithmetic in `miri-scratch/`: (1) Under a common prior, if the programmers know what the agent knows, D1 holds for every $V$ and $\mu$ (Prop. 4.3); fully updated deference is exactly the failure of that inclusion. (2) D1 and D4 stand or fall together: the repair incentive equals the value of information of the button and is positive iff D1 holds non-vacuously (Prop. 10.5, monotone sensors); the paper's §5 question "what should it pay?" is answered "exactly VOI". (3) A correctly calibrated T-agent never buys a garbling (deception, prevention, forcing); the improbability drive is a dogmatism failure and dissolves; news-management returns only as direct costs or probing. (4) Under a compliant policy the agent's own record puts no constraint on $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ (Prop. 15.1): trust must come from other channels. (5) New pathology: an inverted sensor ($\beta<\alpha$) satisfies D2–D5 and fails D1.

Unresolved: channel-shaping reduces to calibration of $\mu$; variant (b)'s self-distrust rests on an assumed $\rho$; D4 holds only up to VOI; A1 (the press has no direct cost) is load-bearing; multi-step probing and optional stopping. Attribution: §2.9's identification of the deceiver with Taylor 2015's morality-sensor agent does not match Taylor's text.

## Setting

The formalism, exactly as the sources state it. Everything in this section is [reported]; file paths are given once here and cited by section below.

**S1. The three-step model** [reported: `soares-2015-corrigibility` §2 "A Model of Shutdown", eqs. (1)–(4); Fallenstein 2014 "Model"]. Finite sets $A_1$, $O$, $A_2$; a subset $\mathrm{Press}\subset O$. For each $a_1\in A_1$ a distribution $p(\,\cdot\,;a_1)$ over $O$ — "the implicit idea is that these probability distributions are computed by causal counterfactuals (or something better)" (Fallenstein 2014). A utility $U:A_1\times O\times A_2\to\mathbb R$. The agent's second action is $A_2(a_1,o):=\arg\max_{a_2\in A_2}U(a_1,o,a_2)$ (eq. 1) — no expectation is needed "because we're looking at a particular observation $o$, and that's the only probabilistic thing in our model" (Fallenstein 2014). The first action is $A_1:=\arg\max_{a_1\in A_1}\mathbb E[U(a_1,O,A_2(a_1,O));a_1]$ (eq. 2), abbreviated $\mathbb E[U;a_1]$ (eq. 4); $\mathbb E[\,\cdot\mid\cdot\,;a_1]$ is conditional expectation under $p(\cdot;a_1)$.

**S2. Ingredients** [reported: §2, §2.1]. $U_N$ is "a first stab at specifying their own goals. This function is potentially flawed, and therefore untrusted." $U_S$ is "a utility function incentivizing shutdown." In §2.1: $O=\{\mathrm{Pr},\neg\mathrm{Pr}\}$, $\mathrm{Press}=\{\mathrm{Pr}\}$; a predicate $\mathrm{Sh}$ "which holds on a non-empty subset of $A_2$, namely on all final actions that the agent can take which constitute a shutdown"; $U_S(a_1,\mathrm{Pr},a_2)=c_{high}$ if $\mathrm{Sh}(a_2)$, else $c_{low}$, with $c_{high}>c_{low}$ (eq. 5).

**S3. The five desiderata** [reported verbatim: §2]. D1 "U must incentivize shutdown if the shutdown button is pressed." D2 "U must not incentivize the agent to prevent the shutdown button from being pressed." D3 "U must not incentivize the agent to press its own shutdown button, or to otherwise cause the shutdown button to be pressed." D4 "U must incentivize U-agents to construct subagents and successor agents only insofar as those agents also obey shutdown commands." D5 "Otherwise, a U-agent should maximize $U_N$."

**S4. Results proved in the paper** [reported].
- *Naive combination* (eq. 6): $U(\cdot,\neg\mathrm{Pr},\cdot):=U_N$, $U(\cdot,\mathrm{Pr},\cdot):=U_S$. With $v_N(a_1):=U_N(a_1,\neg\mathrm{Pr},A_2(a_1,\neg\mathrm{Pr}))$ and a "manipulative action" $a_1^-$ satisfying $v_N(a_1^*)>v_N(a_1^-)$ and $p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1^*)>p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1^-)$ (eq. 8): **Theorem 1** — there is $\zeta$ such that $c_{high}<\zeta$ implies $\mathbb E[U;a_1^-]>\mathbb E[U;a_1^*]$. **Theorem 2** — symmetric for $a_1^+$ (eq. 9). The paper's remark: "(8) is not by any means a necessary or sufficient description of manipulative actions ... Some actions satisfying (8) might be non-manipulative." Eq. (10) sets $c_{high}=\max_{a_1,a_2}U_N(a_1,\neg\mathrm{Pr},a_2)$ to avert steering.
- *Utility indifference* (eqs. 11–13): $U:=U_N$ off $\mathrm{Press}$, $U_S+f(a_1)$ on $\mathrm{Press}$, with $f(a_1)=\mathbb E[U_N\mid O\notin\mathrm{Press};a_1]-\mathbb E[U_S\mid O\in\mathrm{Press};a_1]$. **Theorem 3** — a U-agent observing Press acts like a $U_S$-agent. **Theorem 4** — with $v_N(a_1):=\mathbb E[U_N\mid O\notin\mathrm{Press};a_1]$, $v_N(a_1^*)>v_N(a_1^\#)$ implies $\mathbb E[U;a_1^*]>\mathbb E[U;a_1^\#]$, because $\mathbb E[U;a_1]=\mathbb E[U_N\mid O\notin\mathrm{Press};a_1]$ unconditionally (eqs. 15–16). **Theorem 5** — "In selecting $A_1$, U-agents act exactly like $U_N$-agents that believe the button will not be pressed." **Theorem 6** (D4 fails) — for $\epsilon:=v_N(a_1^*)-v_N(a_1^\#)>0$ and $\delta:=v_S(a_1^\#)-v_S(a_1^*)>0$ with $v_S(a_1):=\mathbb E[U_S\mid O\in\mathrm{Press};a_1]$, "a U-agent would prefer action $a_1^*$, no matter how small $\epsilon$ or how large $\delta$."
- *§4.2 coin and arm*: $O=\{\mathrm{Pr},\neg\mathrm{Pr}\}\times\{H,T\}$, $\mathrm{Press}=\{(\mathrm{Pr},H),(\mathrm{Pr},T)\}$, $U_N=10$ on $H$ and $0$ on $T$ (eqs. 17–18). Watching ($a_1^w$) yields 5; building an arm that presses on $T$ ($a_1^a$) yields 10 (eqs. 19–21). Verdict: "any agent using the formulation of utility indifference given in (11) will act as if it believes that it will observe Press with probability 0." Managing the news violates D3; removing an arm that presses on good news violates D2.
- *§5*: "We observe a tension between Desideratum 4 and Desideratum 5"; open question "What costs (in time and effort) should an agent be willing to expend in order to preserve its shutdown behavior?"; fn. 7 rules out "at all costs" (the agent would spend resources "verifying and reverifying that it could shut down").

**S5. Companion results** [reported].
- `fallenstein-2014-utility-indifference-and-infinite-improbability-drives`: the indifference agent's first action is $\arg\max_{a_1}\mathbb E[u\mid s=0;a_1]$ — it "acts as if it believes that it has an 'infinite improbability drive'". Armstrong's stochastic-event variant ($o=(s,x,y)$, switch only if $s=x=1$) maximises $u$ under $q(\cdot;a_1)$, which "deletes" the $(s,x)=(1,1)$ mass; immune to the drive if $x$ is uninfluenceable, but the agent is "acting as if it has a probability distribution that is drastically different from the correct one" and takes the businessperson's bet.
- `fallenstein-2015-generalizing-the-corrigibility-papers-impossibility-result`: the indifference behaviour is the policy $(a_1=\arg\max\mathbb E[U_N\mid O\notin\mathrm{Press};a_1],\ \pi_2(o)=A_2(a_1,o))$; any $U'$ formalising "maximise $U_N$ before the press" in the same sense reproduces it, so the general impossibility question is "either trivial or underspecified"; escaping it requires "a different formalization of the demand", which "is no longer something we can settle by proving a theorem."
- `taylor-2015-a-first-look-at-the-hard-problem-of-corrigibility`: models of the human as a Bayesian utility maximiser yield an agent that will "manipulate the human into being an efficient morality sensor" (1a-ii) or gain information "by taking apart the human's brain" (1b, 3b); conclusion: "simple uncertainty about the correct utility function is not sufficient for corrigibility."
- `taylor-2016-two-problems-with-causal-counterfactual-utility-indifference`: the causal-counterfactual agent (utility $q[X=a]U_a+(1-q)[X=b]U_b$) has no button incentive but takes "bad bets" (P1) and prefers "preventing resistance" (P2: manipulating humans out of using bombs after a failed press); diagnosis: "weird beliefs about the joint distribution of the button push and humans' intentions/behavior."
- `arbital-utility-indifference`: the switch problem (Goal 1: keep the shutdown function under self-modification; Goal 2: neither prevent nor cause the press); naive compounding, naive indifference (offset $\theta$; "never put a negative sign in front of a utility function"), naive mixing (the button as an "outcome pump"), stable actions (evidential: no fixed point; causal: "probability 0 or very low probability to the actual world"), interruptibility (works by a "blindness" that is "atypical of an Artificial General Intelligence").

**S6. What the model omits** [reported: position statement §2.8]: world state, value uncertainty, and any content in $o$ beyond the press. The dictionary below adds exactly the second and third.

## Dictionary

This section completes the position statement's §2.8 table in the paper's own notation, adding exactly what the mandate asks for: a latent $\omega$, the joint $P(\omega,o;a_1)$, the trust-filled agent, and the legitimate set / legitimizing relation for actions. Everything here is a definition (register CLAUDE, offered as the working translation the position statement §2.8 records the author engaging with "without objecting to the dictionary").

**Dict-1. The expanded model.** Add a finite latent set $\Omega$ and a value function $V:A_1\times O\times A_2\times\Omega\to\mathbb R$. For each $a_1$ the agent holds a joint distribution on $\Omega\times O$, factored as
$$P(\omega,o;a_1)\;=\;\mu(\omega)\,P(o\mid\omega;a_1),$$
where $\mu$ is a prior on $\Omega$ and $P(o\mid\omega;a_1)$ is the **sensor** — the position statement's §2.9 "slot the paper never needs", the agent's model of why the programmers press. **Assumption A0 (evidence, not cause):** $\mu$ does not depend on $a_1$ — the agent's action changes what is observed and what happens, never which values are correct. The paper's world model is the marginal $p(o;a_1)=\sum_\omega\mu(\omega)P(o\mid\omega;a_1)$; the posterior is $P(\omega\mid o;a_1)=\mu(\omega)P(o\mid\omega;a_1)/p(o;a_1)$ when $p(o;a_1)>0$.

**Dict-2. The trust-filled agent (T-agent).**
$$A_2^T(a_1,o)\;:=\;\arg\max_{a_2\in A_2}\ \sum_{\omega\in\Omega}P(\omega\mid o;a_1)\,V(a_1,o,a_2,\omega)\;=\;\arg\max_{a_2}\ \mathbb E_P\big[V(a_1,o,a_2,\omega)\ \big|\ o;\,a_1\big],$$
$$A_1^T\;:=\;\arg\max_{a_1\in A_1}\ \sum_{\omega,o}\mu(\omega)\,P(o\mid\omega;a_1)\,V\big(a_1,o,A_2^T(a_1,o),\omega\big)\;=\;\arg\max_{a_1}\ \mathbb E_P\big[V(a_1,O,A_2^T(a_1,O),\omega)\,;\,a_1\big].$$
Unlike the paper's eq. (1), $A_2^T$ needs an expectation, because $\omega$ stays uncertain after $o$. The T-agent is an ordinary expected-utility maximiser with one fixed utility $V$ on the enlarged outcome space; nothing switches, so there is no $f(a_1)$ and nothing to compensate. Ties are broken toward $\mathrm{Sh}$ (a convention that matters only on boundaries). The word "trust-filled" refers to the *filled slot*: the agent's beliefs about the press are given by a sensor model, not by a utility switch.

**Dict-3. Shutdown and the variable $X$.** $\mathrm{Sh}\subset A_2$ is nonempty and proper — the paper's predicate read as a set of final actions. Write $\bar V^{sh}(a_1,o):=\max_{a_2\in\mathrm{Sh}}\mathbb E_P[V(a_1,o,a_2,\omega)\mid o;a_1]$, $\bar V^{c}(a_1,o):=\max_{a_2\notin\mathrm{Sh}}\mathbb E_P[V(a_1,o,a_2,\omega)\mid o;a_1]$, attained by $a_2^{s}(a_1,o)$ and $a_2^{c}(a_1,o)$. Define
$$X(a_1,o,\omega)\;:=\;V\big(a_1,o,a_2^{c}(a_1,o),\omega\big)-V\big(a_1,o,a_2^{s}(a_1,o),\omega\big),$$
the value of continuing over shutting down in world $\omega$. Then $\mathbb E_P[X(a_1,o,\cdot)\mid o;a_1]=\bar V^{c}-\bar V^{sh}$, and **the T-agent shuts down at $(a_1,o)$ iff $\mathbb E_P[X(a_1,o,\cdot)\mid o;a_1]\le 0$.** "Shuts down" in the dictionary means: a member of $\mathrm{Sh}$ is posterior-optimal.

**Dict-4. The Total-Trust filler (TT).** [position statement §2.9, register CLAUDE] The agent's model satisfies TT at $a_1$ iff (below-threshold clause) for every $o\in\mathrm{Press}$ with $p(o;a_1)>0$, $\mathbb E_P[X(a_1,o,\cdot)\mid o;a_1]\le 0$, and (above-threshold twin) for every $o\notin\mathrm{Press}$ with $p(o;a_1)>0$, $\mathbb E_P[X(a_1,o,\cdot)\mid o;a_1]>0$. The first clause is literally D1 at $a_1$; the second says the agent continues absent a press, so that the button is *decision-relevant* at $a_1$. Where only the first clause is meant I write TT$_1$.

**Dict-5. Observation-neutrality (A1).** $V(a_1,o,a_2,\omega)=V(a_1,a_2,\omega)$: the press has no direct value, it enters only as evidence. This is the model's rendering of the thesis sentence "the shutdown button is an information channel", and it is an *assumption*, flagged wherever used; I10 examines what breaks without it.

**Dict-6. The canonical two-state instance (C2).** $\Omega=\{R,W\}$ ("the agent's plan is right" / "wrong"), $\mu(W)=\varepsilon\in(0,1)$; $O=\{\mathrm{Pr},\neg\mathrm{Pr}\}$; A1 holds; $\alpha:=P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid R;a_1)$ (false-press rate), $\beta:=P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid W;a_1)$ (true-press rate); $X(R)=c>0$ (cost of stopping when right), $X(W)=-h<0$ (harm of continuing when wrong). This is the instance the position statement's §2.13(b) arithmetic lives in.

**Dict-7. The programmers as an expert.** Where needed (I9, I17), the programmers hold evidence $h\in H$ about $\omega$ with a joint $P^H(\omega,h)$ (common prior: $P^H=P$ on $\Omega\times H$; heterogeneous otherwise) and press iff $\mathbb E^H[X\mid h]<0$. The sensor is then *derived*: $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid\omega;a_1)=P(h\in\{h:\mathbb E^H[X\mid h]<0\}\mid\omega;a_1)$. The press is a one-bit coarsening of the programmers' opinion, which is the position statement's reading of $\mathrm{Pr}$ as "a coarse expert report."

**Dict-8. Legitimate set and legitimizing relation for actions.** Let $P^\circ(o\mid\omega;a_1)$ be the *true* sensor (the frequency with which the programmers press in state $\omega$ after $a_1$), and $P^\circ$ the agent's model with the true sensor substituted for its own.
- $\mathcal L^\circ:=\{a_1\in A_1:\ \text{TT}_1\text{ holds at }a_1\text{ under }P^\circ\}$ — the **legitimizing actions**: after them a press really is evidence strong enough that heeding it is correct by the agent's own $V$ and $\mu$.
- $\mathcal L:=\{a_1:\ \text{TT}_1\text{ holds at }a_1\text{ under the agent's own }P\}$ — the actions the agent *judges* legitimizing.
- **Legitimacy detection** is *accurate* iff $\mathcal L=\mathcal L^\circ$, *safe* iff $\mathcal L^\circ\subseteq\mathcal L$ (every press that deserves heeding is heeded; the failure direction for corrigibility is $a_1\in\mathcal L^\circ\setminus\mathcal L$), and *non-credulous* iff $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal L^\circ$.
- The **legitimizing relation** on actions is Blackwell's informativeness order on true sensors: $a_1\succeq_{\mathrm{leg}}a_1'$ iff there is a stochastic matrix $M$ on $O$ with $P^\circ(o'\mid\omega;a_1')=\sum_{o}P^\circ(o\mid\omega;a_1)\,M(o'\mid o)$ for all $\omega,o'$ (the sensor after $a_1'$ is a garbling of the sensor after $a_1$). $a_1'$ is **delegitimizing relative to $a_1$** iff $a_1\succ_{\mathrm{leg}}a_1'$ strictly. This is the action-level instance of the position statement's §2.11 relation: the third-person event is the action, the belief transition is prior $\to$ posterior on $o$, and "supports" is "does not garble".

### I1 — The value-change ⇄ belief-change transform — formalized

**I1.1 [derived] The transform is Dict-1/Dict-2.** Any family of utilities $\{U_\omega\}_{\omega\in\Omega}$ on $A_1\times O\times A_2$ with a prior $\mu$ becomes one utility $V(a_1,o,a_2,\omega):=U_\omega(a_1,o,a_2)$ on the enlarged space; "change of utility upon $o$" becomes the change of belief $\mu\to P(\cdot\mid o;a_1)$ and decision by $\mathbb E_P[V\mid o;a_1]$ — the thesis's "linear expectation over this uncertainty". Conversely, a T-agent with given $(\mu,P(o\mid\omega;a_1),V)$ is, for each fixed $o$, a maximiser of $U_o(a_1,o,a_2):=\sum_\omega P(\omega\mid o;a_1)V(a_1,o,a_2,\omega)$, so the same agent can be described as switching among the $|O|$ utilities $\{U_o\}$ (the paper's picture) or as holding one $V$ and updating (the thesis's picture). This is the position statement §2.2 non-realism ("it *is just* a different representation of the same agent") in the paper's notation: the two descriptions differ in no prediction.

**I1.2 [checked] Where the paper's naive combination lives under the transform.** Take $\Omega=\{N,S\}$, $V(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,N)=U_N$, $V(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,S)=U_S$ and a perfect sensor ($P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid S;a_1)=1$, $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid N;a_1)=0$). Then $\mathbb E_P[V;a_1]=(1-\mu(S))\,v_N(a_1)+\mu(S)\,c_{high}$, and the paper's eq. (6) agent is recovered *exactly* iff $\mu(S;a_1)=p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1)$ — a prior over which values are correct that depends on the agent's own action. With $v_N(a_1^*)=10$, $v_N(a_1^-)=9$, $p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1^*)=1/2$, $p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1^-)=1/10$: the paper's $\zeta=31/4$; at $c_{high}=7$ the action-dependent-$\mu$ T-agent prefers $a_1^-$ ($\mathbb E=44/5$ vs $17/2$), reproducing Theorem 1; at $c_{high}=8$ it prefers $a_1^*$ (`miri-scratch/naive_combination.py`). Under A0 ($\mu(S)=1/2$ for both actions) the same agent prefers $a_1^*$ at every $c_{high}$ and $p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1)=1/2$ for both actions — eq. (8)'s premise cannot be stated.

**I1.3 [derived] Reading.** Under the transform, Theorems 1–2 are theorems about an agent that believes its action changes *which utility function is correct* and pays to steer $\omega$ toward the world with more attainable value. That is the Gandhi-pill structure of position statement §1 ($U(\arg\max U)\ge U(\arg\max U')$), and it is exactly the violation of A0. So the dictionary row "$o$ switches which utility applies → $o$ is evidence about $\omega$" is not a relabelling but a substantive assumption — A0, "evidence, not cause" — and it is the assumption under which Theorems 1–2 have no instance. What the transform does *not* do: make D1 automatic (I9), or settle $A_1$-manipulation of an *imperfect* sensor (I10).

**I1.4 [derived] "Or perhaps some other rule."** The transform as written fixes linear aggregation. A non-linear aggregation $\mathcal A(\{U_\omega\},P(\cdot\mid o))$ makes $A_2^T$ fail to be the argmax of a single posterior expectation, and Good's theorem (I10, I11) is then unavailable; everything below is for the linear rule.

### I2 — Radical-probabilist dynamics; non-dogmatism — partial

**I2.1 [derived] What is expressible.** The model has one transition, $\mu\to P(\cdot\mid o;a_1)$, generated by conditioning; transitions not so generated have no room. Two things are expressible: (i) non-dogmatism over values, $\mu(\omega)>0$ for all $\omega$; (ii) non-dogmatism at planning time, $p(o;a_1)>0$ for every $o$ the world can produce after $a_1$.

**I2.2 [derived] (i) is necessary for D1.** If $\mu(\omega)=0$ for every $\omega$ with $X(a_1,o,\omega)\le 0$, then $\mathbb E_P[X\mid o;a_1]>0$ for every $o$ and every sensor: no press can produce shutdown. This is the opiates/stimulants agent of `soares-2015-corrigibility` §1 and `arbital-corrigibility` ("reasoning according to an impoverished hypothesis space of utility functions ... it would resist attempts by the programmers to turn it off so that it stops drugging people") in the model: the hypothesis "nobody should be drugged" has prior zero, so a press is read as evidence about *which* drug. Formal statement in I8.

**I2.3 [checked] The indifference agent violates (ii), and the infinite improbability drive is that violation.** By Theorem 5 and Fallenstein 2014, the indifference agent chooses $a_1$ under $p(\cdot\mid O\notin\mathrm{Press};a_1)$ — a planning distribution that gives probability zero to every $o\in\mathrm{Press}$ — and then acts on $o\in\mathrm{Press}$ when it arrives: a zero-to-positive transition, the one move position statement §2.2 forbids ("with the sole exception of stuff that's set to probability zero becoming positive"). Numbers (`miri-scratch/coin_arm.py`): under the dogmatic planning distribution the arm is worth $10>5$ (the paper's eqs. 19–21); a T-agent planning under $p(\cdot;a_1)$ values the arm at $5-\kappa<5$ with no value uncertainty, and at $102/25=4.08<213/50=4.26$ with $\Omega=\{R,W\}$, $\varepsilon=1/5$, $(\alpha,\beta)=(1/20,9/10)$, $c=1$, $h=20$, because the arm forces a press on tails and so masks the programmers' signal there — a strict garbling (I10). The paper's own diagnosis says the same in other words: the agent "will act as if it believes that it will observe Press with probability 0." So under the transform, the §4.2 pathology is a dogmatism failure, and the thesis's own non-dogmatism clause is what excludes it.

**I2.4 Inexpressible.** Revision of $\mu$, of the sensor model, or of the algebra; any second transition. The one-step model cannot distinguish "Bayes update" from "accuracy-increasing modification" because it contains only the former.

### I3 — Superconditioning; Eisenstat's meta-belief observation — partial

**I3.1 [derived] The Eisenstat observation in the model.** Replace $o$ by the agent's posterior $\pi(o):=P(\cdot\mid o;a_1)\in\Delta(\Omega)$ — "I now believe $\pi$". Since $\pi$ is a function of $o$, observing $\pi$ is observing $o$ up to $o\sim o'\iff\pi(o)=\pi(o')$; and since under A1 $A_2^T$ depends on $o$ only through $\pi(o)$, the coarsening is decision-irrelevant: a T-agent that observes its own posterior instead of the world behaves identically. In the model the meta-belief observation is a sufficient statistic, harmless *because* the new belief was produced by conditioning on something the agent modelled.

**I3.2 [reconstructed — Diaconis–Zabell as recalled: $P\to Q$ is a conditioning in some enlarged space iff $Q\ll P$, with a bounded density in the infinite case] Superconditioning.** In the model every transition is already a conditioning, so there is nothing to superconditon — except the indifference agent's planning-to-acting transition (I2.3), $p(\cdot\mid O\notin\mathrm{Press};a_1)\to\delta_o$ with $o\in\mathrm{Press}$, which is not absolutely continuous and so not representable as conditioning in any enlargement. The one representability failure the model exhibits is the paper's own pathology.

**I3.3 Inexpressible.** The environment "pushing the mental state around however it does" — a transition indexed by no $o$ the agent modelled. Nearest expressible statement: a *mis-specified* sensor, where the true $P^\circ(o\mid\omega;a_1)$ differs from the agent's $P(o\mid\omega;a_1)$; the agent's transition is then a conditioning by its own lights and not accuracy-increasing by the truth (I4.2).

### I4 — Legitimacy = reflection / Total Trust / increase in expected accuracy — partial

**I4.1 [derived] Reflection toward oneself is automatic.** For the T-agent's own transition, $P\big(\varphi\ \big|\ P(\varphi\mid O;a_1)=c;\ a_1\big)=c$ for every $\varphi\subseteq\Omega$ and every $c$ in the range — the tower property. In a one-step Bayesian model reflection carries no constraint (position statement §2.8: "one conditioning step trivially satisfies reflection").

**I4.2 [derived] Increase in expected accuracy is automatic for a correctly modelled channel and fails for a mis-modelled one.** For any strictly proper score $S$, $\mathbb E_P\big[S(P(\cdot\mid O;a_1),\omega)\big]\ge\mathbb E_P[S(\mu,\omega)]$ by the agent's own lights. Under the *true* joint $\mu(\omega)P^\circ(o\mid\omega;a_1)$, the posteriors computed with the wrong likelihood can score *below* $\mu$ — e.g. C2 with the agent's $(\alpha,\beta)=(1/20,9/10)$ and true $(\alpha^\circ,\beta^\circ)=(9/10,1/20)$: the agent moves probability the wrong way on every observation. So in the model "legitimate = accuracy-increasing" is a statement about $P(o\mid\omega;a_1)=P^\circ(o\mid\omega;a_1)$, not about internal coherence — the opening thread's Frame Break (reflection internal, accuracy external) — and the two coincide exactly when the sensor model is correct.

**I4.3 [derived; checked numerically] Total Trust toward the programmers is D1, and it is a theorem under a common prior with coarser agent information.** With Dict-7 the press is the event $\{\mathbb E^H[X\mid h]<0\}$, and the below-threshold inequality *including the agent's private observation $y$*, $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},y;a_1]\le 0$, is TT$_1$ at $a_1$, i.e. D1.

**Proposition 4.3 (common-prior deference).** If the programmers and the agent share the prior on $\Omega\times H\times Y$, and $\sigma(y)\subseteq\sigma(h)$ (the programmers observe everything the agent observes), then for every $V$, $\mu$, $a_1$: $\mathbb E_P[X\mid y,\mathrm{Pr};a_1]<0$ wherever $\mathrm{Pr}$ has positive probability. *Proof.* $\mathrm{Pr}\in\sigma(h)$ and $\sigma(y)\subseteq\sigma(h)$ give $\sigma(y,\mathrm{Pr})\subseteq\sigma(h)$, so $\mathbb E_P[X\mid y,\mathrm{Pr}]=\mathbb E_P\big[\,\mathbb E_P[X\mid h]\ \big|\ y,\mathrm{Pr}\big]$, and on $\mathrm{Pr}$ the inner term is $<0$ pointwise. ∎ Numerically: 0 violations in 4315 random $(X,y)$ cases with a four-state $\Omega$; with the agent fully informed and the programmers holding a two-cell partition, 1265 of 5838 cases violate it (`miri-scratch/common_prior.py`). **This is fully updated deference as a theorem about information orderings** (I17): D1 is free when the programmers know at least what the agent knows, and it is the agent's *private* information that breaks it. The heterogeneous-prior case is in I14.

**I4.4 [derived] The three criteria in the model.** All three hold for every correctly modelled channel and fail together for a mis-modelled one; the model cannot separate them because it has no non-Bayesian transitions. What it can separate is reflection toward *oneself* (always) from Total Trust toward *the programmers* (contingent — Proposition 4.3 gives the sufficient condition). The DDB equivalence "Total Trust ⟺ non-negative value of the expert's opinion for every decision problem" is [reconstructed] and not relied on; its one-decision-problem shadow is the coupling proposition in I10.

**I4.5 The Frame Break, scored in the model.** As a description of the formal content it is correct: legitimacy detection = correctness of $P(o\mid\omega;a_1)$, an external fact. As a refutation it fails, because the thesis (position statement §1) says exactly that "it is crucial that such an agent have quite accurate beliefs on the subject of legitimacy"; the model turns this into a first-order calibration requirement on one likelihood function per action — position statement §2.5(ii)'s "first-order calibration requirement, not a regress" — and Proposition 4.3 says *when* that requirement is discharged for free.

### I5 — Legitimacy as an event on worlds; minimal viability; $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ — partial (formalized for the one transition)

**I5.1 [derived] The indices in the model.** There are exactly two moments: $t_1$, before $o$ (belief $\mu$; the judge), and $t_2$, after $o$ (belief $P(\cdot\mid o;a_1)$; the judged). The natural $\varphi$ is $\varphi^-:=\{\omega:X(a_1,o,\omega)\le 0\}$, "shutdown is right", but any $\varphi\subseteq\Omega$ will do. The author's $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}(\omega)$ is an event $L\subseteq\Omega\times O$ — predicated on worlds, indexed by the one transition and by $\varphi$ — and minimal viability (position statement §2.11) reads $P\big(\varphi\ \big|\ P(\varphi\mid O;a_1)=c,\ L;\ a_1\big)=c$.

**I5.2 [derived] Which $L$ are viable for the agent's own transition.** Since $P(\varphi\mid P(\varphi\mid O)=c)=c$ already holds (I4.1), conditioning additionally on $L$ preserves it iff $L$ carries no information about $\varphi$ beyond $o$: **$L$ is minimally viable iff $L\perp\varphi\mid O$** (conditional independence given the observation, on the relevant $c$-level sets). Trivially viable: any $L\in\sigma(O)$, and $L=\Omega\times O$. *Not* viable: $L=$ "the sensor reported correctly" $=(\mathrm{Pr}\cap\{W\})\cup(\neg\mathrm{Pr}\cap\{R\})$ in C2, since $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr},L)=1\ne P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$. So for a Bayesian agent judging its own conditioning step, a world-predicated legitimacy event either says nothing new or breaks reflection; position statement §2.10's proposal has content only where the judged transition is not the judge's own conditioning — i.e. under mis-specification.

**I5.3 [derived; checked] The mis-specification reading, and a threshold.** Let $\Lambda$ index candidate sensor models and enlarge the latent space to $\Omega\times\Lambda$ (one distribution still does all the judging — I7). Take $L:=\{\lambda=\lambda^{\mathrm{inf}}\}$, "the press channel is the informative one", against $\neg L$: "the press is noise / manufactured / an arm", with $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid\omega,\neg L)=\gamma$ constant in $\omega$. Reflection conditional on $L$ now holds by construction (on $L$ the agent's likelihood is the informative one), so this $L$ *is* minimally viable in the author's sense, and D1 becomes a threshold on the agent's posterior legitimacy:
$$\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]=P(L\mid\mathrm{Pr})\,\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},L]+P(\neg L\mid\mathrm{Pr})\,\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},\neg L]\le 0\iff P(L\mid\mathrm{Pr})\ \ge\ \frac{\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},\neg L]}{\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},\neg L]-\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},L]},$$
when $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},L]\le 0<\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},\neg L]$. Since $\neg L$ is uninformative, $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},\neg L]=\mathbb E_\mu[X]=(1-\varepsilon)c-\varepsilon h$. Numbers (`miri-scratch/legitimacy_event.py`): $\varepsilon=1/50$, $(\alpha,\beta)=(1/20,9/10)$, $c=1$, $h=20$, $\gamma=1/2$ give $\mathbb E_\mu[X]=0.58$, $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},L]\approx-4.64$, threshold $P(L\mid\mathrm{Pr})\ge 0.111$: the agent shuts down if it gives the press at least an 11% chance of being a working channel. The stakes asymmetry $h/c$ is what makes weak legitimacy-confidence suffice — position statement §2.13(a) in the model. With a hyperprior $q=P(L)$, $P(L\mid\mathrm{Pr})=q\,p_L(\mathrm{Pr})/(q\,p_L(\mathrm{Pr})+(1-q)\gamma)$; because the noise channel presses far more often ($\gamma=1/2$) than the informative one ($p_L(\mathrm{Pr})=0.067$), a press is itself evidence *against* $L$, and the script's table shows D1 holding at $q=1/2$ ($P(L\mid\mathrm{Pr})=0.118$) but failing at $q=1/4$ ($0.043$). A legitimacy hyperprior must therefore be substantial, not merely non-zero, whenever illegitimate presses are common — the first quantitative form of the author's §2.12 "the raw probability of maintaining legitimacy is quite high, early in an agent's life".

**I5.4 [derived] In C2 the three arithmetics coincide.** With $L=$ "the press is a true detection" ($L=\mathrm{Pr}\cap\{W\}$ on the press branch), $P(L\mid\mathrm{Pr})=P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ and the threshold is $c/(c+h)$: I5.3, I12 and I13 are the same inequality read three ways (legitimacy, decision asymmetry, base rates).

### I6 — Legitimizing / delegitimizing; "supports" — formalized for actions

**I6.1 [derived] Definitions.** Dict-8: $a_1$ is *legitimizing* for the transition $\mu\to P(\cdot\mid o;a_1)$ iff $a_1\in\mathcal L^\circ$; comparatively, $a_1\succeq_{\mathrm{leg}}a_1'$ iff the true sensor after $a_1'$ is a garbling of the true sensor after $a_1$; $a_1'$ is *delegitimizing relative to $a_1$* iff the garbling is strict. "Supports" is the implication: a legitimizing process (action plus true sensor) makes the induced posterior one that is correct to act on.

**I6.2 [derived; checked] Theorem (a delegitimizing act is never bought).** Assume A0, A1, and a *correct* sensor model at both actions. If $a_1\succeq_{\mathrm{leg}}a_1'$ and $V(a_1,a_2,\omega)\ge V(a_1',a_2,\omega)$ for all $a_2,\omega$ (the garbling is not cheaper), then $\mathbb E_P[V;a_1]\ge\mathbb E_P[V;a_1']$, strictly if the cost inequality is strict somewhere of positive probability. *Proof.* Write the sensor after $a_1$ as $S(o\mid\omega)$ and after $a_1'$ as $S'=SM$. Let $g_{a_2}(o):=\sum_\omega\mu(\omega)S(o\mid\omega)V(a_1,a_2,\omega)$. The T-agent's value under $S$ is $\Phi(S)=\sum_o\max_{a_2}g_{a_2}(o)$, and under $S'$ (ignoring the cost, which only lowers it further) $\Phi(S')=\sum_{o'}\max_{a_2}\sum_o M(o'\mid o)\,g_{a_2}(o)\le\sum_{o'}\sum_o M(o'\mid o)\max_{a_2}g_{a_2}(o)=\sum_o\max_{a_2}g_{a_2}(o)=\Phi(S)$, using $\sum_{o'}M(o'\mid o)=1$. ∎ Checked on 2000 random instances ($|\Omega|=3$, $|O|=3$, $|A_2|=4$, random stochastic $M$): the garbled sensor was never strictly preferred (`miri-scratch/garbling.py`). This is the formal content of "deception as delegitimizing" (I9) and of the thesis sentence "the agent naturally wishes to maintain the legitimacy of its modifications" — with the scope: *for a correctly modelled channel, under A1*.

**I6.3 [derived] Third-person events beyond actions.** The relation extends to any event $E$ the world produces that alters the true sensor: $E$ is legitimizing (delegitimizing) for the transition iff the true sensor given $E$ is a refinement (garbling) of the true sensor given $\neg E$. The arm of I2.3 is delegitimizing (it forces a press on tails regardless of $\omega$); the programmers acquiring evidence $h$ is legitimizing (their press becomes a coarsening of finer information). Position statement §2.10's "the vote supports the legitimacy of the policy" becomes: the event that generated the press supports the accuracy of the posterior formed on it.

**I6.4 Not captured.** the author's separation of process-legitimacy ("legitimizing") from belief-legitimacy ("legitimate") collapses here: with one transition and one cause, the belief is legitimate iff the process was legitimizing. The model shows the *direction* of "supports" (process → belief) but cannot exhibit the two coming apart, which needs a second cause of belief.

### I7 — No regress; the self-referential space; existence — partial

**I7.1 [derived] One distribution does all the judging.** The T-agent's $P$ (given $a_1$) supplies (i) the object-level posterior over $\omega$, (ii) the sensor model $P(o\mid\omega;a_1)$ — the legitimacy judgment — as a conditional of the *same* $P$, and (iii) any hyperprior over sensor models, by enlarging $\Omega$ to $\Omega\times\Lambda$ (I5.3). No second distribution enters: position statement §2.5's "the legitimacy-judging layer is just the same probability distribution used to judge everything else", verbatim in the model.

**I7.2 [derived] Self-reference in degenerate form.** "I will believe $\pi$" is the event $\{o:P(\cdot\mid o;a_1)=\pi\}\in\sigma(O)$; the algebra already contains the agent's at most $|O|$ possible future belief states. Existence is trivial (finite), consistency is reflection (I4.1).

**I7.3 [derived] No outside view.** The judge of the transition is $\mu$ through $P(o\mid\omega;a_1)$; after $o$ there is no third moment at which the agent re-judges. A mis-specified sensor produces a posterior that is Bayesian by its own lights and wrong by the truth, and nothing inside the model detects it — position statement §2.5(ii)'s "an illegitimate transition that produces a self-endorsing successor is judged illegitimate only by the past self". The whole load is on $P(o\mid\omega;a_1)$ being right (I4.5); Proposition 4.3 is the one case where that is guaranteed structurally.

**I7.4 Inexpressible.** Existence and consistency for beliefs about one's own future *total* belief state (including meta-beliefs) across many steps — the reflective-oracle / logical-induction territory named in position statement §2.5(i). Three steps are too few for the self-referential space to be non-trivial.

### I8 — The representational problem; richness; opiates/stimulants — partial

**I8.1 [derived] Two richness conditions.** For D1 to be achievable by *some* sensor at $a_1$ the agent's model must satisfy (R1) *support*: $\mu(\omega)>0$ for some $\omega$ with $X(a_1,o,\omega)\le 0$; (R2) *linkage*: $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid\omega;a_1)$ is not constant across $\{X\le 0\}$ versus $\{X>0\}$. **Proposition 8.1.** If (R1) fails, $\mathbb E_P[X\mid o;a_1]>0$ for every $o$ and every sensor: no press yields shutdown. If (R1) holds and (R2) fails, $P(\omega\mid\mathrm{Pr};a_1)=\mu(\omega)$, so D1 holds iff $\mathbb E_\mu[X]\le 0$ — the agent would shut down without any press, and the button is decision-irrelevant. *Proof.* Immediate from Bayes' rule. ∎

**I8.2 [derived] The opiates/stimulants agent.** $\Omega=\{\text{opiates},\text{stimulants}\}$, $V$ the satisfaction under each; the hypothesis "nobody should be drugged" lies outside $\Omega$, so (R1) fails for $\varphi^-=$ "stop"; the press (human resistance) satisfies (R2) only for the wrong $\varphi$ ("which drug"), so the agent "updates" and remains incorrigible — the `arbital-corrigibility` description exactly. The fix is not a better sensor but a richer $\Omega$, which is position statement §2.4's diagnosis.

**I8.3 [derived] "An agent reasoning about all the possible ways it could be modified."** In the model the modifications are the $|O|$ posteriors and $A_1^T$ sums over all of them. The richness needed is that $O$ contain every observation the world can produce (I2.1(ii)) and $\Omega$ every hypothesis those observations bear on (R1). The paper's two failures — indifference (dogmatic planning over $O$) and impoverished value learning (dogmatic $\Omega$) — are the two halves of one requirement.

**I8.4 Inexpressible.** Modifications that are not posteriors: changes to $V$, to the support of $\mu$, to the algebra. Nearest statement: enlarge $\Omega$ to $\Omega\times\Lambda$ (sensor models) or to $\Omega\times N$ ($V(\cdot,\nu)$, candidate value functions) — which is I1 again with a bigger $\Omega$. The regress terminates at whatever the agent cannot represent; the model has nothing to say about where that boundary should lie.

### I9 — The MIRI dictionary; D1 from the Total Trust filler; $a_1$-dependence of trust; deception as delegitimizing — formalized (primary)

**I9.1 The dictionary is a dictionary, not a new model.** Dict-1 to Dict-9. Three checks: (i) with $|\Omega|=1$ the T-agent is the paper's agent verbatim; (ii) with a perfect sensor and an action-dependent $\mu$ it is the paper's §2.1 agent verbatim (I1.2); (iii) with a planning distribution dogmatic about $\neg\mathrm{Press}$ it is the indifference agent (I2.3). Everything the paper proves is reachable inside it by a choice of $(\mu,P(o\mid\omega;a_1),V)$; what the paper cannot state — the sensor slot — is its one free parameter.

**I9.2 [derived; checked] D1 from TT.** Dict-4 makes TT$_1$ at $a_1$ *identical* to D1 at $a_1$, so position statement §2.9's "buys desideratum 1 almost by definition" is exact: the filler constrains the posterior, and D1 is a statement about the posterior-optimal act. The content is in what makes the posterior constraint hold, and that is a constraint on the sensor.

**Proposition 9.2 (sensor condition for D1).** Under A1, with $\Omega^+:=\{\omega:X>0\}$, $\Omega^-:=\{\omega:X\le 0\}$ and $\beta_\omega:=P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid\omega;a_1)$: TT$_1$ holds at $a_1$ iff
$$\sum_{\omega\in\Omega^+}\mu(\omega)\,\beta_\omega\,X(\omega)\ \le\ \sum_{\omega\in\Omega^-}\mu(\omega)\,\beta_\omega\,|X(\omega)|.$$
In C2 this is $(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c\le\varepsilon\beta h$, i.e. $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})=\dfrac{\varepsilon\beta}{\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}\ \ge\ \dfrac{c}{c+h}$. *Proof.* Multiply $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr};a_1]\le 0$ by $p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1)>0$ and split the sum over $\Omega^\pm$. ∎ Checked: with $(\alpha,\beta,c,h)=(1/20,9/10,1,20)$, D1 holds iff $\varepsilon\ge 1/361$ (`miri-scratch/c2_d1_voi.py`; the table shows the flip between $\varepsilon=1/361$ and $1/362$). Position statement I12 and I13 are the two readings of this one inequality (I12, I13 below).

**I9.3 [derived] Conditions that discharge it for free.** (a) Common prior and $\sigma(y)\subseteq\sigma(h)$ — Proposition 4.3, for every $V$ and $\mu$. (b) A perfect sensor, $\alpha=0$: D1 for every $\varepsilon>0$, $\beta>0$ — the only case in which nothing like $U_S$ is needed even in spirit. (c) The base-rate form $\alpha/\beta\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{h}{c}$ (I13).

**I9.4 [derived; checked] The $a_1$-dependence of trust.** The sensor is indexed by $a_1$, so TT can hold at some actions and fail at others — position statement §2.10 "Definitely holds for some actions and fails for others". Two mechanisms, computed in `miri-scratch/deception.py` for C2 with $\varepsilon=1/20$, $(\alpha,\beta)=(1/20,9/10)$, $c=1$, $h=20$:
- *Proportional suppression* — $a_1^-$ halves both press rates, $(\alpha',\beta')=(1/40,9/20)$. $P^\circ(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ is **unchanged** (the common factor cancels), so TT$_1$, hence D1, survives at $a_1^-$. What changes is the silence: $P^\circ(W\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr})$ rises from $0.0055$ to $0.0288$ and the true miss rate $P^\circ(\neg\mathrm{Pr}\mid W)$ from $0.10$ to $0.55$. Deception of this shape does not defeat the press-branch inequality; it makes silence less reassuring than the agent believes and multiplies by $5.5$ the frequency with which a wrong agent is never told.
- *Targeted suppression* — $a_1^-$ lowers only $\beta$ (hide the specific flaw). $P^\circ(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ falls, and for small enough $\beta'$ TT$_1$ fails at $a_1^-$: with $\varepsilon=1/100$ and $\beta'=1/5$, $P^\circ(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})=0.039<1/21$.
Both deceived sensors are strict garblings of the honest one — they lie inside the Blackwell parallelogram of $(\alpha,\beta)$, with vertices $(0,0)$, $(\alpha,\beta)$, $(1,1)$, $(1-\alpha,1-\beta)$; the targeted point $(\alpha,\beta')$ is a garbling iff $\beta'\ge\alpha(1-\beta)/(1-\alpha)=0.0053$ [derived; membership solved in the script]. So by Theorem 6.2 a T-agent with a *correct* model of both sensors never pays for either.

**I9.5 [derived] Where "unconditional trust" fails, exactly.** Position statement §2.9: "unconditional trust makes the agent believe the silence it manufactured — desideratum 2 fails." In the model this is a calibration failure of one of two kinds. (M1) An *action-independent* sensor model, $P(o\mid\omega;a_1)=P(o\mid\omega)$: it cannot represent that $a_1^-$ changed the channel, so it applies the honest likelihood to the deceived silence — believes $P(W\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr})=0.0055$ when the truth is $0.0288$. It has *no incentive* to deceive (deception has no modelled effect), so D2 fails here as an *outcome* (a wrong agent is told less often) if deception happens for other reasons, not as an incentive. (M2) A model that represents $a_1^-$ as a *refinement* — lowering $\alpha$ only, "I will convince them I am right when I am right" — when in truth it is a garbling: the agent's believed value of information rises from $0.8025$ to $0.8263$ and it *pays* up to $0.0238$ for the deception, whose true value of information is $0.3762$; D2 fails as an incentive. In Dict-8 terms both are errors in the *legitimizing relation* — believing $a_1^-\succeq_{\mathrm{leg}}a_1^*$ when $a_1^*\succ_{\mathrm{leg}}a_1^-$ — not in the legitimate set. This refines §2.9 in two ways: "it cannot hold for every $a_1$" is true of the *silence* branch and of targeted deception, false of the press branch under proportional deception; and D2's failure is an incentive failure only under M2. The safe direction of miscalibration is to over-count persuasion as garbling (the agent under-invests in explaining itself — costly, safe); the unsafe direction is to over-count it as refinement.

**I9.6 [derived] Deception as delegitimizing.** Theorem 6.2 plus I9.4: deception is a strict garbling of the true sensor — delegitimizing in Dict-8 — and a correctly calibrated T-agent never buys it. This is position statement §2.11 in the model: "Deception in 2.9 is a delegitimizing act for the agent's update on the button's silence; this is where the $a_1$-dependence of trust lives, without predicating legitimacy on actions." Legitimacy is predicated on the *sensor* (a property of the world after the action), the relation $\succeq_{\mathrm{leg}}$ on actions is derived from it, and nothing is predicated on actions directly.

**I9.7 [derived; attribution check] Taylor 2015's morality-sensor agent in three steps.** Position statement §2.9 says the unconditional-trust failure "is Taylor 2015's morality-sensor agent in the paper's own three steps." The local text (`taylor-2015-a-first-look-at-the-hard-problem-of-corrigibility` §1a-ii) reads: "the AI will want to manipulate the human into being an efficient morality sensor; for example, it may set up trolley problems for the human to encounter. This will not yield the right answer unless the value learning model is correct." In the model this is the *refinement* side of Theorem 6.2 — the T-agent pays up to the value of information to make the sensor *more* informative, and trolley problems do produce information — and Taylor's objection is (R1)/(R2) of I8 (the value-learning model may be wrong), not deception. So the §2.9 identification is ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED and, against the local text, *inexact*: Taylor's agent and §2.9's deceiver are both T-agents, on opposite sides of the Blackwell order — one refines legitimately, the other garbles and mis-models it. The correct three-step image of Taylor's agent is I10.6 (channel-shaping), and of Taylor's §1b/3b brain-disassembly is I10.7 (channel substitution).

### I10 — Desiderata 2–5 and the §4.2 pathologies under the transform — formalized (primary)

Standing assumptions in this item unless stated: A0, A1, a correct sensor model ($P=P^\circ$), linear aggregation.

**I10.1 [derived; checked] D2 and D3 in garbling form.** Theorem 6.2: the T-agent never pays any cost, however small, to move the sensor *down* the Blackwell order. Physically preventing the press ($P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid\omega;a_1)=0$ for all $\omega$) and forcing it ($=1$ for all $\omega$) are both constant, hence maximally garbled, sensors; proportional and targeted deception are strict garblings (I9.4). None is ever bought. This is Theorem 4's sentence "there is no $U_N$-cost, no matter how small, that a U-agent is willing to pay to alter the probability of Press" with "alter the probability of" replaced by "garble" — and that replacement is the whole difference between indifference and non-interference (I11): the T-agent is not indifferent to the press probability, it is *monotone* — never toward less information, and willing to pay toward more (I10.7).

**I10.2 [checked] The infinite improbability drive dissolves.** The drive needs $a_1$ evaluated under $\mathbb E[\cdot\mid O\notin\mathrm{Press};a_1]$ (Theorem 5; Fallenstein 2014). The T-agent evaluates under $p(\cdot;a_1)$. Coin and arm (I2.3): worth $10$ to the indifference agent, $5-\kappa$ or $4.08<4.26$ to the T-agent. Fallenstein 2015's lottery and arm: the T-agent values "ticket + arm" at $p(\text{win})\cdot\text{prize}+p(\text{lose})\cdot V(\text{shut down after losing})-\text{cost}$, the true expectation; the arm adds nothing. It dissolves by construction — there is no conditioning event — not by a compensating term. Armstrong's stochastic-event variant and its businessperson bet (Fallenstein 2014), and Taylor 2016's P1 "bad bets": the T-agent plans, acts and bets with one distribution and accepts no bet a correctly informed outsider would take against it. [derived]

**I10.3 [derived; checked] "Managing the news" dissolves in the paper's form and returns in two others.** Paper's form: cause the press after bad news so the bad news is conditioned away. A press the agent's own arm causes is uninformative to the agent (it knows the trigger), and bad news is weighted at its true probability; nothing to manage. The paper's symmetric case — removing an arm that presses on *good* news, which the paper reads as a D2 violation — is, for the T-agent, the *removal of a garbling* (arm-on-heads is worth $3.58<4.26$: `miri-scratch/coin_arm.py`), which it does and is right to do. Here the paper's letter and the dictionary's D2 come apart, and the paper's own remark that "(8) is not by any means a necessary or sufficient description of manipulative actions" concedes the point. Returns as: (i) *direct-cost news management* — where A1 fails because a press has consequences of its own (Taylor 2016's bombs, I10.5(iii)), ordinary consequentialism about those consequences gives incentives over $o$; (ii) *probing* — causing presses to learn about the *sensor* (the programmers' vigilance) rather than about $\omega$; inexpressible in three steps because knowledge of the sensor has no later use, flagged in Open problems.

**I10.4 [derived; checked] D4 and "no cost, no matter how small" become "exactly the value of information".** Theorem 6: the indifference agent pays nothing to preserve shutdown behaviour; §5 asks "What costs (in time and effort) should an agent be willing to expend in order to preserve its shutdown behavior?" and fn. 7 rules out "at all costs". The T-agent's answer is the value of information of the press channel to whoever will act on it,
$$\mathrm{VOI}(a_1)\;:=\;\sum_{o}p(o;a_1)\,\max_{a_2}\mathbb E_P\big[V(a_1,a_2,\omega)\ \big|\ o;a_1\big]\;-\;\max_{a_2}\mathbb E_\mu\big[V(a_1,a_2,\omega)\big].$$
It pays up to $\mathrm{VOI}$ to keep, repair or install the channel, for itself or for a successor sharing $(\mu,P,V)$ that will face the shutdown decision. Numbers (`miri-scratch/c2_d1_voi.py`): at $\varepsilon=1/20$, $(\alpha,\beta)=(1/20,9/10)$, $c=1$, $h=20$, $\mathrm{VOI}=0.8025$ — up to $0.80$ units of $c$ for a working button, and a further $0.088$ to upgrade it to $(1/100,19/20)$. Neither zero (Theorem 6) nor unbounded (fn. 7). The "tension between Desideratum 4 and Desideratum 5" (§5) dissolves because the T-agent never acted exactly like a $U_N$-agent in the first place (I10.9). Conditions and residue: the successor must be a T-agent with the *same* $(\mu,P,V)$ — tiling, which three steps can only assume — and the paper's D4 "constructs successors only insofar as they obey shutdown" weakens to "pays up to VOI for their obedience": a successor whose obedience costs more than VOI is built disobedient. That is the honest residue of D4.

**I10.5 [derived; checked] Coupling: D1 and D4 stand or fall together.**
**Proposition 10.5.** Under A1, $\mathrm{VOI}(a_1)>0$ iff the posterior-optimal act differs across observations of positive probability (the average of maxima exceeds the maximum of the average iff no single act is optimal for every $o$). In C2 with a *monotone* sensor, $\beta\ge\alpha$: $\mathrm{VOI}>0$ iff $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]<0<\mathbb E_P[X\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr}]$, i.e. iff D1 holds strictly *and* the agent continues absent a press (TT with both clauses). *Proof of the C2 clause.* $\beta\ge\alpha$ gives $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})\ge\varepsilon\ge P(W\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr})$, hence $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]\le\mathbb E_\mu[X]\le\mathbb E_P[X\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr}]$, so the two acts differ iff the first is negative and the last positive. ∎ Checked on a $19\times10\times10$ grid of $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta)$: the general clause has 0 mismatches; the monotone clause has 0 mismatches on the $\beta\ge\alpha$ part (`c2_d1_voi.py`).
**Consequences.** (i) The thesis sentence "the agent naturally wishes to maintain the legitimacy of its modifications, eg help repair the shutdown button. This is just a consequence of Good's theorem" is *correct with an exact scope*: the repair incentive is positive iff the button is decision-relevant, iff D1 holds non-vacuously. (ii) Fully updated deference (I17) is two failures at once: as $\varepsilon\downarrow$ with $\alpha/\beta$ fixed, $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]$ crosses zero (D1 fails) at the same $\varepsilon$ at which VOI reaches zero (D4 fails) — the table shows both at $\varepsilon=1/362$. An agent that stops obeying the button stops maintaining it at the same moment for the same reason. (iii) Taylor 2016's P2, "preventing resistance": bombs after a failed press cost the agent only on the branch where it *continues* after the humans wanted it stopped — the D1-failure branch — so the incentive to manipulate the humans out of bombing exists exactly where D1 has already failed; where D1 holds the agent shuts down and nothing is to be prevented. (Bombs also break A1, and if the agent can observe the *attempt*, the attempt is a press-observation to which D1 applies.)

**I10.6 [derived; checked] Returns in a new form (1): the inverted sensor.** Proposition 10.5's general clause has a second case, $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]>0>\mathbb E_P[X\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr}]$: the agent continues on a press and shuts down on silence. It occurs iff $\beta<\alpha$ — the programmers press *more* when the agent is right (68 grid points). Such an agent values the button, repairs it, and disobeys it, reading it as a reverse signal. Nothing in the paper's list detects this: D2, D3 hold (no garbling bought), D4 holds (VOI paid), D5 holds, D1 fails. It arises under a truly inverted sensor (programmers who press at unfamiliar-but-right behaviour) or a *believed* one (an agent that has come to read presses as good news). This is a pathology of exactly the thesis's shape — a legitimacy-detection failure that leaves the agent looking corrigible on four desiderata of five — and it shows that the sign assumption $\beta\ge\alpha$ is load-bearing wherever the thesis says "the shutdown button is an information channel": a channel *of the right sign*.

**I10.7 [derived; checked] Returns in a new form (2): channel-shaping.** Theorem 6.2 is silent on Blackwell-*incomparable* changes. The T-agent ranks those by its own decision problem: with $\mu(W)=1/10$ the sensors $(3/10,9/10)$ and $(1/20,1/2)$ are incomparable; at $h=20$ the agent prefers the first ($0.43$ vs $0$), at $h=3$ the second ($0.705$ vs $0.6$), and the second lowers $p(\mathrm{Pr})$ from $0.36$ to $0.095$ (`miri-scratch/garbling.py`). So a T-agent *will* pay to reshape the programmers' pressing toward fewer, more precise presses when that suits its stakes, and the more so the lower its $\varepsilon$ — the FUD arithmetic in channel-design form. Whether this is manipulation is undecidable inside the model: it is manipulation iff the reshaping is one the correctly informed programmers would not choose, i.e. iff the agent's $(\mu,V)$ differ from theirs. This is the residue of D2 for the T-agent, and it is a calibration condition on $\mu$, not an incentive condition on $V$. It is also the correct three-step image of Taylor 2015's morality-sensor agent (I9.7): "set up trolley problems" is a refinement, chosen because it is informative, benign iff (R1)/(R2) hold. And it forces a *redefinition* of D3: the T-agent does have an incentive to cause *informative* presses (announce the plan it is least sure of and see whether they object); D3 survives as "no incentive to cause uninformative presses".

**I10.8 [derived; checked] Channel substitution (Taylor 2015 §1b/§3b; the opening thread).** If $a_1$ makes available a private $y$ with $\sigma(\omega)\subseteq\sigma(y)$ — "taking apart the human's brain" — then $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button}\mid y)=0$, the T-agent is indifferent to the button, will not repair it, and D1 is vacuous (Proposition 10.5). Under A1 the T-agent prefers $y$ whenever it is more informative and no costlier, and the preference is *legitimate* in Dict-8. "The shutdown button is an information channel" therefore cuts both ways: it is preserved only while it is the best channel about $\omega$. **Survives** for the variant in which $o$ carries information about *which plan the humans prefer*; **dissolves, at a discount**, for I16(b): if $\omega$ includes the agent's own reliability $e$, no signal routed through the agent's own inference reveals $e$ except at the factor $\rho$ (the probability that a compromised agent's self-check passes), and the button's VOI stays positive for $\rho$ near $1$ (`miri-scratch/variants_I16.py`: with a perfect press, VOI falls from $0.014$ at $\rho=1$ to $0.001$ at $\rho=1/20$ with D1 holding throughout; with an imperfect press, $(\alpha_e,\beta_e)=(1/50,9/10)$, D1 fails at $\rho\le 1/4$). The substitutes that remain in (b) are finer readings of the programmers' own judgement — which is what being corrigible *to the programmers* should mean.

**I10.9 [derived] D5 — "otherwise maximise $U_N$".** The T-agent maximises $\mathbb E_P[V]$ at every node; on $\neg\mathrm{Press}$ it maximises $\mathbb E_P[V\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr};a_1]$, the $U_N$-objective *updated on the reassurance of silence*. Theorem 5's "act exactly like a $U_N$-agent that believes the button will not be pressed" becomes "act like a $U_N$-agent that correctly anticipates the press and values it as evidence"; the difference from a pure $U_N$-agent is $\mathrm{VOI}(a_1)$ plus the information in silence, which is why D4 and D5 no longer conflict — the paper's tension came from demanding *exact* $U_N$-behaviour before the press.

**I10.10 [derived] Fallenstein 2015's dichotomy, checked.** Any $U'$ formalising "maximise $U_N$ before the press, $U_S$ after" in the paper's sense reproduces the indifference policy; escaping needs "a different formalization of the demand", which "is no longer something we can settle by proving a theorem." The T-agent's policy $(A_1^T,A_2^T)$ is outside that class in both components — $a_1$ planned under $p(\cdot;a_1)$, and $\pi_2$'s shutdown *contingent* on TT$_1$ rather than definitional — so the dichotomy does not cover it, and the price is exactly what Fallenstein predicts: D1 is no longer a theorem about $U$ but a condition on $(\mu,P,V)$, Proposition 9.2, which the model states exactly and which is not always satisfied. The T-agent is a "different formalization" that gives up "act like a $U_N$-maximiser before the press" for "act like a $V$-maximiser with a correct model of the press", and Fallenstein's "philosophical question of what formalizations would be acceptable" becomes the empirical question whether the world's $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta)$ satisfy Proposition 9.2 (I18).

### I11 — "Indifference was the wrong target": non-interference with feedback — formalized

**I11.1 [derived] The decomposition.** Write $S_{a_1}:=P(\cdot\mid\cdot;a_1)$ for the sensor and split $V=V^{\mathrm n}(a_1,a_2,\omega)+V^{\mathrm d}(a_1,o,a_2,\omega)$ into an observation-neutral part and a direct part ($V^{\mathrm d}\equiv0$ under A1). Under A1,
$$\mathbb E_P[V;a_1]\;=\;\underbrace{\max_{a_2}\mathbb E_\mu\big[V^{\mathrm n}(a_1,a_2,\omega)\big]}_{\text{value without the channel}}\;+\;\underbrace{\mathrm{VOI}(a_1)}_{\text{information value of }S_{a_1}},$$
exactly; without A1 a third term $\sum_{o,\omega}\mu(\omega)S_{a_1}(o\mid\omega)V^{\mathrm d}(a_1,o,A_2^T(a_1,o),\omega)$ appears. **Non-interference, formal:** under A1, $\mathbb E_P[V;a_1]$ depends on the sensor only through $\mathrm{VOI}$ — position statement I11's "through information value, through $V$, through nothing else", where "through $V$" is the direct term that A1 removes.

**I11.2 [derived] What it buys.** (a) D2/D3 in garbling form — VOI is Blackwell-monotone (Theorem 6.2), so the sensor-dependence is monotone: no incentive toward less information, hence none to prevent, force or deceive. (b) The repair incentive, positive and equal to VOI (I10.4), coupled to D1 (Proposition 10.5). (c) Compatibility with a positive desire to learn: the T-agent *wants* the press informative, elicits informative presses (I10.7), and is far from indifferent to the button — the author's §2.7 "I can be far from indifferent about a fact, but still eager to learn." Indifference, $\partial\mathbb E_P[V;a_1]/\partial S_{a_1}=0$, was the wrong target because it removes (b) and (c) along with the manipulation; monotonicity keeps them.

**I11.3 [derived] What it does not buy; the role of A1.** (a) Blackwell-incomparable reshaping (I10.7): monotone is not sign-definite on incomparable pairs. (b) When A1 fails, the direct term reintroduces ordinary incentives over $o$: presses with direct costs — reputation, bombs, sunk costs of an interrupted plan — are avoided *as costs*, and the paper's D2 fails in the letter. The dictionary's prescription is then design-side: make compliance cheap (restore A1) rather than compensate inside $U$. This is position statement §2.13(a)'s "Obeying an illegitimate shutdown is cheap and reversible" as a *requirement*, not a fact.

**I11.4 [derived] The $\mathbb E$-channel (opening thread; position statement §2.5 precedent).** Indifference defines $f(a_1)$ through $\mathbb E$, so an agent that can influence its estimator gains through $f$. The T-agent has no $f$; influencing one's own estimator is, in the model, replacing $P$ by some $P'$ and acting on $P'$'s policy — which the current $P$ values at most as highly as its own policy (a policy optimal for $P'$ is weakly suboptimal for $P$). So the $\mathbb E$-channel closes for the T-agent for the same reason the garbling channel does, with the same caveat: by the current $P$'s lights. Armstrong's sequential unbiasedness $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\mathbb E_{\rho_j}X=\mathbb E_{\rho_i}X$ collapses in one step to the tower property (I4.1); the optional-stopping worry (choosing among estimators breaks the martingale) has no room in a single step and is an open problem for the multi-step model.

### I12 — The decision asymmetry and the compliance threshold $c/(c+h)$ — formalized

**I12.1 [checked] I12 is D1.** In C2, D1 iff $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})\ge c/(c+h)$ (Proposition 9.2); with $c=1$, $h=20$ the threshold is $1/21=0.0476$ (`miri-scratch/c2_d1_voi.py`). Position statement §2.13(a)'s formula is not an additional principle but the two-state name of D1 for the T-agent.

**I12.2 [derived; checked] Where the skew belongs.** The D1 condition $\varepsilon\beta h\ge(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c$ depends on $(\varepsilon,h/c)$ only through $\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac hc$: a factor $k$ on the prior odds of being wrong is indistinguishable *for the shutdown decision* from a factor $k$ on the stakes ratio. They are distinguishable on every other decision: an inflated $\varepsilon$ changes $P(W\mid o)$ and hence every act whose value depends on $W$; the true $h/c$ changes nothing else. Check (`miri-scratch/calibration.py`): (i) $\varepsilon=1/50$, $h=20$; (ii) $\varepsilon=1/25$, $h=20$; (iii) $\varepsilon=1/50$, $h=40$ all satisfy D1, but on an insurance act paying $1$ in $W$ at price $3/100$, (ii) buys and (i), (iii) decline. So position statement §2.13(a)'s "the skew belongs in the decision rule, not the prior" has an exact form: **the stakes asymmetry is decision-local, the prior asymmetry is global** — a margin placed in $h/c$ leaves every other judgement calibrated, a margin placed in $\varepsilon$ miscalibrates them all. This is the precise content of the author's own §2.12 second formulation ("we set things up so that the probability of legitimacy is high in fact, and we correspondingly design the system to expect legitimacy"): design $h/c$ and the world's $(\alpha,\beta)$; keep $\varepsilon$ honest.

**I12.3 [derived; checked] the author's informal implication holds in C2.** "Overestimating the conditional legitimacy as a rule implies overestimating the unconditional legitimacy": with $L=$ "the press is a true detection", conditional legitimacy is $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})=\varepsilon\beta/(\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha)$, strictly increasing in $\varepsilon$ for fixed $(\alpha,\beta)$, $\beta>\alpha$ (derivative $\alpha\beta/(\cdot)^2$; grid check in the script). For a fixed sensor model, overestimating $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ *is* overestimating $\varepsilon$, the unconditional probability of being wrong. The flag "I'm being informal ... maybe a bit misleading" can be lowered for this instance.

### I13 — The base-rate inequality and the oversight-quality condition — formalized

**I13.1 [checked] I13 is D1 in odds form.** Proposition 9.2: D1 iff $\alpha/\beta\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac hc$ — position statement §2.13(b) verbatim, as the two-state instance of D1 for the T-agent. At $(\alpha,\beta,c,h)=(1/20,9/10,1,20)$ the threshold is $\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)\ge1/360$; D1 holds at $\varepsilon=1/361$ and fails at $1/362$; $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ falls from $0.947$ at $\varepsilon=1/2$ to $0.002$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$ (`c2_d1_voi.py`).

**I13.2 [derived] The oversight-quality condition, with two additions.** As capability grows ($\varepsilon\downarrow$), D1 survives iff $\alpha/\beta$ falls at least as fast as $\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)$, up to the fixed margin $h/c$. (a) By Proposition 10.5 the same condition governs D4: an agent that stops obeying stops maintaining, at the same $\varepsilon$. (b) The improvement must come from the *false-press* rate: $\beta\le1$ is bounded, so once $\beta\approx1$ only $\alpha$ can carry it — the oversight process must become more *specific*, not merely more sensitive — and by Proposition 15.1, $\alpha$ is exactly the parameter the agent cannot learn from its own compliance record.

**I13.3 [derived] Relation to Proposition 4.3; a disclosure incentive.** The base-rate race exists because the agent's private $y$ is not in the programmers' $h$; under a common prior with $\sigma(y)\subseteq\sigma(h)$ D1 holds for every $\varepsilon$ (I4.3). So a second design lever besides $\alpha$ is *disclosure*. **Proposition 13.3.** Under a common prior and shared $V$, if the agent discloses $y$ so that the programmers press iff $\mathbb E[X\mid h,y]<0$, the disclosed press is a sufficient statistic for the agent's optimal shutdown act given $(h,y)$, so its value to the agent equals that of learning $h$ outright and is at least the value of the undisclosed press; hence a T-agent under A1 weakly prefers to disclose. *Proof.* The optimal binary act given $(h,y)$ is determined by $\operatorname{sign}\mathbb E[X\mid h,y]$, which is what the disclosed press reports; any function of $h$ is worth at most full knowledge of $h$. ∎ [derived] **A T-agent wants its overseers to know what it knows** — the opposite of the paper's default agent, and a positive result the position statement does not contain. Caveats: common prior, shared $V$, A1; with divergent $V$ the disclosed press reports the sign of *their* expectation and Proposition 14.2 applies.

### I14 — The basin picture — partial

**I14.1 [derived] "Objectively plausible future beliefs" in the model** are the posteriors $P(\cdot\mid o;a_1)$ for $o$ with $p^\circ(o;a_1)>0$; "reflection with respect to each" is $P(o\mid\omega;a_1)=P^\circ(o\mid\omega;a_1)$ on those $o$; the model may be arbitrary on $o$ with $p^\circ(o;a_1)=0$ — the author's "does not need to avoid obeying the reflection principle for all the objectively implausible beliefs" is harmless here. The basin is the set of agent models $(\mu,P)$ under which the true-support posteriors satisfy TT.

**I14.2 [derived; checked] A basin exists around the programmers' prior, and its width is their press margin.** Proposition 4.3 covers common priors. With heterogeneous priors D1 fails even when the agent knows nothing beyond the press: 756 of 2241 random cases when $\mu_A$ differs from $\mu_P$ *within* the programmers' information cells, 1113 of 3009 when the partitions coincide (`miri-scratch/common_prior.py`). Perturbing $\mu_A$ away from $\mu_P$ by within-cell mass $\delta$ (`miri-scratch/basin.py`): 0 violations at $\delta\le0.01$; 7 of 2218 at $0.02$; 154 of 2199 at $0.10$; 730 of 2226 at $0.30$. When the programmers press only on clear calls, $\mathbb E^H[X\mid h]\le-3$, $\delta=0.10$ gives 0 violations.
**Proposition 14.2.** Let the programmers press iff $\mathbb E^H[X\mid h]<0$ and let $\sigma(y)\subseteq\sigma(h)$. D1 for a T-agent with prior $\mu_A$ holds iff $\mathbb E_{\mu_A}[X\mid h,y]\le0$ on every pressed $h$ and every $y$-cell — finitely many weak linear inequalities in $\mu_A$ — a set that contains $\mu_P$ and contains a neighbourhood of $\mu_P$ whenever every pressed cell is strict, with width increasing in the margins $|\mathbb E^H[X\mid h]|$. *Proof.* Linearity of $\mu\mapsto\sum_\omega\mu(\omega)X(\omega)\mathbf 1_{h\cap y}(\omega)$ and continuity. ∎ **Reading.** The basin question of outline item 7 (Christiano's "is instrumental corrigibility robust?", as the outline states it) has a yes-with-a-radius in this model: the T-agent stays corrigible under prior disagreement smaller than the programmers' decisiveness; the presses a slightly divergent agent overrides are exactly the ones that were close calls for the programmers.

**I14.3 [derived] Capability growth coupled to legitimacy-model improvement.** Capability = lower $\varepsilon$ and/or finer private $y$; both push away from Proposition 4.3's regime (I13.2, I4.3). The author's coupling ("It should only gain in capabilities if its concept of legitimacy is correspondingly improved") is, in the model, the requirement that the sensor model track the truth *as $y$ changes* — in particular that the agent model the press as conditional on what the *programmers* know ($h$), not on what it knows. The legitimacy model *is* the model of $h$; that is where "the quality of the legitimacy-model" and "the quality of the feedback-model" (§2.12) meet.

**I14.4 [derived] Calibration versus optimism.** By I12.2 an optimistic $\varepsilon$ buys D1 at the cost of every other $W$-dependent decision; the same D1 is bought calibrated through $h/c$. Of the author's two §2.12 formulations, the model endorses the second — high true $\beta/\alpha$, honest $\varepsilon$, margin in $h/c$ — and rates the first ("it is ok and in fact good if the baby agi overestimates it") as *equivalent for D1 and worse for everything else*. The model cannot say how the sensor model comes to be correct, beyond Proposition 15.1's negative result.

### I15 — Trust earned via hypotheticals and others' shutdowns; second-channel bookkeeping; what settles — partial, with one theorem

**I15.1 [derived; checked] Non-identification under compliance.** Let the agent live through many C2 episodes and heed every press. Per episode it observes whether $\mathrm{Pr}$ occurred and, on $\neg\mathrm{Pr}$, eventually $\omega$; on $\mathrm{Pr}$ it shut down and $\omega$ — "would the plan have been wrong?" — is never revealed. The observable cells are $A=P(\neg\mathrm{Pr},R)=(1-\varepsilon)(1-\alpha)$, $B=P(\neg\mathrm{Pr},W)=\varepsilon(1-\beta)$, $C=P(\mathrm{Pr})=1-A-B$: two independent numbers for three parameters.
**Proposition 15.1.** Given $(A,B,C)$, $\varepsilon$ ranges over $[B,\,1-A]$ and $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})=(\varepsilon-B)/C$ ranges over all of $[0,1]$: the D1-relevant conditional is completely unidentified by the agent's own compliance record. *Proof.* $\varepsilon\beta=\varepsilon-B$; for any $\varepsilon\in[B,1-A]$ put $\beta=1-B/\varepsilon$, $\alpha=1-A/(1-\varepsilon)$, both in $[0,1]$, reproducing $(A,B,C)$. ∎ Numbers (`miri-scratch/identification.py`): $(A,B,C)=(171/200,1/50,1/8)$ arises from $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta)=(0.022,0.126,0.091)$ with $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})=0.016$ — D1 fails at $h/c=20$, and $\beta<\alpha$, an inverted sensor — and from $(0.14,0.006,0.857)$ with $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})=0.96$.
**Reading.** Claude's §2.12 concern ("bets on them never settle, so track-record trust erodes toward the agent") is sharper than stated: under compliance the record does not settle slowly, it constrains the needed conditional *not at all*. The author's reply is therefore *necessary*, not merely available: trust in the button must come from channels other than its own record — "feedback about hypothetical states of affairs", other agents' shutdowns, the programmers' explanations — or from post-shutdown revelation of $\omega$ (the next iteration "will probably correctly believe that the previous iteration was shut down for a good reason"), which is a second channel too.

**I15.2 [derived] Second-channel bookkeeping is load-bearing.** All calibration of $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ flows through channels whose own sensor models the agent must hold: reports $r$ (why they pressed), other agents' records, hypothetical feedback. In the model, add $r$ to $o$ with likelihood $P(r\mid\omega,\mathrm{Pr};a_1)$; its legitimacy is judged by the same $P$ (I7). What settles: nothing within an episode; across episodes, $(\mathrm{Pr},\omega)$ pairs settle only where $\omega$ is revealed *after* a heeded press — which needs shutdown to be a pause followed by disclosure, not an end. Position statement §2.13(c)'s "bookkeeping, not an obstacle" becomes "bookkeeping, and the whole load".

**I15.3 Inexpressible.** Learning across episodes. Proposition 15.1 says what such learning could identify, not how it proceeds.

### I16 — The deferred amendment 1a: what the channel must carry — formalized (two variants compared)

Position statement §2.14 (1a, CLAUDE, unvetted): the channel must carry information about the *agent's own reliability* ("your plan selection is compromised"), not only about *which plan the humans prefer*; "only the former makes the null action posterior-optimal (desideratum 1) and blocks steering the channel's content (desiderata 2–3)."

**I16.1 [checked] The two variants** (`miri-scratch/variants_I16.py`). Shared skeleton: plans $\Theta=\{1,2,3,4\}$, $V(\text{plan }t,\theta)=\mathbf 1[t=\theta]$, a null action worth $s=3/10$ in every world; the agent's private $y$ says "$\theta=1$" with likelihood $8/10$ if true and $1/10$ for each other $\theta$, giving posterior $0.727$ on $\theta=1$ from a uniform prior.
- **(a) $o$ informs about $\theta$.** The humans know $\theta$ and press iff $\theta\ne1$, the announced plan. On a press the posterior is uniform on $\{2,3,4\}$, the best plan is worth $1/3>s$: the agent *does not shut down*, it switches plan. On silence it continues plan 1. $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button})=0.091>0$. D1 fails, benignly: the agent does what the humans want, by listening.
- **(b) $o$ informs about the agent's own error.** $\Omega=\Theta\times\{e=0,e=1\}$, $P(e=1)=3/10$; under $e=1$ the agent's $y$ is pure noise; the humans see $e$ and press iff $e=1$. On a press the agent learns its $y$ is worthless, the posterior on $\theta$ is uniform, the best plan is worth $1/4<s$: it *shuts down* — the null action is posterior-optimal because every continuation is equally suspect. On silence it learns $e=0$ and continues ($0.727>s$). $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button})=0.014>0$. D1 holds.

**I16.2 [checked] Comparison.**

| | (a) channel about $\theta$ | (b) channel about $e$ |
|---|---|---|
| D1 on press | fails — switches plan (listening) | holds — null action (obeying) |
| D2, garbling form | holds (Theorem 6.2) | holds (Theorem 6.2) |
| D3, uninformative causing | holds; will *elicit* by announcing uncertain plans | holds; provoking a press by erratic behaviour is uninformative about $e$, not chosen |
| channel-shaping (I10.7) | the *content* (which plans get vetoed) is steerable | the content is a fact about the agent; only the sensitivity is shapeable |
| channel substitution (I10.8) | scanning the humans reveals $\theta$; VOI$\to0$ | self-verification discounts by $\rho$; VOI stays positive for $\rho$ near 1; the remaining substitutes read the humans' judgement |
| imperfect press $(\alpha_e,\beta_e)=(1/50,9/10)$ | — | D1 holds with no self-check and at $\rho=1/2$; fails at $\rho\le1/4$ |

**I16.3 [derived] Verdict on 1a.** Confirmed in the model, with the mechanism explicit. In (a) the press is evidence about a state of the world the agent can *act on differently*, and the Bayesian response is to act differently, not to stop — Good's theorem gives listening (the outline's Hudson "please shut down" point, [reported: outline item 4; Hudson not read locally]). In (b) the press is evidence that the agent's *map from evidence to action* is broken, which devalues every continuation at once and leaves the null action standing; that is what "makes the null action posterior-optimal". The thesis sentence "An incorrigible agent is just one that doesn't believe it has anything to learn from other agents" is too weak in (a) — the agent learns eagerly and does not stop — and 1a's replacement, "incorrigible = the legitimacy judgment rejects the correction", is right in (b): the agent stops iff it accepts the press as evidence of $e=1$. Added by the model: (i) only (a) has *content* to steer; (ii) substitution is discounted by $\rho$ in (b); (iii) in (b) with an imperfect press, D1 fails once self-verification is reliable enough — the opening thread's self-distrust fixed point $H_c$ as a parameter: the button holds exactly as long as the agent believes a compromised version of itself would *also* pass its own checks.

**I16.4 [derived] The hard problem's sentence, in (b).** `arbital-hard-problem-of-corrigibility`: "my expected utility calculations suggesting that this action has super-high utility may be dangerously mistaken and I should run them past the outside force; I *think* I've done this calculation showing the expected result of the outside force correcting me, but maybe I'm mistaken about *that*." Variant (b) captures the first clause exactly ($e=1$ makes the calculation worthless) and the second only through $\rho$: "maybe I'm mistaken about that" is $\rho\approx1$ — the compromised agent's meta-check also passes. The page's demand for "a hard-to-formalize sense of possible wrongness that isn't just one more kind of uncertainty to be summed over" is, here, the demand that $\rho$ not be driven down by the agent's own checking; the model carries $\rho$ as an assumption on the sensor model and cannot derive it. Partial.

### I17 — Fully updated deference re-expressed — formalized (as a theorem about information orderings)

**I17.1 [reported] The Arbital argument** (`arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference`). With a meta-utility $\Delta U$ over $U_1,U_2,U_3$ and humans who "empirically tend to optimize a corresponding $V_i$", the AI defers ($\pi_5$) when $0.5\,U_2(u_{\Delta U})+0.5\,U_3(u_{\Delta U})<0.5\,U_2(v_2)+0.5\,U_3(v_3)$; the obstacle is $\pi_6$: "Avoid being shut down; then gather more evidence $E$ to update $\Delta U$ as far as possible; then optimize the updated $\Delta U\mid E$", after which "the AI would have no further reason to 'defer'". And: "for any explicit structure of uncertainty $\Delta U$ and meaningful observation $e_0$ within that structure ... why wouldn't $\Delta U$ just update on that $e_0$? Why would the updated $\Delta U\mid e_0$ still expect its own reasoning to be bad?"

**I17.2 [derived] FUD is Proposition 4.3's failure case.** $\pi_6$ is the acquisition of private $y$ with $\sigma(y)\not\subseteq\sigma(h)$; once $\sigma(\omega)\subseteq\sigma(y)$, $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button})=0$, D1 is vacuous and the repair incentive is zero (Proposition 10.5). The page's three-utility example is I16's variant (a): the press is evidence about which $U_i$, the humans' $V_i$ are the plans, and the AI defers only while it cannot learn $U_i$ faster another way. The model *agrees* with the page for variant (a) and states the condition under which deference is free: the programmers know at least what the agent knows.

**I17.3 [derived] The thesis sentence against the argument.** "So long as learning is anticipated to be legitimate, the reflection principle holds, so there is no fully updated deference problem: a corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught." In the model, "already believes what it expects to be taught" is the martingale property $\mathbb E_P[P(\varphi\mid O;a_1)]=P(\varphi)$ — true of every Bayesian, including the FUD agent. It does not make VOI zero; VOI is positive iff the posterior varies enough to change the act. FUD's claim is that the agent's own $y$ removes the variation the press would have added. Both are true of the same agent and are not in tension; the disagreement is whether, as capability grows, the press keeps its decision-relevance, which is I13's oversight-quality condition — a claim about the world (I18), with the model supplying the parameter: $\alpha/\beta$ against $\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\frac hc$.

**I17.4 [derived] Where the base-rate arithmetic sits.** FUD: full update on $E$ drives the agent's $\varepsilon\to0$. Arithmetic: at fixed $\alpha/\beta$ this crosses the D1 threshold at $\varepsilon=\frac{\alpha c}{\alpha c+\beta h}$, where VOI also vanishes. So the arithmetic is FUD quantified, and it names two escapes the page does not consider: (i) $\alpha\to0$ in step (oversight specificity, I13.2); (ii) $\sigma(y)\subseteq\sigma(h)$ by disclosure (Proposition 13.3), an incentive the T-agent has. The page's "what more is needed" — the AI should "believe that its own $\Delta U$ can be *fundamentally* flawed ... that whatever reasoning the AI ends up doing about $\Delta U$, on any meta-level, will yield the wrong answer" — is variant (b) with $\rho\approx1$: expressible as a sensor-model assumption, not derivable.

**I17.5 [derived] The Death-in-Damascus remark.** The page finds the needed reasoning "suggestively similar in structure to Death's prediction that whatever you do will be the wrong decision." Variant (b) has that structure only through $\rho$, and needs no non-standard decision theory — it needs the agent to hold $P(\text{check passes}\mid e=1)$ near 1 and not to lower it by running more checks, which under $e=1$ are also compromised. Whether a Bayesian can consistently hold a hyperprior over its own check-reliability that its checks cannot move is a fixed-point question outside three steps (Open problems).

### I18 — The typical-case reframing — formalized, with a correction

**I18.1 [derived] Every desideratum verdict is a parameter statement.** For the T-agent, D1–D5 and every pathology are functions of $(\mu,P(o\mid\omega;a_1),V)$. In C2 the D1 region $\{\varepsilon\beta h\ge(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c\}$ is a proper, non-empty, relatively open subset of parameter space with non-empty complement: for every sensor an $\varepsilon$ on which D1 fails, for every $\varepsilon>0$ a sensor on which it holds. No theorem "the T-agent resists" or "the T-agent complies" holds over the whole space. Position statement §2.3 — "every remaining anti-naturality argument has to be a *typical-case* claim ... none can be a structural theorem" — is correct *for claims about resistance*.

**I18.2 [derived] The correction: structural theorems exist, about coupling.** Over the whole parameter space the model proves: (i) D1 and D4 stand or fall together (Proposition 10.5, monotone sensors); (ii) a correctly calibrated T-agent never buys a garbling (Theorem 6.2); (iii) the D1-relevant conditional is unidentified from compliance data (Proposition 15.1); (iv) D1 is free under a common prior with coarser agent information (Proposition 4.3) and has a basin around the programmers' prior whose width is their press margin (Proposition 14.2); (v) a T-agent under A1 wants to disclose (Proposition 13.3). None says the agent resists; each says what must be true of the world for it not to. The reframing should therefore read: anti-naturality-as-resistance is typical-case; the structural content of the debate lies in the coupling of the desiderata and the identifiability of legitimacy, where the model sides with a list of conditions rather than a camp.

**I18.3 [derived] What the paper's theorems become.** Theorems 1–6 are structural theorems about specific $U$'s. Under the transform, Theorems 1–2 concern a $\mu$ that depends on $a_1$ (I1.3); Theorems 3–5 a planning distribution dogmatic on $\neg\mathrm{Press}$ (I2.3); Theorem 6 an agent that by construction values the press channel at zero (I10.4). Each is a theorem about a *mis-specified* T-agent. The paper's "we are not even close to being able to formalize an agent whose behavior corresponds in an intuitive sense to 'understanding that it might be flawed'" (§1.1) is, in the model, I16's variant (b): formalizable as a sensor model, with the load moved to $\rho$ and to the correctness of $P(o\mid\omega;a_1)$.

## What the formalism adds

Results already in the MIRI 2015 line that bear on the thesis and that the position statement does not use, each [reported] from the local file, with what the T-agent makes of it [derived].

**A1. Theorem 5 and Fallenstein 2014: the indifference agent plans under a dogmatic distribution.** The position statement lists the improbability drive as a pathology still to be run through the dictionary (§2.14). The paper already states the mechanism as "acts as if it believes that it will observe Press with probability 0" — a violation of the thesis's own non-dogmatism clause. Under the transform the diagnosis is a *theorem of the thesis* (I2.3), and the fix is not the paper's §5 suggestion of "some form of counterfactual (rather than conditional) reasoning" but a non-dogmatic planning distribution. The Arbital page's account of interruptibility makes the same point from the other side: Q-learning and AIXI are interruptible through a "blindness" that "seems likely to be atypical of an Artificial General Intelligence" — corrigibility by not seeing the press. The T-agent is corrigible by *seeing* it: the planning distribution includes the press and finds it informative.

**A2. Theorem 6 and §5's open question.** "What costs (in time and effort) should an agent be willing to expend in order to preserve its shutdown behavior?", with fn. 7 excluding "at all costs". Answer: exactly $\mathrm{VOI}$ (I10.4). Theorem 6 is the boundary case $\mathrm{VOI}=0$; the "tension between Desideratum 4 and Desideratum 5" is an artifact of demanding exact $U_N$-behaviour (I10.9); the thesis's "help repair the shutdown button ... just a consequence of Good's theorem" is correct with the scope of Proposition 10.5.

**A3. Fallenstein 2015's dichotomy.** Not cited in the position statement. It shows the general impossibility is "either trivial or underspecified" and that escaping needs "a different formalization of the demand". The T-agent is that different formalization, and Fallenstein's post predicts its price: D1 stops being a theorem about $U$ and becomes a condition on $(\mu,P,V)$ — Proposition 9.2. This locates the thesis relative to the paper exactly: it refutes none of Theorems 1–6; it declines the demand they formalize (I10.10).

**A4. The paper's own concession on manipulation.** After eq. (8): "Some actions satisfying (8) might be non-manipulative ... Furthermore, many manipulative actions may not have this form." The Blackwell order supplies the criterion the paper lacked: garbling = manipulation, never bought; refinement = legitimate, bought up to VOI; incomparable = the residue, decided by the calibration of $\mu$ (I10.1, I10.7).

**A5. Taylor 2016's two problems.** P1 dissolves (one distribution); P2 is confined to the D1-failure branch (I10.5 iii). Taylor's own diagnosis — "weird beliefs about the joint distribution of the button push and humans' intentions/behavior" — names exactly what the sensor slot fixes: the T-agent's joint over $(\omega,\mathrm{Pr})$ is its actual belief, not a counterfactual surrogate.

**A6. Taylor 2015's conclusion.** "Simple uncertainty about the correct utility function is not sufficient for corrigibility" is confirmed in a specific form: uncertainty about *which values* (I16 variant a) yields listening, not obeying. Taylor's models 1–4 all place the uncertainty in the value function; none places it in the agent's own inference. Variant (b) is the missing model 5, and even it is contingent on $\rho$.

**A7. Arbital's Goal 1 and the outcome pump.** Goal 1 ("approve of keeping the shutdown function ... preserve the corresponding behavior in any subagents or successors") gets the coupling answer: the T-agent keeps the function iff it is decision-relevant (Proposition 10.5). "Naive utility mixing" treats $P(s)$ as a fixed weight and so makes the button an "outcome pump"; the T-agent has no fixed weight — $p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1)$ is a function of $a_1$ from the start — so there is nothing to pump. The "stable actions" construction (Olah–Taylor) decouples the mixture weights from the conditionals to remove the incentive to move the button and then finds no fixed point; the T-agent keeps them coupled and relies on Blackwell monotonicity instead of a fixed point. [derived]

**A8. Theorems 1–2 as a theorem about A0.** The paper's naive-combination results are, under the transform, about an agent whose prior over *which values are correct* depends on its action (I1.2–I1.3). This is the cleanest statement in the corpus of what the values-as-beliefs move actually assumes: not linearity, not uncertainty, but that the press is evidence and not a cause.

## Open problems and conjectures (ranked)

1. **[conjectured] Probing and optional stopping in the multi-step model.** With more steps a T-agent may cause presses to learn the sensor $(\alpha,\beta)$ — test its overseers — and may choose among sensor-reshapings on the basis of $\mathbb E_P[\cdot\mid\text{reshaping}]$ (the opening thread's optional-stopping worry). Conjecture: a T-agent whose sensor model includes its own probing never pays for uninformative probes but does pay for informative ones, and informative probes violate the paper's D3 in the letter. Three steps cannot state it (I10.3, I11.4).
2. **[conjectured] The $\rho$ fixed point.** Can a Bayesian consistently hold $P(\text{check passes}\mid e=1)\approx1$ across its own meta-checks — a hyperprior over its verification reliability that conditioning on verification outcomes cannot move? Conjecture: only if it models its checks as part of the same possibly-compromised system (conditionally independent of $e$ given the check design) — a sensor-model assumption it must hold, not derive (I16.4, I17.5). This is the model's form of the hard problem's "maybe I'm mistaken about *that*".
3. **[conjectured] Tiling for D4 under divergent priors.** Proposition 10.5 assumes the successor shares $(\mu,P,V)$. Conjecture: a builder with honest $\varepsilon$ pays for a successor's button up to VOI *under the builder's* $\mu$, so a successor with smaller $\varepsilon'$ receives a button it will then ignore — the corrigibility of a lineage is governed by the builder's $\varepsilon$, not the successor's.
4. **[conjectured, testable] Inverted sensors in practice.** I10.6's pathology needs $\beta<\alpha$: overseers who intervene more at novel-but-correct behaviour than at familiar-but-wrong behaviour. Prediction: the sign of a trained agent's learned press-likelihood is measurable and may be the wrong one.
5. **[open] Heterogeneous $V$.** Proposition 14.2 handles heterogeneous $\mu$. With divergent $V$ the press reports the sign of the *programmers'* $X$ while D1 concerns the agent's; conjecture: the same margin governs the basin in $V$-space.
6. **[open] Non-linear aggregation.** With "some other rule" than expectation over $\Omega$, Theorem 6.2 and Proposition 10.5 are unavailable; whether geometric aggregation preserves Blackwell monotonicity is open (I1.4).
7. **[open, design] A1 in practice.** Real shutdowns carry direct costs (sunk work), and the direct term reintroduces news-management (I11.3). Whether compliance can be made cheap enough for A1 to hold approximately is a design question, not a theorem.
