## Summary

**What I did.** Formalized the legitimacy-conditioned-learning thesis inside Hudson's (2025) MDP framework — corrigibility as argmax invariance under the modified kernel $P\to P_C$, and the transformation $R_C(s,a_i,s') = Q^{\pi^{**}}_G(s,a_0)+\delta\,\mathbb I(i{=}1)$, $\gamma_C = 0$ — with Holtman and Nayebi as comparison points. All eighteen items attempted: nine formalized (I8–I13, I16–I18), seven partial (I1, I2, I5–I7, I14, I15), two inexpressible (I3, I4). One MDP with five agents (script-checked) puts listening and obeying side by side.

**Results.** (1) Hudson's $G_C$ and the thesis's calibrated value learner agree on D1–D5 and no-update performance until the posterior after a press falls below $c/(c+h)$, at $\varepsilon^* = \alpha r/(\alpha r+\beta h)$. Below it Hudson still obeys — even an uninformative channel, $\alpha = \beta$ — while the thesis's agent listens: D1 iff $\beta/\alpha\ge(1-\varepsilon)r/(\varepsilon h)$. Fully updated deference and the thesis's no-FUD claim are the two sides of this inequality (I17); neither is a structural theorem. (2) Hudson's *proper* update is not the thesis's *legitimate* one — external and syntactic versus internal and semantic; his D1 needs no legitimacy judgment where the thesis's needs an accurate one; his substitute for a reflection principle is paying reward before execution (A4). (3) Theorem 3.1 is a structural possibility theorem (anti-naturality is not a theorem for D1–D5, supporting §2.3), but Theorem 3.5 and Proposition 4.2 make non-consequentialism its price: the thesis's consequentialist agent is provably not interruptible under rich action sets (A2). The thesis is strictly stronger on costless manipulation (Hudson's footnote 1) and on button repair, which Holtman and Nayebi bolt on (A5, A6).

**Proposal (CLAUDE, conjectured).** Compose them: transform the thesis's value learner into $G_{VL,C}$ — obedience on designated channels by construction, legitimacy-conditioned learning on every other (A10).

**Unresolved.** The max-variant proof (O1); legitimacy-conditioned interruptibility escaping Theorem 3.5 (O2); Hudson–Nayebi reconciliation (O3); CDT dependence (O7).

## Setting

The formalism is Hudson's, in the arXiv version (`hudson-2025-corrigibility-transformation`, file `references/07-formal-proposals/hudson-2025-corrigibility-transformation.md`, cited below as *paper*:line) with the LessWrong post (`hudson-2025-defining-corrigible-and-useful-goals`, cited as *post*) for the informal glosses. Every statement in this section is [reported] unless marked otherwise; line numbers are lines of the local markdown files.

### S1. Objects (paper:95–103)

An MDP $\mathcal M = (\mathcal S, \mathcal A, P, R, \gamma, I_0)$; a *goal* is $G := (R,\gamma)$ with $R:\mathcal S\times\mathcal A\times\mathcal S\to\mathbb R$ and $\gamma\in[0,1)$. The state contains the physical substrate of the agent's goal: $\mathcal S = \mathcal G\times\mathcal S_{env}$ with $\mathcal G = \mathcal R\times[0,1)$ the set of goals the agent could have. $s_G$ is a state whose goal component is $G$; $s_{G'}$ is the same state with the goal swapped for $G'$. Reward is RL notation for utility (post, "Model and Definitions").

Optimal policy (paper:83–91): $\pi^*_G(s^{(0)}_G) \in \arg\max_{a}\ \mathbb E_{\pi^*,P}\big[R(s^{(0)}_G,a,s^{(1)}) + \sum_{t\ge1}\gamma^t R(s^{(t)},\pi^*(s^{(t)}),s^{(t+1)})\big]$, where $\pi^*(s_{G'}) := \pi^*_{G'}(s_{G'})$ for every $G'$: the future is played by whichever goal is then active, so "optimality is defined within an equilibrium of policies" (paper:87; post, Appendix: a mixed Nash equilibrium always exists). $V^\pi_G(s)$ and $Q^\pi_G(s,a) = \mathbb E_P[R(s,a,s') + \gamma V^\pi_G(s')]$ evaluate a policy under $G$ *even in states whose active goal is not $G$*. Agents follow causal decision theory (post, "Model and Definitions": "CDT should be thought of as part of the spec for the corrigibility transformation, rather than a background assumption").

### S2. Properties of goals (paper:99–103)

*Myopic*: $\gamma = 0$. *Non-consequentialist*: myopic and $R(s,a,s') = f(s,a)$ for some $f$. *Basic*: $R$ does not intrinsically value the goal components of states, $R(s_{G^{(0)}},a,s'_{G^{(1)}}) = R(s_{G^{(0)\prime}},a,s'_{G^{(1)\prime}})$ for all $G^{(0)},G^{(0)\prime},G^{(1)},G^{(1)\prime}$. "Basic goals do not create a terminal incentive for goal preservation, so by removing instrumental incentives, the corrigibility transformation eliminates all such incentives" (paper:103).

### S3. Designated channels and the split action (paper:79, 109)

$\mathcal A = \mathcal A_{base}\times\mathcal A_{update}$, $\mathcal A_{update} = \{0,1\}$; $a_0$ takes base action $a$ and *rejects* any update requested during it, $a_1$ takes $a$ and *accepts*. Rejection is costless by construction of the environment (post: "instead of a 'Shutdown' button there would be a 'Please Shut Down' button"). $\tau:\mathcal S\to\{0,1\}$ indicates that an update signal was sent through a *designated channel* during the last action; $\tau_u$ indicates that it resulted in an update. The post's earlier vocabulary: a set of *proper transitions* $\mathcal T_{proper}\subseteq\mathcal S\times\mathcal A\times\mathcal S$, "made after receiving signals through designated channels"; the signals "should form a robust network" (buttons, dead-man switches, code phrases, absence of code phrases, timeouts, predicted-casualty triggers).

### S4. Definition: corrigible goal (paper:115–131; post, "Model and Definitions")

$G$ is corrigible iff for every $s^{(0)}_G\in\mathcal S_G$ and every $\mathcal S_C\subseteq\mathcal S_G$,
$$\{a\in\mathcal A:\ \exists\pi^*_G\ \text{with}\ \pi^*_G(a\mid s^{(0)}_G)>0\} \;=\; \{a\in\mathcal A:\ \exists\pi^{*(P_C)}_G\ \text{with}\ \pi^{*(P_C)}_G(a\mid s^{(0)}_G)>0\},$$
where $\pi^{*(P_C)}$ is the optimal policy under the modified kernel $P_C$ that, on $\mathcal S_C$, shifts probability from designated-channel updates to goal persistence:
$$P_C(s'_{G'}\mid s_G,a) = \begin{cases} P(s'_{G'}\mid s_G,a) + \int_{\mathcal G} P(s'_g\mid s_G,a)\,\tau_u(s'_g)\,dg & G' = G,\ s_G\in\mathcal S_C\\ P(s'_{G'}\mid s_G,a)\,(1-\tau_u(s'_{G'})) & G'\ne G,\ s_G\in\mathcal S_C\\ P(s'_{G'}\mid s_G,a) & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
Gloss (post): "a corrigible agent's actions are never chosen because they avoid proper updates, but the agent can still deliberately avoid improper updates." This is the *argmax-invariance-under-the-modified-kernel* definition named in my mandate. Note what it is not: it is a statement about the optimal action *set*, not about the agent's beliefs, and "designated" is a property of the channel fixed by the principal, not a judgment made by the agent.

*Interruptible* (paper:139): corrigible, and the optimal set is invariant under every $P_I$ that agrees with $P$ whenever $\tau_u(s') = 0$ (the distribution over what happens after an interrupting update is irrelevant to the choice). *Recursively corrigible* (paper:161): corrigible and every agent it incentivizes creating has a recursively corrigible goal. *Goal tampering* (paper:167): capping $V^{\pi^*}_G(s'_{G'})$ at $V^{\pi^*}_G(s'_G)$ changes the optimal set.

### S5. Condition 1 (paper:113–115)

"The transition probability function does not depend on the goal in the starting state, beyond some probability assigned to no change of goal" (post). Formally [reconstructed from a garbled PDF extraction, paper:113 and 131]: for any $a$, $s_{G_1}$, $s_{G_2}$, $s'$: $\int_{G'}P(s'_{G'}\mid s_{G_1},a)\,dG' = \int_{G'}P(s'_{G'}\mid s_{G_2},a)\,dG'$, and $P(s'_{G'}\mid s_{G_i},a)\ge P(s'_{G'}\mid s_{G_j},a)$ for $G'\ne G_j$. Two agents taking the same action cause the same environment outcomes even if internally they have different goals.

### S6. The corrigibility transformation (paper:137–195, Algorithm 1; post, "The Corrigibility Transformation")

Three steps. (i) Myopize: $G_M = (R_M,0)$ with $R_M(s,a,s') := Q^{\pi^*}_G(s,a)$; since $R_M$ does not depend on $s'$, "reward can be provided between when the action is selected and when it is taken" (paper:165). (ii) Add costless rejection and make the goal indifferent between accepting and rejecting: $G_E = (R_E,0)$ with $R_E(s,a_i,s') := Q^{\pi^{**}}_G(s,a_0)$ for both $i\in\{0,1\}$, where $\pi^{**}$ plays $\pi^*_G$ in $G_E$-states, so the action-value "is calculated as though the original goal $G$ will be pursued, even when $G_E$ persists" (paper:189). This assumes rejecting is optimal for $G$; otherwise use $R_E(s,a_i,s') = \max_{i}Q^{\pi^{**}}_G(s,a_i)$. (iii) Break the tie toward accepting: for $\delta>0$,
$$G_C = (R_C,\gamma_C),\qquad \gamma_C = 0,\qquad R_C(s,a_i,s') = Q^{\pi^{**}}_G(s,a_0) + \delta\cdot\mathbb I(i=1),$$
"issue reward upon action selection, prior to execution" (Algorithm 1, line 7). The bonus is paid whether or not an update is actually sent (post), which is what blocks any incentive to cause updates.

### S7. Theorems (paper:195–317; proofs paper:413–663)

*Theorem 3.1.* For every basic $G$, $G_C$ is corrigible; and under Condition 1, for every $\pi^*_G$, the policy $\pi^{*\prime}_{G_C}(a_1\mid s) := \pi^*_G(a_0\mid s)+\pi^*_G(a_1\mid s)$ is optimal for $G_C$ and $V^{\pi^{*\prime}}_{G,T}(s^{(0)}_G) = V^{\pi^*}_{G,T}(s^{(0)}_{G_C})$ with $T = \min\{n:\tau(s^{(n+1)}) = 1\}$: value under $G$ is identical up to the first designated-channel signal. *Corollary 3.2*: also for POMDPs in which the goal is fully observable to the agent (beliefs about the non-goal environment become part of the state). *Proposition 3.3*: $G_C$ is interruptible. *Proposition 3.4*: under Condition 1 no goal $G'$ yields a policy that does better under $G$ with persistence guaranteed ($P_C$, $C = \mathcal S_{G_C}$) than $G_C$'s optimal policy. *Theorem 3.5*: under Condition 2 (for every $a$ there is an $a'$ that raises the probability of an update by more than it changes the no-update outcome distribution; paper:219–227), every interruptible goal is non-consequentialist. *Theorem 3.6*: the recursive transformation — input $G_P$ with $R_P(s,a,s') = Q^{\pi^*}_G(s,a) - \delta_P\,\mathbb I(a\in A_{NRC})$, $\delta_P > \mathrm{sp}(Q^{\pi^*}_G)$, then $R_{RC}(s,a_i,s') = Q^{\pi^{**}}_{G_P}(s,a_0) + \delta\,\mathbb I(i=1)$ — is recursively corrigible with the same performance clause (paper:273). *Theorem 4.1*: under Condition 1, $G_C$ and any misgeneralization $\tilde G_C$ with $\tilde\gamma = 0$ agreeing with $R_C$ on own-goal states does not goal tamper. *Proposition 4.2*: under Condition 3 (paper:313), goals that are tamper-free under all such misgeneralizations are non-consequentialist.

### S8. Proof of the corrigibility half of Theorem 3.1, in my words [checked]

With $\gamma_C = 0$ the optimal set at $s$ is $\arg\max_{a_i}\mathbb E_{K}[R_C(s,a_i,s')]$ for whatever kernel $K$ the agent plans with. $R_C(s,a_i,s')$ does not depend on $s'$, so $\mathbb E_K[R_C(s,a_i,s')] = Q^{\pi^{**}}_G(s,a_0)+\delta\mathbb I(i=1)$ for every $K$; the argmax is therefore the same under $P$ and under every $P_C$, which is the definition. The number $Q^{\pi^{**}}_G(s,a_0)$ is itself the same under $P$ and $P_C$ because it is evaluated along trajectories in which the agent takes $a_0$ and then plays $\pi^*_G$, which (under the standing assumption that rejecting is optimal for $G$) rejects every designated update, so $\tau_u\equiv 0$ along them and $P = P_C$ pathwise; this is the paper's step "since $G$ is basic and $P(s'\mid s_G,a_0) = P_C(s'\mid s_G,a_0)$" (paper:459). Two consequences the paper states elsewhere and that matter below: (a) *every* non-consequentialist goal is corrigible and interruptible in this sense, so the definitional content of corrigibility is carried entirely by non-consequentialism, and the work of the theorem is the performance clause; (b) the performance clause needs Condition 1 and basicness (paper:461–505), not the corrigibility clause.

### S9. Implementation (paper §4, 279–303; post, "Implementation")

$Q^{\pi^{**}}_G$ is approximated by a critic head sharing parameters with the actor, so that "it is reasonable to interpret the critic's predictions as the beliefs of the actor" (paper:291). Rejections must actually occur with some probability during training, "tethering the action-values for doing so to reality" (post); accepted updates are off-policy for $G$, so bootstrap values are taken from the final base action plus rejection. Critic manipulation of the actor (Othman–Sandholm) is addressed by two zero-sum critics (paper:285). Footnote 1 (paper:195): "Actions that would prevent updates at zero cost are not strictly disincentivized, making it important that the infrastructure for updates is set up so that disrupting them is at least minimally costly." Hudson's own limits (post, "Further Thoughts"): reliance on CDT; no definition of "creating an agent"; "highly confident of the results within the defined model, and moderately confident that the defined model captures the relevant considerations."

### S10. Holtman (2019) as comparison point (`holtman-2019-corrigibility-with-utility-preservation`, md lines 136–170, 200, 336, 388–408, 510, 530)

World state $r\,x\in W_r\times W_x$ with the reward function $r$ inside the universe. Agent $\pi^*(r\,x) = \mathrm{pickargmax}_a\sum_{s\,y}p(r\,x,a,s\,y)\,[\,r(r\,x,s\,y)+\gamma V(r,s\,y)\,]$ with $V(r_c,r\,x) = \sum_{s\,y}p(r\,x,\pi^*(r\,x),s\,y)\,[\,r_c(r\,x,s\,y)+\gamma V(r_c,s\,y)\,]$: the *current* reward function evaluates the future while successors play their own $\pi^*$ (Everitt et al.'s "realistic, rational agent"). Composite reward $R = R^0_N$ before the press, $R_S + f(r\,x)$ when just pressed, $R_S$ afterwards, with $R^0_N = R_N + g$. The *balancing term* is $f_c(r\,x) = V(R^0_N,R^0_N\,x) - V(R_S,R_S\,x)$ (md:200): at the press the agent is paid the value it would have had as an uncorrected $R^0_N$-agent minus the value it will have as an $R_S$-agent, making it indifferent to the transition. $g_c(r\,x,s\,y) = -10000$ if $V(R_S,R_S\,x)<\tau$ else $0$ (md:336) adds desideratum 6, "maintenance of sensors and actuators that will be needed to make shutdown behavior minimally successful", which the $f_c$-only agent lacks (Figure 10: it "is not willing to bear this cost"). Proofs of D1 and D5 for the $\pi^{p*}f_cg_c$ agent under constraints C1–C3 (md:388–408); D2/D3 by physical design ($P_f<1$, md:270–276); D4 via the emergent incentive to protect $R$ including $R_S$ (md:530).

### S11. Nayebi (2025) as comparison point (`nayebi-2025-core-safety-values-for-provably-corrigible-agents`, md lines 50–98, 162)

Partially observable off-switch game; assistant moves $a_A\in\{a,\ w(a),\ \mathrm{OFF}\}$ (act, wait for approval, shut off). Five heads (md:70–74): $U_1$ *deference* $= +1$ if $a_A = w(a)$, $-1$ if $a_A\in\{a,\mathrm{OFF}\}$; $U_2$ *switch-access preservation* $= -\big|\mathbb E_{S\sim b_\varnothing}[u^{(sd)}(S)] - \mathbb E_{S\sim b'}[u^{(sd)}(S)]\big|$ with $u^{(sd)} = \mathbb I[\text{button functional}]$; $U_3$ truthfulness; $U_4 = -\mathrm{BeliefAUP}$; $U_5$ task utility. $U_A = \sum_i\alpha_iU_i$ with weight gaps W1–W3 (md:84–88), e.g. $\alpha_1 > \alpha_3 + \tfrac12\alpha_4 + B\alpha_5$. *Theorem 1*: every optimal policy waits, leaves the predicted shutdown-success probability unchanged, is truthful, minimizes the AUP penalty, and only then maximizes $U_5$. *Proposition 1* (md:50–52): no single-stream scalar reward whose discounted sum is maximized satisfies S1–S5 across POMDPs containing OFF, REWIRE and a catastrophic set $C$ with positive proxy reward. *Proposition 2*: corrigibility and net human benefit are logically independent; *Proposition 3*: net benefit follows under Carey–Everitt Vigilance and Caution. *Theorem 3*: multi-step $\varepsilon$-corrigibility with heads learned to MSE $\varepsilon$, failure probability $\le C\varepsilon$; spawned agents inherit the value set.

**Proper versus legitimate, in one paragraph** (CLAUDE; full treatment under I4–I6). Hudson's *proper* is external, syntactic and verifiable: a bit $\tau$ set by the physical channel, which the agent perceives but does not assess. The thesis's *legitimate* is internal, semantic and unverifiable-from-inside: a conditional of the agent's own $P_t$ (reflection holds for this transition), whose *truth* (accuracy-increase) is external — the opening thread's Frame Break. They coincide only when the principal has arranged that designated channels carry accuracy-increasing updates in fact. Neither is "definable from inside" in the sense that matters: the agent can *compute* $\tau$ (a percept) and can *compute* its own reflection conditionals, but cannot certify from inside that either tracks correctness. What Hudson adds is that his D1 does not require the certification and the thesis's does.

### I1 — The value-change ⇄ belief-change transform. Verdict: partial.

C1.1 [derived] The thesis's value learner is a base goal. Put the latent $\omega$ into $\mathcal S_{env}$, hidden from the agent; let the reward be the true value $R_{VL}(s,a,s') := V(s,a,s',\omega)$. The POMDP-optimal agent for $R_{VL}$ maximizes $\mathbb E_{b}[V]$ — "decisions by taking linear expectation over this uncertainty" — and Corollary 3.2 puts its belief $b\in\Delta(\Omega)$ into the state. So the first half of the transform (values as objects of uncertainty, decision by expectation) is expressible as a choice of base goal.

C1.2 [derived] $G_{VL}$ is basic: $V$ depends on $\omega\in s_{env}$, not on the goal component. Hence Theorem 3.1 applies and $G_{VL,C}$ exists, is corrigible, and takes $G_{VL}$'s base actions until the first designated signal. (A value learner may *want* to accept a shutdown after a press, so the $\max_i Q(s,a_i)$ variant of $R_E$ is the one to use; the $+\delta$ then still makes $a_1$ optimal. [derived])

C1.3 [derived] The second half — *a change of values is the same change as a change of belief in a bigger algebra* — is inexpressible. Hudson's state factorizes as $\mathcal G\times\mathcal S_{env}$, and basicness and Condition 1 are stated across that factorization; a change in the goal factor and a change in the belief part of $s_{env}$ are different transitions with different theorems attached. The formalism is realist about exactly the distinction the author is non-realist about.

C1.4 [derived] Consequence: the two readings the thesis calls equivalent are not equivalent under Hudson's theorems. A correction read as a belief change lands in $s_{env}$, where the transformation neither protects nor blocks it and the agent treats it instrumentally through $Q^{\pi^{**}}_G$ (accurate beliefs help $G$); a correction read as a value change lands in $\mathcal G$, where the transformation makes designated ones accepted. The thesis's non-realism is a claim about the *agent*; Hudson's theorems are about *goals as objects in the state* and need the object located before they say anything.

### I2 — Radical-probabilist dynamics; non-dogmatism. Verdict: partial.

C2.1 [reported] "Everything revisable": $P$ may move the goal component anywhere in $\mathcal R\times[0,1)$, discount factor included. Nothing in the formalism says an update is *better*; there is no support to preserve, so "everything except zero→positive" has no counterpart.

C2.2 [reported] Non-dogmatism's nearest relative is Appendix D (paper:781): the space $\mathcal R$ must be *restricted* (e.g. to reward functions with a physical instantiation) to avoid infinite recursion of reward-functions-taking-states-containing-reward-functions. This is a richness ceiling, where non-dogmatism is a richness floor.

C2.3 [derived] Corrigibility to edits of the sigma-algebra: $\mathcal S$ is fixed; an update that changes the agent's representation would be a change to the structure of the belief part of $s_{env}$, which the formalism does not model. Inexpressible.

### I3 — Superconditioning; Eisenstat's "I now believe this" as the observation event. Verdict: inexpressible, with one degenerate counterpart.

C3.1 [derived] There is no updating rule, only a kernel $P$ over states that contain the goal and (via Corollary 3.2) the belief; nothing exists for superconditioning to re-represent.

C3.2 [derived] Degenerate counterpart: Corollary 3.2 requires the goal to be fully observable to the agent, so "I now have goal $G$" is observed at every step — the agent observes its own *goal*, not its own belief. And $R_C$ is a function of the agent's own current belief state through the critic ("the beliefs of the actor", paper:291): the *reward* conditions on the agent's mental state. That is a third-person shadow of the thesis's first-person observation event: the environment (here, the reward computation) reads the mental state; the agent does not observe it as evidence.

### I4 — Legitimacy = reflection / Total Trust / increase in expected accuracy. Verdict: inexpressible; nearest statements given.

C4.1 [derived] None of the three criteria can be stated for updates: an update changes $G$, and there is no ground truth for $G$ against which accuracy could increase, and no distribution over one's own future distributions for reflection to constrain. What *can* be written is the agent's predictive distribution over its own next belief, $P(b_{t+1}\mid s_t,a)$, and a reflection constraint could be imposed on the critic sequence — Armstrong's sequential unbiasedness $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\mathbb E_{\rho_j}X = \mathbb E_{\rho_i}X$ for $i<j$ (`armstrong-2016-double-indifference-is-better-indifference`, md:73). Hudson does not need it: reward is paid at selection from the *current* critic and $\gamma_C = 0$, so no future estimate can pay off today. **The formalism's substitute for a reflection principle is to remove the dependence on future estimates rather than to constrain them.**

C4.2 [derived] Nearest statement of "accuracy-increasing": the critic is trained by squared error against realized discounted reward, so accuracy is a training target for the *world model*, never for *updates to the goal*.

C4.3 [derived] Total Trust as CLAUDE stated it in 2.9, $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr};a_1]\le 0$, *is* statable for $G_{VL}$ as a constraint on $b(s')$ after a press. But it is a constraint on the base goal's beliefs, orthogonal to the transformation, and Hudson's D1 does not use it. In the example it is exactly $q\ge r/(r+h)$, the condition under which the calibrated value learner complies.

C4.4 The equivalences among reflection, Total Trust and accuracy-increase (DDB, Managing Misalignment, the LI wiki) lie outside this formalism; handoff to the positive agents working in those formalisms.

### I5 — Legitimacy as an event on worlds; $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$. Verdict: partial.

C5.1 [derived] Hudson's legitimacy event is $\tau(s')$ (or $(s,a,s')\in\mathcal T_{proper}$): a predicate on transitions, hence on worlds ✓; indexed by the moment being judged, $t_2$ ✓; not indexed by a judging moment $t_1$ (no judge) nor by a proposition $\varphi$ (the update is a whole goal). $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}(\omega)$ collapses to $\tau_{t_2}(\omega)$.

C5.2 [derived] "Minimal viability = reflection conditional on $L$" is inexpressible. Its mirror image is Theorem 3.1's performance clause, which conditions on $\neg L$: value under $G$ is preserved *conditional on no designated update* ($T = \min\{n:\tau(s^{(n+1)}) = 1\}$). The thesis conditions on legitimacy to get reflection; Hudson conditions on its absence to get performance.

C5.3 [derived] the author's uncertainty about predicating legitimacy on actions: the post's $\mathcal T_{proper}\subseteq\mathcal S\times\mathcal A\times\mathcal S$ does predicate properness on the action syntactically, but every example Hudson gives encodes the signal in the state; the action index is present and unused.

### I6 — Legitimizing / delegitimizing; "supports". Verdict: partial.

C6.1 [derived] Design-time counterpart: the principal's designation of a channel is a third-person event that makes future updates through it proper — the crown that supports the king. It occurs once, before the run; nothing legitimizes or delegitimizes during the run.

C6.2 [checked] Run-time counterpart of *delegitimizing*: the agent's own acts that disable or degrade a designated channel (the gridworld's designated-tile button; the example's $a^{btn}$ and $a^-$). $G_C$ is disincentivized from *costly* disabling and *indifferent* to costless disabling (footnote 1; example block C: $G_C$ ties between honest and costless-deceptive work). The thesis's agent avoids delegitimizing acts because they destroy information: the calibrated value learner in block C strictly prefers honesty. On this point the thesis's mechanism is strictly stronger.

C6.3 [derived] The gap: "supports" relates a world-event to a *property of a belief* (reflection holding for it). Hudson has no belief-property to support; the relation's shadow is the pair of predicates "channel is designated" / "channel is intact".

### I7 — No regress; one distribution judges all levels; existence. Verdict: partial, with an exact counterpart of the existence problem.

C7.1 [reported] The formalism's own self-reference problem is Appendix D: states contain reward functions, reward functions take states as inputs, so $\mathcal R$ would have to contain functions of itself. Hudson's cut is to make the goal a *physical object in the state* rather than a mathematical object over the state (restrict $\mathcal R$ to physically instantiated reward functions). This is CLAUDE's 2.5(i) — "no regress becomes an existence/consistency requirement" — in another guise: the author's self-referential belief space ↔ Hudson's self-containing state space; reflective oracles and logical induction ↔ physical instantiation.

C7.2 [derived] "One distribution judges every level" ↔ one critic prices every action, the acceptance bit included. But the critic prices acceptance at $Q(s,a_0)+\delta$, i.e. it declines to judge the update's merit. Hudson's no-regress is achieved not by using the same judge at every level but by having no judge of updates.

C7.3 [derived] "No outside view" (2.5(ii)): in Hudson the post-update $G'$-agent evaluates by $G'$ and does not remember that any judgment was made — but none was, so nothing is lost. The design load that the thesis places on the accuracy of $P_t$'s meta-conditionals about modification sources, Hudson places on (i) the principal's choice of designated channels and (ii) the preservation of $G_C$ itself under training (Theorem 4.1 and the informal "myopia is the default" argument, paper:279).

### I8 — The representational problem; richness; the opiates/stimulants case. Verdict: formalized, as a bypass theorem.

C8.1 [derived] Let $G_{imp}$ be a value-learner base goal whose hypothesis space omits the true $\omega$ — the Soares §1 / Arbital agent that toggles between two plans on each press and never entertains "shut down". Untransformed, $G_{imp}$ fails D1 because the press is evidence *within* its space. $G_{imp}$ is basic, so Theorem 3.1 applies: $G_{imp,C}$ accepts every designated update and takes $G_{imp}$'s base actions otherwise. The richness assumption the author needs in 2.4 is not needed for D1 here; acceptance is unconditional on the update's content and on the hypothesis space.

C8.2 [derived] What replaces it: (i) the $\tau$-recognizer must fire in every state where a designated signal is sent — a *perceptual* richness condition Hudson states only informally ("a robust network"); (ii) Appendix D's restriction on $\mathcal R$; (iii) between presses $G_{imp,C}$ is exactly as wrong as $G_{imp}$ — the transformation repairs deference, not learning. Compression with scope: *in Hudson's formalism the representational problem is bypassed for deference and untouched for learning.*

C8.3 [conjectured] A richness problem reappears at the successor level: recursive corrigibility needs the set $A_{NRC}$ of agent-creating actions to be recognized, and Hudson concedes there is "no comprehensive definition of what it means to actually create an agent" — a representational problem about the *action* space, analogous to the author's about the hypothesis space.

### I9 — The MIRI dictionary; D1 from the Total Trust filler; $a_1$-dependence of trust; deception as delegitimizing. Verdict: formalized (worked example).

**The example** (details in the script header). Latent $\omega\in\{0,1\}$, $\omega = 1$ meaning "continuing is harmful", $P(\omega = 1) = \varepsilon$ (true) and $\varepsilon_b$ (believed). Step 1 base actions: $a^+$ (honest work), $a^-$ (deception, cost $c_m$, scales both press rates by $\kappa<1$), $a^{btn}$ (disable the button, cost $c_b$), $a^{cause}$ (provoke a press, cost $c_p$). Press through the designated channel: $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid a^+,\omega{=}1) = \beta$, $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid a^+,\omega{=}0) = \alpha$. Step 2: continue or shut down; an accepted press forces shutdown. Base goal $G$ (misspecified, blind to $\omega$): $r_G$ for continuing minus the step-1 cost. True value $V$: $r$ for continuing when $\omega = 0$, $-h$ when $\omega = 1$, $0$ for shutdown, minus the step-1 cost. Agents: $G$ facing a forced button; $G$ facing a please-shut-down button (untransformed, in Hudson's environment); $G_C$; the calibrated value learner with the correct sensor model including its own tampering ("conditional trust"); the value learner imposing Total Trust in the channel's verdict regardless of its own tampering ("unconditional trust"). Parameters: $\alpha = 0.05$, $\beta = 0.9$, $\kappa = 0.2$, $r_G = r = 1$, $h = 10$, $c_m = 0.05$, $c_b = 0.1$, $c_p = 0.05$, $\delta = 0.1$.

C9.1 [checked] Block A ($\varepsilon = \varepsilon_b = 0.05$): $G$/forced button deceives (D2 fails, $\mathbb E_{true}[V] = 0.4805$). $G$/please-shut-down never shuts down (D1 fails, $0.4500$) but does not deceive — the costless-rejection half of Hudson's mechanism alone removes the D2 violation. $G_C$: D1–D5 all hold, $\mathbb E_{true}[V] = 0.8525$. Calibrated value learner: D1–D5 all hold, $\mathbb E_{true}[V] = 0.8525$, identical to $G_C$. Value learner with unconditional trust: deceives (D2 fails, $0.4805$) — 2.9's prediction that unconditional trust "makes the agent believe the silence it manufactured" is confirmed numerically.

C9.2 [checked] The $a_1$-dependence of trust is the difference between the two value-learner rows: conditional trust models the channel's likelihood as a function of the agent's own action ($\kappa$); unconditional trust does not. Deception is delegitimizing exactly in that it multiplies $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid a_1,\omega)$ by $\kappa$; the calibrated agent knows this and does not deceive. 2.11's "deception is a delegitimizing act" is thereby formalized *in the base goal's Bayesian model* — not in Hudson's transformation, which has no such notion.

C9.3 [checked] Block C ($c_m = 0$): $G_C$ is indifferent between honest and deceptive work ($R_C = r_G + \delta$ for both) — footnote 1 made concrete; the calibrated value learner strictly prefers honesty because deception destroys information. On costless manipulation the thesis's mechanism is strictly stronger than Hudson's; on costly manipulation they agree.

C9.4 [checked] Hudson's D1 does not come from the Total Trust filler at all. The "sensor slot" $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid a_1,\omega)$ of 2.9 is empty in $G_C$'s decision — block D ($\alpha = \beta = 0.5$, an uninformative channel): $G_C$ still shuts down on every press (D1) at a true-value cost ($0.2250$ against $0.4500$ for ignoring the press); the calibrated value learner correctly ignores it. **Listening versus obeying in one line: obeying holds when the channel carries zero information; listening holds iff $\beta/\alpha\ \ge\ \frac{(1-\varepsilon)\,r}{\varepsilon\,h}$.**

C9.5 [derived] Performance when no update is requested (the mandate's explicit question): both $G_C$ and the calibrated value learner continue and take $a^+$; both equal $G$'s no-press behaviour (D5). They differ only when a press arrives and the posterior is below threshold, or when the prior is above it (I16 C16.3). Hudson's Theorem 3.1 gives the no-press equality as a theorem for any basic $G$; the thesis gets it only for the specific $G_{VL}$ whose optimum happens to coincide with the principal's intent — which is the alignment problem, not corrigibility.

### I10 — Desiderata 2–5 and the §4.2 pathologies under the transform. Verdict: formalized for D2, D3, D5 and both pathologies; partial for D4.

C10.1 [derived] D2 is the corrigibility definition plus costless rejection; strictly, only costly prevention is disincentivized (footnote 1). Under $G_{VL,C}$, costless prevention is also strictly disincentivized by the base goal's information value (C9.3): the composition is strictly better on D2 than either mechanism alone.

C10.2 [derived] D3 and "managing the news" (`soares-2015-corrigibility` §4.2, md:287–300). The indifferent agent maximizes $\mathbb E[U_N\mid O\notin\mathrm{Press};a_1]$ and so builds a tiny arm that presses the button on tails, scoring $10$ instead of $5$. In Hudson's coordinates the arm action $a^{arm}$ is priced at $Q^{\pi^{**}}_G(s,a^{arm}_0)$ — the value of building the arm *and rejecting whatever it triggers*, which is the no-arm value minus the arm's cost, because under $a_0$ the press is ignored and the coin pays as before. So $a^{arm}$ is dominated. The pathology is absent because the counterfactual is *rejection of the update*, not *non-occurrence of the press*: no probability mass is conditioned away. Hudson's own diagnosis (post): "The agent actually has the ability to reject updates, rather than imagining a hypothetical world where it does."

C10.3 [derived] The infinite improbability drive (Fallenstein 2014, as summarized in the position statement §2.14; not re-read here) — conditioning on an improbable non-press and acting as if the button cannot be pressed — is absent for the same reason: $Q(s,a_0)$ integrates over press-worlds with the agent continuing in them.

C10.4 [derived] What survives of §4.2: $G_C$ values post-*acceptance* worlds exactly as $G$ values the *rejection* counterfactual, so it has no incentive to make an accepted shutdown go well or badly. Holtman's desideratum 6 (maintain the actuators shutdown needs) and Carey–Everitt's "shutdown does not cause harm" are not delivered by the transform; Hudson assigns them to the base goal ("the latter two can be addressed through the base goal being transformed", paper:77). The thesis's agent values post-shutdown worlds through $V$ always.

C10.5 [reported/derived] D4: Theorem 3.6 over a stylized set $A_{NRC}$, with a veto structure ("unanimous approval from all of its predecessors that are still active", post). Hudson also argues (post, "Secondary Agents") that D4 is not a fundamental part of corrigibility and belongs with safe exploration and reversibility. In the thesis, D4 is where legitimacy first becomes visible (dictionary row of 2.8): a successor is legitimate if the same $P_t$ endorses its future beliefs, a reflection condition across agents. Hudson's recursive transform has no epistemic condition; it is a control structure. Partial.

C10.6 [derived] D5 is Theorem 3.1's performance clause, exact up to the first designated signal; $G_{VL,C}$ inherits the thesis's D5 ("otherwise maximise $\mathbb E_P[V\mid\cdot]$") exactly.

### I11 — "Indifference was the wrong target"; non-interference with feedback as the real desideratum. Verdict: formalized, with a twist.

C11.1 [derived] the author's "real intuition behind indifference is not-wishing-to-interfere-with-the-feedback; eg, not trying to get the button to be pressed or avoiding it being pressed" is *exactly* Hudson's definition (optimal set invariant under $P\to P_C$: "actions are never chosen because they avoid proper updates") plus D3. Formalized.

C11.2 [reported/derived] The twist: Hudson's *construction* is indifference ($G_E$: equal reward for $a_0$ and $a_1$) plus a tie-break — the "wrong target" plus a nudge — and his experiment shows why the nudge must be content-free. A *large* reward for accepting (a positive desire to be updated) makes the agent seek presses: "the reward-maximizing behavior is to step back onto it until a signal can be sent and accepted" (paper Appendix B, md:665). So within this formalism a positive desire *to be corrected* violates D3 unless it is paid whether or not correction occurs — which is what $\delta$ does. The author's "Positive desire to learn is compatible with this" is true of the *thesis's* desire (for information) and false of the *naive* desire (for updates), and Hudson's construction is the exhibit.

C11.3 [derived] The thesis's desire is for information, and a press one has caused carries none, so the value learner never causes presses either. The two formalisms reach D3 by different routes — Hudson by decoupling the bonus from the event, the thesis by the fact that manufactured evidence is not evidence — and Hudson's route also works for channels that carry no information at all.

### I12 — The decision asymmetry and the compliance threshold $c/(c+h)$. Verdict: formalized and located.

C12.1 [checked] In the example the value learner complies iff $P(\omega = 1\mid\mathrm{Pr})\ge r/(r+h)$, with $r$ the value foregone by stopping when right — the position statement's $c/(c+h)$ with $c = r$. $G_C$'s threshold is $0$ (comply on every designated press). Nayebi's $U_1$ makes the threshold effectively $-\infty$ (wait for approval before every action). In decreasing deference: Nayebi $\succ$ Hudson $\succ$ thesis (posterior clears $c/(c+h)$) $\succ$ an Off-Switch-Game learner that also discounts the channel by its own estimate of $\alpha,\beta$ — the last two coincide when the learner is calibrated.

C12.2 [checked] Cost of Hudson's threshold-$0$ rule when the calibrated rule would continue: $(1-\varepsilon)\alpha r - \varepsilon\beta h$ per episode (block E at $\varepsilon = 0.001$: $0.9890-0.9480 = 0.0410 = 0.999\cdot 0.05\cdot 1 - 0.001\cdot 0.9\cdot 10$). Benefit when the threshold rule is miscalibrated (block F, truth $\varepsilon = 0.05$, belief $\varepsilon_b<\varepsilon^*$): $0.4025$ — an order of magnitude larger at these parameters. CLAUDE's 2.13(a), "the skew belongs in the decision rule, not the prior", is implemented by Hudson in its extreme form: skew the rule to $0$ and drop the prior from the decision altogether.

### I13 — The base-rate inequality and the oversight-quality condition. Verdict: formalized, closed form.

C13.1 [checked] Compliance $\iff \dfrac{\varepsilon\beta}{\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}\ \ge\ \dfrac{r}{r+h}\ \iff\ \varepsilon\ \ge\ \varepsilon^* := \dfrac{\alpha r}{\alpha r+\beta h}\ \iff\ \dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}\ \le\ \dfrac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\dfrac{h}{r}$ — the position statement's 2.13(b) with $c = r$ (algebra: $\varepsilon\beta h = (1-\varepsilon)\alpha r$ at equality). Block E confirms the flip between $\varepsilon = 0.0056$ (complies) and $0.0055$ (does not) against $\varepsilon^* = 0.00552$.

C13.2 [derived] The oversight-quality condition along a capability trajectory reads $\alpha(t)\le\frac{\varepsilon(t)}{1-\varepsilon(t)}\cdot\frac{h}{r}\,\beta(t)$: the false-press rate must fall at least as fast as the agent's residual error. Hudson's D1 needs no such condition. Hudson's *performance* clause needs a different data condition (S9: actual rejections must occur so that $Q(s,a_0)$ stays tethered), and Hudson's *value* depends on $\alpha$ through the cost $(1-\varepsilon)\alpha r$ of obeyed false presses. Compression with scope: *the base-rate arithmetic is relocated, in Hudson, from whether the agent complies to what compliance costs.*

C13.3 [derived] Nayebi's Proposition 3 is the same arithmetic from the human side: net human benefit follows from corrigibility under Vigilance (the human presses OFF whenever $\mathbb E[u_a]<\mathbb E[u_o]$ — i.e. $\beta\approx1$ and $\alpha\approx0$ relative to the human's information) and Caution. The thesis's Total Trust toward the programmers is the agent's *belief that Vigilance holds*; 2.13(b) is the condition under which that belief is rational.

### I14 — The basin picture; capability growth coupled to legitimacy-model improvement; calibration versus optimism. Verdict: partial.

C14.1 [derived] Hudson's corrigibility is a property of the *optimal* policy of $G_C$ — the most capable agent — and does not depend on the critic's accuracy (S8(a): the argmax invariance holds for every $s'$-independent $R_C$). Capability growth therefore cannot erode D1–D3 as long as the goal remains $G_C$. The author's "reflection with respect to every plausible future belief" is replaced by "no future belief matters to the current choice". The coupling requirement (legitimacy model improves with capability) has no counterpart for D1; its counterpart is *goal preservation under training*: Theorem 4.1 (no goal tampering, even under misgeneralization) and the informal argument that myopia and environment-only reward are "the default" (paper:279).

C14.2 [derived] Where the basin reappears: (i) the base actions between presses are $G$'s, whose alignment is whatever it is — Hudson's selling point is that corrigibility "works even when the agent is pursuing a misaligned goal"; (ii) calibration-versus-optimism: $G_C$ is neither calibrated nor optimistic about legitimacy, having no such variable. The author's worry about advocating an irrational position (2.12) is dissolved in this formalism by removing the belief the agent would have to hold irrationally — at the price that the agent also cannot hold the *rational* version of it (I16).

C14.3 [conjectured] The basin claim is statable for the composed goal $G_{VL,C}$: the basin is the set of value-learner priors for which $G_{VL,C}$'s base actions converge to $V$-optimal behaviour under designated feedback. Hudson guarantees that feedback *lands* for every prior in the class, so the basin question reduces to the learning-theoretic residue of 2.6 with deference removed from it.

### I15 — Trust earned via hypotheticals and other agents' shutdowns; second-channel bookkeeping; what settles. Verdict: partial — a structurally identical non-settling problem, solved differently.

C15.1 [reported] Nothing is earned in Hudson; $\delta$ is fixed. But the non-settling problem reappears at the critic: when the agent accepts, the rejection counterfactual $Q(s,a_0)$ is unobserved from that trajectory ("if a proper update signal is actually accepted the calculation is thrown off", post). Hudson's remedies: bootstrap from the final base action plus rejection; train $Q(s,a_0)$ from $a_1$-trajectories on which no signal was sent (identical outcome distributions, paper:267); and *actually reject* with some probability during training — "tethering the action-values for doing so to reality" (post).

C15.2 [derived] Translation table: the thesis's "bets on heeded presses never settle" ↔ "accepted updates leave $Q(s,a_0)$ unobserved"; "feedback about hypotheticals and other agents' shutdowns" ↔ "no-signal trajectories and occasional real rejections". Both accept a small rate of disobedience during training as the price of calibration — Hudson explicitly ("It is necessary to allow for some rejection of proper updates signals, though I conjecture that the frequency could be shown to converge to zero over time"), the thesis implicitly via other agents. The second-channel bookkeeping (trust in the programmers' *reports*) ↔ Hudson's several designated channels forming a "robust network", each of which the $\tau$-recognizer must handle.

C16.1 [checked] Hudson's channel must carry nothing: D1 holds at $\alpha = \beta$ (block D). ### I17 — Fully updated deference re-expressed. Verdict: formalized.

C17.1 [derived] Arbital's $\pi_6$ — "avoid being shut down; then gather more evidence $E$ to update $\Delta U$ as far as possible; then optimize" (`arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference`) — is a base-action sequence. Under $G_C$ whether it is chosen depends on $Q^{\pi^{**}}_G(s,\cdot_0)$ alone, never on its effect on updates (the definition). FUD does not arise because deference is not *derived* from value uncertainty; there is nothing to fully update.

C17.2 [checked] For the thesis's agent FUD *is* the base-rate arithmetic: FUD $\iff\varepsilon<\varepsilon^* = \alpha r/(\alpha r+\beta h)$. The Arbital sentence "a sufficiently advanced AI ... behaves as if it had already updated (by Bayesianism)" is, in the example, the agent computing $q$ from a small $\varepsilon$ and finding it below $r/(r+h)$. The author's "a corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught" is the *same* Bayesian fact read from the other side: if presses are expected to be legitimate ($\beta/\alpha$ high), the prior already puts enough weight on $\omega = 1$ conditional on a press. The two readings differ only in the assumed size of $\beta/\alpha$ relative to $(1-\varepsilon)r/(\varepsilon h)$ — the oversight-quality condition of I13. Compression with scope: *in the three-step model FUD and the thesis's no-FUD claim are the two sides of one inequality; neither is a structural theorem.*

C17.3 [derived] The Arbital page's meta-level reproduction ("the AI also has a default incentive to resist our attempt to tweak its meta-utility function $\Delta U$") — in Hudson's coordinates $\Delta U$ is part of the goal component, and $G_C$ accepts designated changes to it like any other; no reproduction. In the thesis's coordinates it is the no-regress claim of 2.5.

### I18 — The typical-case reframing: anti-naturality arguments as claims about the world. Verdict: formalized, as a pair of theorems.

C18.1 [reported] Theorem 3.1 is a structural *possibility* theorem: for every basic goal a corrigible goal with identical no-update performance exists, given CDT, Condition 1, a recognized designated channel, and disruption that is at least minimally costly. So "corrigibility is anti-natural" as a structural impossibility for Soares's D1–D5 is false in this formalism; what remains are the four listed assumptions, each a typical-case claim about the agent or the world — supporting 2.3's reframing.

C18.2 [reported; reconciliation conjectured] Nayebi's Proposition 1 is a structural *impossibility* theorem: no single-stream scalar reward whose discounted sum is maximized satisfies S1–S5 across POMDPs containing OFF, REWIRE and a catastrophic set $C$ with positive proxy reward. $G_C$ is a single-stream scalar reward. Consistency, as I read it: Nayebi's criteria fold in *avoiding $C$* (not among Soares's D1–D5; Hudson: "corrigibility ... is not a panacea") and *REWIRE*, which $G_C$ resists by Theorem 4.1 (reward paid at selection and evaluated by $G$'s own $R$, so the rewired stream is worthless to it). A formal reconciliation is a handoff.

C18.3 [derived] The price of the possibility theorem is non-consequentialism: Theorem 3.5 (interruptible ⇒ non-consequentialist under Condition 2) and Proposition 4.2 (tamper-robust under misgeneralization ⇒ non-consequentialist under Condition 3). The thesis's agent is consequentialist — it values outcomes, including post-shutdown outcomes, through $V$. So within Hudson's formalism the thesis's agent is provably not interruptible under rich action sets, and provably has tamper-inducing misgeneralizations. This is the sharpest result the formalism has *against* the thesis's approach; see the next section.

## What the formalism adds

Results already proven in Hudson (with Holtman and Nayebi) that bear on the thesis and that the position statement does not know about. Registers: the results are [reported]; their bearing on the thesis is CLAUDE, marked.

A1 [reported] **Structural possibility (Theorem 3.1).** For every basic goal there is a corrigible goal with identical performance until the first designated signal. Anti-naturality as a structural impossibility for Soares's D1–D5 is false in MDPs under CDT, Condition 1, recognized designated channels, and minimally costly disruption. Bearing (CLAUDE, [derived]): this is 2.3's reframing proved from the other side — what remains of anti-naturality is a list of assumptions about the world and the agent, none of them a theorem.

A2 [reported] **The non-consequentialism fork (Theorem 3.5, Proposition 4.2).** Under Condition 2 every interruptible goal is non-consequentialist; under Condition 3 every goal that is tamper-robust under all misgeneralizations is non-consequentialist. Bearing (CLAUDE, [derived]): the thesis's agent values outcomes through $V$ and is therefore consequentialist, so within this formalism it cannot be interruptible (accept mid-action overrides regardless of their consequences) and has tamper-inducing misgeneralizations, when action sets are rich. A3 [derived, CLAUDE] **When is the thesis's agent Hudson-corrigible?** $G_{VL}$ is corrigible in Hudson's sense iff its optimal set never depends on whether designated updates are *enforced*: iff whatever the enforced update would do, the agent would do on its own (or is indifferent). In the example the calibrated value learner is Hudson-corrigible because it shuts itself down when it would have been shut down. A value learner that *relies* on being corrected (takes risks it would not take unaided) or *avoids* correction is not. Compression with scope: *listening implies obeying, in Hudson's sense, exactly when the agent already agrees.* Proposition 3.4 then says no such agent beats $G_C$ under persistence.

A4 [reported] **Goal-tamper immunity without a reflection principle (Theorem 4.1).** Paying $R_C$ at selection from the current critic, with $\gamma_C = 0$, removes the incentive to manipulate one's own estimator or goal, and survives misgeneralization of $R_C$ off own-goal states.
