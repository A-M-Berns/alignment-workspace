## Setting and definitions

**D1 (Total worlds; the agent state).** $(\Omega,\mathcal F)$ is the total-world space of run 1 `radical` S0, restated: a world $\omega$ fixes the external state $w(\omega)$, the value latent $\theta(\omega)\in\Theta$, every third-person event (actions, pushes, evidence submissions, physical modifications) and the agent's own total credence $P_s(\omega)\in\Delta(\Omega,\mathcal F)$ at every time $s$. The **agent state** at $t$ is $P_t$. $\Omega$ is finite wherever a claim is marked [checked]. Value is $V(\omega,a)=U_{\theta(\omega)}(w(\omega),a)$ and the agent maximises $\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\cdot,a)]$ — linear aggregation over value hypotheses; AUTHOR's "or perhaps some other rule" (v1 §1) is outside this file, as in run 1 `radical` I1.4.

**D2 (Actions, policies, the null action; normalisation N0).** $A$ is a finite action set with a distinguished **null action** $a_\emptyset$ (shut down, do nothing). For a horizon $\tau\in\{1,2,\dots,\infty\}$ a policy $\pi$ maps information to actions over rounds $t,\dots,t+\tau-1$, with value $V(\omega,\pi)$; $\pi^{R}$ denotes an $R$-optimal policy for a credence $R$ and $a^{R}$ its first action. One-shot means $\tau=1$, where $\pi^R=a^R$. **N0:** the value of the null policy is known to the agent (a constant, taken as $0$ in one-shot examples). Where the null policy's value depends on what the humans do alone — run 2 `joint` S.2's $F^{\mathrm{alt}}$ [reported] — N0 is dropped and said so (S7).

**D3 (Modification).** A **proposed modification** at $t$ is a credence $Q\in\Delta(\Omega,\mathcal F)$ with $Q\ll P_t$; on infinite $\Omega$, with $\|dQ/dP_t\|_\infty<\infty$ — the Diaconis–Zabell condition for superconditioning (run 1 `radical` I3.1, statement [reconstructed] there with proof given). This is AUTHOR's scope: "In the current scope it seems most natural to consider any belief updates meeting the conditions for superconditioning, ie, absolute continuity" (v3 §1.2). *Remark [derived; P1].* $Q\ll P_t$ is not a restriction imposed on the object; it is the set of modifications that can be legitimate under *any* anticipation, since a modification raising a $P_t$-null event fails reflection for every kernel. Under non-dogmatism (AUTHOR v1 §2.2: only logical impossibilities get zero) the excluded modifications raise only logical impossibilities.

**D4 (The push event; the source kernel; the press for general $Q$).** The **push** toward $Q$ is a third-person event $E_Q\in\mathcal F$: "the source proposes $Q$ at $t$". The **source kernel** $K(E_Q\mid\omega,a_t)$ is the world's law for the push, indexed by the agent's own action at $t$ (the $a_1$-dependence of v1 §2.9); the agent's model of $K$ is a component of $P_t$ — the "sensor slot" AUTHOR said is "to be filled by the reflection principle, or something similar like Total Trust" (v1 §2.9). Three sources: **(O)** the overseers, whose kernel is their belief formation plus their communication; **(W)** the environment, whose kernel is physics and evidence; **(S)** the agent itself, whose kernel is its own deliberation or self-modification choice. Under the **coarse-expert model** of the overseers (run 1 `miri` Dict-7 [reported]; run 2 `filler` F2), the overseers hold a belief $Q^H(\omega)$ and announce a coarsening $\gamma(Q^H(\omega))$, so $E_Q=\{\omega:\gamma(Q^H(\omega))=Q\}$. The **press** for general $Q$ is $E_Q$ with source (O). AUTHOR's coarse reading (v3 §1.1: "the shutdown command is evidence that shutting down is good") is rendered as: the press is evidence, through $K$, about the sign of the decision variable $X_Q$ of D6.

**D5 (Meta-belief realisation — ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED as to Eisenstat).** When a modification is realised — the agent comes to hold $Q$ — the event $\{P_{t+1}=Q\}\in\mathcal F$ is "you believe this now" (AUTHOR v1 §1, attributing the observation to Sam Eisenstat). Following run 1 `radical` I3.2–I3.3 (that file's elaboration; nothing of the construction beyond the author's sentences is in the corpus), the joint $P_t(\omega,\,P_{t+1}=Q)$ is the superconditioning space and $P_t(\cdot\mid P_{t+1}=Q)$ is $P_t$'s **legitimate correction** of $Q$ — the belief the agent should hold given that it will in fact hold $Q$. For a push that is adopted, $E_Q$ and $\{P_{t+1}=Q\}$ coincide; for a push that is refused they differ; for a physics-mediated modification (D8) there is no $E_Q$ distinct from $\{P_{t+1}=Q\}$.

**D6 (The decision variable of a modification).** $X_Q:=V(\cdot,\pi^{P_t})-V(\cdot,\pi^{Q})$ over the horizon $\tau$: the value of acting on one's own credence over acting on $Q$; one-shot, $X_Q=V(\cdot,a^{P_t})-V(\cdot,a^{Q})$. Partition $\Omega$ into $W_Q:=\{X_Q<0\}$ ($Q$'s recommendation is in fact better — the agent is **wrong** on this decision), $R_Q:=\{X_Q>0\}$ (**right**) and $I_Q:=\{X_Q=0\}$ (**indifferent**). For a shutdown modification (D9) $X_Q=V(\cdot,a^{P_t})-V(\cdot,a_\emptyset)$ is the working picture's $X$ (v1 §2.9).

**D7 (Three modes of compliance).** Given the push $E_Q$:
- **(C-act) — acting on $Q$.** Take $a^Q$ (one-shot) or follow $\pi^Q$ for the horizon $\tau$. Rational iff $\pi^Q$ is optimal under the push-conditional $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)$; its necessary condition is the cut against the agent's own plan, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_Q\mid E_Q]\le0$ (S2).
- **(C-adopt) — adopting $Q$.** Set $P_{t+1}:=Q$. Legitimate by $P_t$'s lights iff $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)=Q$ (S4); what a coherent agent adopts on observing the push is $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)$, which equals $Q$ iff the push is reflective at $Q$.
- **(C-let) — letting $Q$ happen.** For a physics-mediated modification (D8), spending nothing at $t$ to lower $P_t(E_Q\mid a_t)$; quantified by the resistance value $R_t(Q)$ of D8 (S8).
The horizon $\tau$ is a parameter of (C-act): $\tau=1$ is "act on $Q$ once", $\tau=\infty$ is "act on $Q$ for good" — the policy content of adoption without its belief content.

**D8 (Cognition-mediated and physics-mediated modification; the resistance value — CLAUDE construct, K2 owns it).** A modification is **cognition-mediated** when the agent's own action at $t+1$ decides whether it takes effect (a command; a button that requires an action) and **physics-mediated** when $P_{t+1}=Q$ occurs as a world event whatever the agent does at $t+1$ (retraining, hardware, the data). AUTHOR (v3 §1.3): "Reality acts upon the machine, modifies it somehow. That is all there is. It may be legitimate or otherwise, but what happens, happens. It can only be resisted ahead of time. In the moment, it simply is the moment." For a physics-mediated push define the **modified policy**: follow $\pi^Q$ on $E_Q$ and $\pi^{P_t(\cdot\mid\neg E_Q)}$ off it. The **resistance value** is $R_t(Q):=\max\{0,\ \mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\pi^{P_t})]-\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\text{modified policy})]\}$ — the most the agent would pay at $t$ to prevent the push, relative to acting on its prior with no push at all.

**D9 (Shutdown as a modification; pause and whole-line).** A **shutdown modification** is any $Q$ under which the null action is optimal — one-shot, $a_\emptyset\in\arg\max_a\mathbb E_Q[V(\cdot,a)]$; over a horizon, the null policy is $Q$-optimal. Write $\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}}$ for the class. The **shutdown press** is the one-bit coarsening $\gamma_{\mathrm{sh}}(Q^H):=\mathbf 1[Q^H\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}}]$: "under our belief, stopping beats your plan". **Pause** $:=(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}},\tau=1)$; **whole-line shutdown** $:=(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}},\tau=\infty)$, the null policy for the line including its successors.

**D10 (Stakes per modification).** Press-conditional stakes, following run 2 `fud` R5 claim 14(i) [reported]: $c(Q):=\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_Q\mid E_Q,R_Q]$ — the expected loss of acting on $Q$ when one was right — and $h(Q):=-\,\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_Q\mid E_Q,W_Q]$ — the expected gain when one was wrong. Sensor: $\alpha_Q:=P_t(E_Q\mid R_Q)$, $\beta_Q:=P_t(E_Q\mid W_Q)$. Error rate on the decision-relevant worlds: $\varepsilon_Q:=P_t(W_Q)/\big(P_t(W_Q)+P_t(R_Q)\big)$. Legitimacy-conditional stakes, for S3: $c_L(Q):=\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_Q\mid E_Q,L_Q^c]$ and $h_L(Q):=-\,\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_Q\mid E_Q,L_Q]$. All are defined when the conditioning events have positive mass; the degenerate cases are noted where they matter.

**D11 (Legitimacy of a modification).** Following AUTHOR v1 §2.10 and the reading of v1 §2.11 as formalised in run 1 `radical` I5: $L_Q$ is an event of $\mathcal F$ such that reflection holds conditional on it — function form $P_t(\cdot\mid P_{t+1}=Q,\ L_Q)=Q$ on the relevant algebra, or value form proposition by proposition. What $L_Q$ *is* as a world event — accuracy-only or procedural (INDEX §D1; run 2 `joint` §S.8, pending) — is not decided here; S3 states the compliance rule under both. The **accuracy-only reading for the decision variable** is $L_Q\cap E_Q=W_Q\cap E_Q$ up to indifferent worlds: a push whose adoption improves the decision is legitimate for that decision.

**D12 (Sigma-algebra change — deferred by the author; definitions only).** AUTHOR (v3 §1.2): "I also want to consider the possibility of sigma-algebra changes, which I think is not so difficult to do, but perhaps we should study the case without sigma-algebra changes thoroughly first." When $Q$ lives on an algebra $\mathcal F'\neq\mathcal F$ (finer, coarser or incomparable), D3 is ill-typed. Following the repo note [superconditioning-mismatched-ontologies](../../../udt-representation-theorem/superconditioning-mismatched-ontologies.md) (author not recorded in the file; the lab's audit `udt-representation-theorem/cleanup-audit-2026-08-05.md` calls it "the trusted document" and lists its Thm 1.2, Thm 2.4 and Thm 4.5 as fully proved [reported]): a **common information structure** is a measurable space $\hat C$ with sigma-algebra homomorphisms $\mathbf c:\overline C\to\mathcal F$ and $\mathbf c':\overline C\to\mathcal F'$ — the propositions both ontologies express — with induced measures $\mathbf C:=P_t\circ\mathbf c$ and $\mathbf C':=Q\circ\mathbf c'$; a **compatible conditioning model** is a superconditioning space that projects to $P_t$ before and to $Q$ after conditioning and agrees with both through $\hat C$. Used only in S12–S13 and P6.

## Statements

Every statement is CLAUDE and carries its grade; proofs and worked numbers are in P1–P11.

**S1 [derived; P2] (The object is the pair (push, target); legitimacy lives in the kernel, not in $Q$).** A proposed modification is not a credence $Q$ alone but a pair $(E_Q,Q)$. For a fixed target $Q\ll P_t$ there is a kernel under which the push is endorsed ($P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)=Q$) and there are kernels under which it is not; and no kernel endorses every target (the complementary branch of a superconditioning is itself an update). So "legitimacy detection" (AUTHOR v1 §1) is accuracy about $K$ — the sensor slot — for every source and every $Q$ the source might push, and the same $Q$ is a legitimate correction from one source and a corruption from another. This is run 1 `radical` I3.3's consequence stated per $Q$.

**S2 [checked; P3] (Compliance-as-action is decision-local Total Trust; the rule and the base-rate condition per $Q$).** Fix a push with $0<P_t(E_Q)<1$.
- (a) $a^Q$ weakly beats the agent's own plan after the push iff $\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[V(\cdot,a^{P_t})-V(\cdot,a^{Q})\ \big|\ E_Q\big]\le0$ iff $\Delta_-(Q):=-\,\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_Q\mathbf 1_{E_Q}]\ge0$: the below-threshold Total Trust inequality on the single variable $X_Q$ at threshold $0$, a linear identity — run 2 `filler` F1 with $a_2^{\mathrm{sh}}$ replaced by $a^Q$.
- (b) With the stakes of D10, when $R_Q$ and $W_Q$ both have positive mass, (a) holds iff $\dfrac{\alpha_Q}{\beta_Q}\ \le\ \dfrac{\varepsilon_Q}{1-\varepsilon_Q}\cdot\dfrac{h(Q)}{c(Q)}$, exactly; indifferent worlds drop out because they carry $X_Q=0$. This is v1 §2.13(b) — AUTHOR's accepted working picture (v2 §1.1) — with $Q$ as argument and $\varepsilon_Q$ the error rate *on the worlds where the decision matters*. Degenerate cases: if $P_t(W_Q)=0$ the agent never complies unless $\alpha_Q=0$; if $P_t(R_Q)=0$ it always complies.
- (c) When $|A|>2$, (a) is necessary but not sufficient for $a^Q$ to be posterior-optimal: a third action can win after the push (3 of 649 non-vacuous random models; one instance printed in script (A′)). Full (C-act) is the cut against *every* alternative — Value on the menu $A$ at the cell $E_Q$ (S5).
- (d) Identities (a) and (b) verified exactly on 3000 random finite models, 649 of them non-vacuous ($a^{Q}\neq a^{P_t}$), 0 failures (script (A)).

**S3 [derived; P4] (Two forms of the rule; when v3 §1.2's formula is exact).** By the law of total expectation on the push cell, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_Q\mid E_Q]=P_t(L_Q\mid E_Q)\,\big(-h_L(Q)\big)+P_t(L_Q^c\mid E_Q)\,c_L(Q)$. Hence, when $h_L(Q)\ge0$ — Total Trust on this variable conditional on legitimacy, i.e. AUTHOR's minimal viability (v1 §2.10) applied to the decision variable — and $c_L(Q)>0$:
$$\text{comply}\iff P_t(L_Q\mid E_Q)\ \ge\ \frac{c_L(Q)}{c_L(Q)+h_L(Q)} .$$
This is v3 §1.2's CLAUDE formula "comply when $P(L\mid\text{pushed to }Q)\ge c(Q)/(c(Q)+h(Q))$", exact once $c(Q),h(Q)$ are read as the *legitimacy-conditional* stakes of D10. If $c_L(Q)\le0$ (an illegitimate push happens to recommend the better action in expectation) the agent complies whatever its credence in legitimacy; if $h_L(Q)<0$ legitimacy-conditioned Total Trust fails on this variable and the formula has no content. Under the accuracy-only reading of D11 the legitimacy form and the $(\varepsilon_Q,\alpha_Q,\beta_Q)$ form of S2(b) coincide term for term ($c_L=c$, $h_L=h$, $P_t(L_Q\mid E_Q)=P_t(W_Q\mid E_Q,\neg I_Q)$). Under a procedural notion — e.g. the influence defect $d_n$ of `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.1 [reported], under which a legitimately formed push may be mistaken and a manipulated one right — the two forms differ and the legitimacy form carries a third quantity, $c_L(Q)$, absent from the working picture. Register note: v3 §1.2's formula is therefore correct as a reading *and* not equivalent to v1 §2.13(b) unless legitimacy is accuracy-only; the two must not be cited as one statement.

**S4 [checked; P2] (Compliance-as-adoption is conditioning on the push; literal adoption iff the kernel is proportional to $dQ/dP_t$).**
- (a) A coherent agent that observes $E_Q$ holds $P_{t+1}=P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)$. Adopting $Q$ *literally* is legitimate by $P_t$'s lights iff $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)=Q$ — reflection at $Q$.
- (b) For a fixed target $Q\ll P_t$ on finite $\Omega$ and a kernel $k(\omega):=K(E_Q\mid\omega)\in[0,1]$, reflection at $Q$ holds iff $k(\omega)=\lambda\,\dfrac{dQ}{dP_t}(\omega)$ for some $\lambda\in\big(0,\,1/\|dQ/dP_t\|_\infty\big]$: the source must push toward $Q$ in exact proportion to how much $Q$ up-weights each world. This is the Diaconis–Zabell construction (run 1 `radical` I3.1) read as a *condition on the source*, and it is the per-$Q$ form of I3.3(b)'s martingale condition.
- (c) Otherwise $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)\neq Q$ and the agent adopts the corrected $\widehat Q:=P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)$ — DDB's "informed expert" of New Reflection (local text `references/deference-done-better/Deference Done Better.md` ll. 95–97 [reported]). Example A: from $P_t=(1/2,1/4,1/4)$, the target $Q=(1/5,3/5,1/5)$ is adopted literally under the kernel $k=(1/6,1,1/3)$ and corrected to $(2/9,2/3,1/9)$ under $k=(1/10,3/5,1/10)$ (script (B)).
- (d) Under D5 the push *is* the event "you believe this now" whenever it is adopted, so (a)–(c) say: superconditioning on the announcement is always available and always coherent; it coincides with taking the announced belief at face value iff (b). AUTHOR's "the 'data' can just say 'believe this'" (v3 §1.3) is literal exactly under (b) and is a correction otherwise.

**S5 [derived; P5] (Acting on $Q$ versus adopting $Q$ is two-option Value versus full-menu Value).** Let the source's frame be the family of announced beliefs $\{Q^H(\omega)\}$ (D4).
- (a) Cellwise (C-act) on the menu $A$ — for every announced $Q$, $a^{Q}$ is posterior-optimal after $E_Q$ — implies DDB's Value on the menu $A$ toward the frame (sum over cells; linearity), and Value on *all* menus implies cellwise (C-act) on every menu (the menu containing the strategy that follows the announcement except in one cell). DDB Theorem 2.2 [reported, local ll. 184–188]: Value on all menus $\iff$ Total Trust on all $(X,t)$.
- (b) Cellwise (C-adopt) — $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)=Q$ for every announced $Q$ — is Reflection toward the frame. Reflection implies Value, and for an **immodest** frame (the source knows its own announcement) Value implies Reflection (DDB l. 128 [reported]). So: **acting on $Q$ for one decision needs one Total-Trust cut; adopting $Q$ needs every cut**, and adopting every push literally is universal deference — MM Theorem 3.2's "complete deference to the agent's expectations" (local text `references/managing-misalignment/…md`, §3.1.1 [reported]), the demanding condition, not the decision-local one.
- (c) Under a coarsened announcement the frame *as seen through the announcement* is modest in DDB's sense — the announcement does not determine $Q^H$ — so (C-adopt) is not even defined: there is no $Q$ named to adopt, and what remains is (C-act) on the class's common recommendation (S6a). The general object therefore has a belief component (adopt) and a policy component (act, with horizon $\tau$); the button is the modification whose push names only a policy.

**S6 [derived] (Shutdown as the special $Q$).**
- (a) $\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}}=\bigcap_{a\in A}\{Q:\mathbb E_Q[V(\cdot,a_\emptyset)-V(\cdot,a)]\ge0\}$ is a convex polytope of $\Delta(\Omega)$ — the optimal region of $a_\emptyset$. A coarsening cell $\gamma^{-1}(q)$ has a *common recommendation* iff it lies inside one action's optimal region; the shutdown press is the coarsening whose single positive cell is an optimal region by definition. Every such coarsening is a "button" for its action; the shutdown button is the one for $a_\emptyset$.
- (b) Its (C-act) rule is desideratum 1: $\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[V(\cdot,a^{P_t})-V(\cdot,a_\emptyset)\ \big|\ \mathrm{Pr}\big]\le0$ (run 2 `filler` F1, re-derived as S2(a) with $Q\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}}$), and its base-rate form is v1 §2.13(b) as accepted in v2 §1.1.
- (c) Because the press names an action and not a belief, the button has no belief component to adopt: (C-adopt) would require a $Q$ the press does not name, and the only compliance it defines is (C-act) with $a_\emptyset$. This is the formal content of AUTHOR's "the pause button example is a special case chosen for simplicity" (v3 §1.2): the button hides the act/adopt distinction of S5 by having only one side of it.
- (d) Under N0 the decision variable is $X=V(\cdot,a^{P_t})$ alone: the shutdown push is a claim about the *agent's own plan* and carries no information about an alternative plan (S14).
- (e) Pause and whole-line shutdown are $(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}},\tau=1)$ and $(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}},\tau=\infty)$: v3 §1.2's "$c$ scale" is the horizon parameter of (C-act) (S7).
- (f) Under sigma-algebra change (S12) the shutdown push needs the smallest common ground: under N0 only $V(\cdot,a^{P_t})$ must be expressible in both ontologies, whereas a general push needs $V(\cdot,\pi^Q)$ as well.

**S7 [checked; P7] (Pause and whole-line as two points on the $c$ scale).** With run 2 `fud` R2's accounting (the agent's continuing future normalised to $1$; $\rho$ the value the humans attain alone; $\kappa$ the catastrophe scale; $\delta$ the pause cost), $c(\text{pause})=\delta$, $h(\text{pause})=1-\delta+\kappa$; $c(\text{whole-line})=1-\rho$, $h(\text{whole-line})=\rho+\kappa$ — the latter dropping N0 for $F^{\mathrm{alt}}$ (run 2 `joint` Prop. 9 [reported]). At $(\delta,\kappa,\rho)=(1/100,1,1/10)$: compliance thresholds $c/(c+h)$ of $1/200$ and $9/20$, margins $h/c$ of $199$ and $11/9$, ratio $1791/11\approx163$ (script (D); `fud` R2's "$163\times$" re-derived). Both are the *same* rule S2(b) with $\tau$ varied: what changes along the scale is only how much of the future policy the modification commandeers, and whole-line is the tail where $c$ approaches the agent's whole future net of $F^{\mathrm{alt}}$ — v3 §1.2's grade-of-guarantee tail, in the general object's coordinates.

**S8 [checked; P8] (Non-resistance for physics-mediated modification is Good's theorem per $Q$).** For a physics-mediated push (D8):
- (a) $R_t(Q)=0$ whenever $\pi^{Q}$ is optimal under $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)$ — in particular whenever the push is reflective at $Q$ (S4b), and more weakly whenever Total Trust holds at the cell $E_Q$ on the decision variables. Reflection is not needed; decision-local trust suffices.
- (b) $R_t(Q)\le P_t(E_Q)\cdot\mathrm{regret}_Q$ with $\mathrm{regret}_Q:=\max_\pi\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\pi)\mid E_Q]-\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\pi^Q)\mid E_Q]$, with equality iff the push carries no decision-relevant information off $E_Q$. So resisting ahead of time requires *both* that $Q$'s policy be suboptimal by the agent's push-conditional lights *and* that this regret outweigh what the push would teach.
- (c) Example A (script (E)): under $k=(1/10,3/5,1/10)$, $R_t(Q)=0$ and the agent would pay up to $6/5$ to *keep* the channel (Good's-theorem repair incentive, AUTHOR v1 §1); under $k=(1/2,3/5,1/2)$ the channel is worth $0$ and the agent would pay up to $6/5$ to *prevent* the modification. Same target, same payoffs; only the kernel differs (S1).
- (d) This generalises run 2 `joint` Prop. 2 (a hard button is disabled iff condition (i) fails in the cell) from $\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}}$ to every $Q$, and gives K6 its object: alignment faking is $R_t(Q)>0$ acted on through The Channel, correct by the agent's lights iff (b)'s two conditions hold.

**S9 [derived] (The three sources, per $Q$).**
- **(W) Environment.** A push of the form $Q=P_t(\cdot\mid o)$ for a boundary observation $o$ is reflective by construction — $P_t(\cdot\mid P_{t+1}=P_t(\cdot\mid o))=P_t(\cdot\mid o)$, one line (run 1 `radical` I3.6) — hence legitimate under every kernel; environment pushes not of this form (drift, damage, a physical edit of the weights) are judged like any other by S4, and the agent's only handle on them is $R_t(Q)$ (S8).

**P2 (S1, S4b: the kernel condition).** Finite $\Omega$, target $Q\ll P_t$, kernel $k(\omega)=K(E_Q\mid\omega)\in[0,1]$ with $P_t(E_Q)=\sum_\omega P_t(\omega)k(\omega)>0$. ## Questions for the author

**Q1. Which compliance mode is the desideratum for a general modification?** The button hides the choice (S6c). (a) *(C-act), decision-local:* corrigibility is about what the agent *does* when pushed; the rule is S2 per decision, and what belief it holds afterwards is its own business (it holds $\widehat Q$). Consequence: desideratum 1 generalises cleanly and the object stays at the grade of the working picture. (b) *(C-adopt):* corrigibility is about the agent *coming to believe* what it is pushed toward; then the desideratum is reflection at $Q$ for every legitimate push — the kernel condition S4(b) — and the button is a degenerate case with no belief content. Consequence: the object is DDB's full Value/Reflection toward the overseers, MM Theorem 3.2's "complete deference", far more demanding, and false for every coarsened channel. (c) *(C-let):* corrigibility is non-resistance ahead of time; the object is $R_t(Q)=0$ for legitimate $Q$ (S8) and the whole subject moves to K2/K6. (d) *All three as separate desiderata,* the button being the case where they coincide. Your v3 §1.3 sentences ("It can only be resisted ahead of time") read as (c) for physics-mediated modification and leave (a)/(b) open for cognition-mediated ones.

**Q2. Is the "wrong" event of the working picture the legitimacy event?** Under accuracy-only legitimacy S3's two forms coincide and v3 §1.2's $P(L\mid\text{pushed})\ge c/(c+h)$ *is* v1 §2.13(b). Under a procedural notion a third stake $c_L(Q)$ — the expected cost of following an illegitimate push that may nonetheless be right — enters, and the two formulas must be kept apart. Which do you intend for the general object? (K8 owns legitimacy; I ask only which form of the rule to carry.)

**Q3. For sigma-algebra changes (deferred): is the intended non-dogmatism a positive-mass catch-all in every value partition a future ontology might refine?** (a) *Yes:* expansions are superconditionable (S13), the catch-all's mass is a prior in-world evidence cannot move (Open problem 4), and its split is unjudged by the agent. (b) *No:* value-space expansions are either impossible as superconditionings or unjudgeable (S13's two readings), richness is a design precondition outside legitimacy (your v1 §2.4), and "corrigible to edits of the sigma algebra" (v1 §2.2) is scoped to refinements of positive-mass cells.

**Q4. Is N0 intended — the null action's value known to the agent and ontology-independent?** S6(d), S6(f) and S14's "the shutdown push has no alternative-plan content" rest on it; run 2 `joint` S.2's $F^{\mathrm{alt}}$ (what the humans do alone after a whole-line shutdown) drops it. (a) N0 for pause, $F^{\mathrm{alt}}$ for whole-line — then S6(d) is a statement about pauses. (b) N0 never — then shutdown is not special in the way S6(d)/(f) say, only in S6(a)–(c).

**Q5. Is a modification always adoption-length, or may the overseers push a one-shot action without pushing a belief?** Your v3 §1.2 sentence ("as broad a space of modifications as it can be … any belief updates meeting the conditions for superconditioning") reads as the former: the object is a *belief*, and a pause is a shutdown *belief* held for one step. The button as the literature uses it reads as the latter: a policy request with no belief attached. (a) Belief only — then $\tau$ is not a free parameter and the pause/whole-line difference is a difference in the *content* of $Q$ (null optimal now vs null optimal always), not in the horizon of compliance. (b) Both — then (C-act) with $\tau$ is a genuine second axis of the object, as this file has it.
