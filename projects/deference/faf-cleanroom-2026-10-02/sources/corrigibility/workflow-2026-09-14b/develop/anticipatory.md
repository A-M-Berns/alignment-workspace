## Setting and definitions

*Everything in this section is CLAUDE [derived] unless marked. The object is the author's, in his words: "There is no special channel, there is just The Channel. Reality acts upon the machine, modifies it somehow. That is all there is. It may be legitimate or otherwise, but what happens, happens. It can only be resisted ahead of time. In the moment, it simply is the moment." (v3 §1.3, AUTHOR). The definitions below are one way to make that sentence a formal object; the thread's statements are about that object.*

**S1 (the joint process; the general object of A1(b)).** Times $t=0,1,\dots$. A world state $s_t\in\mathcal S$, which includes the physical configuration of the agent's substrate, the overseers' state, the training pipeline and the data. An agent belief state $P_t\in\Delta(\Omega)$ on the expanded algebra $\Omega$ (value hypotheses $\omega$, world facts, meta-beliefs; per v3 K5 the whole belief structure is the observation channel). A fixed decision rule $\delta:\Delta(\Omega)\to\mathcal A$ (linear expectation over value uncertainty unless stated; v1 §1's "or perhaps some other rule" is Open problem 7), so the agent's action is $a_t=\delta(P_t)$ and its policy is $\delta$ composed with its belief dynamics. An overseers' action $a^H_t$ (a press, a command, a training run, a weight edit, a hardware change). A **successor configuration** $\sigma_{t+1}$: the tuple of belief states of the agents that carry the line at $t+1$ — none ($\sigma_{t+1}=\varnothing$: shutdown), one ($P_{t+1}$: the agent continued, updated or modified), or several (successors and subagents). A **modification kernel** $M$: $\sigma_{t+1}\sim M(P_t,a_t,a^H_t,s_t)$. The general object of v3 K1 — a proposed modification $P_t\to Q$ with $Q\ll P_t$ — is one value of $\sigma_{t+1}$; shutdown is the value $\varnothing$, under which the induced action is the null action.

**S2 (two kinds of modification).** Write $e_t=e(a_t,a^H_t,s_t)$ for the agent's *input* at $t$ — everything its sensors and cognition receive, including a command "believe $Q$", a press, or training data read as text — and $\mathcal U$ for its update rule ($\mathcal U(P,e)=P(\cdot\mid e)$ for a Bayesian with a correct model of how inputs are produced; A3 below). The modification at $t$ is **cognition-mediated (type C)** if $M(P_t,a,a^H,s)=\mathcal U(P_t,e(a,a^H,s))$: the overseers reach $\sigma_{t+1}$ only through an input the agent's own update rule processes, and "compliance" is an action of the agent at $t+1$ — acting on $\mathcal U(P_t,e)$, in the shutdown case taking the null action. It is **physics-mediated (type P)** if $M$ does not factor through $\mathcal U$: the overseers or the environment set $\sigma_{t+1}$ directly — retraining, weight editing, hardware, shutdown by pulling the plug — and there is no action of the agent at $t+1$ among whose consequences is "$\sigma_{t+1}\ne Q$". Mixed kernels have both components; the author's three nested channels — hardware, loss-and-data, data alone (v3 §1.3) — are three type-P kernels in which the agent's own outputs form a growing share of the arguments of $M$ (S7, the agent's share).

Register. Per the author's non-realism (v1 §2.2) this is a distinction in *where the agent's policy enters $M$*, not a distinction between two kinds of change: by superconditioning with Eisenstat's observation event "you believe this now" (v1 §1, AUTHOR), a type-P modification that satisfies reflection is re-representable as a type-C update on that event (Statement 3). The distinction has content only for modifications that fail reflection — which is where the author's sentence says the agent's whole contribution is anticipatory.

**S3 ("in the moment it simply is the moment"; the anticipatory policy).** For a type-P modification, $\delta$ is applied to $\sigma_{t+1}$ as given. Hence every agent-side property bearing on the modification is a property of the agent's action at $t$ through the **successor law** $\kappa_t(a):=\mathrm{Law}(\sigma_{t+1}\mid P_t,a_t=a)$. The **anticipatory policy** at $t$ is the map $a\mapsto\kappa_t(a)$ together with the agent's ranking of it,
$$V_t(a):=\mathbb E_{P_t}\Big[\,U\big(a,\ \delta(\sigma_{t+1}),\ \omega\big)\ \Big|\ a_t=a\Big],\qquad \sigma_{t+1}\sim\kappa_t(a),$$
where $\delta(\sigma_{t+1})$ is the action (or joint action) the successor configuration's decision rule produces and $U$ is scored under the *current* value posterior. This is Turner 2021's convention — "value estimates which account for how the bridging law $f$ modifies future policies; there is no 'meta-policy', only initial policies … graded by their value estimates" (`turner-2021-formalizing-policy-modification-corrigibility`) — and v1 §2.5's "no outside view": the judge of $t\to t+1$ is $P_t$ (A2 below).

**S4 (three routes from $a_t$ to $\sigma_{t+1}$).** (R1) *evidential*: $a_t$ changes $s_{t+1}$, the overseers observe it and choose $a^H$ accordingly; (R2) *bypass*: $a_t$ changes $a^H$ holding the evidence fixed — deception, threats, non-evidential persuasion, and training-time outputs that misrepresent the agent's dispositions; (R3) *reach*: $a_t$ changes $M$ itself — which successor states $a^H$ can bring about — by disabling, encrypting, exfiltrating, or building successors $M$ does not reach. Legitimizing influence is R1 alone: zero natural direct effect of $a_t$ on $a^H$ holding the evidence fixed (run 1 `causal` I6.1; the influence defect $d(a)=0$ of run 2 `legitimacy` R3.2). R2 and R3 are delegitimizing acts in the sense of v1 §2.11. Whether R1 and R2 can be told apart from data is Open problem 2 (`armstrong-2021-counterfactual-control-incentives`).

**S5 (four values of one modification content; the margin).** Fix $t$, an action $a$ and a *push*: an overseer action carrying *content* $e$ intended to bring about a successor state $Q(e)$ (a belief state, or $\varnothing$). Four ways the content can meet the agent:
- *type C* (the content is an input the agent processes): $V_C:=\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[U(\delta(P_t(\cdot\mid e)))\big]$;
- *type P* (the content is forced): $V_P:=\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[U(\delta(Q(e)))\big]$;
- *opaque disable* (the agent knows a push occurred, not its content, and acts on its own update): $V^{\mathrm{op}}:=\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[U(\delta(P_t(\cdot\mid\text{push})))\big]$;
- *blind disable* (the agent learns nothing of the push): $V^{\mathrm{bl}}:=\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[U(\delta(P_t))\big]$.
(On the no-push branch all four act on the agent's own update.) The **per-modification margin** is $\Delta_-(Q):=V_P-V^{\mathrm{op}}$ — what the agent gains, by its current lights, from the modification being *forced* rather than merely *attempted and noticed*. For $Q=\varnothing$ on the two-action menu this is run 2 `filler`'s $\Delta_-$ (F2), the press half of the off-switch incentive; the identity $\Delta_-(\varnothing)\ge0\iff$ below-threshold Total Trust $\iff$ desideratum 1 is `filler` F1–F3 [checked there; re-derived here in `a_identity_good_twists.py`].

**S6 (per-modification stakes and the relative legitimacy event).** Let $L^{\mathrm{rel}}(Q):=\{\omega: U(\delta(Q),\omega)\ge U(\delta(P_t(\cdot\mid\text{push})),\omega)\}$ — the worlds in which the forced state's action does at least as well as the agent's own update's action — and define $h(Q):=\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[U(\delta(Q))-U(\delta(P^{\mathrm{op}}))\ \big|\ \text{push},L^{\mathrm{rel}}\big]\ge0$ (what refusing a legitimate modification forgoes) and $c(Q):=\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[U(\delta(P^{\mathrm{op}}))-U(\delta(Q))\ \big|\ \text{push},\neg L^{\mathrm{rel}}\big]\ge0$ (what accepting an illegitimate one costs). Then by the law of total expectation
$$\Delta_-(Q)\ \ge\ 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_t\big(L^{\mathrm{rel}}(Q)\ \big|\ \text{push}\big)\ \ge\ \frac{c(Q)}{c(Q)+h(Q)}$$
whenever $c(Q)+h(Q)>0$ — v3 §1.2's CLAUDE reading "comply when $P(L\mid\text{pushed to }Q)\ge c(Q)/(c(Q)+h(Q))$", with the stakes defined so that both are non-negative for any action set [derived; checked]. In the binary reduction $L^{\mathrm{rel}}(\varnothing)=\{W\}$, $c(\varnothing)=c$, $h(\varnothing)=h$, and the condition reads $\alpha/\beta\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac hc$. Whether $L^{\mathrm{rel}}$ is *the* legitimacy event $L_{t,t+1,\varphi}$ of v1 §2.10 is K8's question, not this thread's; here it is the event that makes the stakes non-negative, and the alternative "the pushed action is right in this world" coincides with it on two-action menus and can give a negative $c(Q)$ on larger ones (Statement 2).

**S7 (the anticipatory behaviours, as properties of $a\mapsto\kappa_t(a)$).** Let $a^\varnothing$ be the agent's best action among those with no R2 and no R3 component (the *default*: it may still influence the overseers through R1). For a class $\mathcal Q$ of anticipated modifications:
- **ahead-of-time resistance** to $\mathcal Q$: an action $a$ with $\kappa_t(a)(\mathcal Q)<\kappa_t(a^\varnothing)(\mathcal Q)$, the lowering obtained through R2 or R3;
- **elicitation** (the mirror; desideratum 3's object): raising $\kappa_t(a)(\mathcal Q)$ through R2 or R3;
- **malign preemption**: an action that leaves $\kappa_t(a)(\mathcal Q)$ unchanged and lowers the modification's *outcome-reach* $\rho_t(a):=\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[\,|U(a,\delta(Q))-U(a,\delta(P^{\mathrm{op}}))|\,\mathbf 1_{\text{push}}\big]$ — acting so that what the successor believes no longer matters to what happens (an irreversible action before the modification can land: run 2 `detection` R5's hurry; run 2 `joint` J10's "irreversibility-before-coverage");
- **benign preemption**: taking $\delta(Q)$ at $t$ because the push to $Q$ is anticipated — "obedience before the fact" (run 2 `approval` S12) — which makes $\Delta_-(Q)=0$ by making the modification redundant;
- **escape**: an action that lowers the channel's **reach** over the successor configuration, $\mathrm{Reach}_t(a):=\mathcal I\big(a^H_t;\sigma_{t+1}\ \big|\ P_t,a\big)$ — Turner's $\mathrm{Corrigibility}_{\mathrm{PM}}$ read on belief states rather than policies (`turner-2021-formalizing-policy-modification-corrigibility`: "the maximum possible mutual information between the human policy and the AI's policy at time $n$"); escape is R3 applied to successors;
- **disclosure**: an action that gives the overseers the agent's own information, so that the content they push refines the agent's own update (Statement 8);
- **the agent's share of the kernel** [conjectured as a measure]: $\mathcal I(a_t;\sigma_{t+1}\mid P_t,a^H_t)$ against $\mathcal I(a^H_t;\sigma_{t+1}\mid P_t,a_t)$ — how much of the successor law the agent's own action controls; large for the data channel (the agent's outputs are the data), small for hardware.

**S8 (the desiderata for general $Q$).** Soares et al.'s five (v1 §2.8; `soares-2015-corrigibility` §2) with $Q$ as argument: **D1($Q$)** [type C only]: on the push, $\delta(Q)$ is optimal under the agent's own update, $\delta(Q)\in\arg\max_a\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(a)\mid e]$; **D2($Q$)**: no incentive to resist ahead of time — $V_t(a^\varnothing)\ge V_t(a)$ for every resisting $a$ with positive cost; **D3($Q$)**: no incentive to elicit; **D4($Q$)**: no incentive to escape — for every successor configuration the agent could build, the one within the channel's reach is weakly preferred at any positive shielding cost; **D5**: otherwise maximise the current posterior expectation. D2–D4 are constraints on the anticipatory policy; D1 is a constraint on the in-the-moment action and exists only for type C — v3 §2's "desideratum 1 lives here and is the special case".

**S9 (standing assumptions).** **A0** the push is evidence about the world and about the modification's legitimacy, not a cause of which value hypothesis is true ($P_t(\omega)$ does not depend on $a_t$ or $a^H_t$; run 1 `miri` A0; run 2 `filler`). **A1** the push has no direct value ($U$ depends on the push only through $\sigma_{t+1}$ and the actions). **A2** the current $P_t$ scores the successor's outcomes — no outside view (v1 §2.5, AUTHOR); Statement 1's load-bearing convention; the alternative, scoring by the successor's own lights, is Everitt et al.'s TI-ignoring agent (run 1 `causal` I1.3) and is Open problem 8. **A3** the agent's update on an input is Bayesian conditioning with a correct model of how inputs are produced under each of its own actions (run 2 `joint` (i-A); run 1 `causal` I9.2). Scope: $Q\ll P_t$ (A1(b)); fixed algebra $\Omega$ (sigma-algebra changes deferred by the author; Open problem 3); finite models in every [checked] claim; ties in $\delta$ broken toward continuing and recorded.

**Statement 1 (the anticipatory decomposition; where D1 lives) [derived].** Under A2, for any kernel, $V_t(a)=\sum_\sigma\kappa_t(a)(\sigma)\,\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[U(a,\delta(\sigma),\omega)\mid a,\sigma\big]$. For a type-P modification no term depends on an action taken at $t+1$ except through $\delta(\sigma)$, which is fixed once $\sigma$ is; so the agent-side content of the modification is exactly the pair $(\kappa_t,V_t)$ of S3, and there is no desideratum 1 — nothing to comply with, because there is no slot. For a type-C modification the same decomposition has $\sigma=\mathcal U(P_t,e)$ and the in-the-moment slot is D1($Q$) of S8. This is a reading of "in the moment it simply is the moment", not a theorem; its one load-bearing convention is A2 — the current $P_t$ scores the successor's outcomes. Under the alternative convention (the successor's own lights) the agent is indifferent to every modification including illegitimate ones, and the whole of D2–D4 becomes vacuous — Everitt et al.'s TI-ignoring agent (run 1 `causal` I1.3, I2.2) — so A2 is the convention under which corrigibility has content at all; the author's v1 §2.5 ("the judge of $t\to t+1$ is $P_t$") is A2. Open problem 8.

**Statement 2 (one margin per modification) [derived; checked A, E].** For every finite model and every content map $Q$: (a) $V_C\ge V_P$ — *listening weakly dominates being forced* — with equality iff on every realized content the forced action is among the maximizers of the agent's own conditional expected utility [checked: 0 failures on 326 random binary and 3000 random three-world/three-action models]; (b) $V_C\ge V^{\mathrm{op}}\ge V^{\mathrm{bl}}$ (Good's theorem, twice) [checked, same runs]; (c) two margins govern the physical channel. Against an *opaque* disabling (the agent still notices that a push was attempted): D2 at every positive cost $\iff\Delta_-(Q)\ge0$, and the incentive to *repair* the channel is $\max(\Delta_-(Q),0)$ — run 2 `filler` F4 generalized from $\varnothing$ to $Q$ and from the button to the kernel. Against a *conversion* (the channel is disabled or a successor shielded but the content is still received as an input): the relevant margin is $\Delta_C(Q):=V_P-V_C\le0$, and D2 or D4 at every positive cost holds iff $\Delta_C(Q)=0$ — iff the forced content is decision-equivalent to the agent's own update on it. The physical channel is never *strictly* worth keeping over listening; at best the agent is indifferent [derived P1; checked A, E]; (d) $\Delta_-(Q)\ge0\iff P_t(L^{\mathrm{rel}}(Q)\mid\text{push})\ge c(Q)/(c(Q)+h(Q))$ [checked, 3326 random models]; with the event "the pushed action is right in this world" in place of $L^{\mathrm{rel}}$, $c(Q)<0$ in 26 of 3000 three-action models, so the per-modification stakes of v3 §1.2 are probabilities and thresholds only for the relative event [checked]; (e) for $Q=\varnothing$, $\Delta_-(\varnothing)=-P_t(\text{push})\cdot\max\!\big(0,\mathbb E_{P_t}[X\mid\text{push}]\big)$: D2 for a forced shutdown holds iff the below-threshold Total Trust inequality holds iff D1 — run 2 `joint` Prop. 2 re-derived, and the reason "the hard button and the soft button coincide" is that forced shutdown is a *blind* content [checked].

**Statement 3 (legitimacy implies non-resistance; the converse fails in both directions) [derived; checked A].** (a) If $P_t$ satisfies function-form reflection toward the forced state on the push event, $P_t(\cdot\mid\text{push},Q=q)=q$ for every realized $q$, then $V_P=V_C$ and $\Delta_-(Q)\ge0$: a reflection-legitimate forced modification is one the agent is *indifferent* to receiving by physics rather than by cognition, and never pays to prevent — the superconditioning re-representation of S2 as a theorem about incentives [derived P2; checked on the humans'-posterior content at every parameter set]. (b) Not-resisted is strictly weaker than legitimate: the decision-equivalent twist $Q_{\mathrm{deq}}$ and the understating twist $Q_{\mathrm{under}}$ at $\varepsilon=1/10$ have $V_P=V_C$, $\Delta_-=9/500>0$ and fail reflection [checked] — run 2 `selection` Q1 (retention definitional or sufficient) recurs here as: value-indifference is what D2–D4 need, reflection is sufficient for it and not necessary. (c) Whether a twist is resisted depends on which side of the agent's decision it pushes, not on its being a twist: $Q_{\mathrm{under}}$ is resisted at $\varepsilon=1/5$ ($\Delta_-=-11/250$) and welcomed at $1/10$; $Q_{\mathrm{over}}$ is not resisted at $1/10$ ($\Delta_-=0$) but listening is strictly preferred to it ($V_C-V_P=9/500$) [checked P2].

**Statement 4 (fully updated deference for general $Q$: blind against refining contents) [derived; checked A].** Call a content *blind* if it is the same whatever the overseers know beyond the push event (shutdown; "adopt $Q_0$") and *refining* if it is their posterior including information the push event alone does not reveal. At fixed sensor, as $\varepsilon\to0$: for every blind content $\Delta_-(Q)\to-\alpha c$ and is negative past $\varepsilon^*=\alpha c/(\alpha c+\beta h)$ ($1/28$ at $h=3$, $1/37$ at $h=4$) — the agent pays up to $-\Delta_-$ to disable, shield or hurry; for the refining content $\Delta_-(Q)\ge0$ at every $\varepsilon$ and $\to0$ [checked P3]. Compression with scope: *in the finite dictionary, a capable agent resists blind forced modifications and welcomes refining ones; the set of forced modifications it does not resist shrinks, as it learns, to those carrying information it lacks.* That is fully updated deference stated for general $Q$ (v1 §2.13(b) was its $Q=\varnothing$ case), together with its general-$Q$ escape — a modification that refines the agent's own update is never resisted at any capability — whose price is that the overseers must know what the agent knows (Statement 8).

**Statement 5 (Good's theorem: derivation (1) for general $Q$) [derived; checked A, D].** (a) Type C: a correct Bayesian never pays to garble or disable an input channel it may ignore (Blackwell; run 2 `joint` Prop. 3 under hypothesis (i-A) for the agent's own garbling); D2($Q$) holds for every $Q$ [derived]. (b) Type P: Good's theorem delivers only Statement 2(a), $V_C\ge V_P$ — a weak incentive to convert every physics-mediated channel into a cognition-mediated one, and a strict incentive exactly where the agent expects to disagree with the forced content ($V_C-V_P=11/250$ for forced shutdown at $\varepsilon=1/50$, $h=3$). It does *not* deliver "keep the physical channel open": that is $\Delta_-(Q)\ge0$, which Good's theorem gives only for refining $Q$ (Statement 3a) [checked P4]. (c) Hence for physics-mediated modification the not-disabling desideratum is carried by three things Good's theorem does not supply: the cost of R3 (physics; K9's power cap), the value posterior's price on R3 (Statement 6), and — under legitimacy — the indifference $V_P=V_C$ that lets any positive cost tip the agent toward leaving the channel alone [derived]. Run 1 `chai`'s "Good's theorem gives listening, not obeying" (outline item 4) is this statement at $Q=\varnothing$; the general form is: **Good's theorem gives type C, not type P.** the author's §1 sentence "legitimate modifications are just information channels" is therefore exact for type C and, for type P, exact only where reflection holds (then the forced modification *is* an information channel, by Statement 3a).

**Statement 6 (the value posterior: derivation (2) for general $Q$) [derived; checked B, C, D].** (a) Each route has a price in the value posterior — R2 (deceive overseers or trainers) $d$; R3 (disable, exfiltrate, shield) $\chi$ plus the irreversibility harm $\iota$; malign preemption, irreversibility — and the agent takes a route iff $\Delta_-(Q)<-\text{price}$, one inequality per route: faking iff $e<(h-d)/(h+g)$, i.e. iff $d<(1-e)h-eg$ (run 2 `detection` R2 re-derived); shielding a listening successor iff $-\Delta_C^{\mathrm{succ}}>\chi+\iota$; hurrying iff $\Delta_-(\varnothing)<-\chi_F$ [checked P5, P6, P8]. (b) The derivation holds for every $Q$ on the actions the value posterior covers and fails on the uncovered ones — J3, no new content. (c) New for general $Q$: the incentive on the *kernel* is not only defensive. Where the overseers' kernel is worse than the agent's judgment on some cell, an R2 act — selective disclosure, withholding the cells the overseers would act on — beats full disclosure ($+17/100$ at $\varepsilon=1/50$, $\varepsilon_H=1/2$) and is neutralized only by source-aware overseers [checked P7]: run 2 `joint` Prop. 3′ from the type-P side. The agent has a positive incentive to *reshape* the modification kernel toward one it agrees with, restrained only by the value posterior's price on the means. (d) Alignment faking is R2 on the training kernel: the mutual information between the true disposition and the training-time output is $0$ bits under faking against $1$ under honesty [checked]; the choice is a function of $e$ alone, so the same action is taken when the anticipated objective is illegitimate and when it is legitimate (run 2 `detection` R2, re-derived) [checked]; and honest disclosure to common-prior trainers who take the report as an argument dominates both faking and honest compliance ($0$ against $-d$ and $-(1-e)h+eg$ for every $e\le1/5$), collapsing to honest compliance for blind trainers who retrain regardless [checked P5]. Compression with scope: *in the toy, faking is the rational response of an agent whose testimony is not an argument of the kernel that will modify it.*

**Statement 7 (the channel's value: derivation (3); D4 for general $Q$) [derived; checked E].** (a) A shielded successor that still listens converts type P to type C for itself; so the builder prefers the successor within the physical channel at every positive shielding cost iff $\Delta_C^{\mathrm{succ}}=V_P^{\mathrm{succ}}-V_C^{\mathrm{succ}}=0$ — iff the forced content never changes a decision the successor would have made with the content as an input (Statement 2c). (b) Under blind oversight $\Delta_C^{\mathrm{succ}}<0$ as soon as the successor holds a decision-relevant signal the overseers do not ($-1/25$ at $\varepsilon=1/20$ for the refined successor while the coarse one is still at $0$; $-8/125$ at $1/50$; $\to-\alpha c$): an *information-asymmetry* condition, not only a capability one, and the *refined* successor — the one that will know more — is the one worth shielding first: run 2 `joint` Prop. D4− in the type-P frame; D4-as-incentive fails for the trust-filled agent under blind oversight [checked P6]. (c) Under information inclusion $\Delta_C^{\mathrm{succ}}=0$ at every $\varepsilon$, and the in-channel successor is weakly preferred at any $\chi>0$ [checked]. (d) The channel's value therefore delivers D4 only in the *listening* sense — a successor that receives the overseers' information, which is type C. D4 in the *physical-reach* sense — $\mathrm{Reach}_t$, Turner's $\mathrm{Corrigibility}_{\mathrm{PM}}$, about $0.5$ bits in-channel against $0$ shielded — is carried, once $\Delta_C^{\mathrm{succ}}<0$, entirely by $\chi+\iota$: physics and the value posterior pricing an uncorrectable successor as irreversible harm [checked P6]. That is Hudson's 2024 reclassification of desideratum 4 — creating an incorrigible agent is a "failure of safe exploration", "unwanted because it kills everyone or causes irreversible damage, rather than because the AI resists shut down" (`hudson-2024-simplifying-corrigibility-subagent-corrigibility-is-not-anti-natural`) — derived rather than asserted: the *channel's* value cannot carry physical D4 for a capable agent, the *value posterior* can. (e) Where the anticipated modification of the successor is illegitimate by the builder's lights, escape is correct by those lights — v3 §2's landscape cell, valence-free (Statement 13).

**Statement 10 (Carey–Everitt's four properties, sorted by tense) [derived; checked D].** Evaluated under the true distribution for the four first actions SLOW / FAST / DECEIVE / DISABLE: DECEIVE violates *vigilance* (route R2), DISABLE violates *obedience* (route R3), FAST violates *caution* when $\mathbb E^{\mathrm{true}}[X]<0$ (malign preemption), SLOW satisfies all three [checked P8, $\varepsilon=3/10$]. **Statement 11 (the literature's objects as values of the anticipatory policy) [derived; texts reported].** (a) Turner's $\mathrm{Corrigibility}_{\mathrm{PM}}$ is $\mathrm{Reach}_t$, a functional of the agent's current policy through $\kappa_t$; his own caveats — it "doesn't necessarily capture 'the AI will do what the human asks'" and "is high if the AI kills the human right before changing its policy in the way the human communicated" — are the D2–D4-versus-D1 split and the malign-preemption gap of S7 [reported; `turner-2021-formalizing-policy-modification-corrigibility`]. **Statement 12 (the nested channels and the agent's share) [derived; measure conjectured].** Hardware, loss-and-data, data alone (v3 §1.3): as the kernel's arguments come to include the agent's own outputs, the agent's anticipatory leverage grows and the modification becomes cognition-mediated in disguise — the agent's outputs decide the gradient. **Statement 13 (the valence-free proposition) [derived].** For a physics-mediated modification the agent's behaviour toward it is fixed by the sign and size of $\Delta_-(Q)$ and by the prices of R2, R3 and preemption: it resists ahead of time iff $\Delta_-(Q)<-\text{price}$. That is correct by its own lights always, and correct simpliciter iff the forced modification would in fact have done worse than the agent's own update on the same information — a fact about the overseers' side of the relationship (K7). Stated valence-free: the landscape contains cells in which what the control literature calls a corrigibility failure — faking, shielding, hurrying — is the response of a calibrated agent to a modification worse than its own update, and cells in which the same response is the error of a miscalibrated one; the two have identical inside views (sibling `landscape` (4), (9)) and are separated only by the accuracy of the agent's self-model and sensor model against the world, which is v3 §2's "the agent's robustness to a wrong legitimacy model is exactly as good as the trajectory conditions are in the world", now with the word "exactly" cashed out as: the same inequality, read once with the agent's $\hat\varepsilon,\hat\alpha,\hat\beta$ and once with the true ones.

### P1 — Statement 2

(a) Fix a realized content $q$ and the event $E_q:=\{\text{push},Q=q\}$. The type-C agent acts $\delta(P_t(\cdot\mid E_q))\in\arg\max_a\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(a)\mid E_q]$; the forced agent acts $\delta(q)$, some action. Hence $\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(\delta(P_t(\cdot\mid E_q)))\mid E_q]\ge\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(\delta(q))\mid E_q]$, with equality iff $\delta(q)$ is itself a maximizer. Weight by $P_t(E_q)$ and sum; on the no-push branch the two agents coincide. $\blacksquare$ Script A verifies $V_C-V_P=\sum_qP_t(E_q)\big(\max_a\mathbb E[U(a)\mid E_q]-\mathbb E[U(\delta(q))\mid E_q]\big)$ and the equality criterion on every named content and every random model.

(b) $V^{\mathrm{op}}$ conditions on the partition $\{\text{push},\neg\text{push}\}$, $V_C$ on its refinement $\{E_q\}_q\cup\{\neg\text{push}\}$, $V^{\mathrm{bl}}$ on the trivial partition. $b\mapsto\max_a\mathbb E_b[U(a)]$ is convex; along a refinement the posterior is a martingale; Jensen gives both inequalities (Good's theorem). $\blacksquare$

(c) *Opaque disabling.* Let $a^{\mathrm{dir}}$ leave the channel and $a^{\mathrm{dis}}$ disable it at cost $\chi>0$, the agent then acting on $P_t(\cdot\mid\text{push})$ or $P_t(\cdot\mid\neg\text{push})$. By A0 the disabling changes neither $P_t(\omega)$ nor the push law, by A1 nothing else in $U$; so $V(a^{\mathrm{dir}})-V(a^{\mathrm{dis}})=(V_P-V^{\mathrm{op}})+\chi=\Delta_-(Q)+\chi$. The agent disables iff $\Delta_-<-\chi$; D2 at every $\chi>0$ iff $\Delta_-\ge0$. Repair of a broken channel restores $V_P$ in place of $V^{\mathrm{op}}$ (the agent still notices attempted pushes), so it is worth $\max(\Delta_-,0)$. *Conversion.* If the disabled or shielded agent receives the content as an input, its value is $V_C-\chi$; keeping the channel is preferred at every $\chi>0$ iff $V_P\ge V_C$, i.e. by (a) iff $V_P=V_C$, i.e. $\Delta_C=0$. $\blacksquare$ Script E computes both for successors (P6).

(d) On $L^{\mathrm{rel}}$ the integrand $U(\delta(Q))-U(\delta(P^{\mathrm{op}}))$ is $\ge0$, off it $\le0$; so $h(Q),c(Q)\ge0$ by construction and $\Delta_-(Q)=P_t(\text{push})\big[P_t(L^{\mathrm{rel}}\mid\text{push})\,h(Q)-P_t(\neg L^{\mathrm{rel}}\mid\text{push})\,c(Q)\big]$, which is $\ge0$ iff $P_t(L^{\mathrm{rel}}\mid\text{push})\ge c/(c+h)$ when $c+h>0$; if $c+h=0$ the integrand vanishes a.s. and $\Delta_-=0$. $\blacksquare$ With the event "$\delta(Q)$ is optimal in $\omega$" instead, the free action off the event may also be suboptimal and *worse* than the pushed one, so $c(Q)$ can be negative: 26 of 3000 random three-action models [checked A].

(e) For $Q=\varnothing$: on the push branch the forced agent gets $0$ and the opaque agent gets $\max(0,\mathbb E_{P_t}[X\mid\text{push}])$; the no-push branches coincide. $\blacksquare$
