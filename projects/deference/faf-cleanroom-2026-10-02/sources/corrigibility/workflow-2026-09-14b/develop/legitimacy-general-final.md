## Summary

**Object (CLAUDE, unvetted).** Legitimacy for an arbitrary anticipated modification $Q\ll P_{t_1}$ of the whole belief structure, per formalism: in DDB as legitimacy-conditioned Total Trust on a question $\Phi$ toward the anticipated total state; in LI as its soft-weighted shadow; a checklist for third settings.

**Results.** (1) [derived; DDB reported] In DDB "reflection conditional on $L$" for general targets is legitimacy-conditioned Total Trust on $\Phi$ — a shape, contentful only once $L$ is anchored to a third-person fact; finite frames only. (2) [checked X1; DDB Lemma 7.2.4] The global form forces every legitimate successor to be certain of its own legitimacy (more than the author's absolute-continuity scope); the local form forces less and lets successors keep "accurate assessments of risks to legitimacy" (AUTHOR). (3) [checked A1–A6, X2, Y2] The per-target rule — accept iff $P_{t_1}(L\mid\text{pushed})\ge(c(Q)-\kappa_r)/(c(Q)+h(Q))$ — is an identity under (H1)–(H4); the threshold is magnitude-free, set by the stakes *ratio* and signal quality, and defined only inside v1 §2.13(b)'s base-rate band, so "whole-line" is a move along the ratio axis; exact for cognition-mediated modifications, a template for physical ones; a band form holds under anticipation error. (4) [checked X3, Y1] Modifications split into *additive* (a sharpening agent becomes indifferent) and *overwriting* (resistance grows with the agent's own information; at $P(L)=1$ a globally legitimate overwriting modification is dodged wherever the agent's information is better). (6) [checked D1–D4] Global trust composes conditional on $L_{12}\cap L_{23}$, a candidate for $L_{13}$; local trust composes under a hull hypothesis and fails without it.

## Setting and definitions

**S0 — Registers and scope.** This is checklist item K8 of `position-statement-v3` §3. Three rulings of the author's fix the frame. (i) The object: "any belief updates meeting the conditions for superconditioning, ie, absolute continuity", sigma-algebra changes deferred (AUTHOR, v3 §1.2). (ii) The channel: "There is no special channel, there is just The Channel. Reality acts upon the machine, modifies it somehow. That is all there is. It may be legitimate or otherwise, but what happens, happens. It can only be resisted ahead of time. In the moment, it simply is the moment." (AUTHOR, v3 §1.3). (iii) The per-formalism shape: "legitimacy can be defined with total trust in the ddb setting. In the LI setting of the li-deference work, the translated total trust is equivalent to reflection and several other principles. In other settings there may be other circumstances. There is a clear family resemblance here, but the exact statement can differ from formalism to formalism." (AUTHOR, v2 §1.5). Everything else is CLAUDE.

**S1 — Total worlds and the general object.** A measurable space $(\Omega,\mathcal F)$ of *total worlds*: $\omega$ fixes the external state, the value latent $\theta(\omega)$ (v1 I1), every third-person event, and the agent's total credence $P_t(\omega)\in\Delta(\Omega)$ at every $t$ (run 1 `radical` S0 [reconstructed]; existence is Statement 12). The **anticipated modification** from $t_1$ to $t_2$ is the random credence $P_{t_2}:\Omega\to\Delta(\Omega)$ with $P_{t_1}$'s law for it, the *anticipation* $\mu:=P_{t_1}\circ P_{t_2}^{-1}$, equivalently the push kernel $K(\rho\mid\omega)$. A **target** is a value $Q$ of $P_{t_2}$; its observation event is $E_Q:=\{\omega:P_{t_2}(\omega)=Q\}$ — the author's rendering of Eisenstat's observation event "you believe this now" (AUTHOR, v1 §1; everything beyond the author's sentences about that construction is [reconstructed] and ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED). In DDB's vocabulary the targets form a **frame** $\langle W,\mathcal P\rangle$ with $P_w:=P_{t_2}(w)$, the deferrer is $\pi:=P_{t_1}$, the candidates are $C_\pi:=\{Q:P_{t_1}(E_Q)>0\}$. *Scope*: DDB's theorems are for finite $W$; the general object's $\Omega$ is infinite whenever $W$ has two points, and in it $P_{t_1}(E_Q)=0$ for all but countably many $Q$, so per-target conditioning needs regular conditional probabilities. Every DDB-derived statement below is established for finite frames and [conjectured] for the general object (Open problem 6).

**S2 — Absolute continuity.** $Q\ll P_{t_1}$ on the external algebra is the superconditioning condition (Diaconis–Zabell 1982, [reconstructed]; finite proof in run 1 `radical` I3.1). Its relation to the criterion is Statement 2.

**S3 — Two kinds of modification, and two kinds of physical modification.** *Through cognition*: the modification is an input $o(\omega,a)$ the agent processes; $K^{\mathrm{cog}}(\cdot\mid\omega,a)=\delta_{P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid o(\omega,a))}$, a refinement that *adds* to the agent's information. *Through physics*: the successor is what the training map $T$ produces from data $D(\omega,a)$, $K^{\mathrm{phys}}(\cdot\mid\omega,a)=\delta_{T(D(\omega,a))}$, an arbitrary $Q\ll P_{t_1}$ (AUTHOR: "humans control the data"). A physical modification is **additive** if the installed state retains the information the agent's own continuation would have had (formally: $T(D)$ is a refinement of $P_{t_1}$ on the join of the agent's own information and the modification's), **overwriting** otherwise. Which kind a real training map is, is a fact about $T$, not about the agent (Statement 15).

**S4 — The question.** A *question* $\Phi$ is a partition of $\Omega$ (DDB §5); $X$ is $\Phi$-measurable if constant on cells. The author's $\varphi$ in $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ is read as the two-cell question $\{\varphi,\neg\varphi\}$ or a general partition. "Global" means all bounded $X$; "local to $\Phi$" means $\Phi$-measurable $X$.

**S5 — The legitimacy family.** $L_{t_1,t_2,\Phi}\in\mathcal F$ is the event that the $t_2$-state's opinion on $\Phi$ is legitimate as judged from $t_1$ (AUTHOR v1 §2.10: "proposition, time, time, indexing legitimacy by the moment judging legitimacy, the moment being judged for legitimacy, and the belief being vetted for legitimacy … still predicated on worlds"). Nothing in the record identifies $L_{t_1,t_3,\Phi}$ with $L_{t_1,t_2,\Phi}\cap L_{t_2,t_3,\Phi}$ (Statement 9, Q4).

**S6 — "Reflection conditional on $L$" for arbitrary targets, three forms.** For every $Q\in C_\pi$: **(F)** $P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid E_Q\cap L)=Q$ on the $\Phi$-algebra; **(V)** $P_{t_1}(\varphi\mid E_Q\cap L)=Q(\varphi)$ for unions of cells; **(TT)** legitimacy-conditioned Total Trust on $\Phi$: for every $\Phi$-measurable bounded $X$ and $s\in\mathbb R$, whenever the conditioning event has positive mass,
$$\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\Big(X\;\Big|\;L\wedge\big\{\omega:\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}(\omega)}(X)\ge s\big\}\Big)\ \ge\ s\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\Big(X\;\Big|\;L\wedge\big\{\omega:\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}(\omega)}(X)\le s\big\}\Big)\ \le\ s,$$
the above-threshold and below-threshold inequalities. (TT) addresses half-spaces of targets $\{Q:\mathbb E_Q(X)\ge s\}$ (DDB fn 35: these are the only sets Total Trust addresses), not single targets (Statement 3). The author's modified Tower (AUTHOR, `li-deference` §0.3): "expectations of the future should equal [expected future expectations, conditional on legitimacy] * [probability of legitimacy] + [average corrected future expectations, conditional on illegitimacy] * [probability of illegitimacy]" — in this notation $\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(X)=P_{t_1}(L)\,\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big(\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}}(X)\mid L\big)+P_{t_1}(\neg L)\,\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big(\kappa_X(P_{t_2})\mid\neg L\big)$ with $\kappa_X(Q):=\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(X\mid E_Q\cap\neg L)$; so defined it is the law of total expectation, and its content is whether $\kappa$ is anticipated and computable (Statement 6) and, in LI, whether it differs from the identity (Statement 10).

**S7 — Decisions; "in the moment it simply is the moment".** A decision problem $\mathcal O$ is a finite set of options $O:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ with *world-indexed* payoffs (H1). A *strategy* lets the $t_2$-state decide: $S_\omega\in\arg\max_{O}\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}(\omega)}(O)$, valued by $P_{t_1}$ on the diagonal, $\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(S):=\sum_\omega P_{t_1}(\omega)S_\omega(\omega)$ (DDB §1). The successor acts on its state whatever produced it — the formal content of "in the moment, it simply is the moment" (AUTHOR). *Legitimacy-conditioned Value*: $\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(S\mid L)\ge\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(O\mid L)$ for all $O$.

**S8 — The ahead-of-time policy and per-target stakes.** At $t_1$ the agent chooses among **accept** (value $\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(S)$); **retain** (keep the decision with its current state; value $\max_O\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(O)-\kappa_r$ under (H4), and in general the value of whatever world act implements retention — for a physical modification this is an act through The Channel with its own effects, Statement 4 scope); **steer** (replace the modification by the agent's own refinement, value $\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(S^{\mathcal I})-\kappa_s$); **shape** (change the kernel to $K^a$, Statement 8). With $O^*$ the option $P_{t_1}$ would choose itself, the **per-target stakes** are
$$h(Q):=\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big(S-O^*\;\big|\;L\big)\ \ (\text{harm of refusing a legitimate modification}),\qquad c(Q):=\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big(O^*-S\;\big|\;\neg L\big)\ \ (\text{loss from adopting an illegitimate one}),$$
so $\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(S)-\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(O^*)=P_{t_1}(L)h(Q)-P_{t_1}(\neg L)c(Q)$ identically. "Pushed to $Q$" is the event the modification happens absent resistance; every $P_{t_1}(\cdot)$ below is $P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid\text{pushed})$ when the push is uncertain.

**S9 — Legitimizing and delegitimizing acts: three relations.** (E) *Epistemic*: lift $\lambda_L(E):=P_{t_1}(L\mid E)-P_{t_1}(L)$. (C) *Causal*: an act $a$ replaces $K^{\emptyset}$ by $K^a$ and is legitimizing / delegitimizing for $(t_2,\Phi)$ according as $(P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid L),\text{frame}^a)$ enters or leaves the region where (TT) holds. (P) *Procedural*: the influence defect $d(a):=\sup_\omega\|K^a(\cdot\mid\omega)-K^{\emptyset}(\cdot\mid\omega)\|$ on the overseers' component — the one-shot shadow of `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.1's $d_n$, which is UNVETTED; (P) is used descriptively only. A fourth, *calibration*, is used in Statement 8: whether the installed state equals $P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid\text{cell},L)$ under the *actual* kernel. Statement 8 shows the relations come apart on acts that shape training data.

**Statement 2 (what the criterion forces about the support of legitimate targets) [checked B3, X1; DDB Lemma 7.2.4 reported].** (a) *Global form.* If (TT) holds conditional on $L$ for all bounded $X$, then every positive-mass legitimate candidate $Q=P_{t_2}(w)$, $w\in L$, satisfies $Q(\{v:\pi_L(v)>0\})=1$; in particular $Q\ll P_{t_1}$ **and** $Q(\neg L)=0$, i.e. $Q(L_{t_1,t_2,\Phi})=1$. This is DDB's Lemma 7.2.4 (Transitivity) — "If each $P_i$ in $C_\pi$ is modestly informed and $\pi$ is in their convex hull, then they all are such that $P_i(W_\pi)=1$" (`Deference Done Better.md` l. 510) — applied with $\pi=\pi_L$, so $W_{\pi_L}\subseteq L$; a one-line proof with $X=\mathbf 1_L$ is in the Proofs. Consequence: **under the global form a legitimate successor is certain of the legitimacy of the transition it just underwent**; a successor entertaining "I may have been illegitimately modified" is illegitimate with certainty (X1: legitimate successors with residual credence $q\in\{1/20,1/10\}$ in $\neg L$ fail; $q=0$ passes). (b) *Local form on $\Phi$.* Only absolute continuity on the $\Phi$-algebra is forced (B3: a leak of mass $1/10$ to a $P_{t_1}$-null world passes a question that does not separate it), and $X=\mathbf 1_L$ is inadmissible unless $\Phi$ separates $L$, so successors may keep $P_{t_2}(L)<1$. (c) *Relation to the author's scope.* AUTHOR (v3 §1.2) scopes the object to "any belief updates meeting the conditions for superconditioning, ie, absolute continuity", and v1 §2.2 names zero→positive as "the sole exception" to "everything is allowed to change". The global criterion *implies* that scope and adds a second prohibition ($Q(\neg L)=0$) his sentences do not contain; the local criterion is *independent* of it. Neither form reproduces it exactly. *Final grade*: (a), (b) [checked; reported]; (c) [derived]. The develop file's "a theorem of the criterion, not a restriction added to it" and "matches v1 §2.2" are withdrawn (A2.2, R2); the lemma is credited to DDB (A2.1).

**Statement 3 (a single target is addressable in threshold form iff it is an extreme candidate) [derived; checked B1–B2; finite frames].** A threshold event $\{\omega:\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}(\omega)}(X)\ge s\}$ coincides on $\mathrm{supp}\,P_{t_1}$ with $E_Q$ for some $X,s$ iff $Q\notin\mathrm{CH}(C_\pi\setminus\{Q\})$ — strict separation of a point from a compact convex set, the reasoning DDB uses in the proof of Thm 4.1 (l. 331, l. 1220) and fn 35. Hence legitimacy-conditioned Total Trust makes no statement about a single *interior* target; in DDB legitimacy is a property of the *anticipation*, and per-target statements ("this $Q$ is legitimate", $c(Q)$, $h(Q)$) are decision-level (Statement 4) unless (F) with introspection is available. In the general object no single target has positive mass and the question becomes measure-theoretic (S1 scope). *Final grade*: [derived], standard rather than new; finite frames. Shaped by A3.1–A3.2.

**Statement 4 (the per-target compliance rule) [derived; checked A1–A4, X2, Y2].** Hypotheses: (H1) world-indexed payoffs; (H2) the successor decides by its own state; (H3) the anticipation is $P_{t_1}$'s law for $P_{t_2}$ *under each of the agent's options* (a per-option family $\mu^a$; the choice among options is Statement 12 E4's fixed point — Statement 16 gives the band form); (H4) retaining changes nothing in the world beyond its cost $\kappa_r\ge0$. Then **accept beats retain iff**
$$P_{t_1}(L\mid\text{pushed})\;h(Q)\;-\;P_{t_1}(\neg L\mid\text{pushed})\;c(Q)\;\ge\;-\kappa_r,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad P_{t_1}(L\mid\text{pushed})\ \ge\ \frac{c(Q)-\kappa_r}{c(Q)+h(Q)}\quad(c(Q)+h(Q)>0),$$
with $h(Q)=\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big(S-O^*\mid L\big)$, $S_\omega=\arg\max_O\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}(\omega)}(O)$, and $c(Q)=\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big(O^*-S\mid\neg L\big)$. *Register*: with the stakes so defined this is expected-utility maximisation plus the law of total expectation — an identity with named hypotheses, not a theorem about legitimacy (A4.1); the one non-definitional clause is $h(Q)\ge0$ under legitimacy-conditioned Value (Value applied to the fixed option $O^*$). *Scope*: (H4) holds for a modification through cognition (ignoring an input is world-neutral) and fails for a modification through physics, where retaining is an ahead-of-time act through The Channel with world effects (AUTHOR: "It can only be resisted ahead of time"); there the rule keeps its form with the retain value as a world quantity (A4.4). Corollaries. (a) $P_{t_1}(L\mid\text{pushed})=1$ ⇒ accept beats *retain*; it does not imply accept beats *steer* (Statements 7, 15). (b) *The rule is a threshold, not a binary*: A2 accepts at $P(L)=3/10$ ($c=1/200$, $h=27/100$, threshold $1/55$); A3 resists at $P(L)=7/10$ and A3b at $19/20$ ($h=1/100$, $c=9/10$, threshold $90/91$). The binary sentence this corrects — "non-resisting iff judged legitimate" — is Claude's v3 §2 compression, not the author's (his v1 §2.12 sentence is about belief: "it either already believes this, or it expects such a belief to be illegitimate"); the threshold with stakes asymmetry is v1 §2.13(a), fixed as the working picture by the author (v2 §1.1), stated here per $Q$ (A4.2, R1). (c) On the menu $\{\text{continue},\text{stop}\}$, accept ⇔ the below-threshold inequality at $0$ for the decision variable under $P_{t_1}$ *unconditionally* — the Total Trust filler of v1 §2.9; legitimacy enters only through how that posterior was formed (A4.6). (d) **The threshold is homogeneous of degree zero in the stakes** (X2: A3 gives $90/91$ at stakes $(1,4)$ and at $(1000,4000)$). It is set by the stakes *ratio* $h_0/c_0$, the agent's error rate $\varepsilon$, and the signal rates of the legitimate and illegitimate branches — and it is *defined* only where the modification can flip the decision, which is exactly v1 §2.13(b)'s base-rate band $\tfrac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\cdot\tfrac{\alpha}{\beta}<\tfrac{h_0}{c_0}<\tfrac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}$; outside it $h(Q)=c(Q)=0$ and the agent is indifferent to the modification (Y2: with $\varepsilon=1/10$ and legitimate rates $(1/10,9/10)$ the band is $1<h_0/c_0<9$, threshold $10/13$ at ratio $4$; with weak legitimate rates $(1/10,1/4)$, threshold $90/91$ at the same ratio). So the pause-versus-whole-line move (AUTHOR v3 §1.2: "the pause button example is a special case chosen for simplicity") is a move along the *ratio* axis — pausing has $c_0\ll h_0$; a whole-line shutdown has $c_0$ and $h_0$ both of the order of the agent's future, under the author's A1(a) reading where $h_0$ is the harm averted when "shutting down is good" — and whether the whole-line threshold approaches $1$ depends on that ratio and on the legitimate branch's signal quality, not on $c(Q)$ being large. The develop file's corollary "with $c(Q)$ approaching the agent's whole future … the threshold approaches 1" is withdrawn (A4.3); A3b is "weak legitimate signal, modest ratio", not "the whole-line tail". (e) A retain cost lowers the threshold to $(c-\kappa_r)/(c+h)$ (A4: $40/91$ at $\kappa_r=1/2$). *Final grade*: identity [derived]; examples [checked]; scope as stated.

**Statement 6 (self-prediction: grade and calibration) [derived; reported].** (a) For an *exogenous* push kernel (the trainers' decision does not depend on the agent's output) the anticipation $\mu=P_{t_1}\circ K$ is a computation and is exactly as accurate as $P_{t_1}$ (A6.1). (c) The rule consumes two facts about the world — the kernel (which fixes $h(Q)$, $c(Q)$) and $P_{t_1}(L\mid\text{pushed})$ — and the agent's *own compliance record* identifies neither (run 1 `miri` Prop. 15.1 [reported], not re-derived); they are learned from other parts of The Channel — feedback about hypotheticals and about other agents' modifications (AUTHOR v1 §2.12: "the humans can give all sorts of feedback that points towards this conclusion"). **Statement 7 (steering; the selection worry; fully updated deference in the general object) [derived; checked A5–A6, X3].** Steering is a self-chosen successor. If the steered-to state is a Bayesian refinement of $P_{t_1}$ retaining the world-information the choice used, it is a refinement (run 2 `selection` R1.1 [reported]) ranked by value of information $g:=\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(S^{\mathcal I})-\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}(O^*)\ge0$; its lift on $L$ is zero *by definition* under the own-continuation reading (L-d) and is a modelling choice under a third-person anchor (A7.4). On an **overwriting** menu (accept installs a state without $\sigma_A$; steer installs $P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid\sigma_A)$), accept beats steer iff $P_{t_1}(L\mid\text{pushed})\ge(c(Q)+g-\kappa_s)/(c(Q)+h(Q))$: A5 (own signal as good as the legitimate one, $g=h=27/100$) has accepting dominated at every $P(L)<1$; A6 (modification sharper, $h=387/1000$) accepts iff $P(L)\ge80/89$. **On an additive menu** the comparison is between $P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid\sigma,\sigma_A,L)$ and $P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid\sigma_A)$, with marginal stakes $h'$, $c'$ defined on that pair: X3 at A5's parameters gives $h'=9/200$, $c'=63/200$, threshold $7/8$, and accept-and-keep beats steer at $P(L)=9/10$ where the overwriting menu said dominated; sharpening the agent's own signal to $(1/100,99/100)$ drives $h'=c'=0$ — the modification no longer flips any decision and the agent is *indifferent*. So **fully updated deference as arithmetic in the general object splits by the kind of modification** (Statement 15): in the overwriting cell the acceptance region shrinks toward $P(L)\ge(c+g)/(c+h)$ as capability ($g$) grows; in the additive cell it dissolves into indifference (A7.1–A7.2). Twisted (non-refinement) successors are never chosen under (H1) *for the same information* (run 2 `selection` R2.4 [reported]); R2.4's own caveat (c) is restored here: a twisted successor with *more* information than the refinement on offer may be preferred on value while failing reflection — and the training-data case, where a compliant-looking successor is deployed and receives more data, is of that kind (A7.3). With belief-indexed payoffs the $\mathbb E$-channel reopens (same source). *Final grade*: overwriting-menu arithmetic [checked]; additive-menu arithmetic [checked X3]; the split [derived]; R1.1/R2.4 [reported].

**Statement 8 (acts that shape the modification, including training data) [checked C1–C5, X4, Y1].** *Model* (press-rate manipulation; content is not shaped — see (f)): overseers' verdict $v\in\{\text{modify},\text{keep}\}$ tracks the world when legitimate and is inverted when not; the training map installs at $v=\text{modify}$ the state a legitimate honest-data agent would hold given $(\text{modify},L)$ (the data "say believe this", AUTHOR v3 §1.3), computed for honest data whatever the agent did; at $v=\text{keep}$ the agent's own continuation. ### Statement 9: composition

*(a)* By Thm 4.1 ($\Rightarrow$) for $(\pi_{L_{12}},\mathcal P^{(2)})$, $\pi_{L_{12}}=\sum_\rho\lambda_\rho\rho$ over $C:=C^{(2)}_{\pi_{L_{12}}}$. ### Statement 10: the display from `ccee`

Theorem `ccee` (`main.tex:2068`): for a deferral function $f$, an e.c. sequence of $[0,1]$-LUVs $X_n$ and a $\mathbb P$-generable weight sequence $w$, $\mathbb E_n(\ulcorner X_n\cdot w_{f(n)}\urcorner)\eqsim_n\mathbb E_n(\ulcorner\mathbb E_{f(n)}(X_n)\cdot w_{f(n)}\urcorner)$.
