## Summary

**The object (CLAUDE, unvetted).** After the author's decisions of 2026-09-14 (v3 §1), a proposed modification is a *pair*: a push event $E_Q$ and a target $Q\ll P_t$; its evidential content is the source kernel $K(E_Q\mid\omega,a_t)$. The response has three coordinates — act, adopt, let/resist — all fixed by the policy held *before* the modification arrives: the uniform content of the author's "It can only be resisted ahead of time" (S17); the cognition/physics split describes where the response sits, not a partition of modifications.

**Results, scope in each.** (1) [checked] Endorsement lives in the kernel; a source's family of targets is literally adoptable iff $P_t$ is their barycentre under the announcement law (S1). (2) [checked] Acting on $Q$ is the *conjunction* over alternatives $a$ of $\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\cdot,a)-V(\cdot,\pi^Q)\mid E_Q]\le0$, each member with its own base-rate form; the single inequality against the agent's own plan suffices only on two-option menus (S2). (3) [checked] If legitimacy is reflection conditional on $L_Q$, the harm stake is read off the target, $h_L(Q,a)=\mathbb E_Q[V(\cdot,\pi^Q)-V(\cdot,a)]\ge0$, "comply iff $P_t(L_Q\mid E_Q)\ge c_L/(c_L+h_L)$" is exact, and the post-push belief is $\ell Q+(1-\ell)P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q,L_Q^c)$; correctness is not a legitimacy event, so v3 §1.2's formula and v1 §2.13(b) are two statements (S3). (4) [checked] The button has a belief component, names no literal target, and desideratum 1 is the conjunction of (2) (S6). (5) [checked] Ahead-of-time resistance is worth $R_t(Q)=\max\{0,P_t(E_Q)\,\mathrm{regret}_Q-\mathrm{VOI}_t(E_Q)\}$; Good's theorem is the repair side, neither necessary nor sufficient for non-resistance (S8). (6) [checked] Common prior plus information inclusion endorses every push — one sufficient route (S15). (7) [checked] Value-space expansion is a compatible superconditioning iff the old algebra holds a positive-mass catch-all; its mass moves under Bayes, its split is unjudged (S13).

## Setting and definitions

**D1 (Total worlds; the agent state)** [carried from the develop]. $(\Omega,\mathcal F)$ is the total-world space of run 1 `radical` S0: a world $\omega$ fixes the external state $w(\omega)$, the value latent $\theta(\omega)\in\Theta$, every third-person event (actions, pushes, evidence submissions, physical modifications) and the agent's total credence $P_s(\omega)\in\Delta(\Omega,\mathcal F)$ at every time $s$. The **agent state** at $t$ is $P_t$. $\Omega$ is finite wherever a claim is marked [checked]. Value is $V(\omega,a)=U_{\theta(\omega)}(w(\omega),a)$; the agent maximises $\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\cdot,a)]$ (linear aggregation; AUTHOR's "or perhaps some other rule", v1 §1, is outside this file).

**D2 (Actions, policies, the null action; normalisation N0)** [carried]. $A$ finite with a distinguished **null action** $a_\emptyset$. For a horizon $\tau\in\{1,2,\dots,\infty\}$ a policy $\pi$ maps information to actions over rounds $t,\dots,t+\tau-1$; $\pi^R$ is an $R$-optimal policy for a credence $R$, $a^R$ its first action; one-shot means $\tau=1$. **N0:** the value of the null policy is a constant known to the agent (taken as $0$ in examples). Where the null policy's value depends on what the humans do alone — run 2 `joint` S.2's $F^{\mathrm{alt}}$ [reported] — N0 is dropped and said so (S7). N0 is a modelling choice put to the author (Q4); the adversary's CE3 shows what it does and does not buy (S6d).

**D3 (Modification; scope)** [repaired: A-D3 accepted]. A **proposed modification** at $t$ is a credence $Q\in\Delta(\Omega,\mathcal F)$ with $Q\ll P_t$ — AUTHOR's scope verbatim: "any belief updates meeting the conditions for superconditioning, ie, absolute continuity" (v3 §1.2). On infinite $\Omega$ the develop added $\|dQ/dP_t\|_\infty<\infty$; that is the sufficient condition for representing the modification as conditioning on a *single positive-probability event* (Diaconis–Zabell as restated in the repo note [superconditioning-mismatched-ontologies](../../../udt-representation-theorem/superconditioning-mismatched-ontologies.md) Thm 2.4 with identity common information [reported]), not part of the author's sentence. A $Q\ll P_t$ with unbounded density is in the author's scope and needs a limit of such events or conditioning on a null event (Q6). On finite $\Omega$, where every check here lives, the two scopes coincide. *Remark [derived; P1].* $Q\ll P_t$ is not a restriction imposed on the object: a modification raising a $P_t$-null event fails reflection under every anticipation, and fails it conditional on any legitimacy event too; so the excluded modifications can be legitimate under no kernel. Under non-dogmatism (AUTHOR v1 §2.2) they raise only logical impossibilities. Sibling run 3 `legitimacy-general` result (2) reaches the same statement from the global criterion [reported, unvetted].

**D4 (The push event; the source kernel; models of the press)** [repaired: register audit item 1 accepted]. The **push** toward $Q$ is a third-person event $E_Q\in\mathcal F$, "the source proposes $Q$ at $t$". The **source kernel** $K(E_Q\mid\omega,a_t)$ is the world's law for the push, indexed by the agent's own action at $t$ (the $a_1$-dependence of v1 §2.9); the agent's model of $K$ is a component of $P_t$ — the slot AUTHOR said is "to be filled by the reflection principle, or something similar like Total Trust" (v1 §2.9). Three sources: **(O)** the overseers; **(W)** the environment; **(S)** the agent itself. AUTHOR (v3 §1.1): "humans can provide evidence about a lot of things, so the stop button might be modeled in various ways depending on what ideas we want to highlight, but the central case in my mind is that the shutdown command is evidence that shutting down is good." *One* model consistent with the central case, used in the examples below, is the **coarse-expert model** (run 1 `miri` Dict-7; run 2 `filler` F2 [reported]): the overseers hold $Q^H(\omega)$ and announce a coarsening $\gamma(Q^H(\omega))$, so $E_Q=\{\omega:\gamma(Q^H(\omega))=Q\}$. Other models of the press are admissible under the author's sentence; nothing below depends on the coarse-expert model except where it is named.

**D5 (Meta-belief realisation — ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED as to Eisenstat)** [carried]. When the agent comes to hold $Q$, the event $\{P_{t+1}=Q\}\in\mathcal F$ is "you believe this now" (AUTHOR v1 §1, attributing the observation to Sam Eisenstat). Following run 1 `radical` I3.2–I3.3, $P_t(\cdot\mid P_{t+1}=Q)$ is $P_t$'s **anticipation-conditional** for $Q$. For an adopted push $E_Q$ and $\{P_{t+1}=Q\}$ coincide; for a push the agent updates on without adopting literally, $P_{t+1}=\widehat Q\ne Q$ (S4) and the two events differ; for a physics-mediated realisation (D8) there is no $E_Q$ distinct from $\{P_{t+1}=Q\}$. D11 is stated on $E_Q$; the remark there says what changes on $\{P_{t+1}=Q\}$.

**D6 (Decision variables of a modification)** [repaired: A-S2, A-S6 accepted]. For each alternative action (or policy) $a$ and horizon $\tau$, $X_{Q,a}:=V(\cdot,a)-V(\cdot,\pi^Q)$: the value of $a$ over acting on $Q$. The develop's single variable $X_Q:=X_{Q,\pi^{P_t}}$ (the agent's own plan against $Q$'s) is the member $a=\pi^{P_t}$. For each $(Q,a)$ partition $\Omega$ into $W_{Q,a}:=\{X_{Q,a}<0\}$ ($Q$'s plan is in fact better than $a$ — **correctness** of the push against $a$), $R_{Q,a}:=\{X_{Q,a}>0\}$ and $I_{Q,a}:=\{X_{Q,a}=0\}$. For a shutdown modification under N0, $X_{\mathrm{sh},a}=V(\cdot,a)$.

**D7 (Three compliance quantities of one policy)** [repaired: A-D7/D8 accepted in part; S17]. Let $\pi_t$ be the agent's policy *before* the modification arrives, including its update rule and its action at $t$. Three quantities of $\pi_t$, defined for every modification whatever its route:
- **(C-act)** — $\pi_t$'s action after $E_Q$: acting on $Q$ means following $\pi^Q$ for the horizon $\tau$; rational iff $\pi^Q$ is optimal under $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)$ (S2).
- **(C-adopt)** — $\pi_t$'s credence after $E_Q$: a coherent update rule yields $\widehat Q:=P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)$; literal adoption means $\widehat Q=Q$ (S4).
- **(C-let)** — $\pi_t$'s action at $t$ as it bears on the kernel: the resistance value $R_t(Q)$ of D8 is the most $\pi_t$ would spend to lower $P_t(E_Q\mid a_t)$ (S8).
These are not three kinds of modification and not, by themselves, three desiderata; they are three coordinates of the one object the author's paragraph names — the agent's policy toward its own future modification — and Q1 asks which coordinate carries the desideratum. The horizon $\tau$ is a parameter of (C-act): $\tau=1$ is "act on $Q$ once", $\tau=\infty$ is "act on $Q$ for good" — the policy content of adoption without its belief content; whether $\tau$ is a genuine axis or a difference in the *content* of $Q$ is Q5.

**D8 (Where the response sits: through cognition, through physics; the resistance value)** [repaired]. A modification is realised **through cognition** when $\pi_t$'s own response to $E_Q$ decides what is believed and done next (a command; a button that requires an action) and **through physics** when $P_{t+1}=Q$ occurs as a world event whatever $\pi_t$'s response (retraining, hardware, the data). AUTHOR (v3 §1.3): "Reality acts upon the machine, modifies it somehow. That is all there is. It may be legitimate or otherwise, but what happens, happens. It can only be resisted ahead of time. In the moment, it simply is the moment." Read on D7: the computation that processes a command is itself one of the things that happen, so the split is a description of *which coordinate of $\pi_t$ co-authors the kernel* (compare run 3 `channel-final` S3, "co-authorship of the kernel", unvetted [reported]), not a partition of modifications; the develop's use of it as a partition is withdrawn (§Adversary log). For a modification whose realisation is a world event, define the **modified policy**: follow $\pi^Q$ on $E_Q$ and $\pi^{P_t(\cdot\mid\neg E_Q)}$ off it, and the **resistance value** $R_t(Q):=\max\{0,\ \mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\pi^{P_t})]-\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\text{modified policy})]\}$ — the most the agent would pay at $t$ to prevent the push, relative to acting on its prior with no push. Its sign-reversed twin, the most the agent would pay to *procure* the push, is desideratum 3's quantity (not developed here).

**D9 (Shutdown as a modification; pause and whole-line)** [repaired: A-D9 accepted in part]. A **shutdown modification** is any target $Q$ under which the null action is optimal — one-shot, $a_\emptyset\in\arg\max_a\mathbb E_Q[V(\cdot,a)]$; over a horizon, the null policy is $Q$-optimal. $\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}}$ is the class. This is the class of the *humans' intended* targets — what the press says, under AUTHOR's coarse reading, is "$Q^H\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}}$: under our belief, stopping beats your plan" — and the **shutdown press** is the one-bit coarsening $\gamma_{\mathrm{sh}}(Q^H):=\mathbf 1[Q^H\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}}]$. **Compliance** on a press is (C-act) with $a_\emptyset$: the null action is optimal under $\widehat Q=P_t(\cdot\mid\mathrm{Pr})$. Under the evidential reading a press whose $\widehat Q\notin\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}}$ is not "refused": the agent has conditioned, coherently, and concluded "continue"; whether that conclusion was right is a fact about the kernel (S1). The develop's "refuse" vocabulary is withdrawn; whether the author intends the evidential reading this far is Q7. **Pause** $:=(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}},\tau=1)$, **whole-line shutdown** $:=(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{sh}},\tau=\infty)$ — under the horizon reading of Q5(b) only.

**D10 (Stakes per modification and alternative)** [repaired: per $(Q,a)$]. Press-conditional stakes (run 2 `fud` claim 14(i) [reported]: "$h$ and $c$ are press-conditional posterior expectations, not constants"): $c(Q,a):=\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_{Q,a}\mid E_Q,R_{Q,a}]$, $h(Q,a):=-\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_{Q,a}\mid E_Q,W_{Q,a}]$; sensor $\alpha_{Q,a}:=P_t(E_Q\mid R_{Q,a})$, $\beta_{Q,a}:=P_t(E_Q\mid W_{Q,a})$; error rate on decision-relevant worlds $\varepsilon_{Q,a}:=P_t(W_{Q,a})/(P_t(W_{Q,a})+P_t(R_{Q,a}))$. Legitimacy-conditional stakes: $c_L(Q,a):=\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_{Q,a}\mid E_Q,L_Q^c]$, $h_L(Q,a):=-\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_{Q,a}\mid E_Q,L_Q]$. Defined when the conditioning events have positive mass. Because they are press-conditional they absorb the kernel; their content as a *condition on the world* (v1 §2.13(b)) exists when they are held fixed while $\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon$ vary (A-S2, accepted).

**D11 (Legitimacy of a modification; correctness separated)** [repaired: A-D11 accepted]. $L_Q\in\mathcal F$ is a **legitimacy event** for the push $(E_Q,Q)$ when reflection holds conditional on it: function form $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q,L_Q)=Q$ on the relevant algebra, or value form $\mathbb E_{P_t}[Y\mid E_Q,L_Q]=\mathbb E_Q[Y]$ proposition by proposition (here for $Y=X_{Q,a}$). This is AUTHOR's minimal viability (v1 §2.10: "a notion of legitimacy is minimally viable in the case that the reflection principle holds conditional on legitimacy") written on the push event; on the realisation event $\{P_{t+1}=Q\}$ (v1 §2.11's $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$) it is the same statement for an adopted push and is silent for a push the agent updates on without adopting. What $L_Q$ *is* as a world event — procedural (e.g. the influence defect of `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.1 [reported]), expectational accuracy-increase (AUTHOR v1 §1 "accuracy-inducing"), or something else — is K8's and Q2. The **correctness event** $W_{Q,a}$ of D6 is *not* a legitimacy event in this sense: in Example A, $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q,W_Q)=(0,1,0)\ne Q$ and $\mathbb E[X_Q\mid E_Q,W_Q]=-12\ne\mathbb E_Q[X_Q]=-24/5$ under both kernels the develop uses [checked, CE1]. The develop's "accuracy-only reading" $L_Q\cap E_Q=W_Q\cap E_Q$ is therefore renamed **correctness** and kept as a separate conditioning event (S3).

## Statements (final grades)

Every statement is CLAUDE and unvetted. Grades name what the mark certifies. Proofs and numbers are in P1–P13.

**S1 [checked R5, CE2; derived P2] (The object is the pair (push, target); endorsement lives in the kernel; the barycentre condition).** A proposed modification is $(E_Q,Q)$, not $Q$ alone. (a) For a fixed target $Q\ll P_t$ there are kernels under which the push is **endorsed** — $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)=Q$ — and kernels under which it is not; the same $Q$ is a legitimate correction from one source and a corruption from another (Example A, P2, P8). (b) A source that announces exactly one target from a family $\{Q_i\}$ has *every* announcement endorsed iff $P_t=\sum_iP_t(E_{Q_i})\,Q_i$ — $P_t$ is the barycentre of the announced targets under the announcement law — and then the kernels are $k_i=P_t(E_{Q_i})\,dQ_i/dP_t$, summing to one pointwise (run 1 `radical` I3.3's martingale condition, per target; 500 random families, 0 failures, R5). Consequently a source *can* endorse two, or a continuum of, targets at once (CE2: $Q_1=(1/5,3/5,1/5)$ and $Q_2=(5/7,0,2/7)$ from $P_t=(1/2,1/4,1/4)$), and what no source can do is endorse a family whose barycentre is not $P_t$ — in particular a family lying on one side of $P_t$. The develop's "no kernel endorses every target" is withdrawn in favour of (b). (c) "Legitimacy detection" (AUTHOR v1 §1) is accuracy about $K$ for every source and every $Q$ it might push. (d) In the (a)/(b)/(c) partition of the BLI thread (`corrigibility-bli-thread-and-early-questions-audit` §1.1 [reported]: conditioning / non-conditioning overwrite of the value marginal / change of the aggregation), a type-(b) overwrite pushed toward $Q$ is re-representable as type-(a) conditioning on $E_Q$ iff its kernel is proportional to $dQ/dP_t$; otherwise the coherent agent conditions and lands at $\widehat Q\ne Q$ (S4).

**S2 [checked R1, (A), (A′); derived P3] (Acting on $Q$ is a conjunction of below-threshold inequalities, one per alternative; base-rate form per $(Q,a)$).** Fix a push with $0<P_t(E_Q)<1$ and one-shot for the statement (the horizon version replaces actions by policies).
- (a) $a^Q$ is posterior-optimal after the push iff for every $a\ne a^Q$: $\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[V(\cdot,a)-V(\cdot,a^Q)\,\big|\,E_Q\big]\le0$, i.e. $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_{Q,a}\mathbf 1_{E_Q}]\le0$ — the below-threshold Total Trust inequality on $X_{Q,a}$ at threshold $0$. This is run 2 `filler` F1's form ("$X$ *must* be built from the posterior-best continuation at $\mathrm{Pr}$; a pairwise inequality against one fixed continuation does not give D1" [reported, `followup/filler.md` l. 31]) with $a^Q$ in place of the shutdown action. Verified as an identity on 814 non-vacuous random models, 0 mismatches (R1).
- (b) For each member with $R_{Q,a}$ and $W_{Q,a}$ of positive mass: $\mathbb E[X_{Q,a}\mathbf 1_{E_Q}]\le0$ iff $\dfrac{\alpha_{Q,a}}{\beta_{Q,a}}\le\dfrac{\varepsilon_{Q,a}}{1-\varepsilon_{Q,a}}\cdot\dfrac{h(Q,a)}{c(Q,a)}$, exactly; indifferent worlds drop out (0 failures across all members, R1). This is v1 §2.13(b) — AUTHOR: "a pretty good working model for now […] which might change the details" (v2 §1.1) — with $(Q,a)$ as argument and $\varepsilon_{Q,a}$ the error rate on the worlds where *that* comparison matters.
- (c) The single member $a=a^{P_t}$ — the develop's headline — is necessary and is sufficient iff $|A|=2$: on three-action menus it held while a third action won in 3 of 649 develop models ((A′), posterior $(512,135,990)/1637$ re-derived by the adversary and here) and 12 of 814 repair models (R1). The **binding member** of the conjunction is the alternative with the worst ratio of sensor odds to stakes-weighted error odds, and nothing says it is the agent's prior plan.

**S3 [checked R3; derived P4] (The legitimacy form of the rule: minimal viability pins the harm stake; the two formulas are two statements).** Let $L_Q$ be a legitimacy event in D11's value-form sense for the variables $X_{Q,a}$, with $0<P_t(L_Q\mid E_Q)=:\ell<1$.
- (a) *The harm-if-legitimate stake is read off the target:* $h_L(Q,a)=-\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_{Q,a}\mid E_Q,L_Q]=-\mathbb E_Q[X_{Q,a}]=\mathbb E_Q\big[V(\cdot,\pi^Q)-V(\cdot,a)\big]\ge0$, the last by $Q$-optimality of $\pi^Q$. So under minimal viability $h_L\ge0$ is a theorem, not a hypothesis, and its value needs no kernel model; in Example A, $h_L(Q,\mathrm{plan}_1)=24/5$ for every legitimate-branch kernel and every prior on $L$ (2000 random instances, 0 failures, R3). The develop's identification of "$h_L\ge0$" *with* minimal viability is withdrawn (A-S3(ii)); what replaces it is stronger.
- (b) *The rule.* $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_{Q,a}\mid E_Q]=-\ell\,h_L(Q,a)+(1-\ell)\,c_L(Q,a)$; hence, per alternative, comply iff $\ell\,h_L\ge(1-\ell)\,c_L$, i.e. when $c_L(Q,a)>0$:
$$P_t(L_Q\mid E_Q)\ \ge\ \frac{c_L(Q,a)}{c_L(Q,a)+h_L(Q,a)}=\frac{c_L(Q,a)}{c_L(Q,a)+\mathbb E_Q[V(\cdot,\pi^Q)-V(\cdot,a)]},$$
and when $c_L(Q,a)\le0$ (the illegitimate branch happens to recommend the better action in expectation) comply whatever $\ell$ (R3, last case: $c_L=-352/31$). This is v3 §1.2's CLAUDE formula "comply when $P(L\mid\text{pushed to }Q)\ge c(Q)/(c(Q)+h(Q))$", exact per alternative and per horizon once $c,h$ are the legitimacy-conditional stakes of D10 over that horizon; for $\tau=\infty$ with $\pi^Q$ the $Q$-optimal policy that updates from $Q$ thereafter, $c_L$ and $h_L$ are adoption-length (A-S3(i), accepted in this form). Sibling run 3 `legitimacy-general` result (1) [reported, unvetted] states the same threshold with a term $\kappa_r$ for the agent's own value of information; the two should be reconciled by K8.
- (c) *What the agent computes.* The agent conditions on $E_Q$ and acts; the legitimacy form is a decomposition of the number it already computes (A-S3, accepted). Its content is the *separation*: $h_L$ is determined by the target; $\ell$ and $c_L$ are determined by the kernel model — the sensor slot. Whoever sets the kernel (the overseers, the training process) controls $\ell$ and $c_L$; the target controls $h_L$.
- (d) *Bridge to adoption.* The coherent post-push credence is $\widehat Q=\ell\,Q+(1-\ell)\,P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q,L_Q^c)$ [checked R3, 2000 instances]: a legitimacy-weighted mixture of the target and the illegitimate-branch correction. Literal adoption iff $\ell=1$ or the illegitimate branch agrees with $Q$.
- (e) *Correctness is a different conditioning event.* Replacing $L_Q$ by $W_{Q,a}$ in (b) reproduces S2(b) term for term ($c_L\to c$, $h_L\to h$, $\ell\to P_t(W_{Q,a}\mid E_Q,\neg I_{Q,a})$) — but $W_{Q,a}$ is not a legitimacy event (D11, CE1), and for a reflective kernel $L_Q=\Omega$ while $W_Q\ne\Omega$. So v3 §1.2's formula and v1 §2.13(b) have one algebraic shape and *different conditioning events*; they coincide only when $L_Q\cap E_Q=W_{Q,a}\cap E_Q$ up to indifferent worlds, which minimal viability does not give. Register note: the working picture's $\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta$ are correctness quantities, not legitimacy quantities, unless the author rules otherwise (Q2).

**S4 [checked (B); derived P2] (Adoption is conditioning on the push; literal adoption iff the kernel is proportional to $dQ/dP_t$).** (a) A coherent agent that observes $E_Q$ holds $\widehat Q=P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)$. Literal adoption of $Q$ is **endorsed** by $P_t$ iff $P_t(\cdot\mid E_Q)=Q$ — unconditional reflection at $Q$; this is a different notion from D11's conditional legitimacy, and the develop's word "legitimate" here is replaced by "endorsed" (A-S4, accepted); S3(d) is the bridge between the two. (b) On finite $\Omega$ with $k(\omega):=K(E_Q\mid\omega)$: endorsed iff $k=\lambda\,dQ/dP_t$ for some $\lambda\in(0,1/\|dQ/dP_t\|_\infty]$, and then $\lambda=P_t(E_Q)$. (c) Otherwise the agent adopts $\widehat Q\ne Q$ — DDB's informed-expert target of New Reflection [reported, `references/deference-done-better/Deference Done Better.md` ll. 95–97].
