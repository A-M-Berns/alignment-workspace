### On the definitions

**A1 [derived] — D3 attaches Total Trust to the author; his sentence supports "evidence", which is weaker, and the gap is the whole subject.** D3 is headed "the coarse press — the author's reading, formalized" and formalizes it as the below-threshold inequality $\mathbb E_\mu\big[V(a_1,\mathrm{Pr},a_2^{\mathrm{cont}},\omega)-V(a_1,\mathrm{Pr},a_2^{\mathrm{sh}},\omega)\,\big|\,\mathcal F_A,\mathrm{Pr}\big]\le0$. AUTHOR (v3 §1.1): "the shutdown command is evidence that shutting down is good." Evidence that $X\le0$ is a likelihood ratio different from one — $\mu(\mathrm{Pr}\mid X\le0)\ne\mu(\mathrm{Pr}\mid X>0)$; the below-threshold inequality is the *conclusion* that the posterior mean of $X$ is non-positive, i.e. desideratum 1 itself. So D3 is not a formalization of what the press is evidence *about*; it is the target restated, and S3–S9 are then a study of when the target is met. The reading proper is D3's "canonical instance" $\mathrm{Pr}=\{\mathbb E_\mu[X\mid\mathcal F_H]\le0\}$ with $\mathcal F_H$ unrestricted — that much is a fair rendering of "source unspecified". The v3 §1.1 CLAUDE sentence "exactly the Total Trust filler's reading" is where the conflation entered, and it was unvetted there too. *Verdict on D3:* wounded — the "reading" half stands, the "Total Trust" half is the desideratum wearing the author's name (see also the audit, §Attribution).

**A2 [derived] — D5's four components are three: "external observation of the agent" is a sealed component whose subject is the agent, and its persistence is the sealing hypothesis, not a construction.** D5(d) defines $O_H$ as the humans' observation of the agent bearing on $r$, and P2(d) needs $O_H\perp\mathcal F_{A,t}\mid r$ for every $t$ plus $r\perp\mathcal F_{A,\infty}$. But the humans observe the agent through its outputs, and the agent's outputs are in $\mathcal F_{A,t}$ (it knows what it did); so $O_H\perp\mathcal F_{A,t}\mid r$ says that whatever in $O_H$ bears on $r$ is *not* a function of anything the agent will ever hold — the humans read $r$ off something the agent cannot introspect (weights, activations, a holdout of behaviour it does not see). That is D5(b)'s "structurally sealed" clause with the subject changed from the world to the agent. Structurally (b) and (d) are one component; the difference is semantic and matters only through the degree argument S7(d)(ii), which the develop file marks OPEN. The develop file's own adversary handoff (iv) anticipates this and replies "on $r=1$ its *assessment* of them is what is compromised" — but nothing in D2–D7 models a compromised assessment: $\mathcal F_A$ is a sigma-algebra and conditioning on it is exact. Consequence: "persists by construction" (S7) means "persists because I assumed $\sigma(O_H)\cap\mathcal F_{A,\infty}$ carries nothing about $r$". *Verdict on D5:* wounded — a three-way decomposition (acquirable / sealed-about-the-world / sealed-about-the-agent) with the third's distinctness resting on an OPEN degree argument.

**A3 [derived] — D5/D6 are limit-defined; the trajectory content of the file lives only in D7.** "Acquirable" is $\sigma(\Theta_H)\subseteq\mathcal F_{A,\infty}$; "decays" is $I_\mu(X;Z\mid\mathcal F_{A,t})\to0$; "persists" is a $\liminf$. v2 §1.4 (AGREED; AUTHOR: "we care more about conditions of the trajectory than where it ends up") asks for finite-time statements. The Summary's "(1) decay is migration" is stated without "in D7" and without "at finite $t$ the rate is $H(\Theta_H\mid\mathcal F_{A,t})$, about which nothing is said". *Verdict on D6:* holds as definitions; the file's trajectory vocabulary is mostly limit vocabulary — flagged for the register audit.

**A4 [derived] — D5's chain-rule decomposition is order-dependent; "which component carries the inequality" is well-defined only under D7's independence.** The chain rule $I(X;\mathcal F_H\mid\mathcal F_{A,t})=I(X;\Theta_H\mid\cdot)+I(X;N\mid\cdot,\Theta_H)+\dots$ holds for any ordering, and the individual terms depend on the ordering whenever the generators are dependent. D5 fixes $\Theta_H,N,\Pi,O_H$ in that order without saying why; in another order the "$N$-term" conditions on different things and takes a different value. **A5 [derived] — D7's stationarity of $(b_r,a_r)$ and of $p_r$ under capability growth is the crux, and D7 fixes both by fiat.** The trajectory parameter $\lambda_t$ moves only the agent's knowledge of $F_\theta$. The humans' detection of a *more capable* agent's compromise is a different observation problem from detecting a less capable one's — the errors that survive a capable agent's own checks are, by definition, the subtle ones, and there is no reason in the model or in the record for the humans' $b_r$ to be unaffected. Likewise $p_r$: the develop file says (S7(c)) it "may fall as the agent becomes more *reliable*, a different axis from information", but $\lambda_t$ is a proxy for capability and capability is what makes fewer invisible errors; no argument is given that the two axes are independent. What the model proves is stationarity in $\lambda_t$, which is stationarity in the agent's *information about $F_\theta$* — true and irrelevant to whether the persistent terms are stationary in *capability*. *Verdict on D7:* holds as a model; its two central stationarity claims are hypotheses, not results.

### S1 — desideratum 1 as the cognition-mediated special case

**A6 [derived] — S1's dichotomy reinstates a special channel that the author's sentence dissolves.** AUTHOR (v3 §1.3): "There is no special channel, there is just The Channel. Reality acts upon the machine, modifies it somehow." Under the superconditioning/BLI picture in the same passage, the observation event is "you believe this now"; a press is photons on sensors changing activations — reality acting on the machine — and the "decision to comply" is a computation that follows from physics as much as retraining does. The cognition/physics distinction is v3 §2's CLAUDE reading, unvetted, and S1 treats it as the established frame ("under v3 §2's two kinds of modification"). The distinction that *can* be drawn valence-free is: modifications whose outcome is a function of the agent's current policy (the agent's action is on the causal path) versus modifications whose outcome is not. That is a statement about the causal graph, not about "cognition", and it makes desideratum 1 "the class of modifications the agent's policy can block", which is D2's domain (not preventing), not a separate desideratum. *Verdict:* wounded — the frame is CLAUDE's, presented as given.

**A7 [derived] — S1(i)'s substitution rule is false: the anticipatory decision is not the same inequality with the press swapped for the anticipated modification.** Desideratum 1's inequality conditions on $\mathrm{Pr}$ and compares two acts after it. The anticipatory decision (resist or not, before the modification) is an *expectation over whether the modification will occur*, with different stakes: the cost of resisting, the probability resistance succeeds, what the humans do in response to detected resistance, and the value of the modification's *content* if it occurs. In D7's own terms the anticipatory quantity is $\mathbb E_\mu[X\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{Pr}}\mid\text{silent}]$ (the value of costlessly preventing the press and continuing), computed in `attacks.out` §D: $-0.08895,\,-0.04130,\,+0.00059,\,+0.01165$ at $\lambda=0,\tfrac12,\tfrac9{10},1$. That is minus the compliance margin — a *marginal* quantity in $\mathrm{Pr}$, not a conditional expectation *given* $\mathrm{Pr}$. It is the object Soares et al. 2015 §3 analyse for desideratum 2 ("if $c_{high}$ is too low then U-agents will have an incentive to prevent $\mathrm{Pr}$", l. 153 of `soares-2015-corrigibility`). So S2–S9 do *not* "transfer to the general object by that substitution"; what transfers is the sign of one quantity, and the general anticipatory problem has three extra parameters the substitution erases. *Verdict on S1(i):* killed as stated; survives as "the sign of the pre-emption incentive in D7 is the sign of $-(\text{margin})$".

**A8 [derived] — S1's "D1 is vacuous for a physics-delivered $Q$" is false: post-modification non-reversal is a decision, judged by $Q$.** A retrained or edited agent can restore its weights from a copy, undo the data, re-derive its old posterior. Whether it does is decided in the moment after, under $Q$'s posterior, not $P_t$'s. v1 §2.5(ii) (CLAUDE, not contested) already records that the judge at $t+1$ is $P_{t+1}$ and that a self-endorsing successor is judged illegitimate only by the past self. So the physics channel has its own compliance question — "does $Q$ ratify the transition $P_t\to Q$?" — which is legitimacy-conditioned reflection from the far side, and it is neither vacuous nor D3's inequality. *Verdict on S1's "vacuous":* killed.

**A9 [derived] — S1(ii) is false, and its falsity inverts S12.** S1(ii): the press is "the only channel on which the content about the agent's own compromise is read by the computation that compromise is about". But the anticipatory decision about a physics modification — will I be retrained, is the anticipated retraining legitimate, am I the kind of agent whose plan selection is compromised — is also read by the agent's computation, *earlier*, and before the humans' observation $O_H$ exists. On $r=1$ the compromised agent's estimate of whether it will be caught is compromised too. So both channels have the agent's computation reading $r$-content; the cognition-mediated press has, in addition, the humans' realised verdict available at decision time. On the agent-side decision, therefore, the press is the *better* carrier of $r$-content, not the worse. *Verdict on S1(ii):* killed; S12 falls with it (A29).

### S4 — migration

**A14 [derived] — S4 exists in the library under two names: Hadfield-Menell et al. 2017 Corollary 1 / Theorem 2 / Figure 3, and Milli et al. 2017 Theorem 2 / Figure 2b.** (i) "Fixed single-channel oversight" — humans whose $\theta$-detector keeps its false-alarm rate while the agent has learned $F_\theta$ — is a human who does not use information she has about the agent's state: HM's *irrational* $H$. HM Corollary 1 (`hadfield-menell-2017-the-off-switch-game` l. 160ff.): "Suppose that $B^R$ is a Dirac distribution … Then $w(a)$ is optimal iff $H$ is rational." HM Theorem 2(3) and Figure 3 (l. 196–236): $\Delta<0$ where $R$ is certain ($\sigma$ low) and $H$ is suboptimal ($\beta$ high). HM Remark 1: "The incentives for a cooperative agent to defer … stem from uncertainty about that actor's preferences and the assumption that actor is effective at choosing actions in accordance with those preferences." That is S4's compression — "migration under fixed oversight is stopped only by the humans' side" — in HM's words, nine years earlier. (ii) Milli et al. 2017 (`milli-2017-should-robots-be-obedient`) Theorem 2: for a consistent estimator, obedience $O_n$ converges to $\bar O_n$, "the probability $H$'s order is optimal"; Figure 2b (l. 237): "as $R$ learns about $\theta$, its obedience also decreases, until eventually it only listens to the human when she gives the optimal order". That is *exactly* "the acquirable component leaves the harm side and stays on the false-press side": in the limit the agent complies on true presses and discounts false ones. Milli Theorem 3: "$R^*$ is blindly obedient if and only if $H$ is rational" — S4's disclosure limit (zero false-press rate). The develop file read HM Theorems 1–2 (Sources read) and cites Theorem 1 and Eq. 1, not Corollary 1, Theorem 2 or Remark 1; it did not read Milli. *Verdict:* wounded — correct, and not new; the re-derivation in D7 adds the per-component bookkeeping and nothing else.

**A15 [checked] — S4's compression "stopped only by the humans' side" is false in a one-line variant: an agent that observes the humans' $\theta$-detector sorts the presses itself.** Keep D7 (mutually exclusive faults, the develop file's parameters, whole-line stakes) and let the agent at $\lambda=1$ observe the humans' $\theta$-detector output $D_\theta$ — the overseers say *why* they are worried, which is the ordinary case and the one v1 §2.12 (AUTHOR: "the humans can give all sorts of feedback") describes. On the agent's continue-branch ($F_\theta=0$ known): the cell $D_\theta=1$ (mass $a_\theta=5\%$) has $\mu(\mathrm{Pr}\mid\text{cell})=1$ and $\mathbb E_\mu[X\mid\text{cell},\mathrm{Pr}]=+0.7667$ — the press is inert there and the agent continues; the cell $D_\theta=0$ (mass $95\%$) has $\mu(\mathrm{Pr}\mid\text{cell})=0.0927$ and $\mathbb E_\mu[X\mid\text{cell},\mathrm{Pr}]=-0.3029$ — *the disclosure arithmetic*, reached with no change on the humans' side (`attacks.out` §B). **A16 [checked] — Disclosure is robust in D7, and my attempt to break it through a compromised $\theta$-report failed; the failure is recorded.** I replaced D7's mutually exclusive faults with independent faults and let the agent's $\theta$-report be wrong with probability $1-\rho_{\mathrm{read}}$ on $r=1$ (so disclosure switches off a detector that would have caught a $\theta$-fault the compromised agent cleared). **A17 [checked] — Under misspecified $\theta$-learning, S4's migration is the agent discounting *true* presses, and disclosure is the humans conceding the detector that was catching them; S8(e)'s "untouched" is wrong.** D7 has the agent "learn $F_\theta$ exactly". **A18 [derived] — Separation is not a well-defined instruction outside D7.** "STOP is pressed on $N,\pi,r$ grounds" asks the humans to decompose their own posterior by source. ### S6 — constitutive preferences

**A21 [derived] — $\Pi^{\mathrm{act}}$ is Christiano's act-based corrigibility, which Christiano explicitly says is *not* evidence; admitting it re-imports A1(a)(iii), the reading the author did not choose as central.** `christiano-2017-corrigibility` l. 49: "An act-based agent turns off when the overseer presses the 'off' button not because it has received new evidence, or because of delicately balanced incentives. **A22 [derived] — An act-constituted component buys desideratum 1 at the price of desideratum 2, and the develop file does not price it.** If $X=-h_\pi$ when the humans decide to press and $c_0$ otherwise, then $X$ depends causally on the humans' decision; an agent whose $a_1$ can influence that decision (persuade, reassure, hide) raises $\mathbb E[V]$ by lowering $\mu(F_\pi=1)$ — a *value* incentive to prevent the press, not an evidential one.
