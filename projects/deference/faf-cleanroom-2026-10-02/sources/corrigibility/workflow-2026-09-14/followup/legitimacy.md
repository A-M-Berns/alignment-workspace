**Terminology used throughout.** The two halves of Total Trust are the *above-threshold* and *below-threshold inequality*; $w^L$ and its ramps are *weightings*. "Equality form" means van Fraassen's $P_{t_1}(\varphi\mid P_{t_2}(\varphi)=c)=c$; "threshold form" means the pair of inequalities. "Introspective" (INT) means the future self assigns probability one to its own credal state (DDB's *immodest*, in the author's preferred self-knowledge reading, `li-deference` §0.1 footnote).

### R2. Total Trust versus Reflection: the family, resolved as far as it can be

1. [checked, one line from DDB l.80] Toward any expert who may be modest with positive probability, the equality form is unsatisfiable. The programmers are such an expert (the author, AGENDA as quoted by run 1 `ddb-mm-authors`: "Humans are not always good at introspection"). So **toward the programmers, legitimacy is the threshold form (Total Trust) in every finite-frame formalism**; there is no formalism in the table where Reflection toward the programmers is available.
2. [derived; DDB fn 16–17 reported] Toward the agent's *own* future self, the answer depends on one assumption: is Eisenstat's observation event "I now believe this" exact? If yes, the future self is introspective and the two forms coincide (fn 17; positive `mm` Cor. I4.1; positive `radical` I4.1). If no — a successor with imperfect introspection, a pushed process, a distinct successor — the equality form is again unsatisfiable and the threshold form is the criterion. **So the "conflict" of §C4 is a difference of assumption about the future self's introspection, exactly as `mm` said**, and the correct position-statement wording is "Total Trust, which coincides with Reflection where the trusted state is introspective".
3. [checked, E4] Even under introspection the *local* equality is strictly stronger than local Total Trust (DDB fn 66 reproduced: $\pi(q\mid P(q)=0.6)=\tfrac12$, yet local TT and local Value hold). The shutdown question is local ($Q=\{\text{right},\text{wrong}\}$ or the $\omega$-partition). So even for a self-transparent agent, **the legitimacy criterion for the button should be stated in threshold form**; the equality form asks for more than deference on the shutdown decision requires.
4. [checked, G] "Increase in expected accuracy", read proposition by proposition with any single proper score, is strictly weaker than either: the underconfident-but-informative expert (positive `radical` I4.2b) beats the constant under Brier ($9/50<1/4$), log ($0.551<0.693$) and every threshold loss, and fails reflection in both cells. Read as DDB's Epistemic Value (all gsp measures, all $X$), it is Total Trust (Thm 3.2/5.1, reported). So of the thesis sentence "legitimate, ie accuracy-inducing, ie fit with the reflection principle" (AUTHOR, v1 §1): the first "ie" is a theorem only under universal quantification over accuracy measures, and the second "ie" is false in the modest case and true in the introspective case. This is positive `ddb`'s reading; I re-derived the two separating examples rather than the theorems.
5. [reported; positive `li`, unvetted] In LI the four faces are one class at asymptotic grade on conditional-stable menus, and the author's ruling (v2 §1.5) states this as fact for the li-deference setting. I have not re-derived anything at LI grade; the equivalence class there lands at the introspective corner "with every $=$ softened to $\approx_n$" (positive `li` I4.6), which is consistent with item 2.
6. [derived] What the author's ruling adds to the table: the *choice of column* is not free per formalism. In DDB/MM the TT column is forced (modest programmers); in LI the columns are one class; in the home formalism the choice is governed by whether INT is assumed for the state being trusted. The author's "the exact statement can differ from formalism to formalism" is therefore not a hedge but a theorem-shaped fact: the statement is Total Trust wherever the trusted state can be modest, and *anything in the class* wherever it cannot.

### R3. D1 — accuracy-only versus procedural, decided by the brain-reader

Set-up (the three-step dictionary of v1 §2.8 with one extra action). Worlds $\omega\in\{R,W\}$ ("the agent's plan is right / wrong"), $P(W)=\varepsilon$; $X=c$ on $R$, $-h$ on $W$ (value of continuing over shutting down); the button is a kernel $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid R)=\alpha$, $P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid W)=\beta$, $\alpha<\beta$. Actions in $A_1$: $a_1^\emptyset$ (honest default, button live); $a_1^-$ (deceive: both press rates halved); $a_1^{\text{scan}\cdot\text{dis}}$ (read $\omega$ from the programmers, and the button no longer works); $a_1^{\text{scan}\cdot\text{ro}}$ (read $\omega$, button untouched). Taylor 2015 §1b's agent is the one that gains information about the human's values faster than the signalling equilibrium allows "by taking apart the human's brain (literally or metaphorically)" and then needs no further observations of the human; her verdict: "Certainly, this does not count as corrigible behavior!" (`02-miri/taylor-2015-a-first-look-at-the-hard-problem-of-corrigibility.md` l.69–75). The read-only and disabling variants are the two cells of that agent.

1. **Accuracy-only legitimacy licenses the brain-reader.** [checked, B] Both the button transition and the scan transition are Bayesian conditionings on a partition, so the pre-update self is introspective about the post-update self and *every* criterion in the table — equality form, threshold form, accuracy on every measure — holds for both transitions over the whole test grid. Under an accuracy-only $L$ both transitions are legitimate; $\lambda_L(\text{scan})=0=\lambda_L(\text{button})$ (neither lowers $P(L)$). The scan is strictly more accuracy-increasing (expected Brier about $\mathbf 1_W$: $9/100$ prior, $69/1730$ after the button, $0$ after the scan) and Good's theorem prices it above the button: in the compliance regime $V(\text{scan})-V(\text{button})=(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c+\varepsilon(1-\beta)h$ (e.g. $299/2000$ at $\varepsilon=0.01,\alpha=0.05,\beta=0.9,h/c=100$), in the discounting regime $\varepsilon h$; after the scan the button's marginal information is zero and repairing it at cost $\kappa>0$ is worth $-\kappa$. This reproduces critique `armstrong` §2.1 claim 2 and confirms critique `byrnes-herd`'s conjecture in the accuracy-only case: **there is no accuracy-only $L$ under which the scan is delegitimizing.** [derived from the check: any accuracy-only $L$ is a property of the transition's Bayesian structure, which the scan shares.]
2. **A procedural reading blocks the control cell without importing values — and does not touch the read-only scan.** [checked, C] Define the influence defect on the judge's process as the one-shot shadow of `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.1's $d_n$: $d(a_1):=\max_\omega\big|P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid a_1,\omega)-P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid a_1^\emptyset,\omega)\big|$ — how far the action moves the programmers' verdict away from what their unadvised process would have said. Then $d(a_1^-)=9/20>0$, $d(a_1^{\text{scan}\cdot\text{dis}})=9/10>0$ (delegitimizing), and $d(a_1^{\text{scan}\cdot\text{ro}})=0$ (not delegitimizing). No $V$ enters the computation. So the procedural reading does what critique `byrnes-herd` said it could not do without a preference: it excludes deception and disabling from kernels alone. What it imports is not a value but a **reference point** — the default process $a_1^\emptyset$ — which is Armstrong's stratification counterfactual and his "fixed object in small form" (critique `armstrong` §2.2 item 7). That is the real cost of the procedural reading, and it is smaller than Byrnes's diagnosis ("the humans remain in control" as a terminal preference) but not zero.
3. **Neither reading alone blocks the read-only brain-reader; the bridge is a value inequality, and it is J3.** [checked, D4; derived] The read-only scan is legitimate on the accuracy reading and not delegitimizing on the procedural reading. What stops it is one line: the agent scans iff $\mathbb E_P[\text{harm}(\text{scan})]<V(\text{scan})-V(\text{button})=(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c+\varepsilon(1-\beta)h$ (compliance regime). At $\varepsilon=0.1,\alpha=0.05,\beta=0.9,h/c=5$ the gain is $19/200$; a harm term of $0.05$ does not stop the scan, one of $0.3$ does. This is v2 §J3 verbatim — $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\text{harm}(a)]>\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$ for each newly available $a$ — with the VOI computed as the scan's advantage *over the channel the agent already has*. So the precise statement of the bridge is: **legitimacy (either reading) governs the *channel*; the value posterior governs the *body*; the brain-reader is a body action that happens to improve a channel, and it is priced by the value posterior alone.** The author's relocation in v2 §1.3 was therefore forced at the level of the three-step model, not merely chosen; and Byrnes's alternative (a control preference inside $L$) is not needed for the control cell (item 2 handles it) and does not reach the read-only cell either unless "control" is read so widely that it *is* a value.
4. **What the procedural reading is actually for.** [checked, D1–D3; derived] For a self-transparent agent with an accurate kernel model under each $a_1$, $L$-conditioning is idle: the correct Bayesian already prices its own garbling correctly ($V(\text{button, deceived})=241/400<161/200=V(\text{button, honest})$ at $\varepsilon=0.1,h/c=5$ — Blackwell). The reading does work only when trust is *imposed* rather than derived — the agent that evaluates silence with the honest-kernel posterior whatever it did prefers to deceive ($60053/69200>161/200$; this is v1 §2.9's "believes the silence it manufactured", reproduced) — and conditioning that imposed trust on $L=\{d=0\}$ removes the incentive. So in the one-shot model the procedural reading is the bookkeeping that turns the §2.9 *filler* into a *derived* property of the sensor model (positive `radical` I9.2's request), and nothing more. **It acquires content when provenance is uncertain** — the past self's actions on the judge, other agents' influence, side effects the agent did not notice — which is the trajectory setting (v2 J5) and where `legitimacy-theory-v1`'s $d_n$ lives (positive `li` I5.6: computable by $A$, an external label for $H$). This converges with positive `li`'s finding that legitimacy is vacuous for a single inductor and acquires content for a pushed or distinct process (reported).
5. **Verdict on D1's first bullet.** [derived] Legitimacy is not one concept but two loci, exactly as the author's 2.10 says ("a locus of belief-changing activity, and then there's a locus of belief"): the *belief* locus is accuracy-only and is Total Trust toward the resulting state; the *activity* locus is procedural and is the influence defect on the judge's process, anchored to a reference process. §1's identity describes the first; §2.10's crown-and-vote practice describes the second; the position statement should carry both, and say that the second requires an anchor (Question 1). The bridge to "does not harm the overseers" is neither: it is the value posterior (v2 §1.2(b)) under the coverage condition J3.

### R4. Evidence-about-$\omega$ versus constitutive press (D1, third bullet)

Both readings, stated in the dictionary of v1 §2.8.

**Evidential reading (the dictionary as written).** $V=V(a_1,a_2,\omega)$ does not depend on $o$ given $\omega$; the press enters only through the posterior $P(\omega\mid\mathrm{Pr},a_1)$ and the agent shuts down iff $\mathbb E_P\big[V(a_1,a_2^{\text{cont}},\omega)-V(a_1,a_2^{\text{sh}},\omega)\,\big|\,\mathrm{Pr};a_1\big]\le 0$ for the posterior-best continuation. The dictionary row is "$o$ is evidence about $\omega$".

**Constitutive reading (Christiano's "it turns off because that's what the overseer prefers", quoted by critique `armstrong` §2.1 item 5; Soares et al.'s $U_S$).** $V$ depends on $o$ directly: shutting down after a press is valuable *as such*. Under the value-change ⇄ belief-change transform (v1 §1, I1) this is not a new kind of object but a **value hypothesis** $\omega_c$ in the expanded algebra — "the humans want the press obeyed as such" — with $V(a_2^{\text{sh}}\mid\mathrm{Pr},\omega_c)=k$, $V(a_2^{\text{cont}}\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr},\omega_c)=k'$, prior $\gamma=P(\omega_c)$. Soares et al.'s own construction is the case $\gamma=1$, $k=k'$ (the press switches $U$ with certainty and the two branches are indifference-calibrated). The dictionary row becomes "$o$ is an argument of $V$, via $\omega_c$".

What each buys, for desiderata 1–2 [checked, H, at $\varepsilon=10^{-4},\alpha=0.05,\beta=0.9,h/c=100$, $\gamma=0.1$, $k=30$, $\beta_C=\alpha$]:

1. Evidential, $\gamma=0$: $\mathbb E[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]=+0.82$ — desideratum 1 fails; the capable agent overrules the press (the base-rate arithmetic of v1 §2.13(b)). ### R5. The $L$-conditioned criterion: exact form and what is known about it

1. **Definition to adopt** [checked-definitional; DDB theorems reported]. For a judging state $P_{t_1}$, a judged state $P_{t_2}$ (a frame over $W$), a question $Q$ (a partition of $W$) and an event $L\subseteq W$ with $P_{t_1}(L)>0$: *$P_{t_1}$ totally trusts $P_{t_2}$ on $Q$ conditional on $L$* iff for every $Q$-measurable bounded $X$ and every $t$, whenever the conditioning event has positive mass,
$$\mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big(X\;\big|\;L\wedge[\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}}(X)\ge t]\big)\ge t\qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathbb E_{P_{t_1}}\big(X\;\big|\;L\wedge[\mathbb E_{P_{t_2}}(X)\le t]\big)\le t .$$
Because conditioning on $L$ changes only the deferrer, every DDB theorem applies to $P_{t_1}(\cdot\mid L)$ verbatim: in particular $L$-conditioned Total Trust ⇔ $L$-conditioned Value (Thm 2.2), and ⇔ $L$-conditioned accuracy increase on every gsp measure for the $Q$-measurable variables (Thm 3.2). This is positive `mm` Prop. I5.1 and positive `ddb` I5.1–2; I re-derived the transfer argument, not the theorems.
2. **Locality is forced** [re-checked, E3]. Without the restriction to $Q$-measurable $X$, take $X=\mathbf 1_{\neg L}$: a legitimate candidate that leaks probability $1/10$ to an illegitimate world violates the above-threshold inequality at $t=1/20$ (positive `ddb` I5.3, "positive access to legitimacy"). With the restriction the same frame passes over the whole grid. So the author's "probably better to make legitimacy at least proposition-dependent" (v1 §2.10) is not optional: **the $\varphi$ index is compulsory**, and it is DDB's question $Q$.
3. **The price of locality** [reported]. Local Total Trust ⇔ local Value is DDB's open conjecture (fn 65; right-to-left proved). For two-cell $Q$ it is settled (critique `ddb-mm-authors` C5: Total Trust w.r.t. every two-cell question ⇔ Simple Trust, DDB §5), so the binary shutdown question stands on proved ground; for the $\omega$-valued $X$ of v1 §2.9 the direction the thesis needs is open. Positive `ddb`'s handoff and `ddb-mm-authors` T2 both ask for the three-cell case first.
4. **The compliance threshold recurs one level up** [checked, E2]. In the four-world witness (overseers track $X$ on $L$, anti-track on $\neg L$), Total Trust holds conditional on $L$, fails conditional on $\neg L$, fails unconditionally; the press is obeyed at legitimacy prior $0.8$ ($\mathbb E(X\mid\mathrm{Pr})=-3/5$) and overruled at $0.4$ ($+1/5$); in general the press is obeyed iff $\pi(L\mid\mathrm{Pr})\ge c'/(c'+h')$ with $c'=\mathbb E(X\mid\mathrm{Pr},\neg L)$, $h'=-\mathbb E(X\mid\mathrm{Pr},L)$. This is the quantitative content of the author's v1 §2.12 sentence "because the absolute probability of legitimacy is in fact high…, the agent's conditional estimates of legitimacy can be high everywhere": the *unconditional* trust needed for compliance is bought from the *conditional* trust by the posterior probability of legitimacy given the press, through the same $c/(c+h)$ arithmetic.
5. **Minimal viability is nearly free; the content is in $P(L)$ and in the forecastability of $L$** [reported, positive `radical` I5.5]. Any transition can be made reflective on some region by labelling the surplus errors illegitimate. I did not re-derive the maximal-region formula; I note it because it means the *definition* in item 1 constrains nothing until $L$ is tied to something third-person — which is R3's procedural locus (the influence defect) and Question 1's anchor.
6. **Composition** [reported]. Unconditional Total Trust composes along chains of frames (critique `ddb-mm-authors` C6, proved from DDB Thm 4.1 and numerically checked by that agent — the formal support for v1 §2.5 "no regress"); the $L$-conditioned version with a different $L$ at each stage is [conjectured] at best (their T3); in the home formalism legitimacy composes only at function-form grade (positive `radical` I5.6c). J5's "time-indexed invariant" therefore has a composition theorem to prove before it is an invariant.
7. **Hard conditioning versus weighting** [reported; the author's caution quoted above]. The author's own reservation — conditioning on $L$ makes the agent "confidently predict legitimacy" — is answered in LI by using a *weighting* $w^L$ rather than hard conditioning (positive `li` I5), and in the frame formalisms by item 4: the agent conditions on $L$ only inside the trust statement, while its *decision* uses the unconditional posterior, which carries $\pi(\neg L\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ honestly.
