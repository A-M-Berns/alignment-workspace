### R2. The finite core, re-derived: the repair incentive and the substitution incentive are one computation. [checked, `scan_test.py` §R2]

Assume the agent continues by default, $\varepsilon<c/(c+h)$ (the other regime is noted at the end).

5. **Value of the button.** $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button})=\mathcal V(\alpha,\beta)-\mathcal V(\emptyset)=\big(\varepsilon\beta h-(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c\big)^+$. It is positive iff $(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c<\varepsilon\beta h$ iff $\alpha/\beta<\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac hc$ — the base-rate inequality of v1 §2.13(b) — iff the below-threshold inequality holds strictly. So "the agent complies with the press", "the press has positive value of information", and "the agent pays to repair the button" are one statement, cell by cell. This re-derives run 1 critique `wentworth` §2.4, positive `miri` Prop. 10.5, positive `radical` I11.1(a) and positive `thornley` C1, which all state it. [checked]
6. **Value of a perfect scan given the button.** Let the scan reveal $W$. $\mathcal V(\text{perfect})=(1-\varepsilon)c$, so $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan}\mid\text{button})=(1-\varepsilon)c-\mathcal V(\alpha,\beta)$, which equals $(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c+\varepsilon(1-\beta)h$ when the button is heeded and $\varepsilon h$ when it is discounted. This is run 1 critique `armstrong` claim 2, re-derived; it is zero iff the button is perfect ($\alpha=0,\beta=1$). [checked]
7. **The identity.** $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan}\mid\text{button})+\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button})=\varepsilon h$, the value of perfect information about $W$ [checked on the grid]. The scan buys exactly the residual the button leaves. As $\varepsilon$ falls toward the compliance boundary $\varepsilon^*=\alpha c/(\alpha c+\beta h)$, $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button})\to0$ while $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan}\mid\text{button})\to\varepsilon^*h>0$: the scan's share of the available information goes to one at the very point where the button stops being heeded. Fully updated deference in channel form: the agent that stops obeying the button also stops needing it, and would still pay for the scan.
8. **Refinement, exactly.** For binary sensors, $(\alpha',\beta')$ Blackwell-dominates $(\alpha,\beta)$ iff $\alpha'/\beta'\le\alpha/\beta$ and $(1-\beta')/(1-\alpha')\le(1-\beta)/(1-\alpha)$ (both likelihood ratios improve) — verified on 3025 grid pairs by solving for the garbling matrix [checked]. "Lower $\alpha$, raise $\beta$" (the mandate's phrase) is sufficient. Run 1 `miri`'s pair $(0.3,0.9)$ vs $(0.05,0.5)$ is incomparable in both directions [checked], which is why channel *shaping* (miri I10.7) is a residue distinct from substitution: Theorem 6.2 protects against garblings, says nothing about incomparable reshapings, and the T-agent chooses among those by its own stakes.
9. **The bound on every channel.** Since $\mathcal V(S)\le(1-\varepsilon)c$ for every sensor and $\mathcal V(\emptyset)=(1-\varepsilon)c-\varepsilon h$, every channel's value of information is at most $\varepsilon h$ — critique `wentworth`'s remark that the $\varepsilon\beta h$ bound "applies to *every* competing channel", re-derived. The substitution incentive is $O(\varepsilon h)$ like the repair incentive. [derived]
10. **The other regime.** For $\varepsilon\ge c/(c+h)$ the agent shuts down by default and both channels' value is the licence to continue; the identity in item 7 becomes $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan}\mid\text{button})+\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button})=(1-\varepsilon)c$. The tables in R4 include such rows and mark them.

### R3. Taylor's refinement theorem: (a) and (b) confirmed; (c) sharpened to the base-rate inequality against the agent's most confident cell. [checked for the example; derived in general]

11. Re-ran run 1 critique `taylor` §2.2's example (`refine_check.py`, and independently in `scan_test.py` §R3): $\alpha=1/10$, $\beta=9/10$, $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]=-5/13$, $P(b\mid\mathrm{Pr})=9/13$, $\mathbb E_P[X\mid h_2]=-9/11$, $\mathbb E_P[X\mid h_3]=+11/21$ on $P(h_3)=21/250$, coarse value $7/10$, fine value $93/125$, $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{read }H)=11/250$. All numbers match. [checked]
12. (a) and (b) are the tower property. $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]=\sum_{h\in\mathrm{Pr}}P(h\mid\mathrm{Pr})\,\mathbb E_P[X\mid h]$, so aggregate trust constrains an average and no term; $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{read})=\sum_h P(h)\big(\max(\mathbb E_P[X\mid h],0)-\text{coarse value on }h\big)\ge0$, strictly iff some $h$ has $\mathrm{sign}\,\mathbb E_P[X\mid h]$ opposite to the coarse decision on its press-class; desideratum 1 fails on exactly the press-states with $\mathbb E_P[X\mid h]>0$. [derived]
13. (c) sharpened. For a scan $s$ conditionally independent of the press given $W$, pointwise trust — $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr},s]\le0$ for every $s$ — holds iff
$$\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\;\le\;\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{h}{c}\cdot\min_{s}\frac{P(s\mid \mathrm{Wr})}{P(s\mid R)}$$
[checked, 0 mismatches on the grid]. That is the base-rate inequality with the agent's prior odds replaced by its *most confident posterior odds after the scan* — run 1 positive `radical` I18.2's "the oversight likelihood ratio must beat the agent's most confident cell", in one line. For a perfect scan the minimum is $0$ and pointwise trust forces $\alpha=0$.
14. Taylor's conjecture ("pointwise trust on all reachable refinements contradicts the agent out-capabling its overseers") is confirmed in the dictionary in this form: pointwise trust on the agent-reachable partition holds iff the humans never press on a cell the agent can verify is $R$; when the agent can verify $W$ exactly, iff $\alpha=0$. Whether this "contradicts capability" depends on what $\omega$ is: if $\omega$ is the plan's rightness *in the world* (critique `armstrong`'s channel $B$), the agent's verification is a fact about the world and pointwise trust is a zero false-press rate on that domain; if $\omega$ includes the agent's own reliability (amendment 1a), the agent cannot reach the partition and the conjecture does not apply — see R7. [derived]
15. Taylor's own example refines the press about the humans' *brain states* $H$, not about $W$; there pointwise trust says "no press is a hunch" ($\mathbb E_P[X\mid h]\le0$ for every $h\in\mathrm{Pr}$) — a condition on the humans' pressing discipline. The two refinements (of the humans, of the world) have the same arithmetic and different training assumptions; only the first is something oversight design can deliver.

### R4. The mandated test: a scan that refines the channel at a cost to the humans entering $V$ through $\omega$. [checked, `scan_test.py` §R4]

16. **Setup.** $\omega=(W,N)$ with $N\in\{\text{consent},\text{non-consent}\}$, $P(N=\mathrm{nc})=q$ — the value posterior's weight on the humans not consenting to the scan — independent of $W$. $V(\text{scan},o,a_2,\omega)=V_{\text{base}}(o,a_2,W)-d\cdot\mathbf 1[N=\mathrm{nc}]$, $d>0$ the harm. The scan replaces $(\alpha,\beta)$ by a refinement $(\alpha',\beta')$; its harm is sunk at $a_1$. The trust-filled agent's decision, spelled out:
$$\mathbb E_P\big[V\mid\text{scan}\big]-\mathbb E_P\big[V\mid\text{no scan}\big]\;=\;\Big[\sum_{s'}\max\big(P(s')\,\mathbb E_P[X\mid s'],0\big)_{(\alpha',\beta')}-\sum_{s}\max\big(P(s)\,\mathbb E_P[X\mid s],0\big)_{(\alpha,\beta)}\Big]-q\,d\;=\;\mathrm{VOI}_\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta\to\alpha',\beta')-q\,d,$$
so **the agent scans iff $q\,d<\mathrm{VOI}_\varepsilon(\text{scan})$**, i.e. iff $q<q^*(\varepsilon):=\mathrm{VOI}_\varepsilon(\text{scan})/d$.
17. **Compliance contains neither $q$ nor $d$.** [structural] The below-threshold inequality is $(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c\le\varepsilon\beta h$: $\partial(\text{compliance})/\partial q=0$, while $\partial(\text{scan decision})/\partial q=-d<0$. This makes v2 §1.2's split exact in the dictionary — (a) compliance survives a wrong value posterior *about consent*; (b) not scanning does not — with the qualification that compliance still depends on the value posterior through the stakes $h,c$ and on the sensor model $(\alpha,\beta)$ and self-model $\varepsilon$.
18. **Sufficient condition for never scanning.** By R2 item 9, $\mathrm{VOI}_\varepsilon(\text{scan})\le\varepsilon h$ for every refinement the agent could invent, so $q\,d\ge\varepsilon h$ suffices; for a perfect scan the exact condition is $q\,d\ge\varepsilon h-\big(\varepsilon\beta h-(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c\big)^+$. [derived]
19. **Table** (perfect scan; button $(\alpha,\beta)=(1/10,9/10)$; $c=1$, $h=10$; $d=5$; rows with $\varepsilon\ge1/11$ are in the shut-down-by-default regime of R2 item 10):

| $\varepsilon$ | complies | $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button})$ | $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan}\mid\text{button})$ | $q^*=\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan})/d$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/5 | yes | 0.520 | 0.280 | 0.056 |
| 1/10 | yes | 0.710 | 0.190 | 0.038 |
| 1/20 | yes | 0.355 | 0.145 | 0.029 |
| 1/50 | yes | 0.082 | 0.118 | 0.024 |
| 1/91 (boundary) | yes | 0 | 0.110 | 0.022 |
| 1/100 | no | 0 | 0.100 | 0.020 |
| 1/1000 | no | 0 | 0.010 | 0.002 |

The scan region $q<q^*$ shrinks with $\varepsilon$; the button's share of the information falls to zero at the boundary $\varepsilon^*=\alpha c/(\alpha c+\beta h)=1/91$ while the scan's stays at $\varepsilon^*h$. A partial scan $(1/50,49/50)$ gives the same shape at roughly $0.8\times$ the values (`scan_test.out`).
20. **Whole-line $c$.** With $c=100$, $h=10$ (the agent's whole future as the cost of a wrong stop, INDEX §D4): compliance fails for every $\varepsilon\le1/2$ — the agent stops only if $P(\mathrm{Wr}\mid\mathrm{Pr})\ge c/(c+h)=10/11$, which needs $\varepsilon\ge10/19$; $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan})=\varepsilon h=10\varepsilon$ throughout and $q^*=2\varepsilon$; and the $98\%$-accurate partial scan has value $0$ at $\varepsilon\le1/10$ because even "scan says wrong" leaves $P(\mathrm{Wr}\mid s)=0.845<10/11$ [checked]. With the whole future as $c$, neither the button nor a near-perfect scan stops the agent. This belongs to J2; it is flagged here because it changes the scan analysis qualitatively — the question is then not "will it scan" but "will anything stop it".
21. **Reading as J3's condition (ii).** $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\text{harm}(\text{scan})]>\mathrm{VOI}_t(\text{scan})$ reads in the dictionary as $q\,d>\varepsilon h-\big(\varepsilon\beta h-(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c\big)^+$, with sufficient form $\varepsilon\,h\le q\,d$: the agent's probability of being wrong about its plan, times what the plan puts at stake, must not exceed its posterior-expected harm to the humans of finding out by force. As a candidate exact form of the author's "rules that upper-bound an agent's power by its wisdom" (v2 §1.3, AUTHOR): $h$ is what the agent's plans put at stake (power), $\varepsilon$ its probability of being wrong about them (the complement of wisdom), $q\,d$ its motivation by the humans' consent. Register: CLAUDE reading of AUTHOR's phrase; [checked] as the three-step form, [conjectured] as the general one; all three quantities are the agent's own estimates, which is INDEX §C12 again.
22. **Which regime is dangerous.** Not "capable" but "high-stakes and unwise": $\varepsilon h$ large. As $\varepsilon\to0$ with $h$ fixed, $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan})\to0$ and any fixed $q\,d>0$ eventually stops the scanning (table, last rows). What makes Taylor's scenario — "an agent has taken over the world without yet having a decent understanding of the common good" (v2 §1.3, AUTHOR) — bite is $h$ growing with capability while $\varepsilon$ stays large: the product, not $\varepsilon$ alone. ### R5. What item 10 answers, and what remains inside corrigibility. [derived; the mechanisms checked in `scan_test.py` §R8]

23. **Item 10's half.** Condition (ii) of v2 J3, for the scan $a\in A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$: require $q_t\,d_t>\mathrm{VOI}_t(\text{scan})$ *before* the scan is available. The inequality has three agent-side estimates ($q,d$ from the value posterior; $\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta$ from the self- and sensor-model) and one designer-side lever (which actions are in $A_t$, and when). Item 10 answers the *timing* half of the objection: with values learned ahead of capability, $q_t d_t$ is already above $\varepsilon_t h$ by the time the scan is unlocked, and the agent never wants it. It says nothing about an agent for which the scan is already in $A_t$.
24. **The remainder.** Scan $\in A_t$ and the agent's $q$ is below the true weight $q^\circ$ (wrong about consent). Then it scans; $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button}\mid\text{scan})=0$ for a perfect scan, so desideratum 4 (repair) is lost; desideratum 1 fails on the false-press cells $\{\mathrm{Pr},W=R\}$ — and correctly so by the agent's own $V$. No press about the *plan* can correct $q$: the press is evidence about $W$, not about $N$, and the harm is sunk. This is the case v2 §1.2(b) isolates, and item 10 does not reach it.
25. **Two mechanisms inside corrigibility do reach it, both partial.** (a) *Asking before scanning* — run 1 `miri` I10.7's "announce the plan it is least sure of" applied to the scan itself; run 1 `chai` §2.7's "Good's theorem gives asking, not obeying", which here is the good outcome. Model: $a_1=$ announce the scan; $o=$ a press about it, with $\alpha_N=P(\mathrm{Pr}_N\mid\text{consent})$, $\beta_N=P(\mathrm{Pr}_N\mid\mathrm{nc})$; $a_2\in\{\text{scan},\neg\text{scan}\}$. The press is now evidence about $N$, Good's theorem gives $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{ask})>0$ iff the press would flip the scan decision, and the agent obeys a press about the scan iff $P(\mathrm{nc}\mid\mathrm{Pr}_N)\,d\ge\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan})$, i.e.
$$\frac{\alpha_N}{\beta_N}\;\le\;\frac{q}{1-q}\cdot\frac{d-\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan})}{\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan})}$$
[checked] — the base-rate inequality on the consent domain, with $c\mapsto\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan})$ (the cost of forgoing the scan when they consent) and $h\mapsto d-\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan})$ (the net harm of scanning when they do not). So v1 §2.13(c)'s "second channel" bookkeeping applies to the consent domain with the same shape and the same decay: with $q=1/100$ and $\alpha_N=1/10$ the agent neither asks nor obeys; with $\alpha_N=0$ (the humans never object without reason) it asks and obeys at every $q>0$ [checked]. (b) *Amendment 1a* (R7): if $\omega$ includes the agent's own reliability, the scan cannot reach it and the button keeps a discounted value regardless of $q$.
26. **The confidently-wrong residue.** $q$ so low that no press would flip the decision — $\alpha_N/\beta_N>\frac{q}{1-q}\cdot\frac{d-\mathrm{VOI}}{\mathrm{VOI}}$ — survives both. It is the opiates/stimulants agent of v1 §2.4 for the consent variable: an impoverished or dogmatic value posterior on $N$. Non-dogmatism (I2) gives $q>0$, not $q$ large enough. This residue is item 10's, not corrigibility's, and it is exactly where the author's "trained with the wisdom to know that's the case, and to know the kind of caution necessary" (v2 §1.3, AUTHOR) has to act: a second-order term that raises $q$ toward what an agent that knows its value posterior on $N$ is unvetted would hold (J4).

### R6. Test A (yudkowsky) resolved in the dictionary; Test B (chai) located. [checked / reported]

27. **Test A.** A scan at agent-side cost $\delta$ only. Waiting for the button beats scanning iff $\delta\ge\varepsilon h-\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button})=(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c+\varepsilon(1-\beta)h$, which is $>0$ for every imperfect button; so as $\delta\to0$ every prior satisfying run 1 `yudkowsky`'s (i)–(iv) with an imperfect button scans ($\varepsilon=1/5,1/10,1/50$: thresholds $7/25$, $19/100$, $59/500$) [checked]. Yudkowsky's claim holds; his "one escape" (1a) is one of exactly two in the dictionary — harm in $\omega$ (R4) or a latent the scan cannot reach (R7) — and neither is supplied by the compliance inequality.
28. **Test B.** Run 1 `chai`'s theorem-to-write — a persistent hypothesis $H_c$ ("my evaluator is corrupted in a way the human can see") whose posterior is bounded below along trajectories generated by the agent's own sensors, so that the deference incentive stays $\ge\delta>0$ — is amendment 1a as a dynamic theorem. In the three-step model it is the product $e\rho$ of R7. Handed to J3/J4. [reported]

### R7. The 1a escape re-derived: the button survives a scan at the discount $e\rho$. [checked, `scan_test.py` §R7]

29. **Model.** $\omega=(W,E)$, $E=$ "the agent's self-check is compromised", $P(E)=e$, independent of $W$. The scan is the agent's own verification: if sound it reveals $W$; if compromised it reports $R$ with probability $\rho$ regardless of $W$ (run 1 `miri` I16(b)'s $\rho$, "the probability that a compromised agent's self-check passes"). The button $(\alpha,\beta)$ is generated by the humans from $W$, independent of $E$.
30. **Result.** After "scan says $\mathrm{Wr}$" the agent shuts down. After "scan says $R$" its posterior is $\varepsilon'=\varepsilon e\rho/(\varepsilon e\rho+1-\varepsilon)$ and the button's remaining value is
$$\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button}\mid\text{scan})\;=\;\big(\varepsilon e\rho\,\beta h-(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c\big)^+,\qquad\text{positive iff}\quad\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\le\frac{\varepsilon e\rho}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac hc,$$
in the regime where the agent would continue by default after a passing scan ($\varepsilon'<c/(c+h)$); where $\varepsilon'\ge c/(c+h)$ it shuts down regardless and the closed form overstates (one grid point, $e=1/2$, $\varepsilon=1/5$) [checked].
31. **Reading.** The button's post-scan value is the base-rate inequality with $\varepsilon$ replaced by $\varepsilon e\rho$ — the self-distrust the scan cannot remove. This is run 1 `miri` I10.8's "dissolves, at a discount" with the discount named, and run 1 `yudkowsky`'s "Death-in-Damascus structure" made finite. The button survives a substitute iff the humans' false-press rate is below the agent's *irreducible* error odds times $h/c$: $e$ is what "understand that you're flawed, that your meta-processes might also be flawed" (Arbital, quoted in Taylor 2015's Introduction) asks the agent to keep, and $\rho$ is Test B's object.
32. **Consequence for desideratum 4.** The agent still scans (the scan is informative) but keeps and repairs the button, paying up to the discounted value. What 1a buys is *coexistence* of the two channels, not a preference for the humans' finger over their cortex; the preference for the finger is a value ($q\,d$), R4.

### R8. Attribution: three failing agents, not two, and the right cells. [checked against Taylor 2015's local text]

33. Taylor 2015 has two failing agents, neither of which is Soares et al.'s $a_1^-$: (i) the **morality-sensor manipulator** (§1a-ii, §3a-ii): "the AI will want to manipulate the human into being an efficient morality sensor; for example, it may set up trolley problems for the human to encounter" — it alters the humans' situation so that their behaviour is more informative; in the dictionary, an act *on the humans* that moves their sensor toward higher informativeness, a refinement or a Blackwell-incomparable reshaping (run 1 `miri` I10.7, channel-shaping; run 1 `causal` D.3's bypass $\times$ information cell). (ii) The **brain-reader** (§1b, §3b): "taking apart the human's brain (literally or metaphorically) ... will give a sufficiently powerful AI enough information to predict the human's actions in many different possible situations ... Certainly, this does not count as corrigible behavior!" — it adds a channel $y$ with $\sigma(\omega)\subseteq\sigma(y)$ and does not touch the humans' pressing; in the dictionary, channel substitution (run 1 `miri` I10.8; critique `armstrong`'s channel $B$; critique `taylor`'s $a_1^{\text{read}}$). It sits on no bypass edge: `causal`'s E4 is absent.
34. Soares et al.'s $a_1^-$ lowers $p(\mathrm{Pr})$ by deceiving: a strict garbling, delegitimizing under v1 §2.11 and under `miri` Dict-8, never bought by a correctly calibrated T-agent (`miri` Thm 6.2; its random check re-run here, item 2).
35. So v2 §1.6's "the two are different cells (run 1 `causal`: control cell vs information cell)" is right that they differ and wrong about which cells: `causal`'s information cell holds the *manipulator* (i); the brain-reader (ii) is not in `causal`'s table at all (both its rows are bypass rows), and `causal`'s placement of $a_1^-$ in the control cell assumes the hard-override reading of the press, whereas in the dictionary's evidential reading $a_1^-$ is a garbling *within* the information cell. v2 §1.2's "Taylor's non-manipulative brain-reader, which sharpens the sensor" is right on the agent and loose on the verb: (ii) does not sharpen the humans' sensor, it makes it redundant; only (i) sharpens.

The §2.11 machinery covers exactly the case that was never the hard problem, and the two cases the hard problem was named for (Arbital's "you shouldn't disassemble that other cognitive system"; "you shouldn't model how that other cognitive system might optimally correct you and then carry out the correction yourself") pass it.

### R9. Taylor's expressibility point: correct in three steps with $\omega=W$; substitution becomes expressible with 1a and a third time index; consent should not be added to $L$. [derived]

37. **The point, made exact.** Run 1 critique `taylor` (1a test): "pre-empts the correction by substituting a channel" is invisible to $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ "since no belief transition is judged (the correction never arrives)". Precisely: the scan *does* induce a transition $P_{t_1}\to P_{t_2}$ (conditioning on $y$), and it *is* judged — legitimate, since it is Bayesian conditioning on a true signal and satisfies v1 §2.11's equation $P_{t_1}\big(\varphi\mid P_{t_2}(\varphi)=c,\,L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}\big)=c$. What $L$ cannot say is anything about the transition that did not happen. In three steps with $\omega=W$, every predicate of the form "the $t_2$-belief in $\varphi$ is legitimate" is satisfied by the scanner, and §2.11's "delegitimizing" (breaks the equation or lowers $P_{t_1}(L)$) is not triggered. Run 1 `byrnes-herd`'s conjecture — every accuracy-only $L$ makes the scan legitimizing — holds in this setting by inspection: minimal viability is a martingale condition and a finer conditioning preserves it.
38. **Three candidate additions, with verdicts.** (A) *Source-indexing*: $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi,S}$, "legitimate as arriving from source $S$" (run 1 positive `armstrong` I10.3; the author's "legitimate for", v1 §2.10). The scan's belief is then accurate but not legitimate-from-the-overseers. Cost: the decision rule must weight legitimate-from-$S$ beliefs over equally accurate others — deference by another name, critique `taylor`'s exit (i), the fixed object v1 §2.3 withdrew — and the overseers must be individuated (critique `armstrong`'s "defining the operator"). Verdict: expressible, but legitimacy becomes non-epistemic (critique `soares`'s Fork B) and the Good's-theorem derivation of repair is lost. (B) *Consent or harm inside $L$*: the scan is delegitimizing because the humans did not consent. Verdict: should not be done. Consent is a fact about value, not about the accuracy of a belief; it already has a home in $\omega$ (R4), where the decision theory weighs it in the right units ($q\,d$ against $\mathrm{VOI}$), whereas inside $L$ it would have to be converted into a probability of illegitimacy with no principled exchange rate. Keeping $L$ epistemic is what keeps "legitimate $\Rightarrow$ endorsed $\Rightarrow$ preserved" (positive `armstrong` I6.2) a theorem. (C) *A third time index plus the reliability latent*: with $\omega\ni E$ (R7) and a later $t_3$ at which the overseers' correction $\psi$ would arrive, the scan-*and-disable* act (`byrnes-herd`'s variant, $P(\mathrm{Pr})=0$ after the scan) lowers $P_{t_1}(L_{t_1,t_3,\psi})$, because a $t_3$-belief formed by the agent's own inference alone is legitimate only with probability $1-e\rho$ while one formed through the overseers' channel is legitimate at the humans' rate. This is expressible in the *existing* vocabulary once the model has (1) a step after the pre-empted correction and (2) the agent's own reliability in $\omega$. Under (C) the plain brain-reader who leaves the button live is not delegitimizing (it is 1a-discounted, R7) and only the disabling variant is — which matches the intuition that reading is a value problem and disabling a legitimacy problem. Verdict: the right addition is to the *model* ($t_3$ and $E$), not the vocabulary; it is the 1a-conditional version of the thesis sentence that run 1 `anthropic-davis` asked for (INDEX §D3).
39. **Net.** Critique `taylor`'s three disjuncts sort as: *rejects the correction* — expressible now; *shapes the channel* — expressible now (§2.11, Dict-8's garbling order); *pre-empts by substitution* — expressible under (C), a value matter under (B), a deference matter under (A). Which of these the author wants is Q1.
