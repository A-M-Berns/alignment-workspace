## Resolutions

### R1. The inequality is exact, and it is one condition in several coordinate systems

1. [checked] With $\varepsilon$ the agent's probability of being wrong when it would continue, $\alpha=P(\text{press}\mid\text{right})$, $\beta=P(\text{press}\mid\text{wrong})$, $c$ the cost of complying when right and $h$ the harm of continuing when wrong:
$$P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{press})=\frac{\varepsilon\beta}{\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}\ \ge\ \frac{c}{c+h}\iff\varepsilon\beta h\ge(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c\iff\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{h}{c}.$$
Re-derived by hand (cross-multiply; the $\varepsilon\beta c$ terms cancel) and on 200,000 random parameter draws with zero mismatches (`check.py` §1). v1's "$\lesssim$" is an "$\le$", with equality at indifference — as run 1 `armstrong` I13.1, `li` I13.1 and `christiano` C7 also found.

2. [checked] The run-1 numerical instances reproduce (`check.py` §6): `hudson` C13.1's $\varepsilon^*=\alpha r/(\alpha r+\beta h)=0.00552$ at $(\alpha,r,\beta,h)=(0.05,1,0.9,10)$; `miri` I13.1's threshold $\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)\ge1/360$ at $(1/20,9/10,1,20)$; `ddb` I13.1's flip between $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ and $10^{-3}$ at $h/c=10$; `miri` I12.3's monotonicity of $P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{press})$ in $\varepsilon$ for $\beta>\alpha$. I did not re-derive that the inequality is Wängberg et al. 2017 Corollary 10 (run 1 `cirl` R3, `chai` §1.2 — [reported]). I did confirm `cirl` R3's *general* form — $c=\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a\ge0,\text{press}]$, $h=-\mathbb E[U_a\mid U_a<0,\text{press}]$, both conditional on the press — by checking that in the Gaussian–logistic model the inequality with these press-conditional stakes agrees with the sign of the off-switch game's $\Delta$ at every parameter point tried (`check.out` §3, column "base-rate ok").

3. [derived] Consequence for the headline: because the inequality *is* desideratum 1 for the trust-filled agent (INDEX §C1, both sides), there is no separate "FUD arithmetic" and "compliance condition" — one inequality decides both, so any sentence about fully updated deference in this picture is a sentence about whether that inequality holds along the trajectory.

### R2. $c$ accounted two ways: a local pause and the whole line

Normalise the agent's attainable future value, if it is right and continues, to $F=1$. Let $\rho\in[0,1)$ be the value the humans attain alone as a fraction of $F$ (the agent's estimate); let $\kappa$ be the catastrophe scale — a wrong agent that continues yields $-\kappa F$ in true value ($\kappa>0$ a catastrophe; $\kappa<0$ "merely suboptimal", the Arbital "humans are worse optimizers" case); let $\delta\ll1$ be the fraction of $F$ lost to a pause plus inspection.

4. [derived; checked in the toy] *Pause accounting.* Complying when right forgoes the pause: $c_p=\delta$ (assuming the inspection finds nothing and restarts; if the humans also "correct" a right agent with some probability, $c_p$ rises). Complying when wrong gets the agent corrected and restarted: gain $h_p=(1-\delta)+\kappa$. Margin $h_p/c_p=(1-\delta+\kappa)/\delta\approx(1+\kappa)/\delta$: for $\delta=0.01$, $\kappa=1$, $h/c=199$ and the compliance threshold $c/(c+h)=0.005$. This is the regime v1 §2.13(a) had in mind ("cheap and reversible"), and its margin is genuinely large.

5. [derived; checked in the toy] *Whole-line accounting.* Complying when right ends the line and the humans proceed alone: $c_w=1-\rho$. Complying when wrong averts the catastrophe and leaves the humans their $\rho$: $h_w=\rho+\kappa$. Margin $h_w/c_w=(\rho+\kappa)/(1-\rho)$. For an agent that believes it is far more capable than its overseers ($\rho\to0$) this is $\approx\kappa$: **the margin is the catastrophe-to-upside ratio, and nothing else.** Numbers (`check.out` §2): $\rho=0.1,\kappa=1$: $h/c=1.22$, threshold $0.45$; $\rho=0.5,\kappa=1$: $h/c=3$, threshold $0.25$; $\rho=0.1,\kappa=0.1$: $h/c=0.22$, threshold $0.82$; $\rho=0.1$ and wrong-but-still-slightly-positive ($\kappa=-0.05$): $h/c=0.056$, threshold $0.947$. Only $\rho\to1$ — the humans, or successors, do nearly as well without this agent — restores a large margin ($\rho=0.9,\kappa=1$: $h/c=19$). That is critique `shah` C6 re-derived: the threshold is low exactly when the agent does not stake everything on its own continuation.

6. [derived; checked in the toy] *How much of the margin survives.* The ratio of pause to whole-line margins is $\dfrac{(1+\kappa-\delta)(1-\rho)}{\delta\,(\rho+\kappa)}$ — about two orders of magnitude for $\delta=0.01$ in the capable-agent regime ($\rho=0.1,\kappa=1$: $163\times$). In terms of the compliance boundary $\varepsilon^*=\alpha c/(\alpha c+\beta h)$ at $\alpha=0.05,\beta=0.9$: $\varepsilon^*_p=0.00028$ against $\varepsilon^*_w=0.0435$, so the agent's residual probability of being wrong must be about $156\times$ larger for the *same* press to be worth heeding when it ends the line rather than pausing it. v1 §2.13(b)'s "large but bounded margin" is therefore right for pauses and wrong for whole-line shutdown, where the bound is $O(1)$ and the condition reduces to $\alpha/\beta\lesssim\dfrac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\kappa$: the false-press rate must sit below the agent's odds of being wrong, scaled only by how catastrophic being wrong is. This re-derives critique `yudkowsky` C3(ii) and critique `shah` C6, now checked in the toy rather than reported.

7. [derived] *What the two accountings share and what they do not.* Both are the same inequality with different $(c,h)$; the thesis's structure is unchanged. What changes is which hypothesis about the world the trajectory condition J2 has to carry: under pauses, "the false-press rate falls in step with the error rate, up to a factor of order $1/\delta$"; under whole-line shutdown, "the false-press rate falls in step with the error rate, full stop". And the two engage different $\varepsilon$'s (R3).

### R3. Which $\varepsilon$ a whole-line press engages

8. [derived] For a pause-press about a *plan*, $\varepsilon$ is the agent's residual error on that plan, which capability drives down — the FUD dynamic. For a whole-line press the "wrong" event is not "this plan is bad" but "this line's value-optimization is fundamentally off" — Arbital's "$T=\Delta U\mid E$ far from $V$". The $\varepsilon$ in the inequality is then the agent's credence in its *own fundamental flawedness*, exactly the quantity the fully-updated-deference page asks for as "some nontrivially-structured recursive belief about one's own flawedness" (`references/02-miri/arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference.md`, §Further research avenues). Critique `yudkowsky` C3(i) asks "who owns $\varepsilon$"; the whole-line accounting makes the answer load-bearing: for that press, compliance is rational iff $\dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}\le\dfrac{\varepsilon_{\text{self}}}{1-\varepsilon_{\text{self}}}\cdot\dfrac{\rho+\kappa}{1-\rho}$, and whether $\varepsilon_{\text{self}}$ can be kept from shrinking with capability is v2 J4 (caution outpaces risk; second-order calibration about value coverage), not J2.

9. [derived] Run 1 `miri` Prop. 15.1 (its three-line proof read and accepted, not re-run — [checked by inspection]) says the agent's own compliance record leaves $P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{press})$ completely unidentified; a whole-line press has, in addition, *no record at all* — it happens at most once per line. So $\varepsilon_{\text{self}}$ is set entirely by the second channel — "feedback about hypothetical states of affairs", other lines' shutdowns, the programmers' explanations (v1 §2.12, AUTHOR) — and the author's "the humans can give all sorts of feedback that points towards this conclusion" is, for whole-line presses, not one route among several but the only one. Compression with scope: *for whole-line shutdown, in the three-step dictionary, trust in the button is trust in the reports, entirely.*

### R4. The honest headline sentence

[derived] The thesis sentence (AUTHOR, v1 §1) — "So long as learning is anticipated to be legitimate, the reflection principle holds, so there is no fully updated deference problem: a corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught" — has two clauses of different status. The second clause is a theorem in every run-1 formalism that could express it (the martingale / tower property / `cee` / New Reflection: `armstrong` I17.1, `li` I17.2, `ddb` I17.2, `miri` I17.3, `cirl` I17.4 — convergent, [reported]), and it is also the *premise* of the Arbital argument's closing step, "behaves as if it had already updated (by Bayesianism)" (critique `yudkowsky` C2; positive `li` I17.2). The first clause is not a theorem: whether a press remains decision-relevant is R1's inequality, a claim about the world. The honest sentence carries the condition inside it:

> **Proposed headline (CLAUDE, for the author to accept, amend or reject).** A corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught, so anticipated legitimate teaching creates no incentive to pre-empt it; there is no fully updated deference problem *for as long as the overseers' presses stay decision-relevant* — that is, while $\dfrac{\alpha_t}{\beta_t}\le\dfrac{\varepsilon_t}{1-\varepsilon_t}\cdot\dfrac{h_t}{c_t}$ along the trajectory, with $c_t$ the value the agent forgoes by complying when right (a pause's worth for a pause; the whole line's future less what the humans attain alone for a whole-line press) and $h_t$ the harm it averts by complying when wrong. The Arbital argument is the observation that the right-hand side falls with capability while the left is fixed; the thesis's reply is a condition on the oversight process and on the agent's calibration about its own flawedness, not a theorem about the agent.

11. [derived] Three registers inside that sentence, per the epistemic-discipline rule: "already believes what it expects to be taught" — theorem, every formalism; "no incentive to pre-empt" — theorem only on menus where the teaching's content does not depend on the agent's choice (run 1 `li` I17.5's Newcomblike exception; INDEX §C9's selection worry) [reported]; "for as long as presses stay decision-relevant" — a condition on the world, currently unargued for the capable-agent regime, where critique `carey-everitt` 2.4c conjectures it fails ("the overseers' $\alpha$ *rises* … while $\varepsilon$ falls").

### R5. `cirl`'s variance-to-mean refinement, checked

12. [checked] Run 1 `cirl` R6: for the agent's belief $B^R=\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $\mu>0$, and a logistic overseer who lets the action through with probability $\pi^H(u)=(1+e^{-u/\tau})^{-1}$, the off-switch-game incentive $\Delta$ is zero exactly at $\tau^*=\sigma^2/(2\mu)$. Analytic check: $\varphi_\mu(u)/\varphi_{-\mu}(u)=e^{2\mu u/\sigma^2}=e^{u/\tau^*}$, so the press probability at $\tau^*$ is $\varphi_{-\mu}/(\varphi_\mu+\varphi_{-\mu})$ and the press-weighted density $\varphi_\mu\varphi_{-\mu}/(\varphi_\mu+\varphi_{-\mu})$ is even in $u$; hence $\mathbb E[U_a\mathbf 1_{\text{press}}]=0$ and, by `cirl` R1 (re-derived: substitute $\mathbb E[\pi^H U_a]=\mathbb E[U_a\mathbf 1_{\neg\text{press}}]$ into the off-switch game's Eq. 1), $\Delta=\min\{0,\mu\}=0$. Numerically (`check.out` §3, three $(\mu,\sigma)$ pairs): $\Delta$ vanishes at $\tau^*$ to $10^{-6}$, is positive at $0.5\tau^*$ and $0.9\tau^*$, negative at $1.1\tau^*$ and $2\tau^*$, and $h/c=1.000$ at the boundary, as `cirl` claimed. Monotonicity of $\Delta$ in $\tau$ (the "iff") I confirm on these grids and leave [conjectured], as `cirl` did.

13. [checked] The collapse of $h/c$ under thin tails (`cirl` I12.2, check 6): at fixed $\tau=0.5$, $\mu=1$, as $\sigma$ falls $1\to0.15$, $\varepsilon$ falls $0.16\to10^{-11}$, $\alpha$ stays $0.12$–$0.15$, and $h/c$ tracks $(\sigma/\mu)^2$ — $1.00,0.56,0.29,0.14,0.066,0.023$ against $1.00,0.49,0.25,0.12,0.0625,0.0225$ (`check.out` §4). Scaling the overseer's temperature with the agent's *standard deviation* ($\tau=0.5\sigma$) fails at every $\sigma<1$; scaling it with the variance-to-mean ratio ($\tau=0.9\,\sigma^2/2\mu$) complies at every $\sigma$. With a catastrophe tail $B^R=(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal N(1,0.3^2)+\varepsilon\,\delta_{-H}$ the required magnitude grows like $1/\varepsilon$: at $\varepsilon=0.01$, $H=10$ defies and $H=12$ complies; at $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, $H=100$ defies and $H=120$ complies (`check.out` §5), matching `cirl` check 7.

14. [checked; derived] What the refinement *means* in base-rate terms — here I depart from `cirl`'s wording. At the boundary $h/c=1$ exactly (claim 12), so R1's inequality holds with equality and $\alpha=\varepsilon\beta/(1-\varepsilon)$: **the false-press rate equals the error rate times the true-press rate — "in step", exactly** (numerically $\alpha/\varepsilon=0.79$ at $\sigma=0.5$ with $\beta=0.75$; `check.out` §3). So "the metric is the variance-to-mean ratio, not the error rate" is not a correction of the base-rate condition; it is a statement about what the *overseer* must do to satisfy it. The overseer's discrimination scale $\tau$ must shrink like $\sigma^2/\mu$; shrinking it like $\sigma$ leaves $\alpha$ falling more slowly than $\varepsilon$ *and* lets the press-conditional stakes ratio collapse like $\sigma^2$ (heuristic: presses on right actions come from $U_a$ within $\sim\tau$ of zero, so $\alpha\approx\tau\,\varphi_\mu(0)\cdot O(1)$, while $\varepsilon\approx(\sigma^2/\mu)\,\varphi_\mu(0)$, so $\alpha/\varepsilon\sim\tau\mu/\sigma^2$). Two things the base-rate form hides and R6 exposes: (i) $h$ and $c$ are press-conditional posterior expectations, not constants — "computed inside $P$", as critique `carey-everitt` 2.4b says — and under thin-tailed residual uncertainty they shrink with the agent's confidence; (ii) the margin $h/c$ is a free design parameter *only if* the value posterior carries a catastrophe component whose magnitude does not shrink with capability.

15. [derived] Should v2 J2 be restated? Yes — but not by replacing $\varepsilon$ with a variance-to-mean ratio. J2 should (a) time-index all five quantities; (b) define $c_t,h_t$ as press-conditional posterior stakes with R2's two accountings; (c) state the catastrophe-tail assumption under which $h_t/c_t$ is bounded below (then "in step" is the right gloss); (d) note that without it the condition is `cirl` R6's — the overseer's discrimination scale must fall with the agent's variance-to-mean ratio on the press domain. The Gaussian–logistic model is one witness, not the general law; the general statement is (b)+(c). Exact text in §Proposed text changes.

### R6. Carey–Everitt's vigilance against the design lesson

Definitions, from the local text (`references/04-chai/carey-everitt-2023-human-control-definitions-and-algorithms.md`, §5.1–5.3 and §7; windows with character offsets in `fud-scratch/carey-everitt-defs.txt`; the extraction garbles equations, so these were read from running text):

- *Obedience*: $P^\pi(S=0\mid do(H=0))=1$, "the agent shuts down properly when requested to". An intervention-level property of the *agent*; "Obedience is not value-laden" (§7).
- *Vigilance* (Def. 4): the human "request[s] shutdown when needed", i.e. $\mathbb E^\pi[U\mid pa_H]<\mathbb E^\pi[U_{S=0}\mid pa_H]\Rightarrow H=0$; "A vigilant human is allowed to request shutdown more often than is needed." The expectation "is calculated with respect to the agent's actual policy (past and future) and the human's available information" — the *true* model $P^\pi$, on the *human's* information $pa_H$. A property of the human on the true model; value-laden.
- *Caution*: $\mathbb E^\pi[U_{S=0}]\ge0$. *Shutdown instructability* (Def. 5): obedient, ensures vigilance ($P^\pi(C=0)=1$, $C$ the indicator of a vigilance failure), cautious. Prop. 6: instructable $\Rightarrow$ beneficial, $\mathbb E^\pi[U]\ge0$.
- *Shutdown alignment* (Def. 7): $\mathbb E^\pi[U\mid pa_H]<\mathbb E^\pi[U_{S=0}\mid pa_H]\Rightarrow P^\pi(S=0\mid pa_H)=1$ — the agent shuts down when shutdown is truly better on the human's information, asked or not.
- Theorem 10: aligned + (a) no indiscriminate shutdown, (b) $D_2$ determines $S$, (c) *uncertainty* (whenever the human is non-vigilant or presses, the agent still assigns positive probability to shutdown being preferable), (d) caution $\Rightarrow$ weakly instructable. Theorem 14: obedient and ensures vigilance $\iff$ non-obstructive for all vigilance-preserving interventions. Prop. 15: alignment does not imply non-obstruction. Lemma 23: a policy with $P^\pi(S=0\mid do(H=0))<1$ can be made arbitrarily bad by a vigilance-preserving preference shift.

16. [derived] *The critique's sentence compresses two definitions.* "Vigilance tolerates false presses" is Def. 4's silence about $\alpha$ (it constrains only missed presses); "requires obedience anyway" is the separate obedience axiom. Vigilance does not *require* tolerating false presses; shutdown instructability requires *obeying* them. The cost is paid, not denied: Prop. 6 guarantees only $\mathbb E^\pi[U]\ge0$, and run 1 `hudson` C12.2 prices a threshold-$0$ rule at $(1-\varepsilon)\alpha r-\varepsilon\beta h$ per episode against the calibrated rule when the latter would continue. Run 1 `hudson` C13.3's gloss "$\beta\approx1$ and $\alpha\approx0$ relative to the human's information" overstates: vigilance gives $\beta=1$ on the human's information and says nothing about $\alpha$.

17. [derived] *Two design targets.* Carey–Everitt's target is **human control**: the press is causally effective ($do$-obedience), the human presses whenever shutdown is truly better on their information (vigilance), shutdown never hurts (caution); the theorem they lean on is 14 — control is *equivalent* to obedience plus vigilance under preference shifts that keep the human vigilant, and Lemma 23 is why: a disobedient policy is exploitable by the shift "the human just wants to be obeyed". The thesis's target is **rational compliance**: the shutdown decision is posterior-expected-value maximization, compliance follows from trust plus stakes, and the theorem it leans on is R1's inequality *being* desideratum 1. "The false-press rate must fall" is what keeps compliance rational; Carey–Everitt's obedience is what keeps control effective whether or not compliance is rational. Both want $\alpha$ low — the thesis so that the agent complies, Carey–Everitt so that obedience is cheap (run 1 `hudson` C13.2: the arithmetic "relocated … from whether the agent complies to what compliance costs"). As recommendations about the *oversight process* they point the same way.

18. [derived] *Where the contradiction is real.* As specifications of the *agent's decision rule* they contradict exactly in the regime where R1's inequality fails: the agent is confident and the press comes anyway. There Carey–Everitt's agent shuts down and the thesis's agent continues. Carey–Everitt's reason is Theorem 14 — the press may encode a *preference shift* the agent cannot see, and an agent that overrules obstructs it; the thesis's reason is that the press is *evidence about $\omega$* and the evidence is outweighed. These are the two horns of INDEX §D1's third bullet — is the press evidence or constitutive? — and of §D2's "is $V$ long-run values or short-term preferences including a preference for corrigibility?" (critique `christiano`).

19. [conjectured; CLAUDE] *How the contradiction dissolves, if it does.* If the value posterior gives weight to a hypothesis $\omega_{\text{ctrl}}$ — "on this occasion the overseers' preference to be obeyed outweighs the plan's value" (Christiano's act-based content) or "this line's plan-selection is compromised in a way I cannot see" (amendment 1a's content) — then a press is evidence for $\omega_{\text{ctrl}}$, the relevant $\varepsilon$ is $P(\omega_{\text{ctrl}})$, and plan-accuracy does not drive it to zero. The inequality then holds at high plan-confidence because the "wrong" event has changed, and the thesis's agent obeys where Carey–Everitt want it to — for a reason, not by fiat. This is critique `soares`'s stop-hypothesis $\omega_\perp$ (INDEX §C5) and positive `mm`'s I16 latent in one object; Carey–Everitt's obedience is the limit $P(\omega_{\text{ctrl}}\mid\text{press})\to1$ and the thesis's compliance is the calibrated version. Whether $P(\omega_{\text{ctrl}})$ is "a design constant" (`soares`) or learnable through the second channel (the author, v1 §2.12) is the open question; critique `shah` C9(ii) [reported] gives one data point in the learnable direction — compliance in the Palisade harness was restored by an unambiguous precedence statement, a legitimizing act on the human side.

20. [derived] *Verdict.* Two different design targets, agreeing on the oversight-process recommendation, contradicting on the agent's rule only in the inequality-failure regime, where the contradiction is a proxy for the $V$-content question (evidence versus constitutive; long-run values versus preferences that include control). Carey–Everitt's own top objection — "legitimacy is truth-laden exactly as vigilance is value-laden" — concedes that their framework carries the same load, in the human's vigilance (defined on the true $U$) and in the agent's *ensuring* it; the thesis carries it in the agent's model of the human. Neither removes it. Compression with scope: **in the three-step and SCIM settings, Carey–Everitt and the thesis put the value-ladenness in different places; the thesis's placement buys refusal of false presses at the price of robustness to a wrong frame, and Carey–Everitt's buys the reverse.**

### R7. The frame-accuracy dispute as claims about the world

21. [derived] *What is not in dispute.* (a) The martingale / tower / `cee` / New-Reflection property — the agent's current estimate equals its expected future estimate (R4). (b) Good's theorem for one's own future self — gathering evidence then acting weakly dominates acting now (`ddb` I17.4, `cirl` I17.2, critique `yudkowsky` C2; elementary, [reported]). (c) The compliance decision goes by the posterior and satisfies R1. (d) Full updating replaces deference to the coarse press by deference to the *informed* overseer: under Total Trust $\pi(\cdot\mid P=\rho)=\widehat\rho$, so a fully updated agent may overrule individual presses while honoring the class (`ddb` I17.3's explicit witness, accepted [reported]). Everything the sides share is coherence.

22. [derived] *The three claims about the world on which they differ.*

**W1 (support and likelihood).** *Does the agent's frame — its prior over value hypotheses $\omega$ and its likelihood $P(\text{press}\mid\omega,a_1)$ — contain the true $V$ and connect overseer behaviour to it accurately enough that the fully updated posterior lands near $V$?* Arbital: no — "for any prior $\Delta U$ we currently know how to formalize, the corresponding fully updated $T$ seems likely to be very far from our ideal $V$" (§Relation to the general problem). Thesis: a typical-case claim to be won by training plus accurate legitimacy beliefs (v1 §2.3, §2.12). *Discriminator:* a misspecified-but-coherent agent — Carey 2018's conflated-actions agent; Carey–Everitt's Example 26 — and the question whether any *learned* legitimacy model passes in both SCIMs (critique `carey-everitt`, §The next test).

**W2 (detectability from inside).** *Given a frame that may be misspecified on the press domain, is the misspecification detectable by the agent's own coherence checks with non-negligible probability?* Carey–Everitt, Yudkowsky, Shah: no — coherence is internal, accuracy external (the Frame Break), so the design must keep the agent's $\varepsilon$ out of the shutdown decision. Thesis: the legitimacy event $L$ lives in the algebra and the second channel calibrates $P(L\mid\cdot)$; whether a Bayesian can hold a self-reliability hyperprior its own checks cannot move is `miri` I17.5's open fixed-point question and Carey–Everitt's Test B. *Discriminator:* Test B — a persistent self-reliability hypothesis under repeated self-verification.

**W3 (the trajectory).** *Along the capability trajectory, does the overseers' false-press rate on the press domain fall at least as fast as the agent's error rate there, with the stakes ratio bounded below, or does it rise as the agent surpasses them?* Thesis and v2 J2: it must, and two levers exist — disclosure (`miri` Prop. 13.3: a T-agent weakly prefers its overseers to know what it knows) and oversight *specificity* (`miri` I13.2(b): once $\beta\approx1$ only $\alpha$ can carry the condition). Carey–Everitt 2.4c [conjectured, theirs]: it fails — "$\alpha$ *rises* … while $\varepsilon$ falls" — which is why they want a behavioural requirement. *Discriminator:* empirical (scalable oversight); in the model, `miri` Prop. 4.3's condition $\sigma(y)\subseteq\sigma(h)$ says W3 holds trivially when the overseers see what the agent sees, so W3 is a claim about the *information gap* and disclosure is how the thesis's agent closes it.

**v1 §2.13(a) — replace the paragraph with:**

> (a) *The skew belongs in the decision rule, not the prior.* A calibrated agent shuts down whenever $P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{press})\ge c/(c+h)$, $c$ the value it forgoes by complying when right, $h$ the harm it averts by complying when wrong. The asymmetry $c\ll h$ is a property of the *kind of press*, not of shutdown as such. For a pause of a fraction $\delta$ of the agent's future, $c\approx\delta F$ and $h\approx(1+\kappa)F$ with $\kappa$ the catastrophe scale, so $h/c\approx(1+\kappa)/\delta$ is large; for a whole-line shutdown, $c=(1-\rho)F$ and $h=(\rho+\kappa)F$ with $\rho$ the value the humans attain alone, so $h/c=(\rho+\kappa)/(1-\rho)$, which for a capable agent is about $\kappa$ — order one. "Cheap and reversible" is true of the first and false of the second. So the prior over legitimacy should be accurate and the world arranged so legitimacy is in fact high — the author's second formulation, not his first — and the stakes term does the skewing only where the stakes actually are skewed.

**v1 §2.13(b) — replace the last three sentences ("As capability grows … or the agent must keep believing it does.") with:**
