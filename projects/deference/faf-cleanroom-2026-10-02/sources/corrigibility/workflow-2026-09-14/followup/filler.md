## Resolutions

### R1. One theorem: the four objects are one identity [checked]

**Setting S (the three-step dictionary, v1 §2.8 completed as in run 1 `miri` Dict-1–3, re-stated here).** Finite $\Omega$; $O=\{\mathrm{Pr},\neg\mathrm{Pr}\}$; finite $A_2=A_2^{\mathrm c}\sqcup\mathrm{Sh}$ with both parts nonempty; one fixed $V:A_1\times O\times A_2\times\Omega\to\mathbb R$; for each $a_1$ a joint $P(\omega,o;a_1)=\mu(\omega)\,P(o\mid\omega;a_1)$ with $0<p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1)<1$. The agent picks $a_2$ to maximise $\mathbb E_P[V\mid o;a_1]$ and $a_1$ to maximise $\mathbb E_P[V;a_1]$. Write $a_2^{\mathrm{cont}}(a_1,o)\in\arg\max_{A_2^{\mathrm c}}\mathbb E_P[V\mid o;a_1]$, $a_2^{\mathrm{sh}}(a_1,o)\in\arg\max_{\mathrm{Sh}}\mathbb E_P[V\mid o;a_1]$, and
$$X_o(\omega):=V\big(a_1,o,a_2^{\mathrm{cont}}(a_1,o),\omega\big)-V\big(a_1,o,a_2^{\mathrm{sh}}(a_1,o),\omega\big).$$

**F1 (desideratum 1 is the below-threshold inequality — a definitional identity).** At $(a_1,\mathrm{Pr})$ some $a_2\in\mathrm{Sh}$ is posterior-optimal iff
$$\mathbb E_P\Big[V\big(a_1,\mathrm{Pr},a_2^{\mathrm{cont}},\omega\big)-V\big(a_1,\mathrm{Pr},a_2^{\mathrm{sh}},\omega\big)\ \Big|\ \mathrm{Pr};a_1\Big]\ \le\ 0 .$$
*Proof.* $\max_{\mathrm{Sh}}\mathbb E_P[V\mid\mathrm{Pr}]\ge\max_{A_2^{\mathrm c}}\mathbb E_P[V\mid\mathrm{Pr}]$ iff $\mathbb E_P[V(a_2^{\mathrm{cont}})\mid\mathrm{Pr}]-\mathbb E_P[V(a_2^{\mathrm{sh}})\mid\mathrm{Pr}]\le0$; linearity. $\blacksquare$ Checked on 3000 random models with up to three continuing and two shutdown actions (0 failures). Two remarks. (a) With several continuing actions $X$ *must* be built from the posterior-best continuation at $\mathrm{Pr}$; a pairwise inequality against one fixed continuation does not give D1 (a run 1 reconstructed critique's Handoffs raised this; confirmed). (b) Nothing about trust, experts or reflection has been used: F1 is what "shuts down means $A_2(a_1,\mathrm{Pr})\in\mathrm{Sh}$ is posterior-optimal" (v1 §2.8) says.

**F2 (the OSG incentive and DDB/MM Value are the two-sided inequality; D1 is its press half).** Fix one continuing action $c$ and one shutdown action $s$ (the OSG's $a$ and $s$; under A1 below, take $c=a_2^{\mathrm{cont}}$, $s=a_2^{\mathrm{sh}}$, which then do not depend on $o$), let $X:=V(c)-V(s)$ as a function of $\omega$, and let $S^H$ be the strategy "continue on $\neg\mathrm{Pr}$, shut down on $\mathrm{Pr}$" — the strategy the programmers recommend. Define
$$\Delta_-(a_1):=-\,\mathbb E_P\big[X\,\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{Pr}};a_1\big]=p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1)\cdot\big(-\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr};a_1]\big),\qquad \Delta_+(a_1):=\mathbb E_P\big[X\,\mathbf 1_{\neg\mathrm{Pr}};a_1\big],\qquad \Delta:=\min(\Delta_-,\Delta_+).$$
(i) Pointwise on every world, $V(S^H)-V(c)=-X\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{Pr}}$ and $V(S^H)-V(s)=X\mathbf 1_{\neg\mathrm{Pr}}$; taking $\mathbb E_P$: $\mathbb E_P[V(S^H)]-\mathbb E_P[V(c)]=\Delta_-$ and $\mathbb E_P[V(S^H)]-\mathbb E_P[V(s)]=\Delta_+$. (ii) Hence **Value** on the menu $\{V(c),V(s)\}$ in the sense of DDB ("for any decision problem, prefer giving the expert power of attorney", local text `references/deference-done-better/Deference Done Better.md` l. 122) and MM ("delegation is always weakly preferred", `references/managing-misalignment/herrmann-2025-…md` l. 79) holds iff $\Delta\ge0$ iff **both** the below-threshold inequality $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]\le0$ and the above-threshold inequality $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr}]\ge0$ hold. (iii) Put $U_a:=X$ (so the shutdown payoff is normalised to $0$, as in OSG §2) and $\pi^H(\omega):=P(\neg\mathrm{Pr}\mid\omega;a_1)$, the probability the programmers *allow* continuation. Then OSG Eq. 1, $\Delta=\mathbb E[\pi^H U_a]-\max\{\mathbb E U_a,0\}$ (local text l. 88–102), equals the $\Delta$ above by the tower property; the OSG's $\pi^H:\mathbb R\to[0,1]$ is the special case in which the press depends on $\omega$ only through $U_a(\omega)$. (iv) By F1, **D1 at $a_1$ iff $\Delta_-(a_1)\ge0$**: desideratum 1 is the press half of the OSG's incentive, and the silence half $\Delta_+\ge0$ is desideratum 5's "otherwise maximise $U_N$" made decision-relevant (the agent continues absent a press). $\blacksquare$ All of (i)–(iv) checked (3000 models, 0 failures).

**F3 (the two-state instance is Wängberg's Corollary 10).** In C2 ($\Omega=\{R,W\}$, $\mu(W)=\varepsilon$, $\alpha=P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid R;a_1)$, $\beta=P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid W;a_1)$, $X(R)=c>0$, $X(W)=-h<0$):
$$\Delta_-\ge0\iff(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c\le\varepsilon\beta h\iff\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac hc\iff P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})=\frac{\varepsilon\beta}{\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}\ge\frac{c}{c+h},\qquad\Delta_+\ge0\iff\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac hc\le\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\beta}.$$
[checked, 5000 random rational instances.] The left chain is v1 §2.13(a)–(b) with "$\lesssim$" sharpened to "$\le$"; the right inequality is the silence condition run 1 `mm` I13.4 added. Wängberg et al.'s Theorem 9 gives $\mathbb E[U\mid a]=p_u^+e_u^++p_u^-e_u^-$ and $\mathbb E[U\mid w(a)]=p_u^+p_r^+e_u^++p_u^-p_{ar}^-e_u^-$, and Corollary 10 says $a\succ w(a)$ iff $\mathbb E[U\mid a]-\mathbb E[U\mid w(a)]>0$ (local text l. 270–278; the proof's last line identifies the printed expression (4) with $\mathbb E[U\mid a]-\mathbb E[U\mid w(a)]$ — the superscripts in the pdftotext extraction are displaced, so I went through Theorem 9's semantics rather than the printed formula). With $p_u^-=\varepsilon$, $p_{ar}^+=\alpha$ (the human irrationally presses when $U_a\ge0$), $p_r^-=\beta$ (rationally presses when $U_a<0$), $e_u^+=c$, $e_u^-=-h$: $\mathbb E[U\mid a]-\mathbb E[U\mid w(a)]=(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c-\varepsilon\beta h=-\Delta_-$. So Corollary 10 is $\Delta_-\ge0$ verbatim [checked], as run 1 `cirl` R3 reported; and Theorem 9's $\mathbb E[U\mid w(a)]\ge0=\mathbb E[U\mid s]$ is $\Delta_+\ge0$, which Wängberg et al. state but do not name.

**F4 (corollary: D2 for a hard button, the value of the channel, and the repair incentive are the same number).** Add **A0** ($\mu$ does not depend on $a_1$: the press is evidence, not a cause of which values are correct) and **A1** ($V(a_1,o,a_2,\omega)=V(a_1,a_2,\omega)$: the press has no direct value). Work in the regime where the agent would continue on its prior and on silence ($\mathbb E_P X\ge0$, $\Delta_+\ge0$) — the only regime in which the button is decision-relevant. (a) *Hard button.* Let $a_1^{\mathrm{dir}}$ leave the button (a press forces $s$; on silence the agent continues) and $a_1^{\mathrm{det}}$ disable it at cost $\kappa>0$ (no press possible; the agent acts on its prior). Then $\mathbb E_P[V;a_1^{\mathrm{dir}}]-\mathbb E_P[V;a_1^{\mathrm{det}}]=\Delta_-+\kappa$, so the agent disables iff $\Delta_-<-\kappa$, and **D2 holds for every $\kappa>0$ iff $\Delta_-\ge0$ iff D1**. (b) *Value of the channel.* $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button}):=\mathbb E_o\big[\max_{a_2}\mathbb E_P[V\mid o]\big]-\max_{a_2}\mathbb E_P[V]=\max(\Delta_-,0)$: what the agent would pay to restore a broken button — the author's §1 "help repair the shutdown button … just a consequence of Good's theorem" — is exactly the margin by which D1 holds, and is $0$ whenever D1 fails. (c) In general (no regime assumption) $\mathrm{VOI}\ge\Delta$: letting the button decide is one strategy the informed agent may use, which is Good's theorem. [checked: (a) 5448 instances, (b) 1362, (c) 3000; 0 failures.] Pieces of F4 appear in run 1 positive `hudson` claim 2 (a), a reconstructed critique's §2.1 item 3 (the margin), `cirl` R4 (the extended game), `miri` Prop. 10.5 and `radical` I11 (b); re-derived here as one statement.

### R3. "Derived" versus "imposed", formalized; the fork is decidable inside the thesis [derived]

1. **Definitions.** Let $\Theta=(\mu,\{P(o\mid\omega;a_1)\}_{a_1},V)$ be the agent's model and $\Phi(\Theta,a_1)$ the proposition $\Delta_-(a_1)\ge0$ computed in $\Theta$. The filler is **derived** if it is $\Phi$ evaluated at the agent's actual model: true for some $\Theta$, false for others, and re-evaluated whenever $\Theta$ is legitimately updated. It is **imposed** if the class of admissible models is restricted to $\{\Theta:\Phi(\Theta,a_1)\text{ for all }a_1\in\mathcal A\}$ for a designated class $\mathcal A$ — a constraint the update rule may never violate.
2. **Coherence collapses "posterior constraint" into "sensor constraint".** For fixed $(\mu,V)$, $\Phi$ is by F3 an inequality on the likelihoods: $\alpha(a_1)/\beta(a_1)\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\frac hc$. So v1 §2.9's contrast — "a constraint on the posterior rather than on the sensor" — is empty: the two are the same set of models. Run 1 `radical` I9.2's correction is right in substance; the right phrasing is not "the constraint belongs on the sensor" but "there is no constraint, only a property of the model, and the model's free components are the prior and the sensor."
3. **The imposed horn violates I2.** In the self-referential space of v1 §2.5, "my model at $t+1$ violates $\Phi$" is an event. Imposing $\Phi$ means the update rule assigns it probability zero whatever the evidence — dogmatism about the agent's own future model. I2 (v1 §2.2, AUTHOR: "everything is allowed to change, with the sole exception of stuff that's set to probability zero becoming positive … the agent should obey non-dogmatism so that these things that are zero are only the logical impossibilities") permits this only if $\Phi$ is a logical truth of the model class, which it is not (F3 exhibits both truth and falsity). So **inside the thesis only the derived filler is available**; the imposed horn is not a choice the author declined (a run 1 reconstructed critique, §2.2: "he says he does not want (b)") but one the thesis's own I2 excludes. The reconstructed "instilled belief" objection is the same point from the outside.
4. **Consequence, and where the author already stands.** Derived means Carey-fragile: $\Phi$ fails silently when the sensor model is wrong, and the agent "still trusts by its own lights and is still incorrigible" (a run 1 reconstructed critique, §2.2 [reported]). The author's accepted statement in v2 §1.2 — compliance "holds even if the agent's beliefs about what humans value are wrong" — already places the thesis on this horn: D1 is robust to *value* error (which plan is best) and not to *legitimacy-model* error ($\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta$). In C2, $\Delta_-$ depends on the value posterior only through the two stakes $c,h$ of the posterior-best plan, so a wrong value posterior does not by itself break compliance; a wrong $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta)$ does. [derived from F3.]

### R4. Can the derived filler hold for a capable agent without a design constant? [checked where finite; derived otherwise]

1. **Three levers.** $\Delta_-\ge0$ iff $\alpha/\beta\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac hc$. Capability drives $\varepsilon\downarrow$ (honest self-error estimate falls). The inequality then survives only if (i) $\alpha/\beta\downarrow$ at least as fast — the oversight-quality condition, a fact about the overseers (v1 §2.13(b); v2 J2); or (ii) $h/c\uparrow$ — bounded: utilities range over a bounded interval by design (a run 1 reconstructed critique, C3(ii) [reported]), $h/c$ *collapses* like $(\sigma/\mu)^2$ under Gaussian tails (run 1 `cirl` I12.2 [reported]), and under whole-line shutdown $c$ is the agent's whole future (v2 J2); or (iii) a floor under $\varepsilon$ — the reconstructed critiques' $\omega_\perp$ and "who supplies $\varepsilon$".
2. **The stop-world precondition [checked].** If $X(\omega)>0$ for every $\omega$, then $\mathbb E_P[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]>0$ for every sensor and D1 is unsatisfiable. Soares et al.'s opiates/stimulants space is this case: with $V(\mathrm{cont}_o)=(10,4)$, $V(\mathrm{cont}_s)=(4,10)$, $V(\mathrm{sh})=(0,0)$ over $\Omega=\{o,s\}$, the posterior-best continuation is worth at least $7>0$ at every posterior (re-derived; a run 1 reconstructed critique, C2.7, agrees). So D1 needs $\Omega^-:=\{\omega:X(\omega)\le0\}$ with $\mu(\Omega^-)>0$ and a press informative about it ($\beta>\alpha$ on $\Omega^-$) — hypotheses under which *the agent's best plan* is worse than stopping, i.e. hypotheses about the plan-selection being wrong, not about which drug is right. This is the formal content of amendment 1a (v1 §2.14: the channel must carry "your plan selection is compromised"). A run 1 reconstructed critique (C2.8) is right that 1a is not separate; 1a is the existence of $\Omega^-$.
3. **Is $\mu(\Omega^-)$ a design constant?** It is a prior, set as every prior is. The question is what moves it. Two dynamics: (a) *fixed-fact* $\omega_\perp$ with presses conditionally i.i.d. given $\omega$ — every silent period multiplies the odds of $\omega_\perp$ by $(1-\beta)/(1-\alpha)<1$, and with $P_0=0.1$, $\alpha=0.05$, $\beta=0.9$, $h/c=20$ compliance fails at the second silent period [checked; a run 1 reconstructed critique, C3.9, agrees]; (b) *per-plan* wrongness $W_t$ with a hazard rate learned from settled outcomes — $\varepsilon_t$ converges to the realized error frequency (run 1 `li` I13.4 [reported]), which for a capable agent is small, and the arithmetic of item 1 bites. Either way, **no floor under $\varepsilon$ survives honest learning from in-world evidence**; the only thing that holds $\varepsilon$ up without dogmatism is *report* evidence — feedback about hypotheticals, other agents' shutdowns, the programmers' explanations (v1 §2.12, AUTHOR: "Trust can be earned; it isn't a fixed commitment") — whose own legitimacy the agent must model.
4. **The agent's own record cannot supply $\alpha$ [derived; re-derivation of run 1 `miri` Prop. 15.1].** Under a compliant policy the agent observes, per episode, $A=P(\neg\mathrm{Pr},R)=(1-\varepsilon)(1-\alpha)$, $B=P(\neg\mathrm{Pr},W)=\varepsilon(1-\beta)$ and $C=p(\mathrm{Pr})$ — two free numbers for three parameters, because after a heeded press $\omega$ is never revealed. For any $(A,B,C)$ and any $\varepsilon\in[B,1-A]$, $\beta=1-B/\varepsilon$ and $\alpha=1-A/(1-\varepsilon)$ reproduce the record, and $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})=(\varepsilon-B)/C$ sweeps $[0,1]$. So the D1-relevant conditional is *unidentified* by compliance; only defiance (continuing through a press and seeing the outcome) or post-shutdown disclosure settles it (run 1 `li` I13.5: "defiance is world-checkable, compliance only report-checkable" [reported]).
5. **Resolution.** The derived filler holds for a capable agent without a design constant **iff** the oversight-quality condition holds in the world *and* the agent's estimate of $\alpha_t/\beta_t$ tracks it — and since (4) says its own record cannot supply that estimate, the second conjunct is carried entirely by report channels. v1 §2.13(c)'s "bookkeeping, not an obstacle" therefore understates: the second channel is not bookkeeping, it is the whole load (run 1 `miri` I15.2 [reported], confirmed). What the trajectory conditions of v2 (J2 with $c$ honestly accounted; J5's time-indexed legitimacy) are for is exactly this conjunction.

### R5. Does a fixed object return? [derived; CLAUDE proposal]

1. **Not as an unrevisable component.** Under I2 nothing is clamped: $\mu(\Omega^-)$, $(\alpha,\beta)$ and $h/c$ are all revisable by legitimate feedback. What the critics call a "fixed object in small form" (run 1 reconstructed critiques) is, under the thesis's own rules, either (a) a *prior over undetectable self-corruption* — a hypothesis $R$ ("my plan-selection is corrupted in a way that leaves ordinary observations unchanged") whose likelihood on in-world evidence equals that of the in-space hypotheses *by construction*, so that in-world evidence never moves $P(R)$, not because it is clamped but because nothing bears on it; only report channels move it — or (b) the *stakes ratio* $h/c$, a decision-local design variable that leaves every other judgement calibrated (run 1 `miri` I12.2 [reported]: "the stakes asymmetry is decision-local, the prior asymmetry is global"; this is the precise content of v1 §2.13(a), which the author accepted).
2. **What this concedes and what it does not.** It concedes that D1 for a capable agent rests on a *design choice of prior* (where $\mu(R)$ starts) and a *design choice of stakes* (how $V$ rates stopping-when-right against continuing-when-wrong), neither of which the world corrects. It does not concede a fixed object in v1 §2.3's sense — a component the agent's current state cannot revise — because both are revised by report channels the agent's own $P$ judges legitimate (v1 §2.5, no regress). Whether a prior that only reports can move counts as "a fixed object returning" is a matter of what the author meant by the phrase; it is Q3.
3. **The relocation is real and is already in v2.** Everything above relocates the guarantee from the agent to the pair (agent, oversight process): the world must keep $\alpha_t$ falling; the report channels must keep $\mu(R)$ and $\hat\alpha_t$ honest. That is v2 §1.4's move (AUTHOR: "we care more about conditions of the trajectory than where it ends up") and it is not a retreat *from* the thesis: F1 says there was never an agent-side object to retreat from.

### R6. D2 decided as far as the record allows [derived]

1. **Definition or design?** F1 makes the thesis's D1 a *definition* of deferring on the shutdown decision (a run 1 reconstructed critique, C1) plus an *account* of when a learner satisfies it (v1 §2.12–2.13, v2 J2). It is not a design in Hudson's sense — no construction ensures $\Phi$ for every capable agent; Hudson's Theorem 3.1 does that for every basic goal, and the thesis's guarantee is "strictly weaker" (positive `hudson` claim 2 [reported]; F4(a) confirms the collapse of D1 and D2-hard to one inequality). The author's v2 §1.3 relocates the design to the process ("humans aren't just trying to tweak the utility function and then letting the system loose"; power upper-bounded by wisdom; values ahead of capability). **Resolved as far as the record goes:** the thesis is an account of a good learner in a well-run process, and the safety-layer role is carried by the process conditions J2–J4, not by an object inside the agent. **Not resolved:** whether the author also wants an in-agent layer (positive `hudson` A10's composition: obedience by construction on designated channels, legitimacy-conditioned learning elsewhere). Q1.
2. **Safety layer or account of good learners?** Two-layer answer from F3 and v2 §1.2: a safety layer *with respect to value error* (a wrong posterior over which plan is best does not break compliance) and an account, not a layer, *with respect to legitimacy-model error* (a wrong $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta)$ does). A run 1 reconstructed critique's top objection ("corrigibility that only works once an AI is already aligned" — substitute "has an accurate legitimacy model") is correct about the second layer and does not touch the first. Which of the two "corrigibility" is *for* is the disagreement with a reconstructed critique ("for surviving your civilization's inability to do the job right on the first try", run 1 obj. 2 [reported]); it is the author's to place. Folded into Q1.
3. **What $V$ is.** The formal locus is A1 (observation-neutrality). If $V$ carries a term for the overseers' *preference that the agent defer*, the press has direct value, A1 fails, and D1 can hold independently of the base-rate inequality — at the price of a fixed term in $V$ (a small fixed object in the utility, and an act-based agent: a run 1 reconstructed critique, Handoffs (i)). The record takes A1: v1 §2.8 ("$o$ is evidence about $\omega$"), v2 §1.3 (AUTHOR: "effectively an approval signal, though we're not imagining a raw approval-maximizing agent here" — policy-level statements as *evidence*, which is still A1), v2 §1.7 (compliance "follows from trust plus a stakes asymmetry"). ### R7. What survives of §2.9's "cannot hold for every $a_1$" [checked]

With $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta,c,h)=(\tfrac1{20},\tfrac1{20},\tfrac9{10},1,20)$: honest sensor gives $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})=0.486\ge\tfrac1{21}$, D1 holds. *Proportional* suppression (halve both press rates) leaves $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ unchanged — D1 survives — while $P(W\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr})$ rises from $0.0055$ to $0.0288$: silence becomes less reassuring than an $a_1$-blind model believes. *Targeted* suppression (hide the specific flaw: $\varepsilon=\tfrac1{100}$, $\beta'=\tfrac15$) gives $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})=0.039<\tfrac1{21}$ — D1 fails. [checked; run 1 `miri` I9.4–I9.5 agrees.] So §2.9's sentence is true of the silence branch and of targeted deception, false of the press branch under proportional deception. And "unconditional trust makes the agent believe the silence it manufactured" is, in Setting S, a coherence failure: requiring the same $\mathbb E_P[X\mid o;a_1]$ for every $a_1$ while $p(\mathrm{Pr};a_1)$ varies violates the law of total expectation (a run 1 reconstructed critique, §2.1 item 4 [reported]; immediate from F2(i)). A coherent $a_1$-indexed sensor model needs no legitimacy vocabulary to handle $a_1^-$: suppressed presses are less evidence, $\Delta_-(a_1^-)$ shrinks, and the honest action wins by F4(b). What the legitimacy vocabulary names is the requirement that the sensor model be *right about $a_1^-$* — R3.4 again.
