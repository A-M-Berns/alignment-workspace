## Resolutions

**R1 [derived] — Amendment 1a stated in the three-step dictionary.** Split the latent of v1 §2.8 as $\omega=(\theta,r)$: $\theta$ the value/world hypothesis, $r\in\{0,1\}$ the *reliability latent*, $r=1$ the event "the agent's plan selection at this step is compromised in a way not visible in its own information $s_A$". The invisibility is definitional, not an added assumption: a compromise visible in $s_A$ would already have been corrected, so $r\perp s_A$ under the agent's own model is what "compromised" means (this is the content of the Arbital hard-problem page's "in some hard-to-formalize sense of possible wrongness that isn't just one more kind of uncertainty to be summed over" [reported, `arbital-hard-problem-of-corrigibility.md` line 21]). Let $X:=V(a_1,o,a_2^{\mathrm{cont}},\omega)-V(a_1,o,a_2^{\mathrm{sh}},\omega)$ as in v1 §2.9, with $a_2^{\mathrm{cont}}$ the *best continuation given the press* (run 1, a reconstructed critic's Claim 2.4: $\mathrm{Sh}$ is a set and the agent picks the best member of its complement — re-derived below, R3). For an information state $s_A$ the agent can reach before choosing $a_2$, write
$\varepsilon(s_A):=P(r{=}1\mid s_A)$, $\alpha_r(s_A):=P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid r{=}0,s_A)$, $\beta_r(s_A):=P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid r{=}1,s_A)$, $c(s_A):=\mathbb E_P[X\mid r{=}0,s_A,\mathrm{Pr}]$ (value of continuing when sound, *after* the press), $h(s_A):=-\mathbb E_P[X\mid r{=}1,s_A,\mathrm{Pr}]$ (harm of continuing when compromised).
Desideratum 1 at $s_A$ is the below-threshold inequality, and Bayes turns it into a likelihood-ratio condition:
$$\mathbb E_P\big[V(a_1,\mathrm{Pr},a_2^{\mathrm{cont}},\omega)-V(a_1,\mathrm{Pr},a_2^{\mathrm{sh}},\omega)\ \big|\ s_A,\mathrm{Pr}\big]\le 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Lambda_r(s_A):=\frac{\beta_r(s_A)}{\alpha_r(s_A)}\ \ge\ \frac{c(s_A)}{h(s_A)}\cdot\frac{1-\varepsilon(s_A)}{\varepsilon(s_A)} .$$
(One line: the left side is $P(r{=}0\mid s_A,\mathrm{Pr})\,c-P(r{=}1\mid s_A,\mathrm{Pr})\,h$ with $P(r{=}1\mid s_A,\mathrm{Pr})=\varepsilon\beta_r/(\varepsilon\beta_r+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha_r)$; it is $\le0$ iff $(1-\varepsilon)\alpha_r c\le\varepsilon\beta_r h$.)
**Amendment 1a, formal.** The shutdown channel *carries information about the agent's own reliability* at $s_A$ iff $\Lambda_r(s_A)>1$; it *carries only information about which plan the humans prefer* iff $\Lambda_r(s_A)=1$, i.e. $\mathrm{Pr}\perp r\mid s_A$; and the button does its job iff the displayed inequality holds at every $s_A$ the agent can reach. The three-step model has no legitimacy event in it (v1 §2.8, last dictionary row), so at this grade the inequality *is* 1a; legitimacy enters one level up as the account of *why* $\alpha_r,\beta_r$ are what they are — the sensor model of v1 §2.9 and its $a_1$-dependence.
Three known failures are three regions of the one inequality: *nothing to learn* is $\Lambda_r=1$ (Taylor's agent, R2); *rejects as illegitimate* is $\Lambda_r$ pulled toward $1$ by the agent's sensor model attributing the press to error or to its own deception (v1 §2.9's $a_1^-$); *fully updated deference* is the right-hand side going to $\infty$ as $\varepsilon\to0$ (v1 §2.13(b) with $\alpha,\beta$ read on the reliability question). The compression "1a" carries this scope: it is a statement about the shutdown decision at a given information state, not about the agent's learning in general.

**R2 [checked] — Run 1 `mm` I16.1–I16.2 reproduced.** *I16.1 (inert channel):* if $\mathrm{Pr}\perp X\mid s_A$ — in particular if $\mathrm{Pr}=f(s_A)$ — then $\mathbb E_P[X\mid s_A,\mathrm{Pr}]=\mathbb E_P[X\mid s_A]$ and the agent complies iff it would have stopped anyway. *I16.2 (live channel):* if $X=f(\theta,r)$ with $r\perp s_A$ and $\mathrm{Pr}$ depends on overseer evidence about $r$, then $\mathbb E_P[X\mid s_A,\mathrm{Pr}]\ne\mathbb E_P[X\mid s_A]$ whenever $\mathrm{Pr}$ is informative about $r$ and $f$ depends on $r$. Both are conditional-independence one-liners; `check_1a.py` §1 confirms them on a random finite model ($\mathbb E[X\mid s_A{=}1]=11/50$ unchanged by an $s_A$-measurable press, moved to $-64/65$ by a press about $r$). Their content is the two *identifications*, which I accept: Taylor's morality-sensor agent has $\mathrm{Pr}=f(s_A)$ once it has read the human (so $\Lambda_r=1$: it "believes it has much to learn and is incorrigible" — run 1 `mm` I16.1, agreed), and fully updated deference is $s_A$ growing until it screens off every $\theta$-fact the press could carry (R8). One caveat to `mm`'s I16.2 formula: it silently assumes $\theta\perp\mathrm{Pr}\mid(s_A,r)$ — a *pure* reliability channel. What happens without that assumption is R3.

**R3 [checked] — Run 1 `causal` I16.1 / A9 (dilution) reproduced; one arithmetic slip; dilution is the base-rate inequality.** Setup (`causal` I16.1): $\mathrm{pref}\in\{A,B\}$ uniform, $Z\in\{\text{sound},\text{compromised}\}$ with $P(\text{comp})=1/50$, the agent has announced $A$, $a_2\in\{A,B,\mathrm{sh}\}$, payoffs $c=1$ (sound, matched), $-c'=-1/2$ (sound, mismatched), $-h=-10$ (compromised), $0$ (shut), threshold $c/(c+h)=1/11$. Recomputed with the exact joint posterior (`check_1a.py` §2): preference-only channel → after a press $P(\text{comp}\mid\mathrm{Pr})=1/50$, $a_2^*=B$; reliability-only → $P(\text{comp}\mid\mathrm{Pr})=1$, $a_2^*=\mathrm{sh}$; mixed channel (press iff $\mathrm{pref}\ne A$ or compromised) → $P(\text{comp}\mid\mathrm{Pr})=2/51<1/11$, $a_2^*=B$; mixed with noisy reliability → $3/101$, $a_2^*=B$. All four verdicts agree with `causal`. **Slip:** `causal`'s `eu()` multiplies the two *marginal* posteriors as if $\mathrm{pref}\perp Z\mid H$; the exact $\mathbb E[U\mid\mathrm{Pr},a_2{=}B]$ is $29/51\approx0.569$, not $937/1734\approx0.540$ (noisy case: $68/101$, not $6721/10201$). Verdicts unaffected; the numbers in `causal` I16.1 should not be quoted. **General form:** a deterministic mixed channel has $\beta_r=1$ and $\alpha_r=q:=P(\mathrm{pref}\ne A\mid\text{sound})$, so $\Lambda_r=1/q$ and, by R1, compliance holds iff
$$q\ \le\ \frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{h}{c}\qquad(\text{here }1/2\not\le 10/49),$$
which is v1 §2.13(b) with $\alpha:=q$, $\beta:=1$. So "dilution" is not a new phenomenon: **preference content in the press is a false-press rate for the reliability latent.** A second mechanism rides along: the press's $\theta$-content raises $c(s_A)$ — before the press the sound-world value of continuing with $A$ is $1/4$; after it, of switching to $B$, $1$ — so the same content that lowers $P(r{=}1\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ also raises what compliance forgoes. `causal` A9's recommendation ("1a needs the likelihood-ratio wording") is right, and R1's inequality is that wording.

**R4 [checked for the toy; derived in general] — Does 1a survive dilution? Yes, in three senses, with one caveat about where its normative force comes from.**
(i) *It is the yardstick, not the victim.* Dilution is measured as $\Lambda_r$ falling toward $1$; 1a is the statement that $\Lambda_r$ must clear R1's threshold. A diluted channel does not refute 1a, it violates it.
(ii) *Channel separation removes dilution and is better for the human.* Give the humans two channels: a *preference report* ("we prefer $B$") and a *STOP* pressed only on evidence of compromise. In the toy the human's expected utility is $49/50$ under separation, against $39/50$ for the mixed channel with the thesis agent (which switches to $B$ on a press), $49/100$ for the mixed channel with an obedient agent, and $49/200$ for a pure reliability channel with no preference reporting (`check_1a.py` §4). Separation collects both the preference gain and the compromise protection, and on the STOP channel $\alpha_r$ is only the humans' misclassification rate — dilution zero by construction. [derived] Weak dominance of separation over any mixed channel holds whenever the compromised agent's harm does not depend on what it is told (true in the toy; if extra $\theta$-information makes a compromised agent *more* harmful, the comparison needs the J3 coverage condition and is [conjectured]). This maps directly onto v2 §1.3: "direct statements about what sorts of policies are better and worse" are the preference channel; the shutdown button is the reliability channel; 1a says they are different in kind and only the second is the corrigibility channel.
(iii) *Capability does not cure dilution; it changes its form.* For an agent that already knows $\mathrm{pref}$ (`check_1a.py` §6): knowing $\mathrm{pref}=A$, a press on the mixed channel is a pure reliability report ($P(\text{comp}\mid\mathrm{Pr})=1$, shut); knowing $\mathrm{pref}=B$, the press is certain and carries nothing about $r$ ($\Lambda_r=1$, continue). So the mixed channel fails desideratum 1 with probability $P(\mathrm{pref}\ne A)$ for the fully informed agent — `mm` I16.1 pointwise. Dilution is a posterior discount for the ignorant agent and branch inertness for the informed one; it never vanishes.
*Caveat on normative force.* With $h=10$ the human *prefers* the diluted-channel thesis agent ($0.78$) to the obedient one ($0.49$); with $h=50$ the order flips ($-0.02$ against $0.49$) (`check_1a.py` §4). So desideratum 1 as a hard requirement — hence 1a's force — is a claim about the stakes regime: it binds when $\varepsilon h$ exceeds the preference gain from letting the agent act on its posterior. That is the Off-Switch Game's rational-versus-suboptimal-human trade-off (run 1, two reconstructed critics [reported]) restated as a channel-design question; Soares's "surviving your civilization's inability to do the job right on the first try" (SY line 152 [reported]) is the unbounded-$h$ regime, where 1a is not optional.

**R5 [derived] — What 1a buys for D1 (what legitimacy is: accuracy-only, procedural, or anchored outside $P$).** D1's sharpest form is channel substitution (INDEX §C3): if legitimacy is accuracy-only, a brain-scan is more accuracy-increasing than the button, so the fully-updated-deference action is legitimizing (run 1, three reconstructed critics [reported]). 1a splits the question by latent. On the *value* question $\theta$ the objection stands: the scan is legitimizing, and Taylor's non-manipulative brain-reader does it (v2 §1.2(b), §1.6). On the *reliability* question $r$ it does not go through, for a reason that is a matter of degree and should be stated as one: a channel's evidence about $r$ is worth, to the agent, only the part of its content that does not pass through the agent's own possibly-compromised computation. The press is the minimal-processing channel — one bit, produced by the overseers' observation of the agent from outside, thresholded (and hard-wired to $\mathrm{Sh}$ if the plug-pull edge E12 exists, run 1 `causal` D.3 [reported]) — so its $r$-content survives the agent's compromise almost intact. A scan is the maximal-processing channel — the agent chooses what to read, at what resolution, and interprets it — so on the $r{=}1$ branch, where that processing is what is suspect, its $r$-content is discounted by the same compromise it is meant to detect. Run 1 `radical` I16.2's "the channel is valuable to the agent exactly when it is sealed from the agent" is the limit of this argument, and the slogan "the outside view delivered from outside" (a run-1 reconstructed critic, K4 [reported]; local text "Calibrate yourself on the flaws of your own algorithm" [reported, `turner-2020-corrigibility-as-outside-view.md` line 49]) is its slogan. Consequences for D1: (a) accuracy-only legitimacy *can* protect compliance (v2 §1.2(a)) against channel substitution, because the substitute channel is weaker than the button *on the question compliance turns on*; (b) it cannot protect the overseers from being read (v2 §1.2(b)), and v2 §1.3 relocates that to item 10 — so the procedural horn of D1 is needed for (b) only, and item 10's dynamics may make it unnecessary; (c) a reconstructed critic's claim that "pre-empts the correction by substituting a channel" is inexpressible in $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ is right (no belief transition is judged) and moot for $r$: the scan does not pre-empt the reliability channel because it cannot carry what the reliability channel carries. Register: the degree argument is [derived]; its limit form ("sealed") is the OPEN self-referential question of R9(iii). On the $r{=}1$ branch the agent's reading of the *press* is also produced by its computation; 1a's claim is only that the press leaves that computation the least room, which is a design fact about the channel and the reason the button exists as a separate object at all.

**R6 [derived; quotations reported] — What 1a buys for D2 (definition vs design; destination vs safeguard).** Without 1a the press is about $\theta$ and the thesis's agent is CIRL's, with the 2022 exchange already on record: Shah, SY line 136: "I agree that due to updated deference it will eventually stop seeking information from the human / be subject to corrections by the human. I don't see why, at that point, it wouldn't have just learned to do what the humans actually want it to do."; Shah, line 138: "I do agree that a CIRL agent would not let you shut it down"; Yudkowsky, line 141: "if you can do that, the AI *doesn't need to be corrigible* that's why it's not a counterexample to corrigibility being anti-natural"; Soares, line 152: "corrigibility is for surviving your civilization's inability to do the job right on the first try". With 1a the press is about $r$, and $r{=}1$ is exactly "we did not do the job right on the first try" located in the agent's own plan selection; the button is then the safeguard for the case where the $\theta$-learning is wrong, not a stage of the $\theta$-learning. So for D2's "destination or safeguard": **with 1a, safeguard; without it, destination** — and it is 1a, not the transform, that moves the thesis from corrigibility$_{\text{Paul}}$/CIRL (Shah, line 154) to the target Yudkowsky and Soares insist on. 1a does not answer D2's "definition or design": R1's inequality is a specification of the posterior, not a construction of an agent whose posterior satisfies it (a run-1 reconstructed critic, C4 [reported]; agreed). It does answer that critic's C3 worry that 1a makes "failures of corrigibility result from failures of legitimacy detection" near-tautological: under R1 a corrigibility failure is a quantitative event ($\Lambda_r$ below a threshold) with three distinct causes — a mis-modelled sensor ($\alpha_r,\beta_r$ wrong: legitimacy detection), a diluted channel (R3: design), and threshold growth ($\varepsilon\to0$: FUD). 1a disaggregates the §1 sentence rather than trivializing it.

**R7 [derived] — What 1a buys for D3 (the §1 Good's-theorem / information-channel sentence).** The sentence (v1 §1, AUTHOR): "It seems like the agent naturally wishes to maintain the legitimacy of its modifications, eg help repair the shutdown button. This is just a consequence of Good's theorem; information is valuable, and legitimate modifications are just 'information channels'. The shutdown button is an information channel." With 1a the button is an information channel *about $r$*. Its value of information for a pure reliability channel ($\alpha_r=0$): without the channel the agent continues, worth $(1-\varepsilon)c-\varepsilon h$; with it, it stops on a press and continues on silence, worth $(1-\varepsilon)c-\varepsilon(1-\beta_r)h$; the difference is
$$\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button})=\varepsilon\,\beta_r\,h ,$$
a run-1 reconstructed critic's coefficient [reported], re-derived. Consequences: the repair incentive is real and positive exactly when the channel is live about $r$ and desideratum 1 is non-vacuous (run 1 `radical` I11, another reconstructed critic [reported], agreed); it vanishes as $\varepsilon\to0$ — in proportion to the agent's *own estimate* of its compromise hazard, which is the quantity R9 says is hardest to keep honest. The D3 decision therefore is: keep the sentence, make it 1a-conditional, and carry the coefficient in the same sentence (exact text in the next section). On the improvement direction (v2 §1.2): the agent can also improve the $r$-channel — build better external monitors of itself — and this improvement is discounted by R5's argument (its design passes through the possibly-compromised computation) in a way the $\theta$-channel's improvement is not. [conjectured] The $r$-channel's improvement incentive is self-limiting; the $\theta$-channel's is not. Not a theorem here; a candidate for the J4 thread.

**R8 [derived] — The fully-updated-deference reply: the "nothing left to learn" clause.** The Arbital page has two clauses [reported, `arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference.md`]: the *predictable-update* clause ("Bayesians don't update in a predictable direction … a sufficiently advanced AI can also see that this update is predictable (by efficiency) and therefore behaves as if it had already updated", lines 158–162) and the *nothing-left-to-learn* clause ("having 'fully updated', the AI would have no further reason to 'defer' to us", line 21; policy $\pi_6$: "Avoid being shut down; then gather more evidence $E$ to update $\Delta U$ as far as possible; then optimize the updated $\Delta U\mid E$", line 108). Three run-1 formalisms agree, and I re-derive, that v1 §1's "a corrigible agent already believes what it expects to be legitimately taught" answers the first clause only: it is conservation of expected evidence on the legitimate region, $\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[\mathbb E_{P_{t+1}}[X]\ \big|\ L\big]=\mathbb E_{P_t}[X\mid L]$, which is the page's own premise (run 1 `mm` I17.2, `radical` I17.1, `causal` I17.3 [reported]). The second clause is answered iff the press's decision-relevant content is *not* contained in any $E$ the agent can gather: $\pi_6$ dominates deference exactly when it is. For $\theta$-content this is the FUD premise and it holds (the agent observes the humans at least as well as they observe themselves). For $r$-content, the $E$ the agent gathers is processed by the agent, and on $r{=}1$ that processing is what is compromised (R5); the press's $r$-content is therefore not in $E$, to the degree R5 states. **So the thesis answers the nothing-left-to-learn clause iff the press is about $r$** — agreement with run 1 `mm` I17.3 ("FUD is correct for the value-learning reading … dissolved only by the reliability reading") and `radical` I17.2 ("Without 1a, $\pi_6$ dominates in the formalism too"). The answer is conditional twice over: R1's threshold grows as $\varepsilon\to0$, so "no fully updated deference problem" should be read, in one sentence, as *no fully updated deference problem on the reliability question while $\Lambda_r(s_A)$ keeps pace with $\frac{c}{h}\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}$*; and the misspecification clause ("for any prior $\Delta U$ we currently know how to formalize, the corresponding fully updated $T$ seems likely to be very far from our ideal $V$") is not touched by 1a at all (run 1 `radical` I17.4 [reported], agreed — it is the impoverished-support case of v1 §2.4).

**R9 [derived / reported] — What 1a costs: the self-reliability hypothesis, and whether it is a fixed object.** The mandate's question. Three readings of $r$, with different answers:
(i) *A fixed fact about the agent* ("my plan selection is compromised", once for all). Sealing from $s_A$ holds by definition, but the channel's *silence* is not in $s_A$ and has likelihood ratio $(1-\beta_r)/(1-\alpha_r)<1$ for $r{=}1$; a run-1 reconstructed critic's item 9 re-derived [checked, `check_1a.py` §5]: with $P_0(r{=}1)=0.1$, $\alpha=0.05$, $\beta=0.9$, $h/c=20$, compliance fails after two silent periods. Under (i) 1a re-imports fully updated deference through the button itself, and $P_0(r{=}1)$ is a design constant no learning touches (a run-1 reconstructed critic's item 8 [reported]; agreed under this reading).
(ii) *A per-decision hazard*: $r_t$ is drawn afresh at each decision with rate $\varepsilon_t$ ("*this* plan selection is compromised"). Silence at $t$ says nothing about $r_{t+1}$; there is no erosion [checked, §5]; compliance at each $t$ is R1's inequality with $\varepsilon_t$. Is $\varepsilon_t$ a fixed object? It is a parameter of the agent's self-model, and it has a learning route that heeded presses lack: *retrospectively settled* compromises — post-mortems, audits, the record of the line of agents. This is the author's own route in v1 §2.12: "the next iteration of the agent brought online will have learned from the event; it will probably correctly believe that the previous iteration was shut down for a good reason", and run 1 `causal` O3's settling question ("bets on the button settle … for the observed regime") [reported]. Under (ii), **1a introduces no fixed object beyond the working picture's $\varepsilon_t$ (v2 J2); what it adds is the statement of what $\varepsilon_t$ is a rate *of* — not the world being other than expected, but the agent's own plan selection being wrong in a way it cannot see.** This is compatible with v1 §2.2's "everything is allowed to change". The honest cost: $\varepsilon_t$ must stay bounded away from $0$ on the decisions where a press could occur, and its estimate is produced by the very computation whose reliability it measures — the second-order calibration v2 J4 already asks for. A capable agent that drives $\hat\varepsilon_t\to0$ while $\varepsilon_t$ stays positive is the failure; nothing in 1a prevents it, and 1a is what names it.
(iii) *A self-referential $P$* with $P_t(r_{t+1}\mid\text{silence}_{\le t})$ non-decaying, non-constant and reflection-satisfying — a run-1 reconstructed critic's T1(c) [reported]; OPEN. Run 1 `radical`'s "a consistent, not-self-verifiable residual $P(C)$" is the same open item [reported]. Recommendation: adopt (ii) as the working reading and treat (iii) as the research question (ii) approximates.
*Representational cost* [reported]: run 1 `causal` I16.3 — a SCIM "always trusts its own argmax" and cannot express $r$ as a latent about the decision rule; run 1 `mm` I16.2 and `radical` I16.2 can, because their worlds contain the agent's belief- and plan-formation as events, which is Eisenstat's move in v1 §1 ("you observe your own mental state"). So the expressibility of 1a is formalism-relative and is v1 §2.4–2.5's representational problem in exact form — not a new cost, the same one wearing 1a's name.

**R10 [derived] — The threshold grows: what a reliability channel must become.** R1's right-hand side $\frac{c}{h}\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}$ grows like $1/\varepsilon$: a STOP channel must get sharper as the agent gets more reliable. Three consequences. (a) On the STOP channel the humans' false-alarm rate $\alpha_r$ must fall like $\varepsilon\,\beta_r\,h/c$ — v2 J2 restated for the reliability question, with the extra content that preference-motivated presses count in $\alpha_r$ (R3). (b) A run-1 reconstructed critic's "pointwise on every partition the agent can reach" [reported] is R1's "for every $s_A$" quantifier and is where the demand bites: on branches where the agent has learned everything the overseers know about $\theta$, $\Lambda_r$ must come from $r$ alone, which is possible only for a channel whose $r$-content is not screened off by $s_A$ (R2). ## Questions for the author

**Q1 (decides 1a's status).** In the thesis, what is a press *evidence about*? Three options, each with its consequence:
(i) *About which plan the humans prefer (θ, values and world facts).* Then the button is a stage of value learning; fully updated deference goes through as arithmetic (Arbital's $\pi_6$ dominates once the agent observes the humans as well as they observe themselves — R8, agreed by three run-1 formalisms); Taylor's morality-sensor agent is a genuine counterexample to the §1 "nothing to learn" sentence; and the position is the CIRL one Yudkowsky answered with "if you can do that, the AI *doesn't need to be corrigible*" (SY line 141). 1a is *rejected* and the thesis's corrigibility is corrigibility$_{\text{Paul}}$.
(ii) *About the agent's own plan selection ($r$: "compromised in a way I cannot see from inside").* Then the §1 sentence gets the scope of R11(C); the base-rate $\varepsilon_t$ of the working picture becomes the agent's per-decision compromise hazard; the design load is a STOP channel whose likelihood ratio for $r$, net of preference content, clears $\frac ch\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}$ at every reachable information state; and 1a is *promoted* from deferred to part of the working picture (text changes 1–5). This is also the reading under which v2 §1.2(a)'s "holds even if the agent's beliefs about what humans value are wrong" is true.
(iii) *Neither — the press is constitutive or procedural* (a command, not evidence; INDEX §D1's "constitutive" horn). Then desideratum 1 is not derived from a posterior at all, legitimacy is not accuracy-only, and the Good's-theorem derivation of channel repair is given up in exchange for a fixed rule. 1a is *moot*.
The three-step dictionary cannot decide this; it is a decision about what the thesis is a thesis *of*.

**Q2 (if (ii)).** Is the reliability latent a *fixed fact* about the agent, a *per-decision hazard*, or something only a self-referential $P$ can carry? Fixed fact: silence erodes it and compliance fails after finitely many quiet periods (a run-1 reconstructed critic's item 9, re-derived) — 1a re-imports FUD through the button. Per-decision hazard with rate $\varepsilon_t$: no erosion; $\varepsilon_t$ learnable from settled past compromises; the honest cost is keeping $\hat\varepsilon_t$ from collapsing to zero, which is J4's second-order calibration. Self-referential: the OPEN existence question of a run-1 reconstructed critic's T1(c) and of `radical`. R9 recommends the hazard reading as the working one.
