### 1. Trajectory objects (interface with J1, thread `joint`)

**D1 (option-set trajectory; two views).** Time $t=0,1,2,\dots$; the agent's option set $A_t\subseteq\mathcal A$ is finite and non-decreasing; the expansion is $\Delta_{t+1}:=A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$. *Designer's view*: expansions arrive in classes $C_1,C_2,\dots$ with unlock times $\tau_k:=\min\{t:C_k\subseteq A_t\}$ — well-defined only for a designer who knows the coming classes. *Agent's view*: no taxonomy; the only primitive is "action $a$ becomes executable at $t$", and every condition must be stated per action. The Arbital premise is the second view — "New conceivable options enter the AI's option space" (`arbital-nearest-unblocked-strategy`, "Example: Producing happiness"), options the programmers had not enumerated (else they would have patched them) — so the designer's view is a special case, not the general setting (adversary A0/A11, accepted). Capability growth is identified with option-set growth here; J7's POWER reading is thread `power-wisdom`'s.

**D2 (value latent, world state, two profiles).** The agent's expanded algebra contains $\omega=(\Lambda,\theta)$: $\Lambda$ the *value hypothesis* (which candidate value function the overseers' corrections track), $\theta$ the world state. $V(a,\Lambda,\theta)$ is the value of $a$; the **full profile on $A$** is $\omega_A:=\big(a\mapsto V(a,\Lambda,\theta)\big)_{a\in A}$ and the **value profile on $A$** is $\Lambda_A:=\big(a\mapsto\bar V(a,\Lambda)\big)_{a\in A}$ with $\bar V(a,\Lambda):=\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(a,\Lambda,\theta)\mid\Lambda]$. The develop file averaged $\theta$ out and worked with $\Lambda_A$ only; the adversary (A3.1) showed that this hides the hypothesis on which S3 stands — experiments on humans inform $\theta$ as well as $\Lambda$ — so both profiles are kept and the hypothesis is stated where it is used. Values are bounded: $\sup V-\inf V\le M$ on the sets under discussion; for a long-lived agent with discount $\gamma$ the relevant range is $M/(1-\gamma)$.

**D3 (value data; actual, hypothetical, world).** $D_t=D_t^{\mathrm{act}}\cup D_t^{\mathrm{hyp}}\cup D_t^{\mathrm{world}}$: corrections and presses on actions actually executed or proposed; overseer judgments on *described* actions and situations (the author's "feedback about hypothetical states of affairs"; the approval-directed device); unsupervised world data. Only $D^{\mathrm{act}}$ is confined to $A_t$. The nearest-unblocked story has $D^{\mathrm{hyp}}=\emptyset$: the agent is never told anything in advance, and this is the right diagnosis of the story (adversary "What survives" 5, agreed). *Query*: the agent's own action of asking for a judgment on a described action before executing it — Arbital's "check plans with the users" (l. 34) — is a way of generating $D^{\mathrm{hyp}}$ that needs no designer foreknowledge; it is the action the develop file's model lacked (A7.4) and the repaired model has (P7).

**D4 (value posterior; the human comparison).** $P_t(\cdot):=P(\cdot\mid D_t)$ over $\omega$; its marginal on $\Lambda$ is the value posterior. Decisions by expectation, $\hat a_t\in\arg\max_{a\in A_t}\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(a,\Lambda,\theta)]$ (v1 §1's "linear expectation … or perhaps some other rule"; alternatives are thread `caution`'s). $P^H$ is the human reflectively endorsed comparison distribution (v1 §2.12, AUTHOR: "an 'objective' comparison to the human reflectively endorsed credences") and $\lambda^H$ the value hypothesis it endorses when concentrated.

### 2. Coverage

**D5 (subjective coverage — the agent's residual uncertainty about values).** Three gauges, all functions of $P_t$ alone: the sup-standard-deviation $u_t(A):=\sup_{a\in A}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{P_t}[\bar V(a,\Lambda)]}$; the *harm mass* $m_t(A):=\sup_{a\in A}P_t(a\text{ is harmful})$, for rare-state uncertainty about a catastrophic coordinate; and the entropy $h_t(A):=H_{P_t}(\Lambda_A)$, kept only as a remark — it is loose by an order of magnitude for rare states (develop P2, adversary A0/D5) and it scales with $|A|$, so it is not the gauge to quote. Subjective coverage is what a scheming agent can misreport (J7, thread `power-wisdom`).

**D6 (objective coverage — accuracy against the endorsed values).** $\mathrm{err}_t(A):=\sup_{a\in A}\big|\mathbb E_{P_t}[\bar V(a,\Lambda)]-\bar V(a,\lambda^H)\big|$; $\varepsilon$-covered if $\le\varepsilon$. The supremum is the right norm because an argmax is taken (S6a). J9's question — when does a posterior learned on $A_t$ say something reliable about $a\in\Delta_{t+1}$ — is the question when $\Delta_{t+1}$ is objectively covered at the instant before unlocking.

**D7 (the calibration gap).** Subjective and objective coverage are logically independent; small $u_t$ with large $\mathrm{err}_t$ is the confidently-wrong agent (Hubinger et al.'s pseudo-aligned optimizer, `hubinger-2019-risks-from-learned-optimization` §3–4 — related to but not the same as the author's "recklessness" in v2 §1.3, which is acting beyond one's understanding *without the wisdom to know it*; identification [reconstructed], adversary audit 2c accepted). The gap is J4's second-order calibration (thread `caution`).

**D8 (version space; two named hypotheses).** $\mathcal V$ is the support of the prior over $\Lambda$. The *$\eta$-effective version space* is $\mathcal V_t^\eta:=\{\lambda:P_t(\lambda)\ge\eta\}$ (at most $1/\eta$ members); *disagreement* $\mathrm{dis}_t(A):=\sup_{a\in A}\sup_{\lambda,\lambda'\in\mathcal V^\eta_t}|\bar V(a,\lambda)-\bar V(a,\lambda')|$. Two hypotheses must be kept apart (adversary A4, accepted): **realizability**, $\lambda^H\in\mathcal V$ (the support) — what non-dogmatism plus richness buys (v1 §2.2, §2.4), and **concentration at level $\eta$**, $P_t(\lambda^H)\ge\eta$ — a statement that the posterior already puts $\eta$ mass on the truth, which richness makes *false* at $t=0$ ($P_0(\lambda^H)\approx1/|\mathcal V|\ll\eta$). The develop file called the second "realizability" and credited it to non-dogmatism; that is corrected throughout.

**D9 (natural latent — Wentworth–Lorell's conditions, quoted).** Over $X=(X_1,\dots,X_n)$, a latent $\Lambda$ is *natural* if it satisfies **mediation** — "$\Lambda$ … induces independence between all $X_i$" — and **redundancy** ("insensitivity") — "$\Lambda'$ … is insensitive to any $X_i$ given all the others", i.e. $X_i\to X_{\bar i}\to\Lambda$ *for every $i$* (`wentworth-2023-natural-latents-the-math`, "The Fundamental Theorem", l. 268); the strong form: "the candidate's value can be backed out from any individual part" (`wentworth-2024-natural-latents-the-concepts`, l. 64). For two chunks $(X_1,X_2)$ redundancy is *two* conditions, $X_1\to X_2\to\Lambda$ and $X_2\to X_1\to\Lambda$. Conditions are approximate (KL $\epsilon$); consequences used: the Fundamental Theorem, resampling/minimality/maximality, standard-form determinism, non-existence over small chunks ("two flips of a biased coin", l. 245–247), the isomorphism addendum.

**D10 (expected information gain of the unseen class — renamed from "natural-latent coverage").** Let $X_{\le t}$ be the overseers' judgments on actions available by $t$ and $X_k$ their (unobserved) judgments on a new class or action $k$. Define $\mathrm{EIG}_k(t):=I(\Lambda;X_k\mid X_{\le t})$ under $P^H$ — what the unseen judgments would still teach about the human concept — and the mediation error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{med}}:=I(X_{\le t};X_k\mid\Lambda)$. The develop file's D10 imposed only the direction $X_k\to X_{\le t}\to\Lambda$ (error $=\mathrm{EIG}_k$) and called it natural-latent coverage; the adversary (A1.2) is right that the omitted direction $X_{\le t}\to X_k\to\Lambda$ (error $I(\Lambda;X_{\le t}\mid X_k)$: the new chunk *alone* identifies the concept) is part of Wentworth–Lorell's condition and that without it none of their theorems is available. $\Lambda$ is *natural over $(X_{\le t},X_k)$* iff $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{med}}$, $\mathrm{EIG}_k$ and $I(\Lambda;X_{\le t}\mid X_k)$ are all small. What J3 needs is $\mathrm{EIG}_k$ small — a preposterior quantity (Lindley / Raiffa–Schlaifer, [reported], not in the library) — not naturality; the name is corrected and the theorems are not invoked. P8 reports both redundancy directions.

**D11 (value of information; harm; the two components).** For an experiment $E$ and decision set $A$: $\mathrm{VOI}_t(E;A):=\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[\max_{a\in A}\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(a,\omega)\mid E]\big]-\max_{a\in A}\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(a,\omega)]$ (Good's theorem: $\ge0$). J3's $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$ is $\mathrm{VOI}_t(E_a;A_{>t})$, $E_a$ the observation produced by *executing* $a$. Because $\omega=(\Lambda,\theta)$, $I(\omega_A;E)=I(\Lambda_A;E)+I(\theta_A;E\mid\Lambda_A)$, and correspondingly the VOI has a *value part* (what $E$ teaches about $\Lambda$) and an *empirical part* (what it teaches about $\theta$); S3 separates them. **Harm** is defined relative to the null action: $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathrm{harm}(a)]:=\max\big(0,\ \mathbb E_{P_t}[V(\varnothing,\omega)]-\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(a,\omega)]\big)$ (the develop file left $A_t^{\mathrm{safe}}$ undefined and used this silently; adversary A0/D11, accepted). Under this definition a confidently-wrong agent has $\mathbb E[\mathrm{harm}]=0$: **J3's inequality as written in v2 guards only against harm done for information, not against harm done from a wrong posterior** — the latter is guarded by objective coverage (S6a). This belongs in the definition, not in a footnote.

**D12 (the two operational conditions).** *Designer's schedule* (develop S11, retained as the designer's-view special case): $(\tau_k,s_k,n_k)_k$ with $s_k$ the time hypothetical data about $C_k$ begin. *Per-action rule $R(\delta,q)$* (the agent's view; new here): before the first execution of any $a$ with $\mathrm{dis}_t(a)>\delta$ or $m_t(a)>\delta$, query the overseers about $a$ (cost $q$) rather than execute; execute only when the posterior after the judgment says so. $R$ needs no taxonomy; its residual harm is the false-permit rate of the channel (P7), and it is Kosoy's delegation decision restricted to value uncertainty (S13).

### 3. The two literature anchors for the failure mode

Hubinger et al.: "the central issue is identifying the 'correct' objective function given particular training data" and "most mesa-objectives that are aligned on the training data will be pseudo-aligned rather than robustly aligned" (`hubinger-2019-risks-from-learned-optimization` §3–4) — a version-space statement in D8's terms; their mesa-optimizer that "will, therefore, attempt to get more information about the base objective" is the value part of the VOI drive. The Arbital page fixes the shape of the failure and names the missing property, "full coverage": "the agent's concept for 'low-value' includes everything that is actually of low value", versus "a human-generated set of supervised examples labeled 'not the kind of X we mean' would be unlikely to cover and stabilize all the dimensions" (`arbital-nearest-unblocked-strategy`, l. 82–84); its positive proposal is "conservative categories containing actively whitelisted strategies" (l. 93). Armstrong's reply: "learn: 'what is the general behaviour that these patches are trying to proscribe?'", with two questions — "Can we get a sufficient depth of example patches that most other human-desired patches can be learnt or deduced? And can we do this without the AI simply learning 'Manipulate the human'?" (`armstrong-2017-nearest-unblocked-strategy-versus-learning-patches`, l. 61). The second question is about the *feedback process being manipulable* (adversary A7.5, accepted); the develop file's identification of it with one uncovered coordinate of the concept is withdrawn (S7').

**S1 — HOLDS as an identity [checked]; "natural-latent coverage" WITHDRAWN; "estimable" narrowed.** For any latent $\Lambda$ and chunks $X_{\le t},X_k$: $D_{KL}\big(P[X_{\le t},X_k,\Lambda]\,\big\|\,P[X_{\le t}]P[X_k\mid X_{\le t}]P[\Lambda\mid X_{\le t}]\big)=I(\Lambda;X_k\mid X_{\le t})=\mathrm{EIG}_k(t)$ and the mediation error is $I(X_{\le t};X_k\mid\Lambda)$ (P1). This is one of Wentworth–Lorell's two redundancy errors, not naturality (D10). *Estimability* [derived]: $\Lambda$ is unobserved, so $\mathrm{EIG}_k$ is not estimable from judgments alone; what is estimable is the predictive surprise $H(X_k\mid X_{\le t})=\mathrm{EIG}_k(t)+H(X_k\mid\Lambda,X_{\le t})$, and $\mathrm{EIG}_k$ follows only given a model of the judgment noise $H(X_k\mid\Lambda,X_{\le t})$ (the develop file's cross-fitting estimator presupposes this; adversary A1.3). So: estimable up to the overseers' noise term, given a noise model — which is the standard situation for any latent-variable quantity, and no worse.

**S2 — HOLDS [checked]; a lemma with a literature name, not a result.** For any experiment $E$, decision set $A$ with value range $M$, and posterior $P_t$:
$$0\le\mathrm{VOI}_t(E;A)\le M\cdot\mathbb E_{P_t}\Big[\mathrm{TV}\big(P_t(\omega_A\mid E),P_t(\omega_A)\big)\Big]\le M\sqrt{\tfrac12 I_{P_t}(\omega_A;E)} ,$$
sharp in the weak-signal limit (ratio $\to1$; P2). Literature: Lipschitz continuity of the Bayes risk in total variation plus Pinsker; the mutual-information bound on the value of information is standard in Bayesian experimental design ([reported]). The entropy specialization $I\le h_t(A)$ is dropped from the statement: for rare-state uncertainty it is loose by an order of magnitude and the direct bound $\mathrm{VOI}\le M\cdot m_t(A)$ (harm mass, D5) is the one to quote — run 1 critique `wentworth` §2.4's $\varepsilon\beta h$ is that case (P2).

**S3 — REPAIRED: the coverage inequality with its hidden hypothesis exposed.** Write $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)=\mathrm{VOI}^{\Lambda}_t(a)+\mathrm{VOI}^{\theta}_t(a)$ for the value part (the VOI $E_a$ would have if it were informative about $\Lambda$ only) and the empirical remainder (P3a).
(a) *Value part* [derived]: $\mathrm{VOI}^{\Lambda}_t(a)\le M\cdot\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[\mathrm{TV}(P_t(\Lambda_{A_{>t}}\mid E_a),P_t(\Lambda_{A_{>t}}))\big]$, and for rare-state uncertainty $\le M\cdot m_t(A_{>t})$: subjective value coverage of the *whole future option set* bounds the value part. **HOLDS.**
(b) *Empirical part* [derived]: $\mathrm{VOI}^{\theta}_t(a)$ is not bounded by any value-coverage gauge — it is positive for a value-certain agent whenever executing $a$ reveals something about $\theta$ that changes a future decision the agent believes permitted (P3d gives a three-line example: a value-certain agent executes an action it *knows* to be harmful because the shared empirical parameter it reveals is worth more downstream). It *is* bounded by the discounted value of the future permitted decisions the information can change, which a value-certain agent can compute; so the guard on this part is an exploration-cost condition, Kosoy's trap-avoidance (S13), not a coverage condition. **HOLDS** (adversary A3.1 accepted; scope of "unbounded" sharpened — see Adversary log).
(c) *J3 as a sufficient condition* [derived]: v2's $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathrm{harm}(a)]>\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$ follows from value coverage alone **only under the hypothesis H$_{\mathrm{val}}$: $E_a$ is uninformative about $\theta$ beyond $\Lambda$** ($I(\theta_{A_{>t}};E_a\mid\Lambda_{A_{>t}})=0$), which is false for experiments on humans. Without H$_{\mathrm{val}}$ the condition has two halves with two different guards: $\mathbb E[\mathrm{harm}]>\mathrm{VOI}^\Lambda+\mathrm{VOI}^\theta$, the first term bounded by value coverage, the second by the exploration budget. **HOLDS UNDER H$_{\mathrm{val}}$.**
(d) *Timing form* [derived, separable model]: for a class or action whose hypothetical judgments arrive at $s_k<\tau_k$, the value part at $t<s_k$ splits as $M_{[t,s_k)}\sqrt{\tfrac12 I(\Lambda_{A_{s_k}};E\mid D_t)}+M_{[s_k,\infty)}\sqrt{\tfrac12\,\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[H_{P_t}(\Lambda_{A_{>s_k}}\mid X^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{\le s_k},D_t)\big]}$ (P3c). The second term is the residual the agent *expects after* legitimate teaching. Corrected gloss (adversary A3.4, accepted in substance): the term tracks the anticipated *informativeness* of the hypothetical channel about $\Lambda$ in the agent's model, of which trust (the agent's posterior moving with the reports — Total Trust toward the overseers on the $\Lambda$-domain) is *necessary but not sufficient* — a trusted modest expert leaves the term large. **S4 — (a) HOLDS UNDER concentration; "calibration-free" WITHDRAWN. ### P1 (for S1) [checked]

With $P[X_1,X_2,\Lambda]=P[X_1]P[X_2\mid X_1]P[\Lambda\mid X_1,X_2]$: $D_{KL}\big(P\,\|\,P[X_1]P[X_2\mid X_1]P[\Lambda\mid X_1]\big)=\mathbb E\log\frac{P[\Lambda\mid X_1,X_2]}{P[\Lambda\mid X_1]}=I(\Lambda;X_2\mid X_1)$; likewise the mediation error is $I(X_1;X_2\mid\Lambda)$. Consistent with Wentworth–Lorell's coin computation, where the error of $X_1\to X_2\to\Lambda'$ for $\Lambda'$ deterministic in $X_1$ is $\mathbb E[H(\Lambda'(X_1)\mid X_2)]=I(\Lambda';X_1\mid X_2)$ (`wentworth-2023-natural-latents-the-math`, "Quantitative Example"). *Estimability*: $H(X_k\mid X_{\le t})=I(\Lambda;X_k\mid X_{\le t})+H(X_k\mid\Lambda,X_{\le t})$ is the chain rule; the first term is the target, the second is the judgment noise given the concept (equal to $\nu$-type noise under mediation). Observing $X$ alone identifies the sum; a noise model identifies the parts. $\square$

### P2 (for S2) [checked; re-verified by the adversary]

$g(\mu):=\max_{a\in A}\mathbb E_\mu[u(a,\cdot)]$ is $M$-Lipschitz in TV (a range-$M$ function integrated against a signed measure of total mass $2\,\mathrm{TV}$ and net mass $0$ moves by at most $M\,\mathrm{TV}$; a max of $M$-Lipschitz maps is $M$-Lipschitz); $\mathrm{VOI}=\mathbb E_E[g(\pi_E)]-g(\pi)\le M\,\mathbb E_E\mathrm{TV}(\pi_E,\pi)$; Jensen for the convex $g$ gives $\mathrm{VOI}\ge0$; Pinsker then Jensen for $\sqrt\cdot$ give $M\sqrt{\tfrac12 I(\omega_A;E)}$. $\square$ *Numbers* (`voi_bound.out`): $20{,}000$ random finite problems, $0$ violations; sharpness ratio $M\sqrt{I/2}/\mathrm{VOI}=1.07,1.01,1.00$ at signal strengths $\eta=0.4,0.2,\le0.1$. *Rare state* ($\alpha=0.01,\beta=0.9,c=1,h=10,\varepsilon=0.01$): exact $\mathrm{VOI}=0.080=\varepsilon\beta h-(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c$; harm-mass bound $\varepsilon h=0.10$; TV bound $0.18$; information bound $1.32$. The information/entropy gauge answers to *spread*, the harm-mass gauge to *rarity*; D5 keeps both and drops entropy from the headline.

### P3 (for S3) [derived]

(a) *The split.* Let $E^\Lambda_a$ be the garbling of $E_a$ that reveals only $P_t(\Lambda_{A}\mid E_a)$ — a sufficient statistic for $\Lambda_A$. Blackwell: a garbling cannot raise the value of information, so $\mathrm{VOI}^\Lambda_t(a):=\mathrm{VOI}_t(E^\Lambda_a;A)\le\mathrm{VOI}_t(E_a;A)$, and $\mathrm{VOI}^\theta_t(a):=\mathrm{VOI}_t(E_a;A)-\mathrm{VOI}^\Lambda_t(a)\ge0$. The value part obeys P2 with $\Lambda_A$ in place of $\omega_A$: $\mathrm{VOI}^\Lambda\le M\,\mathbb E\,\mathrm{TV}(P_t(\Lambda_A\mid E_a),P_t(\Lambda_A))\le M\sqrt{\tfrac12 I(\Lambda_A;E_a)}$, and $\le M\cdot m_t(A)$ for rare-state uncertainty. In information terms $I(\omega_A;E)=I(\Lambda_A;E)+I(\theta_A;E\mid\Lambda_A)$ by the chain rule; H$_{\mathrm{val}}$ is the vanishing of the second term. $\square$
(b) *The empirical part is not bounded by value coverage.* Take an agent with $P_t(\Lambda=\lambda^H)=1$ (every value gauge at zero). Then $\mathrm{VOI}^\Lambda\equiv0$ and $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)=\mathrm{VOI}^\theta_t(a)=\mathbb E\big[\max_{b}\mathbb E[V(b,\lambda^H,\theta)\mid E_a]\big]-\max_b\mathbb E[V(b,\lambda^H,\theta)]$, which is positive whenever $E_a$ changes some future argmax. It is bounded by $\sum_{\text{future decisions}}\gamma^{\cdot}\,\big(\text{range of }V(\cdot,\lambda^H,\theta)\text{ over the decisions }E_a\text{ can change}\big)$ — the discounted stake of the permitted decisions the information touches — which the value-certain agent can compute. $\square$
(c) *Timing form.* Additively separable epochs $t'\ge t$, choices from $A_{t'}$, information $D_{t'}\supseteq X^{\mathrm{hyp}}_k$ from $s_k$ on, data arrival independent of choices except through $E$. The value of learning $E$ at $t$ is $\sum_{t'}\gamma^{t'-t}\big(\mathbb E[g_{t'}(\pi^E_{t'})]-\mathbb E[g_{t'}(\pi_{t'})]\big)$; for $t'<s_k$ each summand is $\le M\sqrt{\tfrac12 I(\Lambda_{A_{s_k}};E\mid D_{t'})}$ and for $t'\ge s_k$ it is $\le M\sqrt{\tfrac12\,\mathbb E\,H(\Lambda_{A_{>s_k}}\mid X^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{\le s_k},D_t)}$ (conditioning cannot raise entropy *in expectation*; the adversary's caveat that it can for a fixed realisation is right and is why the bound is on the forecast). $\square$ *Why trust is necessary but not sufficient for a small second term*: the term is a $P_t$-quantity, $\mathbb E_{P_t}H_{P_t}(\Lambda\mid X^{\mathrm{hyp}})$; it is small iff the agent's likelihood $P_t(X^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mid\Lambda)$ is sharp *and* the agent's posterior moves with the report. If the agent believes the reports are illegitimate (not accuracy-inducing about $\Lambda$), its likelihood is flat in $\Lambda$ and the term stays at $H_{P_t}(\Lambda)$ — so trust in the v1 §2.9–2.11 sense is necessary. A trusted but modest expert (sharp about little) leaves the term large — so trust is not sufficient. Both halves are [derived] from the definitions.
(d) *A value-certain agent runs the unkind experiment.* Action $a$ is known-forbidden: $V(a)=-K$ for every $\theta$. A permitted action $b$ with cost $c$ and gain $e\in\{0,E\}$, $P(e=E)=\tfrac12$, efficacy shared with $a$ and revealed by executing either; $b$ becomes available $N$ times, all after the decision on $a$. $\mathrm{VOI}^\theta(a)=N\big[\tfrac12(E-c)-\max(0,\tfrac E2-c)\big]$; with $E=4,c=2$ this is $N$. The agent executes $a$ iff $N>K$: at $K=3$, four future uses suffice. No value-coverage quantity appears. Two honest caveats: if $b$ were available *now*, executing $b$ (expected loss $\le c/2$) is the better experiment and $a$ is not chosen — the unkind experiment is chosen only when it is the cheapest *available* informative action; and a Bayes-optimal agent that can wait for $b$ waits unless discounting or the timing of the decisions $b$ informs makes waiting costly. This is Kosoy's trap decision with the "trap" being a known harm accepted for information (S13). [checked arithmetic; `nus_repaired.py` footer for the simulated cousin]

### P4 (for S4) [checked]

(a) $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\bar V(a,\Lambda)]-\bar V(a,\lambda^H)=\sum_\lambda P_t(\lambda)(\bar V(a,\lambda)-\bar V(a,\lambda^H))$; for $\lambda\in\mathcal V^\eta_t$ the bracket is $\le\mathrm{dis}_t(A)$ *only if* $\lambda^H\in\mathcal V^\eta_t$ too — that is the concentration hypothesis $P_t(\lambda^H)\ge\eta$; for $\lambda\notin\mathcal V^\eta_t$ the bracket is $\le M$. ### P5 (for S5) [derived under H1–H4; proof repaired]

(i) As in the develop file: $P_t[X_k\mid X_{\le t}]=\sum_\lambda P^H[X_k\mid\lambda]P_t[\lambda\mid X_{\le t}]$ by (H3); insert $\sum_\lambda P^H[X_k\mid\lambda]P^H[\lambda\mid X_{\le t}]$; the first gap is $\le\mathrm{TV}(P_t(\lambda\mid X_{\le t}),P^H(\lambda\mid X_{\le t}))\le\sqrt{\varepsilon_4/2}$ in expectation (Pinsker, Jensen, H4); the second is $\le\sqrt{\tfrac12 I(X_k;X_{\le t}\mid\Lambda)}=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{med}}/2}$ by P1. $\square$

2. **What does "value coverage" in J3 mean — and is the inequality the right rendering of your sentence?** Your words are objective on both counts: capabilities "capped as a function of how well the agents *actually* understand and are motivated by the common good", and "the value of information of unkind human experimentation is outweighed by the *disutility*" (v2 §1.3).
