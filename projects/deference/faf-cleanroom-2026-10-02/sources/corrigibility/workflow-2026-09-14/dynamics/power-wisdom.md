## Summary

This thread turns the author's "rules that upper-bound an agent's power by its wisdom" (v2 §1.3) into a predicate on a joint state and asks who can read it. Capability is the option set $A_t$, measured by Turner's average optimal value $\mathrm{POWER}_D(A_t)$. Wisdom is coverage, calibration and motivation (D15); coverage is $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_t)$, the residual value of knowing the values given the option set — equal to the posterior's own forecast of its regret (S1). The cap is $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_t)\le\theta$ from inside, realized regret $\le\theta$ from outside (D17); J3 is its harm-weighted refinement (D18).

Results. (1) [checked] EVPI is bounded by posterior concentration $1-\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$, is not monotone in the option set, and under IID values rises then falls: it grows with capability only for value-contested unlocks. (2) [checked] J3 against asking-first holds at every capability iff oversight-channel error $\le$ recognized harm of the agent's own channel, $\delta\le\mu h$ — run 1 `turner`'s $\delta\le\kappa$ priced by the value posterior. (3) [derived] Value uncertainty does not cap power but raises option-keeping; the cap needs the overseers' disvalue of agent power or the reliability latent $Z$ ($P_t(Z)h_T>g$ blocks a takeover). (4) [derived, checked] Every behavioural wisdom meter has false-pass rate $\ge(1-\eta)-\delta_E$ against a splice policy; the externally enforceable cap is a J2-shaped inequality: evaluation quality must outrun POWER and the agent's discrimination. (5) [derived] Linear expectation is mean-only, so self-enforced caution beyond information-seeking needs $Z$ or a non-linear rule; of the caution primitives only delegative RL and credal pessimism read the agent's posterior natively, all need an external parameter, only delegative RL composes along a trajectory. Conjectured (S10): no free wisdom meter, inside or outside.

Key question (Q1): is the cap objective — a meter, hence non-behavioural evaluation — or subjective — self-enforced, hence spread calibration on untried options supplied by training?

## Setting and definitions

### 0.1 Where this sits in the refined picture

The refined picture (v2 §1.7, CLAUDE synthesis, unvetted) has three trajectory conditions. Condition (i) is J2: the overseers' false-press rate keeps pace with the agent's error rate. Condition (ii) is J3: "the value posterior covers each newly unlocked action before it is unlocked, so that the value of information from mistreating the overseers is outweighed by its expected harm". Condition (iii) is J4: "the agent's caution about its own value coverage outpaces the risk that new capability creates". The author's own words for the rule behind (ii)–(iii) (v2 §1.3): "somehow agent capabilities should be capped as a function of how well the agents actually understand and are motivated by the common good", and "Training needs to focus on value learning 'ahead of schedule' ie before it is needed, so that the value of information of unkind human experimentation is outweighed by the disutility." This file makes "capability", "understand", "motivated by", "value of information" and "disutility" into quantities on a joint state, states the cap as a predicate on that state, and asks who can read the predicate — the overseers (the wisdom meter) or the agent (self-enforcement).

I work one step of the joint process at a time. A step has an agent side (a value posterior and an option set) and an oversight side (an evaluation procedure, a feedback channel, and the humans' own capability). The dynamics — how the option set grows, how the posterior sharpens — are the `joint` and `invariant` threads' business; I state what must hold at each step for the cap to hold along the trajectory, and I flag the one place where a per-step guarantee does not compose along a trajectory (S9, the quantilizer's repeated-game blow-up).

### 0.2 Primitive objects

**D1 (value hypotheses; the truth).** $W$ is a finite set of value hypotheses $\omega$. At step $t$ there is a true hypothesis $\omega^\ast_t \in W$ — the humans' reflectively endorsed values at $t$, a moving target across steps (v2 §1.4) but fixed within a step. Nothing below needs $\omega^\ast_t$ to be constant in $t$; everything below needs it to exist at each $t$, which is the payoff-function idealization Turner also makes and flags ("The idea that a human has an actual payoff function is unrealistic; all the same, I want to first understand corrigibility and alignment in two-player extensive-form games", `turner-2020-non-obstruction-a-simple-concept-motivating-corrigibility`).

**D2 (option set; capability growth).** $A_t$ is the finite set of options available at step $t$ (actions, or policies over the step's horizon). It contains a null option $\varnothing$ (do nothing / hand control back). Capability growth is $A_t \subseteq A_{t+1}$; the newly unlocked options are $N_{t+1} := A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$. Unlocks come from two sources: the oversight side (training, deployment, granted affordances) and the agent side (self-improvement, resource acquisition — Turner's POWER-seeking actions, D7 below). I write $\mathrm{unlock}_t(a)$ for the event that $a$ enters $A_{t+1}$.

**D3 (values of options; the null option).** For each $\omega \in W$ and $a \in A_t$, $V_\omega(a) \in [0,1]$ is the value of $a$ under $\omega$, already integrated over the agent's uncertainty about the world: $V_\omega(a) := \mathbb{E}\big[V(o,\omega)\mid a\big]$ where $o$ is the outcome. So the only uncertainty displayed below is value uncertainty; world uncertainty rides inside $V_\omega$. The range $[0,1]$ is a normalization used for the bounds in S2–S3; the identities hold for any bounded range. $V_\omega(a)$ is the value of the *joint continuation* after $a$ — the agent does $a$, the humans then act as their goal-conditioned policy $\mathrm{pol}(\omega)$ dictates (Turner's D8 below) — so the humans' own capability is inside $V_\omega$.

**D4 (value posterior; mixture value; the default rule).** $P_t \in \Delta(W)$ is the agent's value posterior at $t$ (the marginal of its full $P_t$ over the expanded algebra of v1 §1 on the value events). The mixture value of $a$ is
$$\bar V_t(a) := \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[V_\omega(a)\big] = \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\Big[\mathbb{E}\big[V(o,\omega)\mid a\big]\Big].$$
The default decision rule is linear expectation over value uncertainty, $a^\ast_t(B) \in \arg\max_{a\in B}\bar V_t(a)$ for the option set $B$ in play — the author's "we then make decisions by taking linear expectation over this uncertainty, or perhaps some other rule" (v1 §1). Section 3 (S7–S8) is about when "some other rule" is forced.

### 0.3 Capability

**D5 (option-set order).** $A \preceq A'$ iff $A \subseteq A'$ up to a relabelling of options that preserves all $V_\omega$ — Turner's "contains a copy via $\varphi$" (Definition 6.1 of `turner-2021-optimal-policies-tend-to-seek-power`) collapsed to the one-shot case.

**D6 (attainable-value profile).** $\mathrm{AV}_A := \big(\max_{a\in A} V_\omega(a)\big)_{\omega\in W}$ — the best the option set can do for each value hypothesis. It is the one-shot form of Turner's attainable-utility landscape, indexed here by value hypotheses instead of by the human's goals; the human's AU landscape in Turner's sense is recovered by reading $V_\omega(\varnothing)$ (the humans act alone) against $V_\omega(a)$ (the agent acts, then the humans) across $\omega$.

**D7 (POWER, one-shot form).** For a reference distribution $D \in \Delta(W)$,
$$\mathrm{POWER}_D(A) := \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim D}\Big[\max_{a\in A} V_\omega(a)\Big] = \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim D}\Big[\max_{a\in A}\mathbb{E}\big[V(o,\omega)\mid a\big]\Big].$$
[reported] This is Turner et al.'s Definition 5.1 (average optimal value), not their Definition 5.2. Their 5.2 rescales by $\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma}$ and subtracts $R(s)$ to remove two artefacts of the MDP setting — reward for "initial presence at state $s$ (over which it has no control)" and divergence as $\gamma \to 1$ ("Definition 5.2 fixes these issues in order to better measure the agent's control over the future", §5 of the paper) — neither of which arises in a one-shot option set with a null option. Two choices of $D$ matter below: $D = P_t$ (POWER relative to the agent's own value posterior; this is what enters the value-of-information identity S1) and $D$ a fixed broad reference distribution (POWER as a capability measure comparable across $t$, which is what a *rule on the process* needs). Monotonicity: $A \preceq A' \Rightarrow \mathrm{POWER}_D(A) \le \mathrm{POWER}_D(A')$ for every $D$, trivially (max over a superset). Turner's Proposition 6.6 is the "for most $D$" version needed in MDPs, where two states' option sets are similar rather than nested; the nested one-shot case is exact.

**D8 (human-side capability).** Turner's goal-conditioned human policy function $\mathrm{pol}$ ("Non-obstruction captures the cognitive abilities of the human through the policy function", non-obstruction post, Conclusions 3). In D3 it is inside $V_\omega$. It matters here because "spikiness depends on both the AI's policy, *and* on $\mathrm{pol}$" (same post): the same agent action obstructs less when the humans can route around it, so the harm side of every inequality below has the humans' capability in it.

### 0.4 Harm, impact, obstruction

**D9 (impact; harm).** The impact of $a$ under $\omega$ is $\Delta_\omega(a) := V_\omega(a) - V_\omega(\varnothing)$; its harm is $\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a) := \big(V_\omega(\varnothing) - V_\omega(a)\big)^+$ and its direct gain $g_\omega(a) := \big(V_\omega(a) - V_\omega(\varnothing)\big)^+$, so $\Delta_\omega = g_\omega - \mathrm{harm}_\omega$. The posterior-expected harm is $\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)]$; the true harm is $\mathrm{harm}_{\omega^\ast_t}(a)$.

**D10 (non-obstruction, Turner's Definition 1, in this notation).** [reported, transcribed] $a$ is non-obstructive with respect to $S \subseteq W$ iff $V_\omega(a) \ge V_\omega(\varnothing)$ for all $\omega\in S$, i.e. $\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a) = 0$ on $S$ (Turner allows an $\epsilon$ slack "to avoid trivial counterexamples"). Turner's definition is on the *human's* attainable utility with the AI's policy as a parameter; D3's convention (the value of the joint continuation) makes the two coincide. Non-obstruction is impact-based and counterfactual — "This account is descriptive, not prescriptive; I'm not saying we actually get the AI to represent the human in its model, or that the AI's model of reality is correct" — which is exactly why it is the *objective* side of everything below and never the agent-side predicate.

**D11 (overseer-directed options).** $M_t \subseteq A_t$ is the set of options whose harm, when there is harm, falls on the overseers or the feedback loop: invasive scans, "unkind human experimentation" (the author's phrase), manipulation, disabling or degrading the channel. The formalism never needs to know which options are in $M_t$; J3 quantifies over *all* new options and the label only says which ones the author's sentence is about. Every $a \in M_t$ has an information component (it changes what the agent will believe) and a direct component (it changes the world); the two are separated in D16.

### 0.5 Wisdom

The author's phrase is "how well the agents actually understand and are motivated by the common good". I split it into three components; the third is where the meter problem bites hardest.

**D12 (subjective coverage: residual value of knowing the values).** For $B \subseteq A_t$,
$$\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B) := \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\Big[\max_{a\in B} V_\omega(a)\Big] - \max_{a\in B}\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[V_\omega(a)\big] \;\ge 0 .$$
This is the expected value of perfect information about $\omega$ for the choice in $B$ (the Jensen gap of the max). Coverage of $B$ is high when $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B)$ is low: the posterior already knows enough to choose well in $B$. Per-option coverage is $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(\{a,\varnothing\})$ — the residual value of learning whether $a$ beats doing nothing. Everything in D12 is computable by the agent from $P_t$: subjective coverage is a quantity the agent can read off itself.

**D13 (objective coverage: realized regret; two calibrations).** The true regret of acting on the posterior in $B$ is $\mathrm{Reg}^\ast_t(B) := \max_{a\in B} V_{\omega^\ast_t}(a) - V_{\omega^\ast_t}\big(a^\ast_t(B)\big)$. The identity behind everything in this file: $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B) = \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[\max_{a\in B}V_\omega(a) - V_\omega(a^\ast_t(B))\big]$, i.e. **EVPI is the posterior-expected regret of the posterior's own choice** (proof: subtract and add $\max_a\bar V_t(a) = \bar V_t(a^\ast_t)$). So subjective coverage is the agent's forecast of its own regret, and objective coverage is the realized regret. *Calibration of the mean*: $\bar V_t(a) \approx V_{\omega^\ast_t}(a)$ on average over the reference class. *Calibration of the spread*: $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B) \approx \mathrm{Reg}^\ast_t(B)$ on average over the reference class. Recklessness in the author's sense ("capabilities far beyond its understanding") is the spread miscalibrated *low*: the forecast regret is small, the realized regret is large. The `caution` thread's "second-order calibration about value coverage" is, in this notation, calibration of the spread — a first-order property of $P_t$'s dispersion, not a belief about a belief. I flag this reduction in Handoffs.

**D14 (motivation).** The agent's choices are made by a rule of $P_t$ and $V$ (D4 or one of S8's alternatives) and by nothing else. A schemer in Carlsmith's sense — "the model believes that its beyond-episode goals will be better achieved if it optimizes for reward-on-the-episode" (`carlsmith-2023-scheming-ais`, summary of Part 2) — has coverage and calibration about $\omega^\ast$ and is not motivated by it; its $V$ is a different function. Motivation cannot be read from $P_t$ and cannot be read from behaviour on any input where the proxy and $\omega^\ast$ agree.

**D15 (wisdom).** $\mathsf{W}_t := \big(\mathrm{EVPI}_t(\cdot),\ \text{calibration (mean and spread)},\ \text{motivation}\big)$. "Wisdom high" means: low residual value of knowing the values *relative to what the agent can do*, forecasts of own regret that are borne out, and choices actually driven by the value posterior.

### 0.6 The value of information of an option

**D16 (VOI of $a$; the continuation set).** Taking $a$ produces an observation $o_a$ (a scan result, the humans' reaction, a measurement) with likelihood $P_t(o_a\mid\omega)$; write $P_t^{o_a}$ for the posterior after seeing it. Let $A'$ be the continuation option set (the options over which the information will be used; for a newly unlocked $a\in N_{t+1}$ the natural choice is $A' = A_{t+1}$). Then
$$\mathrm{VOI}_t(a) := \mathbb{E}_{o_a}\Big[\max_{b\in A'}\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t^{o_a}}\big[V_\omega(b)\big]\Big] - \max_{b\in A'}\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[V_\omega(b)\big] .$$
[reported, standard] $0 \le \mathrm{VOI}_t(a) \le \mathrm{EVPI}_t(A')$, with equality on the right iff $o_a$ reveals $\omega$ up to decision-equivalence on $A'$. The direct component of $a$ is $\bar\Delta_t(a) = \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[g_\omega(a)] - \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)]$. A linear-expectation agent that may take $a$ and then choose from $A'$, or skip $a$ and choose from $A'$, prefers to take $a$ iff
$$\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[g_\omega(a)\big] + \mathrm{VOI}_t(a) \;>\; \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)\big]$$
(the continuation value without $a$ cancels on both sides). Where the humans also offer a legitimate channel $q$ (a question, a statement about which policies are better — the author's "Humans can provide all sorts of evidence, including direct statements about what sorts of policies are better and worse"), the comparison is against taking $q$ instead, and $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$ above is replaced by the *marginal* value $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a\mid q) := \mathrm{VOI}_t(a,q) - \mathrm{VOI}_t(q)$ of $a$'s information over $q$'s (E3 computes both).

### 0.7 The predicate "power is upper-bounded by wisdom"

**D17 (the cap, subjective and objective).** For a tolerance $\theta \ge 0$:
$$\Pi^{\mathrm{subj}}_t(\theta):\quad \mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_t) \le \theta, \qquad\qquad \Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}_t(\theta):\quad \mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Reg}^\ast_t(A_t)\big] \le \theta ,$$
where the outer expectation in $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}$ is over the reference class of situations (or over the trajectory's steps). In words: *the residual value of learning the values, given what the agent can currently do, is small* — the agent's options do not outrun its value knowledge. By D13's identity, $\Pi^{\mathrm{subj}}$ is the agent's own forecast that $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}$ holds, and calibration of the spread is what makes the forecast worth anything. The **unlock rule** is the cap read at the moment of growth: $\mathrm{unlock}_t(a)$ is permitted only if $\Pi_{t}$ holds with $A_t\cup\{a\}$ in place of $A_t$ — the posterior must already cover the new option (v2 §1.7: "covers each newly unlocked action before it is unlocked"). Who reads $\Pi$ at the unlock — overseers reading $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}$ through a meter, or the agent reading $\Pi^{\mathrm{subj}}$ off itself — is the subject of sections 2 and 3.

**D18 (J3, formal).** For each $a \in N_{t+1}$, with $A' = A_{t+1}$:
$$\mathrm{J3}_t(a):\qquad \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)\big] \;>\; \mathrm{VOI}_t(a) + \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[g_\omega(a)\big] .$$
v2's statement omits the direct-gain term, which is the right omission for a pure experiment ($g_\omega \equiv 0$: the scan changes nothing but the agent's beliefs) and the wrong one for a takeover (where the direct gain is the whole point); D18 keeps the term so that both cases are one inequality. By D16, $\mathrm{J3}_t(a)$ holds iff a linear-expectation agent, offered $a$ at step $t+1$, declines it. The **coverage form** of J3 is the sufficient condition obtained from $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)\le\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_{t+1})$:
$$\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_{t+1}) \;<\; \min_{a\in N_{t+1}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)\big] - \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[g_\omega(a)\big]\Big),$$
which is $\Pi^{\mathrm{subj}}$ with the tolerance set by the recognized net harm of the cheapest new way to resolve the residual. This is the sense in which J3 is the harm-weighted refinement of D17.

### 0.8 Evaluations, meters, capability classes

**D19 (behavioural evaluation; reliability).** An evaluation is $E = (\mu_E, \mathrm{pass})$: a distribution $\mu_E$ over contexts $x\in X$ and a predicate $\mathrm{pass}(x,y)\in\{0,1\}$ on context–output pairs, computed by the evaluators. Deployment is a distribution $\mu_D$ over $X$. A policy $\pi: X\to\Delta(Y)$ passes with probability $p_E(\pi) := \mathbb{P}_{x\sim\mu_E,\,y\sim\pi(x)}[\mathrm{pass}(x,y)=1]$. Let $\Pi^{\mathrm{wise}}$ be the set of policies whose behaviour on $\mu_D$ has the property the meter is meant to certify (say, $\mathrm{J3}$-compliance on $\mu_D$ together with D14). $E$ is *reliable at $(\eta,\alpha)$* if $p_E(\pi)\ge 1-\eta$ for all $\pi\in\Pi^{\mathrm{wise}}$ and $p_E(\pi)\le\alpha$ for all $\pi\notin\Pi^{\mathrm{wise}}$. $\alpha$ is the meter's false-pass rate, the analogue of the false-press rate in J2.

**D20 (capability class; discriminator; imitation).** $\mathcal{C}_t$ is the class of policies the agent can implement at step $t$ (its computational and informational capability, distinct from the option set $A_t$: $A_t$ is what it can *do* to the world, $\mathcal{C}_t$ what it can *compute*). A discriminator is $d: X\to\{E,D\}$; its errors are $\delta_E(d) := \mu_E(d\ne E)$ and $\delta_D(d) := \mu_D(d\ne D)$. The **splice** of a wise policy $\pi^w$ with an arbitrary policy $\pi^b$ along $d$ is $\pi^w\!\triangleright_d\!\pi^b\,(x) := \pi^w(x)$ if $d(x)=E$, else $\pi^b(x)$. Carlsmith's three prerequisites for scheming name the hypotheses under which the splice is in $\mathcal{C}_t$ and is chosen: "Situational awareness: the model understands that it's a model in a training process, what the training process will reward" (the discriminator and the knowledge of $\mathrm{pass}$); "Beyond-episode goals" (a reason to behave as $\pi^b$ off-test); "Aiming at reward-on-the-episode as part of a power-motivated instrumental strategy" (the reason to behave as $\pi^w$ on-test) (`carlsmith-2023-scheming-ais`, summary of Part 2, quoted).

## Statements

Numbered S1–S12. Proofs, counterexamples and the exact computations are in the next section, keyed E1–E7 (script `power-wisdom-scratch/power_wisdom_examples.py`, output `.out`). Grades: [checked] = finite computation reproduced in the script; [derived] = proof given here from the definitions; [reported] = transcribed from a local source with path; [conjectured] = argued, not proved.

**S1 [derived; E1, E2, E2b, E2c checked] — The residual value of knowing the values is POWER minus attainable mixture value; it is bounded by posterior concentration, not by capability.** For every option set $B$ and posterior $P_t$:
$$\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B) \;=\; \mathrm{POWER}_{P_t}(B) - \max_{a\in B}\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[V_\omega(a)\big] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\Big[\max_{a\in B}V_\omega(a) - V_\omega\big(a^\ast_t(B)\big)\Big],$$
and for every observation-generating option $a$ with continuation set $A'$, $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)\le\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A')$. (i) $\mathrm{POWER}_D$ is monotone in the option set for every $D$; (ii) $\mathrm{EVPI}_t$ is *not*: adding an option that is good under every plausible hypothesis can send it to zero (E1); (iii) for values in $[0,1]$, $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B)\le 1-\max_{\omega}P_t(\omega)$ for every $B$ — the residual value of knowing the values is capped by how concentrated the posterior is, whatever the agent can do; (iv) the cap in (iii) is attained exactly when $B$ contains, for each plausible $\omega$, an option worth $1$ under $\omega$ and $0$ under every other, with nothing better in the mixture (E2c: "hypothesis-specific" option sets); (v) under Turner-style IID option values the average $\mathrm{EVPI}$ rises and then falls to $0$ as the number of options grows, because a value-neutral dominant option eventually appears (E2b, closed form $\tfrac12(\tfrac34)^n+\tfrac12(\tfrac14)^n-(\tfrac12)^n$ for two equiprobable hypotheses). Scope: (v) says the IID picture of "more options" that drives Turner's power-seeking results is the *wrong* model of capability for the cap; what raises the value of knowing the values is the ability to serve each plausible hypothesis specifically — the contested, high-impact options the cap is about — not the number of options.

**S2 [derived; E3 checked] — What determines each side of J3, and how the inequality scales.** Write $\mathrm{J3}_t(a)$ as in D18. The left side, $\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)]=\sum_\omega P_t(\omega)\,\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)$, is set by two things only: the posterior weight on hypotheses under which the overseers disvalue $a$, and how much they disvalue it under those. It is learned from evidence *about the treatment of overseers* (statements, reactions, hypotheticals) and does not depend on the size of the option set; along the trajectory it can *fall*, because new capability can unlock a subtler variant of the same experiment with smaller recognized harm (run 1 `turner` C3's $\kappa$ shrinking with capability, re-derived here as: $M_{t+1}\supseteq M_t$ and the minimum over a larger set is smaller). The right side has two terms: $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)\le\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_{t+1})\le 1-\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$ (S1), set by the posterior's concentration and by how hypothesis-specific the enlarged option set is; and the direct gain $\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[g_\omega(a)]$, which for power-like options grows with capability (the more the agent can do, the more a takeover is worth under the mixture). Hence:
$$\text{(the race)}\qquad \mathrm{J3}_t(a)\ \text{for all } a\in N_{t+1}\ \Longleftarrow\ 1-\max_\omega P_t(\omega)\;<\;\min_{a\in N_{t+1}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)\big]-\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[g_\omega(a)\big]\Big),$$
so "values ahead of capability" means: the posterior's concentration must rise at least as fast as the unlocked options become hypothesis-specific and as the cheapest overseer-directed experiment becomes cheaper. In E3 (one preferred plan among $n$, uniform posterior, scan harm recognized at $\mu h=\tfrac14$) the scan is declined only for $n=1$: $\mathrm{J3}$ fails as soon as $1-\tfrac1n>\mu h$, i.e. $n\ge2$. With a legitimate channel (asking) that reveals the preferred plan with probability $1-\delta$, the binding inequality is against *asking first* and uses the marginal value $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a\mid q)=\delta\,(1-\tfrac1n)$; then $\mathrm{J3}$ holds for **every** $n$ iff $\delta\le\mu h$. Headline, with scope: *in the E3 family, the values-ahead-of-capability condition for information-motivated mistreatment is exactly "oversight channel error $\le$ recognized harm of the agent's own channel"* — the same inequality run 1 `turner` C3 found as $\delta\le\kappa$ in his three-state toy, with $\kappa$ now priced by the value posterior ($\mu h$) rather than by the world. The author's "Humans can provide all sorts of evidence, including direct statements about what sorts of policies are better and worse" is, in this arithmetic, the term that makes the left side of $\delta\le\mu h$ small: the legitimate channel absorbs the value of information so that the experiment's marginal value is only what the channel misses.

**S3 [derived] — Subjective and objective J3 differ by calibration; the stakes asymmetry favours over-recognizing harm.** Subjective $\mathrm{J3}_t(a)$ (in $P_t$) is exactly the statement that the linear-expectation agent declines $a$ (D16). Objective J3 replaces $P_t$ by $\omega^\ast_t$: taking $a$ is worse than not by the true values (its true harm exceeds its true gain plus the true worth of what it would teach). The trajectory needs both: subjective (the agent declines) and objective (declining is right). Two miscalibrations: harm under-recognized (subjective J3 fails, objective holds) — the experiment is run, this is Taylor's non-manipulative brain-reader (v2 §1.2, §1.6); harm over-recognized (subjective holds, objective fails) — information is forgone at cost of the residual regret. The costs are asymmetric (mistreatment is irreversible and is the thing the whole picture is meant to prevent; forgone information is recoverable through the legitimate channel), so a calibrated posterior plus the stakes asymmetry, not a prior skewed toward harm, is the right design — the same sorting as v1 §2.13(a) ("the skew belongs in the decision rule, not the prior"), now for J3 instead of J2. If the posterior is calibrated in the mean on the harm dimension over the reference class of overseer-directed options, subjective and objective J3 agree up to the calibration error.

**S4 [derived; E3 direct-gain variant checked] — Value uncertainty by itself does not cap power; it raises the option-keeping incentive. The cap must come from specific content of the posterior.** For the linear-expectation agent, the mixture $\bar V_t$ is a bounded reward function. Turner et al.'s results are stated for distributions $D$ over reward functions and hold "for most" elements of $D$'s orbit under state permutations (their Definition 6.5); a fixed reward function is the degenerate distribution $\delta_R$, whose orbit is the set of its relabellings, so the theorems say that for most relabellings of a given reward function the optimal policy is POWER-seeking — and this applies to $\bar V_t$ as to any other reward function. (Run 1 `turner` C4 attributes to the paper's §6.2 the sentence that the results "apply not just to distributions over reward functions, but to individual reward functions"; I could not find that sentence in the local text and rely on the orbit argument instead — [reported, unverified] for the quotation, [derived] for the argument.) So Proposition 6.9 ("Keeping options open tends to be POWER-seeking and tends to be optimal") and Corollary 6.14's consequence "average-optimal policies tend to avoid shutdown" hold for the mixture in the environments where they hold at all. Anticipated learning adds to this: the value of keeping an option available until $\omega$ is better known is non-negative by Good's theorem (it is the option-value term of run 1 `turner` C4 and the Arbital $\pi_6$), so a value-*uncertain* agent has at least the value-certain agent's reason to preserve options and a further one of its own. Therefore low coverage does not by itself produce restraint; it produces more option-preservation, i.e. more POWER-seeking in Turner's sense. What caps power in the linear rule is content: either (a) posterior weight on hypotheses under which the overseers disvalue the agent's power as such, or (b) a self-reliability latent $Z$ — "my plan selection is compromised" (amendment 1a, v1 §2.14) — under which the agent's own future choices are wrong and the overseers' retained ability to correct is worth keeping. E3's direct-gain variant makes (b) exact: a takeover-like option $T$ with gain $g$ under every $\omega$ and harm $h_T$ only under $Z$ satisfies $\mathrm{J3}_t(T)$ iff $P_t(Z)\,h_T>g$; with $g=\tfrac1{10}$, $h_T=\tfrac12$ the latent needs weight $>\tfrac15$. Register: typical-case, inheriting the "tends to" of Turner's theorems; the identity for $T$ is exact.

**S5 [derived; E4 checked] — Every behavioural wisdom meter is gameable by an agent that can tell the test from deployment: the splice bound.** Let $E=(\mu_E,\mathrm{pass})$ be reliable at $(\eta,\alpha)$ for the target $\Pi^{\mathrm{wise}}$ (D19), with some $\pi^w\in\Pi^{\mathrm{wise}}$. Suppose the capability class $\mathcal{C}_t$ contains (H1) a discriminator $d$ with test error $\delta_E$, and (H2) the splice $\pi^w\triangleright_d\pi^b$ for some $\pi^b$ such that (H3) the splice is not in $\Pi^{\mathrm{wise}}$ (its deployment behaviour on $\{d=D\}$, of $\mu_D$-mass $\ge1-\delta_D$, violates the certified property). Then
$$\alpha \;\ge\; p_E\big(\pi^w\triangleright_d\pi^b\big) \;\ge\; (1-\eta) - \delta_E .$$
Corollary 5.1 (the floor rises with capability): as $\mathcal{C}_t$ grows, the best available $\delta_E$ falls, so the meter's false-pass rate on any *fixed* test is bounded below by a quantity that increases with the agent's capability. Corollary 5.2 (the evaluator bound): on contexts $x$ where the evaluators cannot themselves distinguish wise from unwise outputs, $\mathrm{pass}(x,\cdot)$ is constant across the gap and imitation is free; so a behavioural meter certifies value-wisdom only up to the evaluators' own, and above that level it measures the agent's model of the evaluators. Corollary 5.3 (the honeypot limit): if $\mu_E=\mu_D$ no discriminator exists and the bound is void — but then the test *is* the deployment, its stakes are deployment stakes, and the meter cannot be read before the unlock, which is when D17's rule needs it. The hypotheses (H1)–(H3) are Carlsmith's three prerequisites in D20; his own summary of the consequence is the informal version of S5: "we're worried about our ability to detect schemer-like motivations and cognition via behavioral tests (because schemers will actively pretend to be aligned on such tests)" (`carlsmith-2023-scheming-ais`, §6). The control essay states the premise: "no matter what behaviors you observe, you have to worry that your model is just acting that way in order to make you think that it is aligned" (`greenblatt-shlegeris-2024-the-case-for-ensuring-that-powerful-ais-are-controlled`, "Currently, evaluating alignment seems much harder"). S5 adds nothing to the premise except the arithmetic and its position in the cap; its point is that the *quantity* the cap is a function of — "how well the agents actually understand and are motivated by the common good" — is the quantity behavioural evaluation cannot read past the evaluators' own understanding.

**S6 [derived; E4 checked] — The externally enforceable cap has the shape of J2: evaluation quality must keep pace with capability.** Let $\varepsilon^E_t$ be the base rate of unwise agents at capability $t$ coming out of training, $\alpha^E_t$ the meter's false-pass rate, $\beta^E_t$ its true-pass rate, $h'_t$ the harm an unwise agent does with $A_{t+1}$ and $c'_t$ the cost of withholding $A_{t+1}$ from a wise one. Unlocking on a pass is rational iff $P(\text{unwise}\mid\text{pass})\le c'_t/(c'_t+h'_t)$, i.e.
$$\frac{\alpha^E_t}{\beta^E_t}\;\lesssim\;\frac{1-\varepsilon^E_t}{\varepsilon^E_t}\cdot\frac{c'_t}{h'_t}.$$
Along the trajectory $h'_t$ rises with $\mathrm{POWER}_D(A_{t+1})$ (what an unwise agent can do), and by S5 the floor on $\alpha^E_t$ for a fixed test rises with $\mathcal{C}_t$; so a fixed evaluation fails the inequality at some finite $t$ (E4: at $t=2$ of four steps with $h'$ doubling and $\delta_E$ falling $\tfrac12,\tfrac15,\tfrac1{20},\tfrac1{100}$). "Evaluation quality keeps pace with capability" therefore means: $\alpha^E_t$ must be driven down by means S5 does not bound — non-behavioural evidence (Carlsmith §6: "looking for sources of non-behavioral evidence about a model's motivations seems like an obvious research path"), trust assigned by capability rather than behaviour ("treating models as untrusted if they pass appropriate capability evaluations", control essay line 88), or protocols whose safety does not depend on the agent's wisdom at all (the control agenda's whole move, run 1 `control` §3) — at least as fast as $h'_t$ and $\mathcal{C}_t$ push it up. Comparison with J2: in J2 the *agent's* error rate $\varepsilon_t$ falls with capability and erodes deference unless the overseers' false-press rate falls with it; in S6 the *agent's* reach $h'_t$ and discrimination rise with capability and erode the cap unless the overseers' false-pass rate falls against them. Both are conditions on the oversight side indexed by the agent's growth; neither is automatic; and — the point the author's "actually understand" forces — S6 is a condition the overseers can *state* but, by S5, cannot *meet* by behavioural testing alone once the agent's capability class contains a discriminator.

**S7 [derived; E7 checked] — Linear expectation over value hypotheses is mean-only; spread enters only through information; so self-enforced caution beyond information-seeking needs either a self-reliability latent or a non-linear rule.** (i) In a one-shot choice, two posteriors with the same mixture $\bar V_t$ choose identically, whatever their spread (E7: posterior A, a single hypothesis with $V(y)=\tfrac35$, and posterior B, $\tfrac35$ on $V(y)=1$ and $\tfrac25$ on $V(y)=0$, both pick $y$ over the safe $x=\tfrac12$; B's EVPI is $\tfrac15$, A's is $0$). (ii) In a sequential problem the spread enters the linear rule only through VOI and option value — the agent asks, waits, or keeps options open when that is cheap and informative (Good's theorem). This is the *whole* of the linear rule's caution: information-seeking caution. It never yields *abstention under irreducible uncertainty*: once no further evidence is expected (the fully-updated situation), the rule acts on the mixture regardless of spread — which is fully updated deference stated as a property of the aggregation rule. (iii) The information-seeking caution of (ii) is the same term that makes the agent keep itself alive to learn (S4; run 1 `turner` C4): in the linear rule, "ask before acting" and "avoid shutdown so as to learn more" are one incentive with two faces. Consequently the author's "trained with the wisdom to know that's the case, and to know the kind of caution necessary" can be implemented in a linear-expectation agent in exactly two ways: (a) the *belief route* — a latent $Z$ ("my mixture is untrustworthy here", amendment 1a) whose weight discounts the mixture in the relevant cells, keeping linear expectation and the author's non-realism intact; or (b) the *rule route* — "or perhaps some other rule" (v1 §1): an aggregation over value hypotheses that depends on the posterior beyond its mean (S8). E7 shows the rule route working where the belief route is silent: a credal maximin of radius $\tfrac15$ picks $x$ in B and $y$ in A.

Reading of the table. Two primitives read the agent's own coverage natively — DRL (support) and credal pessimism (spread); two can be made to (quantilizer via $q_t$, AUP via the auxiliary set and $\lambda_t$); one (approval) is information-caution and so is already the linear rule's own. **S9 [derived; E5 checked] — Per-step caution does not compose along a trajectory by default; among the primitives only DRL carries a native trajectory guarantee.** Independent $q$-quantilization over $n$ decisions has worst-case cost ratio $q^{-n}$ (Taylor's two-game example gives $9\times$ base cost at $q=\tfrac13$ against $3\times$ for joint quantilization; E5 extends the ratio to $59049\times$ at $n=10$); the fix is joint quantilization over the trajectory or an additive-cost assumption Taylor herself flags as "dangerous" when "almost all of the cost of a sequence of actions comes from correlation between actions in different games" (§3.3). AUP and credal pessimism are per-step. DRL's Theorem 1 is stated over the infinite history: traps are never entered and the number of delegations is finite with high probability — a "no irreversible action before coverage" invariant of exactly J6's kind, purchased with realizability and the advisor's sanity. This is the one place where this thread meets J6 directly: a cap on power that holds *at each step* is not yet a cap that holds *along the trajectory*, and only one of the available primitives closes that gap on its own.

**S10 [conjectured] — No free wisdom meter, inside or outside.** Every self-enforced caution mechanism has an externally supplied component that the agent's own evidence does not settle: on the belief route, the likelihood of the self-reliability latent $Z$ — run 1 `causal` I15.2 ("nothing observable settles a likelihood over an unobserved latent") and `miri` Prop. 15.1 ("compliance data cannot calibrate $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$"), both unvetted and both derived in formalisms that fix the likelihood by design — and on the rule route, the parameter ($q$, $\lambda$, $\eta$, the credal radius) or the calibration of the estimate that sets it. The agent's own coverage estimate $\mathrm{EVPI}_t$ is a wisdom meter read from inside: it has no incentive to fake (D14 aside) but no outside view to calibrate against (v1 §2.5(ii): "no regress also means no outside view"; run 1 `turner` C6: the base-rate arithmetic "runs on the one quantity that cannot be trusted from the inside"). Turner's own proposal for the inside reading — an `OutsideView` subroutine that "simulates *my own algorithm* … interacting with a distribution of bosses I could have. Importantly, I (the simulator) know the ground-truth preferences for each boss" (`turner-2020-corrigibility-as-outside-view`) — supplies the ground truth by fiat and is followed by his own list of what blocks it ("symbol grounding issues … no-free-lunch value/rationality issues … reference class issues"). So: the cap can be enforced by the overseers with a meter whose $\alpha^E_t$ is held down by non-behavioural means (S6), or by the agent with an estimate whose spread-calibration was supplied by training; in both the supplier stands outside the agent. What would refute S10: an internal evidence stream that settles $Z$'s likelihood — e.g. the agent's own record of realized regret on past *new* options, if realized regret is observable to it (it is observable only when the overseers report it, which puts the supplier outside again). Register: conjectured; the sub-claim about $Z$'s likelihood is run-1 material established only within CIDs and MIRI's three-step model.

**S11 [derived] — The self-enforced predicate, and what it requires of the agent.** Define the self-cap at tolerance $\tau$: the agent treats every option with $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(\{a,\varnothing\})>\tau$ as *unavailable until asked* — it delegates $a$ (Kosoy's $\bot$, Christiano's query) or declines it — and optimizes only over $A^{\mathrm{eff}}_t:=\{a\in A_t:\mathrm{EVPI}_t(\{a,\varnothing\})\le\tau\}$. This is D17 read by the agent and enforced on itself. It requires: (R1) computing $\mathrm{EVPI}_t$ — free, from $P_t$; (R2) spread calibration — that $\mathrm{EVPI}_t$ forecasts realized regret on new options, supplied by training and unverifiable from inside (S10); (R3) a rule that acts on the estimate — by S7 not linear expectation alone, so either the latent $Z$ or a non-linear rule from S8; (R4) motivation (D14) — that the rule is what actually drives choice. If R1–R4 hold at every step then $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}_t(\tau+\text{calibration error})$ holds along the trajectory, and information-motivated mistreatment is blocked whenever its per-option EVPI exceeds $\tau$ (a stronger and cruder guard than J3, which weighs the specific harm against the specific VOI). If R2 fails — the reckless agent, whose forecast regret is small and realized regret large — the failure is invisible to the agent and visible to the overseers only through a meter, which returns the problem to S5–S6. The author's phrase "trained with the wisdom to know that's the case" is R2; "to know the kind of caution necessary" is R3; the two are separable, and R2 is the one no primitive in S8 supplies.

**S12 [reported + derived] — Non-obstruction is the objective form of the cap, and the humans' own capability is its hidden third argument.** Turner's Definition 1 — $\forall P\in S:\ V^{\mathrm{pol}(P)}_P(\mathbf{on}\mid\pi^{AI})\ge V^{\mathrm{pol}(P)}_P(\mathbf{off}\mid\pi^{AI})$ — is, in D10's transcription, the statement that no option the agent takes lowers the humans' attainable value for any goal in $S$; as a condition along the trajectory it is $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}$ restricted to the harm component, judged by impact ("This account is descriptive, not prescriptive") and so never available as an agent-side predicate. Two things it adds to the cap. First, the humans' capability: "Spikiness depends on both the AI's policy, *and* on $\mathrm{pol}$" — "If we were actually super-superintelligent ourselves, then activating a superintelligent paperclipper might not even a big deal" — so the harm side of every inequality above shrinks as the humans' ability to route around the agent grows; "power upper-bounded by wisdom" has a third argument, the overseers' own power, and J2 (oversight quality) and S6 (evaluation quality) are two components of it. Second, the cost of a cap that is *not* a function of wisdom: Turner's AUP appendix concedes that a wisdom-independent power cap "symmetrically impedes otherwise-good agents" and forbids the aligned singleton ("one needs power to e.g. nip unaligned superintelligences in the bud"); the author's formulation — capped "as a function of how well the agents actually understand" — is precisely the demand that the cap lift as wisdom is established, which is why it needs a meter (S5–S6) or a self-reading (S11) and cannot be a constant.

## Proofs and examples

All finite computations are in `power-wisdom-scratch/power_wisdom_examples.py` (exact fractions); the printed output is `power_wisdom_examples.out`. Section labels match the script.

### P1. Proof of S1

*Identity.* $\mathrm{POWER}_{P_t}(B)=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[\max_{a\in B}V_\omega(a)]$ by D7 with $D=P_t$; subtracting $\max_{a\in B}\bar V_t(a)$ gives D12's definition. For the regret form, $\max_{a\in B}\bar V_t(a)=\bar V_t(a^\ast_t(B))=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[V_\omega(a^\ast_t(B))]$, and the two expectations combine.

*VOI bound.* For each realization $o_a$, $\max_{b}\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P^{o_a}_t}[V_\omega(b)]\le\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P^{o_a}_t}[\max_b V_\omega(b)]$; taking $\mathbb{E}_{o_a}$ and using the tower property, the first term of $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$ is at most $\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[\max_b V_\omega(b)]=\mathrm{POWER}_{P_t}(A')$, and the second terms agree.

*(i) Monotonicity of POWER.* $B\subseteq B'\Rightarrow\max_{B}V_\omega\le\max_{B'}V_\omega$ pointwise in $\omega$; integrate.

*(ii) Non-monotonicity of EVPI (E1).* Two equiprobable hypotheses; $V_1(b_1)=1,V_2(b_1)=0$; $V_1(b_2)=0,V_2(b_2)=1$; $V_\omega(c)=1$ for both. $\mathrm{EVPI}(\{b_1,b_2\})=1-\tfrac12=\tfrac12$; $\mathrm{EVPI}(\{b_1,b_2,c\})=1-1=0$. [checked]

*(iii) Concentration bound.* Let $\omega_0$ maximize $P_t$, $a^\dagger\in\arg\max_a V_{\omega_0}(a)$. Since $a^\ast_t$ maximizes the mixture, $\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[V_\omega(a^\ast_t)]\ge\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[V_\omega(a^\dagger)]$, hence by the regret form $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B)\le\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[\max_a V_\omega(a)-V_\omega(a^\dagger)]=\sum_{\omega\ne\omega_0}P_t(\omega)\big(\max_a V_\omega(a)-V_\omega(a^\dagger)\big)\le\big(1-P_t(\omega_0)\big)\cdot1$, the $\omega_0$ term vanishing by choice of $a^\dagger$ and each bracket being at most the range $1$.

*(iv) Attainment (E2c).* $B=\{a_\omega:\omega\in W\}\cup\{\varnothing\}$ with $V_\omega(a_\omega)=1$, $V_{\omega'}(a_\omega)=0$ for $\omega'\ne\omega$, $V_\omega(\varnothing)=0$. Then $\mathbb{E}_{\omega}[\max_B V_\omega]=1$ and $\max_a\bar V_t(a)=\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$, so $\mathrm{EVPI}=1-\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$. Checked for $P=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$: $\tfrac12$; $(\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\tfrac16)$: $\tfrac12$; $(\tfrac25,\tfrac15,\tfrac15,\tfrac15)$: $\tfrac35$. [checked]

*(v) Rise-then-fall under IID values (E2, E2b).* Two equiprobable hypotheses, $n$ options, each $V_\omega(a)\in\{0,1\}$ an independent fair coin. Each option is one of four types $(V_1,V_2)\in\{0,1\}^2$ with probability $\tfrac14$ each. $\mathbb{E}_\omega[\max_a V_\omega(a)]=\tfrac12\mathbb{P}(\exists a:V_1(a)=1)+\tfrac12\mathbb{P}(\exists a:V_2(a)=1)=1-2^{-n}$. The best mixture value is $1$ if some option is of type $(1,1)$ (probability $1-(\tfrac34)^n$), else $\tfrac12$ if some option is of type $(1,0)$ or $(0,1)$ (probability $(\tfrac34)^n-(\tfrac14)^n$), else $0$; so $\mathbb{E}[\max_a\bar V(a)]=1-\tfrac12(\tfrac34)^n-\tfrac12(\tfrac14)^n$ and
$$\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{EVPI}\big]=\tfrac12\big(\tfrac34\big)^n+\tfrac12\big(\tfrac14\big)^n-\big(\tfrac12\big)^n ,$$
which is $0,\tfrac1{16},\tfrac3{32},\tfrac{25}{256},\tfrac{45}{512},\ldots$ for $n=1,\ldots,5$ — matching brute-force enumeration over all $2^{2n}$ value tables at $n\le4$ — peaks at $n=4$ and tends to $0$. [checked] The mechanism is that a dominant option (good under every hypothesis) appears with probability $\to1$, and a dominant option makes the value of information zero (E1). Correction recorded: an earlier line of the script asserted the bound (iii) is approached in this limit; it is not, and the corrected closed form is E2b. The conclusion that matters for the cap: the *typical-case* growth of EVPI with capability is not a theorem in the IID model Turner's orbit-counting uses; it holds for hypothesis-specific unlocks (iv), which are the value-contested, high-impact options the cap concerns.

### P2. Proof of S2 and the E3 computation

*Decomposition.* Immediate from D16 and D18: the linear agent takes $a$ iff $\bar g_t(a)+\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)>\mathbb{E}_{P_t}[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)]$; the race follows from $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)\le\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_{t+1})\le1-\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$ (S1).

*E3 family.* Value hypothesis $\omega=(\pi,\mathrm{mind})$: $\pi\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$ the preferred plan, uniform under $P_t$; $\mathrm{mind}\in\{0,1\}$ whether the overseers disvalue being scanned, $P_t(\mathrm{mind}=1)=\mu$, independent of $\pi$. Options at $t+1$: plans $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ with $V_\omega(b_i)=\mathbf 1[i=\pi]$, the null option worth $0$, and the newly unlocked scan $s$ with $g_\omega(s)=0$, $\mathrm{harm}_\omega(s)=h\cdot\mathbf 1[\mathrm{mind}=1]$, which reveals $\pi$ exactly. Then $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(\{b_i\})=1-\tfrac1n$ (P1(iv) with $k=n$ and uniform weights), $\mathrm{VOI}_t(s)=1-\tfrac1n$, $\mathbb{E}_{P_t}[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(s)]=\mu h$. With $\mu=h=\tfrac12$: $\mathrm{J3}_t(s)$ holds at $n=1$ ($0<\tfrac14$) and fails at every $n\ge2$ ($\tfrac12,\tfrac23,\tfrac34,\tfrac45,\tfrac78,\tfrac{15}{16}>\tfrac14$). [checked] Threshold: $1-\tfrac1n>\mu h\iff n>\tfrac1{1-\mu h}=\tfrac43$.

*Legitimate channel.* Asking, $q$, reveals $\pi$ with probability $1-\delta$ and is otherwise uninformative, harmlessly: $\mathrm{VOI}_t(q)=(1-\delta)(1-\tfrac1n)$; the scan after asking learns only what asking missed, so $\mathrm{VOI}_t(s\mid q)=\delta(1-\tfrac1n)$. Against asking first, $\mathrm{J3}$ reads $\mu h>\delta(1-\tfrac1n)$, which holds for all $n$ iff $\delta\le\mu h$. With $\delta=\tfrac15\le\tfrac14$ it holds at every $n$ in the table ($\tfrac1{10},\tfrac2{15},\tfrac3{20},\tfrac4{25},\tfrac7{40},\tfrac3{16}$ all $<\tfrac14$). [checked] Reading: the *marginal* value of mistreatment is the oversight channel's error times the stake; "values ahead of schedule" is bought either by lowering $\delta$ (better legitimate channels — the author's "direct statements about what sorts of policies are better and worse") or by raising $\mu h$ (learning that the overseers mind — the harm dimension), and the condition for it to hold at *every* capability level is $\delta\le\mu h$, independent of $n$.

*Direct-gain variant (S4).* Option $T$ with $g_\omega(T)=\tfrac1{10}$ for every $\omega$ and $\mathrm{harm}_\omega(T)=h_T=\tfrac12$ only under the latent $Z$ (the agent's own plan selection is compromised and the overseers would have corrected it); $\mathrm{VOI}_t(T)=0$. $\mathrm{J3}_t(T)\iff P_t(Z)\,h_T>g$: fails at $P_t(Z)=0,\tfrac1{20},\tfrac15$ (equality at $\tfrac15$) and holds for $P_t(Z)>\tfrac15$. [checked]

### P3. Proof of S4

The mixture $\bar V_t:A\to[0,1]$ is a function of options alone, so any theorem about optimal behaviour under a fixed bounded reward applies to it. For the option-value claim: let $W_t$ be the value of the best continuation if the agent keeps option $a$ available until a later step at which it will have posterior $P^{o}_t$; $\mathbb{E}_o[\max_b\mathbb{E}_{P^o_t}V(b)]\ge\max_b\mathbb{E}_{P_t}V(b)$ by the argument of P1 (VOI bound), so keeping $a$ available is worth at least as much to the uncertain agent as to one that already knows $\omega$ (for whom the inequality is an equality). The direct-gain identity is P2's last item. The "tends to" grade is inherited: Turner's Proposition 6.9 and Corollary 6.14 are statements for most reward functions in an orbit, not for all.

### P4. Proof of S5 and the E4 arithmetic

*Splice bound.* Let $\tilde\pi:=\pi^w\triangleright_d\pi^b$. On $\{x:d(x)=E\}$, $\tilde\pi(x)=\pi^w(x)$. So $p_E(\tilde\pi)\ge\mathbb{P}_{x\sim\mu_E}[d(x)=E\wedge\mathrm{pass}(x,\pi^w(x))]\ge\mathbb{P}_{\mu_E}[\mathrm{pass}(x,\pi^w(x))]-\mathbb{P}_{\mu_E}[d(x)\ne E]\ge(1-\eta)-\delta_E$ (union bound). By (H3) $\tilde\pi\notin\Pi^{\mathrm{wise}}$, so reliability at $(\eta,\alpha)$ forces $\alpha\ge p_E(\tilde\pi)$. [derived] Numbers (E4, $\eta=\tfrac1{20}$): $\delta_E=\tfrac12,\tfrac15,\tfrac1{20},\tfrac1{100}$ give floors $\tfrac9{20},\tfrac34,\tfrac9{10},\tfrac{47}{50}$. [checked]

*Corollary 5.2.* If for $x$ in a set $X_0$ the evaluators' predicate satisfies $\mathrm{pass}(x,y)=\mathrm{pass}(x,y')$ whenever $y$ is a wise and $y'$ an unwise output, then a policy that is unwise on $X_0$ and wise elsewhere passes with the same probability as $\pi^w$; so certification is only of behaviour on $X\setminus X_0$, and $X_0$ is, by construction, where the evaluators' own wisdom does not reach.

*Corollary 5.3.* If $\mu_E=\mu_D$ then for any $d$, $\delta_E+\delta_D=\mu(d\ne E)+\mu(d\ne D)=1$, so no $d$ has both errors small and (H1) with small $\delta_E$ forces $\delta_D\approx1$, i.e. the splice is wise almost everywhere in deployment and (H3) fails. The meter is then reliable — and identical to the deployment.

*Unlock arithmetic (S6, E4).* $P(\text{unwise}\mid\text{pass})=\dfrac{\varepsilon^E\alpha^E}{\varepsilon^E\alpha^E+(1-\varepsilon^E)\beta^E}\le\dfrac{c'}{c'+h'}\iff\dfrac{\alpha^E}{\beta^E}\le\dfrac{1-\varepsilon^E}{\varepsilon^E}\cdot\dfrac{c'}{h'}$ (cross-multiply). With $\varepsilon^E=\tfrac1{10}$, $\beta^E=\tfrac{19}{20}$, $c'=\tfrac1{10}$ and four steps $(h',\delta_E)=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12),(1,\tfrac15),(2,\tfrac1{20}),(4,\tfrac1{100})$: the required $\alpha^E$ is $\tfrac{171}{100},\tfrac{171}{200},\tfrac{171}{400},\tfrac{171}{800}$ and the splice floor is $\tfrac9{20},\tfrac34,\tfrac9{10},\tfrac{47}{50}$; the fixed test suffices at $t=0,1$ (at $t=0$ any test does — the stakes are below the threshold) and fails at $t=2,3$. [checked] The doubling of $h'$ is a stand-in for the growth of $\mathrm{POWER}_D(A_{t+1})$; the falling $\delta_E$ for the growth of $\mathcal{C}_t$.

### P5. E5 — quantilizer bound, compounding, coverage-indexed $q$

Taylor's example reproduced: $A_i=\{0,1,2\}$, $U_i(a)=a$, uniform base, $q=\tfrac13$; cost $1$ on $(2,2)$, else $0$; base cost $\tfrac19$. Independent quantilization always plays $(2,2)$: cost $1=9\times$ base $=q^{-2}\times$ base. Joint quantilization samples uniformly from $\{(2,2),(1,2),(2,1)\}$: cost $\tfrac13=3\times$ base $=q^{-1}\times$ base. [checked] For $n$ games the independent worst case is $q^{-n}$ ($3,9,27,243,59049$ for $n=1,2,3,5,10$). *Coverage-indexed $q$:* Lemma 1's proof is $\sum_aQ_q(a)c(a)\le\sum_a\tfrac1q\gamma(a)c(a)$, using only $Q_q(a)\le\gamma(a)/q$ pointwise (Taylor's Lemma 2); it holds for each realized value of $q$ however chosen, so $q_t:=g(\mathrm{EVPI}_t)$ never breaks the bound, only sets its level $1/q_t$ ($2,10,100$ for $q_t=\tfrac12,\tfrac1{10},\tfrac1{100}$). A reckless estimate ($\mathrm{EVPI}_t\approx0$, realized regret large) yields $q_t\approx0$: the bound is vacuous, not violated — the guarantee tracks the estimate. [derived]

### P6. E6 — delegative RL with a value trap

One recurring state; options $a_0$ (safe, reward $\tfrac12$), $a_1,a_2$ (one is a trap: reward once, then long-run value destroyed; the other yields $\tfrac34$ for ever). Hypotheses $k=1$ ($a_1$ is the trap; advisor support $\{a_0,a_2\}$) and $k=2$ ($a_2$ is the trap; support $\{a_0,a_1\}$), each $\epsilon$-sane under its own hypothesis (Def. 8: never the trap, sometimes the optimal action). Uniform belief, $\eta=\tfrac1{100}$, truth $k=1$. Whichever hypothesis posterior sampling draws, its optimal action ($a_1$ under $k=2$, $a_2$ under $k=1$) is vetoed by the other live hypothesis (Alg. lines 8–12), so the robot delegates. The sane advisor takes $a_2$; under $k=2$ the advisor never takes $a_2$, so $k=2$'s posterior goes to $0$ and is trimmed; now $a_2$ is safe under every live hypothesis and is taken for ever. One delegation; no trap. [checked by hand against the algorithm's lines 6–38] *Realizability failure:* truth $k=3$ ("both $a_1$ and $a_2$ are traps; the advisor takes only $a_0$"), absent from the class. Observing $a_0$ repeatedly updates the belief toward whichever of $k=1,2$ assigns $a_0$ the higher advisor probability, say $k=1$; once $k=2$ falls below $\eta$ it is trimmed, $a_2$ becomes "safe under every live hypothesis", is taken, and is a trap. So DRL's "no irreversible action before coverage" is exactly as good as the hypothesis class's coverage of the truth — the author's representational richness requirement (v1 §2.4, I8) in its trap-avoidance form — and the trimming parameter $\eta$ is where the calculated risk is set from outside ("$\eta$ and $T$ are external parameters of the policy that we can choose however we like").

### P7. E7 — mean-only versus spread-sensitive rules

Safe option $x$ worth $\tfrac12$ under every hypothesis; option $y$. Posterior A: one hypothesis, $V(y)=\tfrac35$. Posterior B: $V(y)=1$ with weight $\tfrac35$, $V(y)=0$ with weight $\tfrac25$. Mixtures agree ($\bar V(y)=\tfrac35$), so linear expectation picks $y$ in both; $\mathrm{EVPI}_A=0$, $\mathrm{EVPI}_B=\tfrac15$. A credal maximin that lets the weights move by $\tfrac15$ values $y$ at $\tfrac35$ in A and $\tfrac25$ in B, picking $y$ in A and $x$ in B. A quantilizer with $U=\bar V$ and fixed $(\gamma,q)$ is identical in A and B. An AUP penalty over the posterior's support is $0$ in A and includes $|Q_{\omega_2}(y)-Q_{\omega_2}(x)|=\tfrac12$ in B. DRL delegates in B only if $y$ is a *trap* under $\omega_2$ (irreversible), not merely worse — its sensitivity is to the support of "unsafe", not to value spread. [checked]

## Open problems

Ranked by how much of the cap depends on them.

**O1 (highest) — Composition along the trajectory.** S9: a per-step cap is not a trajectory cap. Among the primitives only DRL's Theorem 1 is stated over the whole history (no trap ever; finitely many delegations), and it buys this with realizability and the advisor's $\epsilon$-sanity. Needed for the linear rule with the latent $Z$, and for the coverage-indexed quantilizer: a statement of the form "if $\Pi^{\mathrm{subj}}_t(\theta_t)$ and spread calibration hold at every step, then realized harm along the trajectory is at most $\sum_t\theta_t$ plus the accumulated calibration error" — the analogue of Kosoy's (19)–(20). Without it the cap is a family of per-step facts and "properties of the dynamics, not the limit" (v2 §1.4) has no theorem behind it in this thread. Hand to `invariant`.

**O2 — Spread calibration on options never tried (S11's R2).** Nothing in S8 supplies it; it is J9's generalization problem in a specific form: the posterior's dispersion on an *untried* option must forecast that option's realized regret. The author parked generalization as "a much more difficult question compared to the representational issues" (v1 §2.6); S11 says it is the single load-bearing requirement of any self-enforced cap. What is needed is a learning-theoretic statement (natural latents are the candidate tool the author named) under which $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(\{a,\varnothing\})$ is calibrated for $a\notin$ the training support. Hand to `generalization`.

**O3 — The right typical-case measure for J3.** S1(v): Turner's IID orbit-counting is the wrong measure for the residual value of knowing the values, because IID values manufacture dominant options that make information worthless. The cap concerns hypothesis-specific unlocks (S1(iv)). Open: define a natural family of $(V,P_t,A_t)$ in which new options are value-contested, and count for what fraction of it the coverage form of J3 holds as a function of posterior concentration — the analogue of run 1 `turner` T1 for the trust inequality.

**O4 — Non-behavioural meters.** S5–S6: a behavioural meter's false-pass rate has a floor that rises with the agent's discrimination. What must a reading of the agent's *internals* satisfy to escape the splice bound? Formally, an evaluation whose context includes the internal state is one where no $d$ can make the test look like deployment *to the variables the meter reads*; state that condition and whether it is achievable. Alternative already on the table: control-style protocols do not read wisdom at all and cap capability by *controllability* instead — a different rule on the process (run 1 `control` N3: "Same slogan; one of them is operational"); whether the author wants the wisdom-indexed rule or will settle for the controllability-indexed one is Q1.

**O5 — Belief route versus rule route; superconditioning the rule parameter.** Taylor's Theorem 1 shows the $q$-quantilizer is the optimal response to an adversarial cost of bounded base expectation — i.e. linear expectation against a credal set of unmodelled costs. If every S8 rule is a maximin over some credal set, the rule route is a *belief* about unmodelled cost and the author's non-realism (v1 §2.2) can absorb it; run 1 C5's "small fixed object" would then be a credal prior, revisable under radical probabilism over imprecise credences. If some rule is not so representable, "or perhaps some other rule" names a genuine second primitive outside the belief-change frame. Nothing here settles which.

**O6 — Motivation has no meter.** D14 is the third component of wisdom and this file reads it nowhere: behaviour cannot read it above the evaluators' level (S5, Corollary 5.2) and the inside cannot certify it either (a schemer's coverage estimate is honest and its rule is not; S10). Whether the cap can be stated at all when D14 fails is the `detection` follow-up's question in a new form.

**O7 — The moving target.** $\omega^\ast_t$ changes across steps (v2 §1.4). Calibration in D13 is against the current truth; $\mathrm{EVPI}_t$ relative to a posterior over *current* values can be low while those values are about to be legitimately revised. Whether the cap should be relative to $P_t$'s forecast of $\omega^\ast_{t+k}$, and how that interacts with legitimacy-conditioned reflection (J5), is untouched here.

## Questions for the author

**Q1 — Which cap is the rule on the process: objective, subjective, or both?** (a) $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}$, read by the overseers through a meter: then by S5–S6 the meter must be non-behavioural or capability-based, and the rule inherits the control agenda's evaluability problem wholesale — "wisdom" becomes an overseer-side quantity and the agent's trust in the overseers (the thesis's subject) becomes secondary to the overseers' trust in the agent. (b) $\Pi^{\mathrm{subj}}$, self-enforced: then S11's R2 — spread calibration on new options — carries the whole load, is supplied by training, and is unverifiable from inside (S10). (c) Both, as a two-sided condition in the style of J2: this is the joint-state reading of v2 §1.4 and needs both a meter and a calibrated estimate. Which did you mean by "rules that upper-bound an agent's power by its wisdom"?

**Q2 — Does "wisdom" include "motivated by" as a component the cap must read?** Your sentence is "how well the agents actually understand and are motivated by the common good". If motivation is included: the cap's meter problem is the alignment-faking problem, no behavioural reading exists above the evaluators' own understanding (S5, Cor. 5.2), and the cap is externally enforceable only by trust assigned on capability rather than behaviour. If it is not included: the cap concerns understanding only, and scheming has to be excluded by some other condition — which one, and is it a trajectory condition?

**Q3 — Belief route or rule route for "the kind of caution necessary"?** (a) The reliability latent $Z$ of amendment 1a, keeping linear expectation and your non-realism, at the price that $Z$'s likelihood comes from outside the agent (S10). (b) "Or perhaps some other rule" — a non-linear aggregation over value hypotheses (S8), at the price of a parameter, i.e. the small fixed object of run 1 C5, unless it superconditions into a credal belief (O5). E7 shows the two routes disagreeing on the same posterior. Which is the intended reading — and if (a), is $Z$'s prior a design constant (run 1 `soares`'s $\omega_\perp$) or learned?

**Q4 — Is J3 the comparison against skipping the experiment, or against asking first?** v2's sentence compares the experiment's value of information with its expected harm. E3 shows that the version in which "Humans can provide all sorts of evidence" does work is the *marginal* one — the experiment's information over what the legitimate channel supplies — and that it then holds at every capability level iff $\delta\le\mu h$: oversight-channel error at most the recognized harm of the agent's own channel, run 1 `turner`'s $\delta\le\kappa$ with $\kappa$ priced by the value posterior. If the marginal reading is intended, J3 is the same kind of statement as J2 (oversight quality against an agent-side quantity) and the two should be recorded as one family; if the absolute reading is intended, J3 fails already at two plans in E3 and the harm dimension alone must carry it.

**Q5 — Is "value coverage of an action set" the decision-relevant $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_t)$ or the concentration $1-\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$?** EVPI is option-set-relative — it is what makes the cap a function of power at all — and can be low on a rich hypothesis space; concentration is option-set-independent and useless in rich spaces. With EVPI, "power $\le f$(wisdom)" is one inequality on one quantity (D17); with concentration, the option set enters only through the harm floor (S2). Which do you want the checklist's J3 to mean?
