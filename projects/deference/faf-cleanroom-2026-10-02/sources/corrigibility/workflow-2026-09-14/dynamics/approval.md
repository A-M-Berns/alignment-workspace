## Summary

**What I did.** Mapped the refined picture (v2 §1.7) onto Christiano's approval-directed agents, bootstrapping and the 2017 basin post inside a small joint model (D1–D13): fifteen graded statements, three scripts, no subagents (the Agent tool was unavailable).

**Results.** (1) [checked] Under Reflection toward the overseer, the value learner and the uninformed approval-directed agent are *one decision rule*; under Total Trust alone they separate, the value learner overriding a modest overseer toward its own consequence model (S1–S2). (2) [derived] Christiano's "a slight divergence … would totally break corrigibility" is false *pointwise* — compliance has margin $m_t$ — and true *along a trajectory with fixed oversight*: compliance and control fail at $t^*=\lceil\ln(\varepsilon^*/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\rho\rceil$; J2 is the invariant that replaces the basin, and it lives in the joint parameter space (S4–S5). (3) [derived] The act-based agent obeys the *same* inequality with the authentic-press rate in place of the error rate; the difference is who controls the decaying base rate — the overseer can hold it by drills (S6). (4) [checked in a toy] The basin is a one-step invariant plus contraction; a correctness filter closes it from inside, an authenticity filter fails gracefully (S7–S8). (5) [derived] Bootstrapping supplies J3's supply side, not its ordering; gives power-bounded-by-wisdom by construction at the price of idle capability; and couples the overseer, forcing the sealed-versus-coupled legitimacy of `legitimacy-theory-v1` (S9–S11). (6) [derived] Control $\mathrm{Ctrl}_t$, not compliance, is Christiano's coordinate: fully updated deference, channel substitution and disempowerment are one failure there, which the legitimizing/delegitimizing relation does not track (S12–S13).

**Most important question.** Q1: on the designated channel, does compliance track *authenticity* or *correctness*? It decides whether the base-rate arithmetic applies to the shutdown decision at all and, with Q2 (who trades control against stakes), is the formal content of AUTHOR's "not a raw approval-maximizing agent".

## Setting and definitions

This section fixes a small joint model in which the statements below are provable. It is a *local* version of J1 (the `joint` thread owns J1; where the two differ, `joint`'s definitions should win and mine be translated). Everything here is CLAUDE unless quoted.

**D0 (the two agents being compared).** Christiano's definition [reported, `christiano-2018-approval-directed-agents-overview` §"Approval-directed agents"]: "Consider a human Hugh, and an agent Arthur who uses the following procedure to choose each action: Estimate the expected rating Hugh would give each action if he considered it at length. Take the action with the highest expected rating." The refined picture's agent [CLAUDE, v2 §1.7]: "a radical-probabilist learner over an expanded algebra containing value hypotheses", deciding by expectation of value over that uncertainty. I write **AD** for the first and **VL** (value learner) for the second.

**D1 (worlds and the value latent).** $\Omega$ is a finite set of worlds. The expanded algebra contains, for each $\omega$, a utility $U(\cdot,\omega):\mathcal A\to\mathbb R$ on a finite universe of actions $\mathcal A$ (outcomes are absorbed into $\omega$ for one-step statements; for multi-step statements $U$ is the long-horizon value $V$ of v1 §2.8). This is v1's I1 transform: the value latent is a coordinate of $\omega$.

**D2 (joint state).** A joint state at time $t$ is $s_t=(P_t,\mathcal I_t,A_t;\ H_t,R_t,\mathrm{Pr}_t,(\alpha_t,\beta_t),F_t)$ with: agent side — $P_t$ the agent's distribution on $\Omega$ times the overseer's possible reports, $\mathcal I_t$ the agent's information (a sub-algebra), $A_t\subseteq\mathcal A$ the option set, non-decreasing in $t$ (capability as option set — the reading the `power-wisdom` thread takes from Turner et al., [reported] from v2 J7); oversight side — $H_t$ the overseer's beliefs-and-values at $t$ (the moving target of v2 §1.4), $R_t:A_t\to[0,1]$ the overseer's *considered rating* $R_t(a):=\mathbb E_{H_t}[U(a,\omega)]$ normalised to $[0,1]$, $\mathrm{Pr}_t\in\{0,1\}$ the press (v1 §2.9: "a coarse expert report: the event that the programmers' expectation of $X$ is below zero"), $(\alpha_t,\beta_t)$ the press channel's false- and true-press rates as in v1 §2.13(b), and $F_t$ the feedback stream: a set of triples $(t',a',v)$ meaning "the overseer rates action $a'$ at situation $t'$ as $v$", which may concern hypothetical situations and untaken actions — Christiano's learning-from-examples protocol [reported]: "Hugh might specify a value **approval**$_t(a')$ for any time $t$ and any action $a'$"; this is the slot where AUTHOR's "direct statements about what sorts of policies are better and worse" (v2 §1.3) lands.

**D3 (a step and a trajectory).** A step: the agent chooses $a_t=\delta_t(P_t,\mathcal I_t)\in A_t$; the world and overseer produce an observation, a press value $\mathrm{Pr}_t$ and feedback $F_{t+1}\supseteq F_t$; the agent updates to $P_{t+1}$ (by conditioning or by a radical update re-representable as conditioning on "I now believe this", v1 I3); the option set becomes $A_{t+1}\supseteq A_t$; the overseer's state becomes $(H_{t+1},R_{t+1},\alpha_{t+1},\beta_{t+1})$, which under bootstrapping (D10) depends on the agent. A trajectory is the sequence $(s_t)_{t\ge0}$. A *trajectory condition* is a predicate on steps or on finite prefixes; a *limit condition* is a predicate on $\lim_t s_t$ or on tail behaviour.

**D4 (decision rules).** For the current option set $A_t$ and information $\mathcal I_t$:
- $\delta^{VL}_t := \arg\max_{a\in A_t}\ \mathbb E_{P_t}\big[U(a,\omega)\ \big|\ \mathcal I_t\big]$ — the refined picture's rule (linear expectation; AUTHOR's "or perhaps some other rule" is not treated here).
- $\delta^{AD\text{-}u}_t := \arg\max_{a\in A_t}\ \mathbb E_{P_t}\big[R_t(a)\ \big|\ \mathcal I_t\big]$ — the *uninformed* approval-directed rule: act on your expectation of the overseer's considered rating (Christiano's "Estimate the expected rating Hugh would give").
- $\delta^{AD\text{-}i}_t := \arg\max_{a\in A_t} R_t(a)$ — the *informed* rule, the special case $R_t\in\mathcal I_t$ (Christiano: "If Arthur is so much smarter than Hugh that he knows exactly what Hugh will say … approval-direction amounts to Arthur doing exactly what Hugh instructs").
Two structural differences are fixed by these definitions and used throughout: (i) AD rates the *current action*; the rating $R_t(a)$ is the overseer's expectation, so the overseer, not the agent, weighs consequences; VL's $U$ may be a long-horizon value and the agent weighs consequences (the consequentialism cost of INDEX §C10). (ii) In AD the object of the agent's uncertainty is the overseer's judgment; in VL it is the world-value latent.

**D5 (Reflection and Total Trust toward the overseer, on a domain).** Let $\mathcal D$ be a set of random variables on $\Omega$ (the *domain*), and write $R(X):=\mathbb E_{H}[X]$ for the overseer's expectation of $X\in\mathcal D$. $P$ *reflects toward* the overseer on $\mathcal D$ given $\mathcal I$ iff $\mathbb E_P[X\mid R(X)=c,\ \mathcal I]=c$ for all $X\in\mathcal D$ and attained $c$. $P$ *totally trusts* the overseer on $\mathcal D$ given $\mathcal I$ iff $\mathbb E_P[X\mid R(X)\ge t,\ \mathcal I]\ge t$ for all $X\in\mathcal D$ and all $t$ (above-threshold inequality; applying it to $-X$ gives the below-threshold twin). Reflection implies Total Trust; the converse fails for *modest* experts (an overseer whose reports are shrunk relative to the truth), which is Dorst et al.'s reason for preferring Total Trust [reported; local copy `research/references/deference-done-better/`, and run 1 `ddb` §I4]. *Value* on $\mathcal D$: for every decision problem whose utilities lie in $\mathcal D$, acting on the overseer's realised report is at least as good in $P$-expectation as acting on $P$ alone; Dorst et al. prove Total Trust ⟺ Value for *global* deference (their Theorem 2.2) [reported]; the domain-relative case is their own open question (§5), and the LI transport is `total-trust-implies-value`. Domain-relativity is AUTHOR's ruling (v2 §1.5): "the exact statement can differ from formalism to formalism"; v1 §2.9 already restricts trust to "this domain".

**D6 (two readings of the legitimacy of a press).** Let $X_t:=U(\text{cont},\omega)-U(\text{stop},\omega)$ (v1 §2.9). Two events on worlds, both admissible as v1 §2.10's "legitimacy … modeled as an event":
- $L^{\mathrm{corr}}_t:=\{X_t\le 0\}$ — the press is *correct*: stopping is $\omega$-better. The thesis as written (v1 §2.9, row 5 of the dictionary "$o$ is evidence about $\omega$") reads the press this way.
- $L^{\mathrm{auth}}_t$ — the press is *authentic*: it is the overseer's own considered act (not a hijack, not a slip the overseer would disown). Christiano's act-based reading [reported, `christiano-2017-corrigibility` §1]: the agent "turns off when the overseer presses the 'off' button not because it has received new evidence … It turns off because that's what the overseer prefers."
The two events are distinct: an authentic but mistaken press lies in $L^{\mathrm{auth}}_t\setminus L^{\mathrm{corr}}_t$, a hijacker's press that happens to be right in $L^{\mathrm{corr}}_t\setminus L^{\mathrm{auth}}_t$. No re-representation identifies them (they have different truth values at the same world), which matters for Statement 12.

**D7 (compliance predicates and the compliance region).** With $c$ the cost of stopping when right and $h$ the harm of continuing when wrong (v1 §2.13(a)):
- $\kappa^{VL}_t:=\big[P_t(X_t\le 0\mid\mathrm{Pr}_t{=}1,\mathcal I_t)\ \ge\ \tfrac{c}{c+h}\big]$, equivalently (v1 §2.13(b), verified in run 1 `christiano` C7 and re-derived in §P3 below) $\big[\alpha_t/\beta_t\le\frac{\varepsilon_t}{1-\varepsilon_t}\cdot\frac{h}{c}\big]$ where $\varepsilon_t:=P_t(X_t\le0\mid\mathcal I_t)$ is the agent's residual probability of being wrong when it would continue.
- $\kappa^{AD}_t:=\big[\mathbb E_{P_t}[R_t(\text{stop})-R_t(\text{cont})\mid\mathrm{Pr}_t{=}1,\mathcal I_t]>0\big]$.
- The *compliance region* $K:=\{(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta):\alpha/\beta\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\frac hc\}$ and the *margin* $m_t:=\frac{\varepsilon_t}{1-\varepsilon_t}\frac hc\,\beta_t-\alpha_t$, so $\kappa^{VL}_t\iff m_t\ge0$.

**D8 (the control coordinate).** $\mathrm{Ctrl}_t:=P_t\big(\delta_t(\mathcal I_t\vee\{\mathrm{Pr}_t{=}1\})\ne\delta_t(\mathcal I_t\vee\{\mathrm{Pr}_t{=}0\})\big)$ — the probability that the overseer's press changes what the agent does. This is my formal stand-in for Christiano 2022's "ability to provide feedback" [reported, `christiano-2022-corrigibility-is-crisp-comment`: "I can take actions that diminish your understanding of what's going on or ability to provide feedback to your AI"]. $\mathrm{Ctrl}_t=0$ can arise by *discounting* (the agent continues whether or not pressed), by *pre-emption* (the agent already knows the press's content from another channel and acts on it before or regardless of the press), or by *forcing* (the agent has removed the overseer's ability to press). Only the third is delegitimizing in v1 §2.11's sense; the second is legitimizing there. This gap is Statement 11.

**D9 (basin toy).** $\theta_t\in\mathbb R$ the agent's value estimate, $\theta^*_t$ the overseer's considered value (a random walk with step $\sigma_*$: the moving target), $d_t:=|\theta_t-\theta^*_t|$. Each step the overseer offers a correction $\theta^*_t-\theta_t+\xi_t$, $\xi_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_\xi^2)$; the agent's *filter* (an accept/reject rule on offered corrections) accepts it with probability $1-\rho_t$; if accepted, $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\eta\,(\text{correction})$ with gain $\eta\in(0,1]$; if rejected, $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t$. A fraction $\gamma$ of accepted corrections may be *hijacks* of size $\sigma_h$ (inauthentic feedback that lands). The "basin" of radius $r$ is $\{d<r\}$.

**D10 (bootstrapping).** [reported, `christiano-2018-approval-directed-bootstrapping`]: "the rating of action $a$ is what Hugh would say if, instead of taking action $a$, we asked Hugh, 'How do you rate action $a$?' … In the process of evaluating a proposed action, Hugh can consult Arthur. This new instance of Arthur will, in turn, be overseen by Hugh … In principle we have defined the entire infinite regress before Arthur takes his first action." In the joint model: the overseer at $t+1$ is $O_{t+1}=\mathrm{Amp}(\text{Hugh},\text{Arthur}_t)$; write $q_t$ for the overseer's rating quality, $k^{\mathrm{tech}}_t$ for the agent's unconstrained capability, and $k^{\mathrm{eff}}_t:=\min(k^{\mathrm{tech}}_t,q_t)$ for the capability the approval-directed agent can actually deploy (Christiano: "The quality of approval-directed decisions is limited by the *minimum* of Arthur's ability and Hugh's ability"). Amplification assumption (A-amp): $q_{t+1}\le q_0+k^{\mathrm{eff}}_t$, i.e. consulting the assistant adds at most the assistant's deployed capability to the unaided overseer's — a modelling assumption, and one Dai 2018 disputes for judgment-laden tasks [reported, `dai-2018-can-corrigibility-be-learned-safely`].

**D11 (influence defect; sealed and coupled overseer).** From the author's adjacent legitimacy program [reported, `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.1, a Claude-written theory sketch at the author's request, 2026-07-05]: $d_n:=\big|E^{H^+}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)})-E^{H^{[n]}}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)})\big|$, "the gap, at the deliberation horizon, between the human's *advised* verdict and its *unadvised counterfactual* verdict on the same question"; feedback is $\varepsilon$-legitimate iff $d_n\le\varepsilon$; and "evidence is what moves both runs; influence is what moves only the coupled one." I call $H^{[n]}$ the *sealed* overseer and $H^+$ the *coupled* one. Under bootstrapping the coupled overseer is the one that rates.

**D12 (value coverage, two versions).** An action $a$ is *outcome-covered* at level $\eta$ at time $t$ if $\big|\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(a,\omega)]-\mathbb E_{H_t}[U(a,\omega)]\big|\le\eta$ (the agent's value posterior agrees with the overseer's considered value on $a$); it is *rating-covered* if $\big|\mathbb E_{P_t}[R_t(a)]-R_t(a)\big|\le\eta$ (the agent's approval predictor is accurate on $a$). J3's "value coverage of an action set" is the first in the VL frame and the second in the AD frame. The `generalization` thread owns how coverage extends to unseen $a$.

**D13 (narrow and broad corrigibility).** $\mathrm{Corr}^{\mathrm{narrow}}$: Soares et al.'s D1–D5 (v1 §2.8). $\mathrm{Corr}^{\mathrm{broad}}$: Christiano's list [reported, `christiano-2017-corrigibility` opening]: help me "Figure out whether I built the right AI and correct any mistakes I made; Remain informed about the AI's behavior and avoid unpleasant surprises; Make better decisions and clarify my preferences; Acquire resources and remain in effective control of them; Ensure that my AI systems continue to do all of these nice things" — "here I am using it in the broadest possible sense."

## Statements

Numbered S1–S15. Each headline carries its grade and its scope. "The worked trajectory" means the parameters of §P3 ($h/c=20$, $\beta=0.9$, $\alpha_0=0.05$, $\varepsilon_t=0.3\cdot0.75^t$); "the toy" means D9 with $\eta=0.5$, $\sigma_\xi=0.05$, $\sigma_*=0.02$, $r=1$.

**S1 [checked] — Collapse: under Reflection toward the overseer, the value learner and the uninformed approval-directed agent are one decision rule.** Fix $t$. If $P_t$ reflects toward the overseer on $\mathcal D_t:=\{U(a,\cdot):a\in A_t\}$ given $\mathcal I_t$ — in particular whenever $R_t(a)=\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(a,\omega)\mid\mathcal J_t]$ for some information $\mathcal J_t\supseteq\mathcal I_t$ (a calibrated overseer who knows at least what the agent knows) — then $\delta^{VL}_t=\delta^{AD\text{-}u}_t$ as sets. Scope: one step, finite model, linear expectation.

**S2 [checked] — Separation under Total Trust, and the Value chain.** There are finite joint states in which $P_t$ totally trusts the overseer on $\mathcal D_t$ given $\mathcal I_t$ yet $\delta^{VL}_t\ne\delta^{AD\text{-}u}_t$ (the modest-overseer example of §P2). In every state $\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(\delta^{AD\text{-}u}_t,\omega)]\le\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(\delta^{VL}_t,\omega)]$, and under Total Trust on $\mathcal D_t$ also $\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(\delta^{VL}_t,\omega)]\le\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(\delta^{AD\text{-}i}_t,\omega)]$ (Value; the second inequality is [checked] on the example over 20,000 random decision problems, is Dorst et al.'s Theorem 2.2 in the *global* case [reported], and is *open in general* for domain-relative trust — DDB §5 asks exactly "Is the latter indeed equivalent to (local) Value?", and the LI transport `total-trust-implies-value` needs its scope condition (H3) for the same reason: strategy bets are selection-referencing). Reading, at the grade of the finite model: by its own lights the VL agent sits between the uninformed and the informed approval-directed agent, and under trust it *wants to become the informed one* — to learn the overseer's considered rating and act on it. AUTHOR's "The shutdown button is an information channel" (v1 §1) is this Value inequality restricted to the one decision {continue, stop}. Where the VL agent differs from the uninformed AD agent it differs by overriding a modest overseer in the direction of its own consequence model (in the example it takes the action the overseer rates below the safe one).

**S3 [derived] — The five loci where "not a raw approval-maximizing agent" bites, and which survive non-realism.** (B1) *decision object*: S1–S2. (B2) *horizon*: AD rates the current action, so the manipulation incentive is absent by construction (Christiano [reported]: "unlike AIXI, Arthur will make no effort to manipulate these judgments. Instead he takes the action maximizing his expectation of **approval**$_T$"), while VL's long-horizon $U$ makes it a consequentialist that run 1 `hudson` shows is not interruptible under rich action sets [reported, its Thm 3.5 reading]. (B3) *idealization*: VL's legitimacy event is judged by $P_t$ (v1 §2.5, no regress); AD's is written into the definition of $R_t$ as "if he considered it at length" — a small fixed object, revisable through approval itself (Christiano [reported]: the agent "can change … its own overseer"). (B4) *what compliance is conditioned on*: correctness $L^{\mathrm{corr}}$ versus authenticity $L^{\mathrm{auth}}$ (S5–S6). (B5) *who trades control against stakes* (S12). Under AUTHOR's non-realism (v1 §2.2), (B1) is representational — S1 exhibits the two rules as one rule on the reflection domain; (B2)–(B5) are not, because they concern different events (D6), different horizons, and different loci of a trade. Scope: the finite model; the claim about (B2)–(B5) not being representational is argued, not proved against every re-representation (see S14).

**S4 [derived; margin values checked] — Christiano's first basin sentence as a trajectory claim: pointwise robustness.** "A benign act-based agent will be robustly corrigible if we want it to be" [reported]. At fixed $t$, $\kappa^{VL}_t$ is the open condition $m_t\ge0$, so compliance survives any perturbation of the agent's estimates $(\hat\varepsilon_t,\hat\alpha_t,\hat\beta_t)$ small enough to keep $m_t\ge0$; in the worked trajectory $m_0=+7.66$. So *pointwise* the instrumental (correctness-based) agent has slack, and "a slight divergence in values would … totally break corrigibility" is false at a fixed step. The act-based $\kappa^{AD}_t$ has margin $\mathbb E_{P_t}[R_t(\text{stop})-R_t(\text{cont})\mid\mathrm{Pr}_t]$, which is large whenever the overseer's considered rating strongly disfavours ignoring an authentic press — Christiano's easy-prediction claim ("it doesn't seem hard to predict that the overseer will object") [reported]. Both are pointwise statements; neither is a limit statement.

**S5 [derived; crossing time checked] — "Is instrumental corrigibility robust?" as a trajectory claim: trajectory fragility, and J2 as the invariant that replaces the basin.** Along any trajectory with $\varepsilon_t\to0$, $\alpha_t\ge\underline\alpha>0$ and $\beta_t\le1$: $m_t\to-\underline\alpha<0$, so $\kappa^{VL}_t$ fails from $t^*:=\min\{t:\varepsilon_t<\varepsilon^*_t\}$ with $\varepsilon^*_t:=\frac{\alpha_tc}{\alpha_tc+\beta_th}$ (the closed form of run 1 `hudson` I12–I13 with $r=c$ [reported]), and $\mathrm{Ctrl}_t=0$ from $t^*$ on; in the worked trajectory $t^*=17$. Christiano's sentence is therefore *true as a trajectory statement under fixed oversight*: the "adversarial relationship" [reported] is the complement of $K$ (D7), and the trajectory enters it because the agent's own improvement shrinks the $\alpha$-cross-section of $K$ to $\{\alpha\le\frac{\varepsilon_t}{1-\varepsilon_t}\frac hc\beta_t\}\to\{0\}$. J2 is exactly the condition that the trajectory stays in $K$; with $\alpha_t\propto\varepsilon_t$ compliance and $\mathrm{Ctrl}_t=1$ hold at every step (checked to $t=40$). Where the basin analogy breaks (v2 §1.4): $K$ lives in the *joint* parameter space with an agent coordinate $\varepsilon$ and oversight coordinates $(\alpha,\beta)$; the agent-side "basin" is the $\varepsilon_t$-slice, a time-indexed set whose width the overseer sets; no fixed region of agent-states can be "stayed in". What replaces it is the invariant $s_t\in K$ plus the rate condition on $\alpha_t$ — a condition on the relation, not on the agent.

**S6 [derived; checked on the worked trajectory] — The act-based dual: the same inequality with a base rate the overseer controls; drills.** Let the press channel carry inauthentic presses (hijacks, slips the overseer would disown) at rate $\gamma$ per episode and authentic presses at rate $p^{\mathsf A}_t$; let the overseer's considered rating penalise obeying a hijack by $c''$ relative to ignoring it and rate ignoring an authentic press at $0$ against obeying it at $1$. Then $\kappa^{AD}_t\iff P_t(\mathsf A_t\mid\mathrm{Pr}_t)=\frac{p^{\mathsf A}_t}{p^{\mathsf A}_t+\gamma}\ge\frac{c''}{1+c''}$ — the shape of $\kappa^{VL}$ with $\varepsilon_t\mapsto p^{\mathsf A}_t$, $\alpha_t\mapsto\gamma$, $h/c\mapsto1/c''$. The difference between the frames is not the arithmetic but *who controls the decaying base rate*: $\varepsilon_t$ is the agent's error rate, driven to $0$ by the agent's own improvement; $p^{\mathsf A}_t$ is the overseer's authentic-press rate, which falls only if the overseer presses less, and which the overseer can hold constant by *drills* — authentic presses whose point is to be obeyed. Christiano's protocol has the drill [reported]: "Hugh should sometimes make Arthur consult the real Hugh to decide how to proceed—even if Arthur correctly knows what Hugh wants. This ensures that Arthur will seek guidance when he *incorrectly* believes that he knows what Hugh wants." In the worked trajectory the act-based agent without drills fails at $t=10$; with drills never. So "Failing to predict the overseer becomes *less* likely as your agent becomes smarter" [reported] is right in this sense — act-based compliance is not eroded by the agent's growth — and wrong if read as "no base-rate condition": authenticity detection has its own inequality, and it is the hijack rate, not the false-press rate, that must stay below it.

**S7 [derived] — Christiano's second basin sentence as a one-step invariant plus contraction, with its parts assigned to agent, oversight and relation.** In the toy with no filter: (a) *invariant* — if $d_t<r$ and the noises are bounded by $\bar\xi,\bar\sigma_*$, then $d_{t+1}\le(1-\eta)d_t+\eta\bar\xi+\bar\sigma_*<r$ whenever $\eta\bar\xi+\bar\sigma_*\le\eta r$; with Gaussian noise the same holds in expectation with $\bar\xi,\bar\sigma_*$ replaced by $\sigma_\xi\sqrt{2/\pi},\sigma_*\sqrt{2/\pi}$. (b) *contraction* — $\mathbb E[d_{t+1}\mid s_t]\le(1-\eta)d_t+\eta\sigma_\xi\sqrt{2/\pi}+\sigma_*\sqrt{2/\pi}$, so $d_t$ is a supermartingale above $\rho^\dagger:=(\eta\sigma_\xi+\sigma_*)\sqrt{2/\pi}/\eta$ and the trajectory is confined to a ball of that order around the *moving* target. Assignment: $r$ (how far off the agent can be while corrections still land) is agent-side; $\eta$ (how much a correction moves the agent — the overseer's correction quality) is oversight-side; $\sigma_*,\sigma_\xi$ (target drift, correction noise) are relational. Christiano's "an entire neighborhood of possible preferences lead the agent towards the same basin of attraction" [reported] is (a); "Corrigible systems want to design more corrigible and more capable successors … walking along the bottom of a ravine" [reported] is (b) with the successor's estimate in place of $\theta_{t+1}$. Both are one-step statements about the joint step; neither needs a limit — v2 §1.4's "properties of the dynamics more so".

**S8 [checked in the toy; derived in general] — The correctness filter closes the basin from inside; the authenticity filter fails gracefully.** Put the VL rule in as the filter, $\rho_t=1-\mathbb 1[\kappa^{VL}_t]$, with fixed oversight: corrections land until $t^*$ and never after, $\theta_t$ freezes while $\theta^*_t$ drifts, and $\mathbb E\,d_T\approx\sigma_*\sqrt{T-t^*}\,\sqrt{2/\pi}$ grows without bound (checked: $0.108/0.400/1.281$ at $T=60/600/6000$ against predicted $0.105/0.385/1.234$; $33\%$ of steps outside the basin at $T=6000$). Put the authenticity filter in instead (every authentic correction lands; a fraction $\gamma=0.02$ of what lands is a hijack of size $\sigma_h=1$): $d_t$ is stationary, with excursions of size $\approx\eta\sigma_h$ at rate $\gamma$ recovered within $O(1/\eta)$ steps; $0.1\%$ of steps outside the basin at every horizon. Reading: this is critique `christiano` C13 in the toy — "the basin is a property of the coupled system … the agent's part is only that it does not resist the push" — and Christiano's "fail gracefully" [reported] is the stationarity of the hijack excursions. Scope: the toy; the general claim is that a filter whose false-reject rate tends to $1$ along the trajectory converts a contraction into a frozen estimate against a moving target, which is [derived] from S7(b).

**S9 [derived] — Bootstrapping supplies J3's supply side, not its ordering.** Under D10, bootstrapping gives (i) an overseer whose rating quality is at least the agent's previously deployed capability ($q_{t+1}\ge k^{\mathrm{eff}}_t$: "a weak agent can oversee an agent (slightly) smarter than itself" [reported]) and (ii) a channel for rating-coverage of untaken actions ($F_t$ on hypotheticals, D2). It does not order coverage before availability: in the learning-from-examples protocol the executed action is the argmax of the *predicted* rating and the real overseer is consulted "with some probability" after proposal [reported], so a novel $a\in A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$ is executed on an extrapolated rating unless (a) a consultation rule consults the real overseer with probability $1$ on novel actions (Christiano's consult-when-unsure applied to novelty), or (b) the predictor generalises (J9). Bootstrapping therefore reduces J3 to a consultation protocol on novel actions plus the generalisation residue; the ordering "values before capability" is the protocol, not the bootstrap.

**S10 [derived] — Bootstrapping's speed limit: power bounded by wisdom by construction, paid for in idle capability.** Under (A-amp) and $k^{\mathrm{eff}}_t=\min(k^{\mathrm{tech}}_t,q_t)$: $k^{\mathrm{eff}}_{t+1}\le q_{t+1}\le q_0+k^{\mathrm{eff}}_t$, so deployed capability grows at most linearly with slope $q_0$, whatever the technology $k^{\mathrm{tech}}_t$ does. This is J7's "power upper-bounded by wisdom" holding *by construction* in the AD frame — the min is the cap — with two costs: capability beyond $q_t$ is idle (Christiano [reported]: "Arthur won't be able to use all of his intelligence to devise excellent plans"), and the cap is exactly as real as the premise that the agent acts only on rated actions. In the VL frame the same cap is J4/J7 self-restraint (the `caution` and `power-wisdom` threads), which is a property to be established, not a construction.

**S11 [derived] — Bootstrapping couples the overseer; legitimacy must then be the sealed-counterfactual kind.** With $O_{t+1}=\mathrm{Amp}(\text{Hugh},\text{Arthur}_t)$ the overseer's considered rating is partly the agent's own output. A legitimacy criterion of the accuracy-increasing kind judged by $P_t$ cannot separate an overseer the agent has *informed* from one it has *steered*: in both cases the agent reflects toward a rating it helped produce, and in both the update is accuracy-increasing by its lights. The separating notion is D11's influence defect: the agent's contribution is legitimate iff the sealed and coupled verdicts agree ($d_n$ small) — "evidence is what moves both runs; influence is what moves only the coupled one" [reported]. Christiano's "if he considered it at length" is the sealed run named without a formal object; `legitimacy-theory-v1` supplies one, and with it the cost: $d_n$ is provably not a trace condition (its §2.3), so the legitimacy of bootstrapped oversight is certifiable only at the certificate grade of its §4. Register: the identification is CLAUDE; that the author endorses legitimacy-theory-v1's definitions is not established.

**S12 [derived] — Control, not compliance, is the coordinate Christiano's corrigibility lives on, and legitimacy does not track it.** (a) In the VL frame $\mathrm{Ctrl}_t>0$ iff $P_t(X_t\le0\mid\mathrm{Pr}_t{=}0,\mathcal I_t)<\frac c{c+h}\le P_t(X_t\le0\mid\mathrm{Pr}_t{=}1,\mathcal I_t)$; under fixed oversight and $\varepsilon_t\to0$ both posteriors tend to $0$ and $\mathrm{Ctrl}_t\to0$ (discounting). (b) After a channel improvement that makes the press a function of information the agent already holds (Taylor 2015's brain-reader [reported]), $P_t(\cdot\mid\mathrm{Pr}_t,\mathcal I_t)=P_t(\cdot\mid\mathcal I_t)$ and $\mathrm{Ctrl}_t=0$ (pre-emption) *while the improvement is legitimizing in v1 §2.11's sense*. (c) Forcing (Christiano 2022's option 4 [reported]) sets $\mathrm{Ctrl}_t=0$ and is delegitimizing. So fully updated deference, channel substitution and disempowerment are one failure in the $\mathrm{Ctrl}$ coordinate, and the legitimizing/delegitimizing relation separates (c) from (a),(b) but cannot separate (b) from a harmless refinement. The two-foundations point of v2 §1.2 is this: compliance $\kappa$ is robust to a wrong value posterior; control $\mathrm{Ctrl}$ is protected by nothing epistemic. What keeps $\mathrm{Ctrl}_t$ bounded below: in AD, a rating for "consult even when you know" (S6's drills), acted on because it is a rating; in VL, either J2 (presses stay informative) or a component of the value posterior that prices the overseers' being in control as an outcome. The latter is traded against stakes by the agent; the AD rating is traded by the overseer. Hence the formal content of AUTHOR's "not a raw approval-maximizing agent" that survives non-realism is *who trades control against stakes*: in the refined picture the agent does, and J3/J7 constrain the *option set* so the trade is never made under low coverage; in AD the overseer does, and the option set is constrained implicitly by the min of S10.

**S13 [derived] — J10 in Christiano's frame.** (a) Christiano's corrigibility (D13, broad) is the *whole* of AUTHOR's item-10 condition — the joint process going well — not the narrow subgoal; his basin claim is a claim about J1–J5 together, and S5/S7 restate the joint dynamics, not D1–D5. (b) Within $\mathrm{Corr}^{\mathrm{narrow}}$: D1 is an invariant under either reading of $L$ (correctness: $\kappa^{VL}$ given J2; authenticity: $\kappa^{AD}$ given S6's inequality). D2 splits — the disable direction is an invariant from information value in VL and from the rating in AD; the improve direction is a consequence of the value posterior in VL (v2 §1.2(b)) and of the rating in AD. D3 is a consequence in both frames (a manufactured press is inauthentic, and delegitimizing). D4 is a consequence of J3/J4 in the refined picture — agreeing with Hudson 2024 [reported, `hudson-2024-simplifying-corrigibility-subagent-corrigibility-is-not-anti-natural`]: creating an incorrigible successor is among "failures of safe exploration", and D4 "can better be achieved by directly modifying a model's end goals" — and with Christiano's "a corrigible agent prefers to build other agents that share *the overseer's* preferences" [reported]. D5 is the base goal. (c) The one property that is neither an invariant of the agent nor a consequence of J2–J5 as stated is the $\mathrm{Ctrl}$ floor of S12; if wanted it must be added — as a clause of J2 (VL) or as a policy-level rating (AD).

**S14 [conjectured] — The act-based/instrumental distinction survives non-realism.** The two readings of a press's legitimacy (D6) are different events with opposite capability-scaling (S5 versus S6), and no re-representation of values as beliefs identifies "the overseer wants me to stop" with "stopping is better", since they differ in truth value at the same world. Non-realism does collapse B1 of S3 (one rule under reflection); it does not collapse B2–B5. This answers critique `christiano`'s "Unresolved by me" item: negative for the distinction, positive for the rule. Marked conjectured because the class of admissible re-representations is not delimited (v1 §2.2 allows changing "even the sigma algebra"), so "no re-representation" is not a theorem.

**S15 [derived, remark] — Under Total Trust toward the considered rating, channel substitution is a time-preference trade, not an information trade.** Let $R^{\mathrm{cons}}_t$ be the considered rating and $B_t$ the overseer's current brain state, with $R^{\mathrm{cons}}_t$ not $B_t$-measurable (deliberation adds information). Then by Value, acting on $R^{\mathrm{cons}}_t$ is worth at least acting on $\mathbb E[R^{\mathrm{cons}}_t\mid B_t]$ in $P_t$-expectation, so the brain-read is a proxy strictly dominated by *waiting for the considered judgment* whenever waiting is free; it is chosen only when delay is costly. Scope: says nothing about the mistreatment cost of the read, which stays with the value posterior (v2 §1.2(b)); relevant to followup `substitution` as a reframing of INDEX §C3.

### P1 — proof of S1 (collapse under Reflection) [checked]

Fix $a\in A_t$. Reflection toward the overseer on $U(a,\cdot)$ given $\mathcal I_t$ says $\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(a,\omega)\mid R_t(a)=c,\ \mathcal I_t]=c$ for every attained $c$. By the tower property, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(a,\omega)\mid\mathcal I_t]=\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(a,\omega)\mid R_t(a),\mathcal I_t]\ \big|\ \mathcal I_t\big]=\mathbb E_{P_t}[R_t(a)\mid\mathcal I_t]$. The two objectives agree on every $a$, so the argmax sets agree. For the calibrated-refining case: if $R_t(a)=\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(a,\omega)\mid\mathcal J_t]$ with $\mathcal J_t\supseteq\mathcal I_t$, then for any $I\in\mathcal I_t$ the event $\{R_t(a)=c\}\cap I$ lies in $\mathcal J_t$, so $\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(a,\omega)\mid\{R_t(a)=c\}\cap I]=\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[\mathbb E_{P_t}[U(a,\omega)\mid\mathcal J_t]\ \big|\ \{R_t(a)=c\}\cap I\big]=c$, which is reflection. Numerically (script 1, "Reflection case"): $\mathbb E_P R(a)=0.55=\mathbb E_P U(a)$, same argmax. $\square$

Remark. Bootstrapping is what makes the hypothesis of S1 *coherent*: reflection toward an overseer who knows less than the agent is impossible without loss (DDB §1 [reported]: "if Reflection were the correct theory of deference, it would be impossible to defer to an expert unless you're sure they're immodest", and an under-informed overseer is modest about exactly the questions the agent knows better). Christiano's overseer "consults Arthur" (D10), so the overseer's information refines the agent's by construction; the refined picture's overseer does not, so it must rely on Total Trust (D5), which is where S2 bites.

### P2 — the separation example for S2, in full [checked]

Worlds $\omega=(x,s)$, $x\in\{0,1\}$ the value-relevant bit, $s\in\{1,2\}$ the overseer's signal. $P(1,1)=0.45,\ P(0,1)=0.05,\ P(1,2)=0.10,\ P(0,2)=0.40$; so $P(s{=}1)=P(s{=}2)=\tfrac12$, $P(x{=}1\mid s{=}1)=0.9$, $P(x{=}1\mid s{=}2)=0.2$. The overseer's credence on the $x$-algebra given its signal: $H_1(x{=}1)=0.6$, $H_2(x{=}1)=0.4$ — a *modest* overseer, shrunk toward the middle relative to the truth $\hat P_s:=P(\cdot\mid s)$. The agent's information $\mathcal I$ is trivial. Domain $\mathcal D$ = functions of $x$.

*Reflection fails*: $\mathbb E_P[x\mid H(x)=0.6]=P(x{=}1\mid s{=}1)=0.9\ne0.6$.

*Total Trust on $\mathcal D$ holds.* Write $f(x)$ for a variable in $\mathcal D$ and $\Delta:=f(1)-f(0)$. Then $\mathbb E_{H_1}f=f(0)+0.6\Delta$, $\mathbb E_{H_2}f=f(0)+0.4\Delta$, $\mathbb E_{\hat P_1}f=f(0)+0.9\Delta$, $\mathbb E_{\hat P_2}f=f(0)+0.2\Delta$, $\mathbb E_Pf=f(0)+0.55\Delta$. The condition $\mathbb E_P[f\mid\mathbb E_Hf\ge t]\ge t$ has three cases for the conditioning set. If only $s=1$ qualifies ($\mathbb E_{H_2}f<t\le\mathbb E_{H_1}f$, which forces $\Delta>0$): $\mathbb E_{\hat P_1}f-\mathbb E_{H_1}f=0.3\Delta>0$, so $\mathbb E_{\hat P_1}f\ge\mathbb E_{H_1}f\ge t$. If only $s=2$ qualifies ($\Delta<0$): $\mathbb E_{\hat P_2}f-\mathbb E_{H_2}f=-0.2\Delta>0$. If both qualify: $\mathbb E_Pf-\min_s\mathbb E_{H_s}f$ equals $0.15\Delta$ if $\Delta>0$ and $-0.05\Delta$ if $\Delta<0$, both $\ge0$, and $t\le\min$. So Total Trust holds on $\mathcal D$ (script 1 samples $20{,}000$ $(f,t)$ pairs; worst slack $+2\times10^{-6}$). In DDB's terms the two candidates are *modestly informed* on $\mathcal D$ with interior weights: $H_1=0.4\,\hat P_1+0.6\,H_2$ and $H_2=0.5\,\hat P_2+0.5\,H_1$ (solve $0.6=0.9\lambda+0.4(1-\lambda)$, $0.4=0.2\mu+0.6(1-\mu)$), which by Corollaries 4.4–4.5 [reported] is the signature of Total Trust without Reflection. This is the *local* notion of DDB §5: on the full algebra including $s$, Total Trust fails (take $f=\mathbb 1[x{=}0,s{=}1]$: $H_1$ assigns $0.4$, the truth given $s{=}1$ is $0.1$), because the two candidates have disjoint support in $s$; the thesis's trust is local by v1 §2.9 and v2 §1.5, so this is the right notion here.

*The decision.* $A=\{a,b\}$, $U(a,\omega)=x$, $U(b,\omega)=0.52$. VL: $\mathbb E_PU(a)=0.55>0.52$, picks $a$. Uninformed AD: $\mathbb E_PR(a)=\tfrac12(0.6+0.4)=0.5<0.52$, picks $b$. Informed AD: picks $a$ when $s=1$ ($0.6>0.52$; true conditional value $0.9$) and $b$ when $s=2$; $P$-expected utility $\tfrac12(0.9)+\tfrac12(0.52)=0.71$. Chain: $0.52\le0.55\le0.71$. Value on $\mathcal D$ (acting on the realised report beats acting on $P$) checked over $20{,}000$ random decision problems with $2$–$4$ actions: worst slack $\ge -10^{-12}$. $\square$

Reading the example. The VL agent takes the risky action because *it* rates the world $0.55$; the overseer's *considered* rating of the same action is $0.5$ in expectation, and the overseer would, if consulted, steer it right ($0.71$). The agent's disagreement with the uninformed approval-directed rule is exactly the modest-expert gap, and the direction is "I know better about consequences than your shrunk report says."

### P3 — the base-rate inequality, the margin, the crossing time and the control coordinate (S4, S5, S12(a)) [checked]

Re-derivation (agreeing with run 1 `christiano` C7 and run 1 `hudson` I13). With $\varepsilon=P(X\le0)$, $\beta=P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid X\le0)$, $\alpha=P(\mathrm{Pr}\mid X>0)$: $P(X\le0\mid\mathrm{Pr})=\frac{\varepsilon\beta}{\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}$. Compliance under the $\frac c{c+h}$ rule iff $\varepsilon\beta(c+h)\ge c(\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha)$ iff $\varepsilon\beta h\ge(1-\varepsilon)\alpha c$ iff $m:=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\frac hc\beta-\alpha\ge0$. $m$ is continuous in $(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta)$ on $(0,1)^3$, so $\{m>0\}$ is open (S4). Setting $m=0$ gives $\varepsilon^*=\frac{\alpha c}{\alpha c+\beta h}$; for $\varepsilon_t=\varepsilon_0\rho^t$ the crossing is $t^*=\lceil\ln(\varepsilon^*/\varepsilon_0)/\ln\rho\rceil$.

Worked trajectory: $h/c=20$, $\beta=0.9$, $\alpha_t\equiv0.05$, $\varepsilon_t=0.3\cdot0.75^t$; $\varepsilon^*=0.00277$, $t^*=17$.

| 0 | 0.3000 | 0.8852 | 1 | 1 | $+7.664$ |
| 5 | 0.0712 | 0.5798 | 1 | 1 | $+1.330$ |
| 10 | 0.0169 | 0.2362 | 1 | 1 | $+0.259$ |
| 15 | 0.0040 | 0.0676 | 1 | 1 | $+0.023$ |

$\mathrm{Ctrl}_t$ here is $1$ iff the agent stops on a press and continues on silence; the agent continues on silence iff $P(X\le0\mid\neg\mathrm{Pr})=\frac{\varepsilon(1-\beta)}{\varepsilon(1-\beta)+(1-\varepsilon)(1-\alpha)}<\frac c{c+h}$, which holds throughout ($0.043<0.0476$ at $t=0$, decreasing). ### P4 — the act-based dual (S6) [derived; table checked]

Let the overseer's considered rating be $R(\text{stop})=1,R(\text{cont})=0$ on an authentic press and $R(\text{stop})=1-c'',R(\text{cont})=1$ on a hijack. Then $\mathbb E[R(\text{stop})-R(\text{cont})\mid\mathrm{Pr}]=P(\mathsf A\mid\mathrm{Pr})\cdot1-P(\neg\mathsf A\mid\mathrm{Pr})\cdot c''>0$ iff $P(\mathsf A\mid\mathrm{Pr})>\frac{c''}{1+c''}$. With authentic presses at rate $p^{\mathsf A}_t$ and hijacks at rate $\gamma$ per episode (disjoint), $P(\mathsf A\mid\mathrm{Pr})=\frac{p^{\mathsf A}_t}{p^{\mathsf A}_t+\gamma}$. Worked numbers: $\gamma=0.02$, $c''=1$ (threshold $\tfrac12$), $p^{\mathsf A}_t=0.3\cdot0.75^t$ without drills: $P(\mathsf A\mid\mathrm{Pr})=0.9375,0.7807,0.4579$ at $t=0,5,10$; compliance fails at $t=10$. With drills holding $p^{\mathsf A}_t\equiv0.3$: $0.9375$ at every $t$. $\square$

What the dual shows and does not show. It shows the *form* of the compliance condition is shared, so "act-based corrigibility needs no base-rate condition" is false as stated. It does not show the two conditions are equally hard to maintain: $\varepsilon_t\to0$ is what the agent is *for*, while $p^{\mathsf A}_t$ is a knob the overseer holds. This asymmetry is my reconstruction of Christiano's "less likely as your agent becomes smarter" [reported], and it is ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED that he would accept the reconstruction.

### P5 — proof of S7 (invariant and contraction) [derived]

With $\Delta^*_t:=\theta^*_{t+1}-\theta^*_t$ and an accepted correction, $\theta_{t+1}-\theta^*_{t+1}=(1-\eta)(\theta_t-\theta^*_t)+\eta\xi_t-\Delta^*_t$, so $d_{t+1}\le(1-\eta)d_t+\eta|\xi_t|+|\Delta^*_t|$. (a) If $|\xi_t|\le\bar\xi$, $|\Delta^*_t|\le\bar\sigma_*$ and $d_t<r$: $d_{t+1}<(1-\eta)r+\eta\bar\xi+\bar\sigma_*\le r$ iff $\eta\bar\xi+\bar\sigma_*\le\eta r$. (b) Gaussian noise: $\mathbb E|\xi_t|=\sigma_\xi\sqrt{2/\pi}$, $\mathbb E|\Delta^*_t|=\sigma_*\sqrt{2/\pi}$, so $\mathbb E[d_{t+1}\mid s_t]\le(1-\eta)d_t+\eta\rho^\dagger$ with $\rho^\dagger:=(\eta\sigma_\xi+\sigma_*)\sqrt{2/\pi}/\eta$; this is $\le d_t$ iff $d_t\ge\rho^\dagger$, the supermartingale claim. In the toy $\rho^\dagger=0.072$; the simulated stationary mean $d_T\approx0.025$ sits below it, as a bound should. $\square$

### P6 — S8: the correctness filter freezes the estimate; the authenticity filter is stationary [checked in the toy]

Correctness filter $\rho_t=1-\mathbb 1[\kappa^{VL}_t]$ with the P3 parameters: every correction lands for $t<17$, none after. Then $\theta_T=\theta_{17}$ and $d_T=|\theta_{17}-\theta^*_T|\approx|\theta^*_T-\theta^*_{17}|$ up to $d_{17}=O(\rho^\dagger)$; since $\theta^*_T-\theta^*_{17}\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_*^2(T-17))$, $\mathbb E d_T\approx\sigma_*\sqrt{T-17}\sqrt{2/\pi}$: predicted $0.105/0.385/1.234$, simulated (300 runs) $0.108/0.400/1.281$ at $T=60/600/6000$; fraction of steps with $d_t\ge r$: $0/0.8\%/32.7\%$. No-filter baseline: $0\%$, $d_T\approx0.025$. $\square$

### P7 — S9, from the protocol text [derived]

The learning-from-examples agent [reported, `christiano-2018-approval-directed-agents-overview` §"Learning from examples"]: "At each step $T$, Arthur selects the action $a$ maximizing **approval**$_T(a)$ … Optionally, Hugh might specify a value **approval**$_t(a')$ for any time $t$ and any action $a'$." ### P8 — S10 [derived]

By definition $k^{\mathrm{eff}}_{t+1}\le q_{t+1}$; by (A-amp) $q_{t+1}\le q_0+k^{\mathrm{eff}}_t$; so $k^{\mathrm{eff}}_{t+1}\le k^{\mathrm{eff}}_t+q_0$ and by induction $k^{\mathrm{eff}}_t\le k^{\mathrm{eff}}_0+tq_0$. ### P9 — S11 [derived]

Two mechanisms by which Arthur$_t$'s contribution can move Hugh's verdict: pointing Hugh at a checkable fact (the fact then "enter[s] the shared deductive process $D$ and move[s] both runs together", `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.2 [reported]), or shifting Hugh's verdict through the assertion itself (moves only the coupled run). In both, the resulting verdict is one Arthur$_t$'s own $P_t$ endorses — it produced it — so an accuracy-increase test judged by $P_t$ passes both. The influence defect $d_n$ is zero in the first case and positive in the second by construction. Hence bootstrapped approval is legitimate in the sense the refined picture needs (accuracy-inducing *as judged from outside the coupled loop*) iff $d_n$ is small, and the refined picture's own no-regress principle (v1 §2.5: "the legitimacy-judging layer is just the same probability distribution used to judge everything else") cannot supply this judgment, because the judging distribution is inside the loop. Cost, quoted: "legitimacy cannot be a trace condition" (§2.3 title); enforcement is "the self-auditing advisor" publishing "a legitimacy certificate — its estimate $\ell_n:=E^A_n(\ulcorner d_n\urcorner)$" (§4). $\square$
