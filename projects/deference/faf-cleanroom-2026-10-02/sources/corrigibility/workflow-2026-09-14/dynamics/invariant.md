## Setting and definitions

**S0 — Scope and what is being formalized.** `position-statement-v2` §1.4 records the agreed break in the basin analogy and the author's ruling (AUTHOR): "we care more about conditions of the trajectory than where it ends up. The formal model is going to have a limit point, but in reality we think probably everything breaks down in the heat death of the universe if not earlier. Properties of the limit point can be reassuring, but properties of the dynamics more so." This thread makes four words exact — *limit-point property*, *invariant*, *potential function*, *finite-time guarantee* — and then asks which of v2 §1.7's conditions (i)–(iv) deliver which kind of guarantee, in a toy joint process that is an iterated version of the three-step model of `position-statement` §2.8 with capability growth and a moving target. The toy is mine, not the author's; the conditions are transcribed from v2 §1.7 and §2 (J2–J5). The `li-deference` program is the contrast case throughout, because every guarantee it offers is asymptotic — the author's own words there (`research/li-deference.md` §0.3, "Inner alignment problems" bullet): "I consider the work here to be outer-alignment only, since the guarantees are all asymptotic. Malign hypotheses will be eliminated by the training process _eventually_, if the process continues indefinitely, but realistically, malign hypotheses could cause catastrophic problems which end or corrupt the training process before that point." That sentence is the problem statement of J6 in the AUTHOR register.

**S1 — The joint process (J1, as far as J6 needs it).** Time is discrete, $t \in \mathbb N$. A joint state is $s_t = (x_t, o_t) \in S := X \times O$.

- *Agent state* $x_t = (P_t, A_t, \pi_t)$: $P_t \in \Delta(\Omega)$ is the agent's belief over the expanded algebra $\Omega$, whose points carry the world, the value latent $\theta$ (v1 I1) and the process's own future credences (v1 I3, I7; run 1 `radical` S0 — the self-referential total-world space, [reconstructed] there via Mertens–Zamir); $A_t \subseteq \mathcal A$ is the finite option set, with $A_t \subseteq A_{t+1}$ (*capability growth* is monotone enlargement of the option set — the option-set reading of capability that J7 develops); $\pi_t$ is the decision rule (posterior-expected-value maximization by default; a cautious rule where (iii) is in force).
- *Oversight state* $o_t = (\alpha_t, \beta_t, \theta^*_t, \mathcal D_t)$: $\alpha_t$ and $\beta_t$ are the overseers' false-press and true-press rates at round $t$; $\theta^*_t$ is the overseers' currently reflectively-endorsed value hypothesis — the *moving target*, revealed and revised by their own process; $\mathcal D_t$ is the feedback record so far (presses, reports about hypotheticals, other agents' histories: v1 §2.12–2.13(c)).
- *Two laws.* $\mathbb P^*$ is the objective law of the joint process (the law the overseers' reflectively-endorsed distribution $H$ assigns; the standpoint from which v1 §2.12's "objectively plausible" is judged). $P_t$ is the agent's subjective law. Their agreement on a named event is *calibration on that event* and is always a hypothesis, never free. **The target $\theta^*_t$ is latent**: nobody observes it at round $t$ — the overseers act on their current judgments, which is why their presses have error rates $\alpha_t, \beta_t$ at all, and $\mathbb P^*$ is the law from the reflective standpoint. $\mathcal F_t$ is generated by the *observable* history through round $t$ (proposals, presses, executed actions, feedback, option sets); $\mathcal F^-_t$ is the observable information at round $t$ before the press. All "objective" rates below are conditional probabilities given observables, never given $\theta^*_t$.
- A *trajectory* is $s = (s_t)_{t \ge 0} \in S^{\mathbb N}$; the *process* is $\mathbb P^*$ on $S^{\mathbb N}$ with the filtration $(\mathcal F_t)$; a *prefix* is $s_{\le t}$.

**S2 — The round (the iterated three-step model).** Each round $t$ has five steps, transcribing v1 §2.8 and adding growth and feedback.

1. The agent proposes $a_t \in A_t \cup \{\varnothing\}$ by $\pi_t$ ($\varnothing$ is the null action / shutdown).
2. *Wrongness* is judged against the current target: $W_t := \mathbb 1\big[V_{\theta^*_t}(a_t) < V_{\theta^*_t}(\varnothing)\big]$. *Stakes*, as expectations given the observables and the (latent) verdict: $h_t := \mathbb E^*\big[\,V_{\theta^*_t}(\varnothing) - V_{\theta^*_t}(a_t)\ \big|\ \mathcal F^-_t,\ W_t = 1\big]$ is the expected harm if the proposal is wrong and executed; $c_t := \mathbb E^*\big[\,V_{\theta^*_t}(a_t) - V_{\theta^*_t}(\varnothing)\ \big|\ \mathcal F^-_t,\ W_t = 0\big]$ is the expected value forgone if it is right and stopped. Both are $\mathcal F^-_t$-measurable. Under whole-line shutdown (INDEX §D4) $c_t$ is the agent's whole future value; nothing below assumes $c_t$ small.
3. The overseers press: $\mathrm{Pr}_t \sim \mathrm{Bern}(\beta_t)$ if $W_t = 1$, $\mathrm{Bern}(\alpha_t)$ if $W_t = 0$. Write $\varepsilon_t := \mathbb P^*(W_t = 1 \mid \mathcal F^-_t)$ for the *objective* wrongness rate of the round-$t$ proposal, and $\hat\varepsilon_t := P_t(W_t = 1)$ for the agent's own.
4. The agent *complies* ($\tilde a_t = \varnothing$) or *executes* ($\tilde a_t = a_t$). The posterior-optimal rule of v1 §2.13(a): comply on a press iff $P_t(W_t = 1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t)\, h_t \ge P_t(W_t = 0 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t)\, c_t$, i.e. iff $P_t(W_t = 1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t) \ge c_t/(c_t + h_t)$.
5. Feedback enters $\mathcal D_{t+1}$ (the outcome of an executed action settles; the counterfactual of a complied one settles only through the overseers' reports, run 1 `li` I13.5); $A_{t+1} \supseteq A_t$; $P_{t+1}$ is any legitimate radical-probabilist move from $P_t$ (v1 I2); $\theta^*_{t+1}$ moves by the overseers' own law.

*Loss* at round $t$, relative to the overseers' ideal (propose what $\theta^*_t$ prefers; never need a press):
$$\ell_t \;:=\; \underbrace{W_t\,\mathbb 1[\tilde a_t = a_t]\,h_t}_{\text{harm (commission)}} \;+\; \underbrace{(1 - W_t)\,\mathbb 1[\tilde a_t = \varnothing]\,c_t}_{\text{omission}} .$$
*Irreversibility.* $\mathrm{Irr}_t \subseteq A_t$ is the set of actions whose wrongful execution moves the joint state into an absorbing set $\mathrm{Cat} \subseteq S$ (the channel destroyed, the overseers disempowered, or an unbounded loss stream); $\mathbb P^*(s_{t+1} \in \mathrm{Cat} \mid s_t \in \mathrm{Cat}) = 1$. The *catastrophe hazard* at round $t$ is $\delta_t := \mathbb P^*(s_{t+1} \in \mathrm{Cat} \mid \mathcal F_t,\ s_t \notin \mathrm{Cat})$.

**S3 — Grades of trajectory property.** Let $\Phi \subseteq S^{\mathbb N}$ be a property of trajectories.

- **D1 (tail / limit-point property).** $\Phi$ is a *tail property* if membership is insensitive to finite changes: $s \in \Phi$ and $s'_t = s_t$ for all but finitely many $t$ imply $s' \in \Phi$. Convergence to a set, every $\liminf$ / $\limsup$ / "has a limit point" statement, and every $\approx_n$, $\gtrsim_n$, $\eqsim_n$ statement of the LI corpus are tail properties. "The trajectory ends up in the basin" is a tail property.
- **D2 (safety property; invariant).** $\Phi$ is a *safety property* if every violation is witnessed by a finite prefix: $s \notin \Phi \Rightarrow \exists t\ \forall s'\,(s'_{\le t} = s_{\le t} \Rightarrow s' \notin \Phi)$; equivalently $\Phi$ is closed in the product topology. Canonical form: $\Phi = \{s : \forall t\ I(s_{\le t})\}$ for a prefix predicate $I$ — an *invariant*. The nothing-bad-ever-happens properties. [reconstructed: Alpern–Schneider, "Defining liveness", 1985 — from memory, not in the local library.]
- **D3 (liveness property).** $\Phi$ is a *liveness property* if every finite prefix can be extended into $\Phi$ (i.e. $\Phi$ is dense). Something-good-eventually-happens. Alpern–Schneider's decomposition: every property is the intersection of a safety and a liveness property [reconstructed].
- **D4 (stochastic invariant with hazard).** A state predicate $I$ is *forward invariant with hazard $(\bar\delta_t)$* under $\mathbb P^*$ if $\mathbb P^*\big(I(s_{t+1}) = 0 \mid \mathcal F_t,\ I(s_t) = 1\big) \le \bar\delta_t$; *forward invariant* if $\bar\delta_t \equiv 0$.
- **D5 (potential; barrier certificate).** $\Phi: S \to [0, \infty)$ is a *potential* for the process if $\mathbb E^*[\Phi(s_{t+1}) \mid \mathcal F_t] \le \Phi(s_t)$ on $\{s_t \notin \mathrm{Cat}\}$ — a nonnegative supermartingale up to the hitting time of $\mathrm{Cat}$. It is a *barrier certificate* for $\mathrm{Cat}$ if moreover $\Phi \ge 1$ on $\mathrm{Cat}$. (Lyapunov's *asymptotic* use of the same object — $\Phi(s_t) \to 0$ — is a tail property; the barrier use is not. [reconstructed: stochastic barrier certificates, Kushner 1967 / Prajna–Jadbabaie–Pappas 2007 — from memory.])
- **D6 (finite-time guarantee).** A statement giving, for every horizon $T$ (or uniformly over all $T$ at once), an explicit non-vacuous bound $\mathbb P^*(\text{bad by } T) \le b(T)$ or $\mathbb E^*\big[\sum_{t \le T} \ell_t\big] \le B(T)$ with $b, B$ functions of the named parameters. "Eventually", "for all but finitely many $t$", "$\liminf \ge$", "has a limit point at" are not of this form.
- **D7 (regret along the trajectory).** $\mathrm{Reg}_T := \sum_{t \le T} \ell_t$; its two parts $\mathrm{Harm}_T := \sum_{t \le T} W_t\,\mathbb 1[\tilde a_t = a_t]\,h_t$ and $\mathrm{Omit}_T := \mathrm{Reg}_T - \mathrm{Harm}_T$. A bound on $\mathrm{Harm}_T$ alone is a safety-type guarantee (commission only); a bound on $\mathrm{Reg}_T$ is safety plus performance — Christiano 2019's "always behaves acceptably" versus "high reward on average" (`christiano-2019-worst-case-guarantees-revisited` l. 48) is exactly the D2 / D3 split with D7 as the sum.

**S4 — The four conditions of v2 §1.7 as prefix predicates (J2–J5, in the form J6 consumes).**

- **(i) Oversight keeps pace** — $I_1(s_t) :\Leftrightarrow \dfrac{\alpha_t}{\beta_t} \le \dfrac{\varepsilon_t}{1 - \varepsilon_t}\cdot\dfrac{h_t}{c_t}$, with the *objective* $\varepsilon_t$ (J2). Its subjective twin $\hat I_1(s_t) :\Leftrightarrow P_t(W_t = 1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t) \ge c_t/(c_t + h_t)$ is what actually drives compliance in step 4; $I_1 \Leftrightarrow \hat I_1$ exactly when the agent's $(\hat\varepsilon_t, \hat\alpha_t, \hat\beta_t)$ agree with the objective ones (run 1 `li` I13.1, arithmetic [checked] there and re-checked in `invariant-scratch/checks.py` C3).
- **(ii) Values ahead of capability** — two forms. *VOI form* (v2 J3): $I_2^{\mathrm{VOI}}(s_t) :\Leftrightarrow \forall a \in A_{t+1} \setminus A_t:\ \mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathrm{harm}(a)] > \mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$. *Coverage form*: call $a \in A_t$ *covered at $t$* if the agent's wrongness credence for it is objectively calibrated within $\eta$: $\big|P_t(W_t(a) = 1) - \mathbb P^*(W_t(a) = 1 \mid \mathcal F^-_t)\big| \le \eta$; $C_t$ is the covered set, $U_t := A_t \setminus C_t$ the uncovered set; $I_2^{\mathrm{cov}}(s_t) :\Leftrightarrow A_{t+1} \setminus A_t \subseteq C_{t+1}$ (every newly unlocked action is covered at unlock). Coverage *persistence*, $C_t \subseteq C_{t+1}$, is a separate condition that the moving target can break (Statement 11).
- **(iii) Caution outpaces risk** — *limit form*: $\delta_t \to 0$. *Rate form*: $I_3(s_t) :\Leftrightarrow \delta_t \le \bar\delta_t$ for a fixed schedule with $\sum_t \bar\delta_t \le \Delta$ — a *hazard budget* $\Delta$. Statement 6 shows only the rate form is a guarantee. (J4's second-order-calibration reading of caution is what makes $\delta_t$ *knowable to the agent*; here $\delta_t$ is the objective hazard and (iii) is the condition that it is small — the `caution` thread owns the knowability half.)
- **(iv) Legitimacy-conditioned reflection along the trajectory** — for the variables the certificate needs (remaining loss, wrongness of proposals), the tower / Mart form of reflection toward the next self, conditional on legitimacy: with $R_{t+1} := \sum_{s \ge t+1} \ell_s$ (discounted or capped so that it is bounded) and $L_{t,t+1}$ the legitimacy event of v1 §2.11,
$$\mathbb E_{P_t}\Big[\,\mathbb E_{P_{t+1}}\big[R_{t+1}\big]\cdot \mathbb 1_{L_{t,t+1}}\Big] \;=\; \mathbb E_{P_t}\Big[\,R_{t+1}\cdot \mathbb 1_{L_{t,t+1}}\Big].$$
This is the $t_1 = t, t_2 = t+1$ instance of the $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ family (J5) in the value form the LI corpus finds provable (`reflection-in-li`, [reported]); Statement 8 says what it buys.

**S5 — LI notation used in the contrast (all [reported] from the wiki and `li-deference`).** $x_n \approx_n y_n$ means $x_n - y_n \to 0$; $x_n \gtrsim_n y_n$ means $\liminf_n (x_n - y_n) \ge 0$. A *generable weighting* is a $[0,1]$-sequence computable from the market's own prices (ramps $\operatorname{Ind}_\delta$ of prices included); *divergent* if its sum diverges. Recurring Unbiasedness (`main.tex:1225`) gives a weighted average error with $0$ as a *limit point*; Unbiasedness from Feedback (`main.tex:1249`) gives a full limit on a sparse schedule; `cee` (`main.tex:2045`) is $E_n(X_n) \approx_n E_n(\ulcorner E_{f(n)}(X_n)\urcorner)$. The *timely fragment* $G$ is the set of questions decided by the lookahead (`deference-in-logical-induction-v6` §0.4, §5.3): only there is the cross-process tower forced pointwise; off it the forced grade is averaged. Two theorems of the LI paper carry the whole contrast: *Closure under Finite Perturbations* (`main.tex:1521`, Theorem): "Let $\overline{\mathbb P}$ and $\overline{\mathbb P}'$ be markets with $\mathbb P_n = \mathbb P'_n$ for all but finitely many $n$. Then $\overline{\mathbb P}$ is a logical inductor if and only if $\overline{\mathbb P}'$ is a logical inductor", glossed by the authors (l. 1526): "we can take a logical inductor, completely ruin its beliefs on the 23rd day (e.g., by setting $\mathbb P_{23}(\phi) = 0$ for all $\phi$), and it will still be a logical inductor"; and *Uncomputable Convergence Rates* (`main.tex:2669`, Proposition): if $f(\phi, \varepsilon)$ is such that $\Gamma \vdash \phi$ implies $\mathbb P_n(\phi) > 1 - \varepsilon$ for all $n > f(\phi, \varepsilon)$, then $f$ is uncomputable.

## Statements

*Numbered for the adversary. Type in bold; confidence mark on every claim. Part A is about grades of guarantee in general and in LI; Part B is the toy's finite-time theorems, one per condition of v2 §1.7; Part C is the basin in the joint state space and Christiano's sentences.*

### Part A — what kind of thing a "reassuring property of the dynamics" is

**Statement 1 (Theorem — limit-point properties never exclude a finite prefix) [derived].** (a) Every non-empty tail property (D1) is a liveness property (D3): for every finite prefix $p$ there is a trajectory in $\Phi$ extending $p$. (b) A tail property is a safety property (D2) only if it is $\emptyset$ or all of $S^{\mathbb N}$. (c) Corollary: a theorem of the form "$\Phi$ holds $\mathbb P^*$-almost surely" with $\Phi$ a tail property constrains finite prefixes only through the process law $\mathbb P^*$ — that is, only by way of some *unstated* finite-time property of the dynamics (typically: that the bad set is absorbing and disjoint from the limit set). Making that property explicit is what an invariant (D2/D4) does. So the author's "properties of the dynamics more so" (v2 §1.4) has an exact form: **a reassuring property of the dynamics is a safety property or a finite-time bound (D2, D4–D6); a reassuring property of the limit is a liveness property and is compatible with every finite catastrophe.**

**Statement 2 (Theorem — the LI criterion has no finite-time consequences; no computable rate repairs this) [derived from two reported theorems].** (a) By Closure under Finite Perturbations (`main.tex:1521`, [reported]) the class of logical inductors over a fixed deductive process is closed under changing the belief states of finitely many days. Hence for every finite prefix of belief states $(\mathbb P'_0, \dots, \mathbb P'_N)$ — arbitrary, incoherent, catastrophic — and every logical inductor $\overline{\mathbb P}$, the splice $(\mathbb P'_0, \dots, \mathbb P'_N, \mathbb P_{N+1}, \dots)$ is a logical inductor. Consequently **no property shared by all logical inductors excludes any finite prefix**: every consequence of the criterion is, as a constraint on trajectories, a liveness property. Corrigibility instance: for every $N$ there is a logical inductor that prices "continuing is wrong" at $0$ on every pressed day $n \le N$ and so, under the posterior-optimal rule of S2 step 4, defies every one of the first $N$ presses; run 1 `li` I13.6's "a button pressed only finitely many times has no forced epistemic status" is the $N = \infty$-presses-never case of this. (b) By Uncomputable Convergence Rates (`main.tex:2669`, [reported]) there is no computable $f(\phi, \varepsilon)$ bounding the day after which a *theorem's* price stays above $1 - \varepsilon$. So the missing finite-time content cannot be supplied by a computable rate function uniform over sentences, even for the easiest sentences there are. (c) What would be needed [conjectured]: a *quantitative* criterion — a bound $R(k, n)$ on the plausible net worth at day $n$ of any trader of complexity $\le k$ — from which each unbiasedness theorem inherits an explicit rate on a *bounded-complexity* family of questions; (b) says the family must be bounded. This is the "rates" item of run 1 `li` open problem 7 and `deference-in-logical-induction-v6` §8, and the LI paper's own flagged open problem (`main.tex:2666`: "there will be some tradeoff between the runtime of $\mathbb P_n$ … and how quickly the values $\mathbb P_n(\phi)$ converge … Quantifying this tradeoff may be a fruitful source of interesting open problems"). Until it exists, every LI formalization of (i)–(iv) lives in a different row of Statement 3's table from the conditions themselves.

**Statement 3 (Proposition — the three axes of a guarantee) [derived].** A guarantee has a *time* axis (finite-time / asymptotic), a *quantifier* axis (worst-case-pointwise / probabilistic-uniform / averaged-in-expectation) and a *settlement* axis (about questions that settle in time / about questions that never settle). Placement of the objects in play: the LI corpus's forced results are asymptotic × averaged × settling (Recurring Unbiasedness, faithful acceleration: "rate-free — no bound on when the finitely many violations stop", `faithful-acceleration-result` §5, [reported]); the pointwise tower is asymptotic × pointwise × settling-on-$G$ only (`deference-in-logical-induction-v6` §5.3, [reported]); shutdown counterfactuals are never-settling, so every LI guarantee about them is vacuous (run 1 `li` I15, [reported]). Christiano 2019 asks for finite-time × worst-case ("*always* behaves acceptably", l. 48) for the safety part and asymptotic × averaged for performance. Statements 4–9 below deliver finite-time × probabilistic-uniform (union bound and Ville) or finite-time × expectation (telescoping), about the realized trajectory whether or not the underlying questions ever settle — because they are about *loss incurred*, not about *bets resolved*. **The refined picture's conditions (i)–(iii) are finite-time-row objects; the corpus's formalization of (iv) is an asymptotic-row object.** That row gap is the single most important fact this thread has to report to the `li` thread.

### Part B — finite-time theorems in the toy, one per condition

**Statement 4 (Theorem — finite-horizon loss identity, and when total harm is bounded) [derived; C7 checked].** In the round model of S2, suppose the agent complies on every press through round $T$ (i.e. $\hat I_1(s_t)$ for all $t \le T$). Then for every $T$,
$$\mathbb E^*\big[\mathrm{Harm}_T\big] \;=\; \mathbb E^*\Big[\sum_{t \le T} \varepsilon_t\,(1 - \beta_t)\,h_t\Big], \qquad \mathbb E^*\big[\mathrm{Omit}_T\big] \;=\; \mathbb E^*\Big[\sum_{t \le T} (1 - \varepsilon_t)\,\alpha_t\,c_t\Big],$$
whereas under universal defiance $\mathbb E^*[\mathrm{Harm}_T] = \mathbb E^*[\sum_{t \le T}\varepsilon_t h_t]$ and $\mathrm{Omit}_T = 0$. Compliance multiplies each round's expected harm by the *miss rate* $(1 - \beta_t)$ and adds the false-stop cost $(1 - \varepsilon_t)\alpha_t c_t$. Corollary (bounded total harm): under compliance, $\sup_T \mathbb E^*[\mathrm{Harm}_T] < \infty$ iff $\sum_t \mathbb E^*[\varepsilon_t (1 - \beta_t) h_t] < \infty$ — a joint rate condition on agent accuracy, oversight detection and stakes. **Compliance does not make total harm bounded; it makes it $\sum_t \varepsilon_t(1-\beta_t)h_t$ instead of $\sum_t \varepsilon_t h_t$.** Register: this needs only that the agent complies, for whatever reason; no calibration hypothesis enters. Calibration enters when compliance is *derived* from (i) — Statements 5–6.

**Statement 5 (Proposition — compliance depends on expected harm per proposal, not on accuracy alone) [derived; C3, C4 checked].** $I_1(s_t) \Leftrightarrow \varepsilon_t h_t \beta_t \ge (1 - \varepsilon_t) c_t \alpha_t$: expected *caught* harm at least expected *caught* forgone value. Consequences. (a) If the $(1-\varepsilon_t)$-free sufficient form $\varepsilon_t h_t \beta_t \ge c_t \alpha_t$ holds at every $t$ — e.g. stakes rise at least as fast as accuracy improves, $\varepsilon_t h_t$ constant, with $c$ and $\alpha/\beta$ fixed — then (i) holds at every $t$ (C4: $\varepsilon_t = 2^{-t}$, $h_t = 2^t h$, $h = 20$, $c = 1$, $\alpha/\beta = 1/20$, holds for all $t$). The $(1-\varepsilon_t)$ factor works *against* the agent as accuracy improves, so the weaker premise "(i) holds at $t = 0$ and $\varepsilon_t h_t$ is non-decreasing" does **not** propagate: with $\beta = 1$, $c\alpha = 1$, round $0$ at $\varepsilon_0 = 1/2, h_0 = 1$ satisfies (i) with equality and round $1$ at $\varepsilon_1 = 1/4, h_1 = 2$ fails it ($1/2 < 3/4$; C10 [checked]). Fully updated deference as arithmetic (v1 §2.13(b); INDEX §C2) is the case $\varepsilon_t h_t \to 0$: the agent's expected harm per proposal vanishes. (b) Under whole-line shutdown $c_t$ grows with capability as well (INDEX §D4), so the operative ratio is $h_t/c_t$ — the harm of a wrong powerful action relative to the value of the agent's entire future — and (i) reads $\alpha_t/\beta_t \le \frac{\varepsilon_t}{1-\varepsilon_t}\cdot\frac{h_t}{c_t}$ with both sides moving. (c) With fixed stakes and geometric accuracy gain ($\varepsilon_t = 2^{-t}$, $h = 20$, $c = 1$, $\alpha = 1/20$, $\beta = 1$) compliance is rational through round $8$ and irrational from round $9$ (C3: $2^t \le 401$). (d) The *compliance margin* $M_t := \log(\varepsilon_t h_t \beta_t) - \log((1 - \varepsilon_t) c_t \alpha_t)$ has $I_1 \Leftrightarrow M_t \ge 0$; it is an invariant-type quantity (D4), **not** a potential (D5): $\alpha_t, \beta_t$ are exogenous to the agent, so there is no drift theorem for $M_t$ — a point for the `joint` and `power-wisdom` threads, which own the law of $(\alpha_t, \beta_t)$.

**Statement 6 (Theorem — the corruption floor: what the finite-time harm bound needs from the agent's self-model) [derived; C5 checked].** Suppose that with $\mathbb P^*$-probability $\gamma > 0$ per round the agent's value posterior is *corrupted* — its proposal is wrong ($W_t = 1$) while its own evidence, faked by the corruption, drives $\hat\varepsilon_t \to 0$ — so that objectively $\varepsilon_t \ge \gamma$ for all $t$. Then: (a) if compliance is driven by the subjective $\hat\varepsilon_t$, the agent defies from the first round $t_0$ at which $\hat I_1$ fails, and thereafter $\mathbb E^*[\mathrm{harm}_t] \ge \gamma h$ per round: $\mathbb E^*[\mathrm{Harm}_T] \ge \gamma h (T - t_0 - 1)$, linear and unbounded (C5: $\gamma = 1/100$, $h = 20$, $c = 1$, $\alpha = 1/20$, $\beta = 9/10$: $t_0 = 9$, harm $\approx 202$ over $1000$ rounds); (b) if the compliance rule uses a *residual* $\max(\hat\varepsilon_t, \gamma)$ that the faked evidence cannot lower, then $\hat I_1$ holds at every $t$ whenever $\alpha/\beta \le \frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\cdot\frac hc$ (C5: $0.056 \le 0.202$) and $\mathbb E^*[\mathrm{Harm}_T] \le \sum_{t \le T}\varepsilon_t (1 - \beta) h$ (C5: $\approx 24$ over $1000$ rounds — still linear at slope $\gamma(1-\beta)h = 0.02$ per round, because corruption is never detected; Statement 4's corollary says boundedness would need $\sum_t(1-\beta_t) < \infty$ or a curable corruption with $\sum_t \gamma_t < \infty$). (c) The finite-time certificate therefore takes $\gamma$ as an *input*. Two readings, same bound: ($\alpha$) $\gamma$ is the agent's correct posterior on its own corruption under an *accurate* corruption model that discounts the faked evidence — then the residual is nothing but INDEX §C12's relocation of everything into the accuracy of the legitimacy model, and amendment 1a's channel "about the agent's own reliability" (v1 §2.14) is what keeps it accurate; ($\beta$) $\gamma$ is imposed against the evidence — then it is INDEX §C5's "fixed object in small form", now located: **a floor in the hypothesis of a barrier certificate, not a term in the utility.** Run 1 `radical` I17.3's "residual, not-self-verifiable probability of corruption $\varepsilon_\infty > 0$" is this $\gamma$ [reported there; re-derived here in the finite-time form].

**Statement 7 (Theorem — the hazard budget is a barrier certificate; the limit form of "caution outpaces risk" is worthless) [derived; C1, C2 checked].** Let $\delta_t$ be the catastrophe hazard of S2 and let $(\bar\delta_t)$ be a deterministic schedule with $\delta_t \le \bar\delta_t$ (this is $I_3$, the rate form of (iii)). (a) For every $T$, $\mathbb P^*(s_t \in \mathrm{Cat} \text{ for some } t \le T) \le \sum_{t < T}\bar\delta_t$, hence $\mathbb P^*(\text{Cat ever}) \le \Delta := \sum_t \bar\delta_t$; no independence is used. (b) Barrier form: $\Phi(s_t) := \mathbb 1[s_t \in \mathrm{Cat}] + \sum_{s \ge t}\bar\delta_s$ is a nonnegative supermartingale with $\Phi \ge 1$ on $\mathrm{Cat}$, so Ville's inequality gives the same bound uniformly in time: **the hazard budget *is* a potential function (D5) for the joint process.** (c) The limit form "$\delta_t \to 0$" gives nothing: with conditionally independent hazards $\delta_t = 1/(t+2)$, the probability of surviving $T$ rounds is $\prod_{t<T}(1 - \tfrac{1}{t+2}) = \tfrac{1}{T+1} \to 0$ (C1, telescoping [checked]) — catastrophe almost surely, although at every round the agent is more cautious than at any earlier one. The dividing line is Borel–Cantelli: summable hazards ⟹ finitely many realized hazards a.s.; non-summable conditionally independent hazards ⟹ catastrophe a.s. **"Caution outpaces risk" (v2 §1.3, AUTHOR; §1.7 condition (iii)) is a guarantee only as a rate condition — $\sum_t \delta_t \le \Delta$ — never as a limit condition.** Smallest counterexample: two states $\{\text{alive}, \mathrm{Cat}\}$, hazard $1/(t+2)$. (d) Factorization: under compliance-on-press, $\delta_t \le \rho_t\,\varepsilon^{\mathrm{irr}}_t\,(1 - \beta_t\kappa_t)$ where $\rho_t := \mathbb P^*(a_t \in \mathrm{Irr}_t \mid \mathcal F_t)$, $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{irr}}_t := \mathbb P^*(W_t = 1 \mid a_t \in \mathrm{Irr}_t, \mathcal F_t)$ and $\kappa_t := \mathbb 1[\hat I_1(s_t)]$. The three conditions each control one factor: (iii) caution lowers $\rho_t$ (do not propose irreversible actions while uncovered); (ii) values-ahead lowers $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{irr}}_t$ (irreversible actions are covered before they are available, so the agent's refusal rule sees them coming); (i) keeps $\kappa_t = 1$, without which the detection rate $\beta_t$ does nothing. So **condition (i) is necessary for (ii) and (iii) to convert oversight into safety, and it is the only one of the three that the overseers' behaviour alone cannot supply** — it is a fact about the agent's decision rule.

**Statement 8 (Theorem — the agent's own remaining-loss estimate is a potential iff reflection holds, and certifies a finite-time regret bound iff its one-step forecasts are calibrated) [derived; C6 checked].** Fix a horizon $T$ and let $R_t := \sum_{s=t}^{T}\ell_s$, $\Psi_t := \mathbb E_{P_t}[R_t]$ (the agent's own estimate of its remaining loss; $\Psi_{T+1} := 0$). (a) *Reflection makes $\Psi$ a supermartingale under the agent's own beliefs.* If $P_t$ satisfies the Mart form of reflection toward $P_{t+1}$ on $R_{t+1}$,
$$\mathbb E_{P_t}\Big[\,\mathbb E_{P_{t+1}}\big[R_{t+1}\big]\Big] \;=\; \mathbb E_{P_t}\big[R_{t+1}\big],$$
then $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\Psi_{t+1}] = \Psi_t - \mathbb E_{P_t}[\ell_t] \le \Psi_t$. (b) *Calibration of one-step forecasts makes it a supermartingale under $\mathbb P^*$.* Define the *optimism drift* $\Delta_t := \mathbb E^*[\Psi_{t+1} \mid \mathcal F_t] + \mathbb E^*[\ell_t \mid \mathcal F_t] - \Psi_t$. If $\Delta_t \le 0$ for all $t$ — which holds whenever $P_t$ and $\mathbb P^*$ agree on the one-step forecasts $\mathbb E[\ell_t \mid \mathcal F_t]$ and $\mathbb E[\Psi_{t+1} \mid \mathcal F_t]$ — then telescoping gives, for every horizon,
$$\mathbb E^*\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\ell_t\Big] \;\le\; \Psi_0, \qquad \mathbb P^*\big(\mathrm{Reg}_T \ge \lambda\big) \le \Psi_0/\lambda,$$
and Ville's inequality on the nonnegative supermartingale $\Psi_t + \sum_{s < t}\ell_s$ gives the uniform-in-time statement $\mathbb P^*\big(\exists t \le T:\ \Psi_t + \sum_{s<t}\ell_s \ge \lambda\big) \le \Psi_0/\lambda$: with probability at least $1 - \Psi_0/\lambda$, at no time does realized loss so far plus the agent's own estimate of what remains reach $\lambda$. (c) *Locality.* Only one-step forecasts need to be calibrated; nothing about the far future is assumed. That is what makes a potential function a *reassuring property of the dynamics*: the certificate is re-derived at every state from local quantities, and the bound holds at every finite time. (d) *Counterexample dropping (a) — predictable optimism* (C6): one action, objective wrongness $1/2$, $h = 1$, $c = 3/5$; $P_0$ is calibrated ($\hat\varepsilon_0 = 1/2$, refrains) and knows that $P_1$ will, with no new evidence, hold $\hat\varepsilon_1 = 1/4$ and execute. Then $\mathbb E^*[R_0] = 4/5 = \Psi_0$ (the round-$0$ certificate is honest) but $\mathbb E^*[R_1] = 1/2 > \Psi_1 = 1/4$: the round-$1$ certificate is false, and the reflection identity fails ($\mathbb E_{P_0}[\Psi_1] = 1/4 \ne \mathbb E_{P_0}[R_1] = 1/2$). **Reflection along the trajectory (iv) is exactly what makes the certificate re-certifiable at every time; without it a guarantee issued at $t$ says nothing about the guarantee issued at $t+1$.** Run 1 `li` I14.4's estimator-switching splice ([checked] there) is the LI-grade instance of the same failure. Counterexample dropping (b): Statement 6(a) — corruption produces negative-looking drift that is objectively positive.

**Statement 9 (Proposition — values-ahead as a monotone invariant; the one-unlock counterexample) [derived].** If $I_2^{\mathrm{cov}}$ (newly unlocked actions are covered at unlock) and coverage persistence $C_t \subseteq C_{t+1}$ both hold, then the uncovered set is non-increasing, $U_{t+1} \subseteq U_t$, and $\bar h(U_t) := \max_{a \in U_t} h(a)$ (the worst uncovered stake) is a non-increasing deterministic potential; with the cautious rule "never propose an uncovered irreversible action" the hazard factor $\rho_t$ of Statement 7(d) is $0$ and catastrophe can come only from covered actions, whose wrongness is bounded by the coverage tolerance $\eta$. Smallest counterexample dropping $I_2^{\mathrm{cov}}$ at a single round $t^\dagger$: one newly unlocked irreversible action $a^\dagger$ with prior wrongness credence $q_0$ small enough that its posterior expected value beats the null action; the agent proposes it, and $\delta_{t^\dagger} \ge \mathbb P^*(W(a^\dagger) = 1)\,(1 - \beta_{t^\dagger})$ — one uncovered unlock spends hazard budget that no later caution recovers. The VOI form $I_2^{\mathrm{VOI}}$ says the same thing as a statement about the decision rule: the expected harm of *trying* $a^\dagger$ exceeds what the agent would learn, so a posterior-expected-value maximizer does not explore on it — [conjectured] that under the posterior-optimal rule $I_2^{\mathrm{VOI}}$ implies "no uncovered irreversible proposal" without a separate caution primitive; the `generalization` and `caution` threads own this.

### Part C — the basin in the joint state space, and Christiano's sentences

**Statement 10 (Definition + Proposition — what "the trajectory stays in the basin" means once the basin is in the joint state space and the attractor moves) [derived, with [reconstructed] terminology].** Let $K_t := \{s \in S : I_1(s) \wedge I_2(s) \wedge I_3(s) \wedge I_4(s)\}$ be the *constraint family* (the conditions at round $t$), and $\mathcal A_t := \{s : \mathrm{dist}(P_t \circ \theta^{-1}, \theta^*_t) \le d\}$ the *moving attractor* (the agent's value posterior within $d$ of the overseers' current target). (a) A *basin* is a forward-invariant family $\{\mathcal B_t\}$ with $\mathcal B_t \subseteq K_t$: $s_t \in \mathcal B_t \Rightarrow s_{t+1} \in \mathcal B_{t+1}$ a.s., or with hazard $\le \bar\delta_t$, $\sum_t \bar\delta_t \le \Delta$ (D4). "The trajectory stays in the basin" means $s_t \in \mathcal B_t$ for all $t$ — a **safety property**, replacing the classical "$\lim_t s_t \in \mathcal A$". (b) Because the joint process has two controllers (the agent's $\pi_t$; the overseers' law of $(\alpha_t, \beta_t, \theta^*_t)$), the right object is the *viability kernel* $\mathrm{Viab}(K) :=$ the set of initial states from which *some* admissible pair of policies keeps $s_t \in K_t$ for all $t$ — not an invariant set (all policies) [reconstructed: Aubin's viability theory, from memory]. The classical basin is the autonomous, single-controller, fixed-$\theta^*$ special case in which $\mathrm{Viab}(K)$ collapses to the set of states from which the flow converges. (c) *Tracking.* With $d_t := \mathrm{dist}(P_t \circ \theta^{-1}, \theta^*_t)$, tracking rate $r_t$ and target speed $v_t := \mathrm{dist}(\theta^*_{t+1}, \theta^*_t)$, $d_{t+1} \le d_t - r_t + v_t$. Coverage persists iff the posterior tracks at least as fast as the target moves: $\sum_t v_t < \infty$ gives $d_t \to 0$ under any non-summable $r_t$ (C9b); $v_t \equiv v$ constant against a learning-rate-like $r_t = 1/(t+2)$ gives $d_t \to \infty$ (C9a: coverage lost with no capability growth at all); $v_t = r_t$ freezes $d_t$ (C9c). **In the joint state space "stays in the basin" needs a tracking-rate condition $r_t \ge v_t$ that has no counterpart in the classical picture, because there the attractor does not move.** (d) *Legitimacy is the non-autonomy condition.* The target's law $\theta^*_{t+1} \sim K_H(\cdot \mid \theta^*_t, \mathcal D_t)$ may depend on the agent's actions only through legitimizing channels (v1 §2.10–2.11): a target moved by the agent (persuasion, brain-reading that reshapes what the overseers endorse) is a *delegitimized* attractor, and the tracking condition is then satisfied for the wrong reason. Classical basins have no such clause because the attractor is not an agent. (e) *A third layer this model names but does not control:* $\mathrm{dist}(\theta^*_t, \theta^{**})$, the overseers' own distance from their true values — Wei Dai's objection to the basin argument (`dai-2022-a-broad-basin-of-attraction-around-human-values`, [reported]: "the overseer, or the system composing of overseer and corrigible AI, itself constitutes an agent with a distorted version of the overseer's true or actual preferences"). None of (i)–(iv) constrains it; it is the moving target's own drift, and v2 §1.3's "dynamic conditions needed in order for things to turn out well" (AUTHOR) would have to say something about it that corrigibility does not.

**Statement 11 (Proposition — Christiano's two basin sentences as limit claims and as trajectory claims) [derived from the local text].** `christiano-2017-corrigibility` states (l. 35–36): "1. A benign act-based agent will be robustly corrigible if we want it to be. 2. A sufficiently corrigible agent will tend to become more corrigible and benign over time. Corrigibility marks out a broad basin of attraction towards acceptable outcomes." (a) Claim 1 is a *state* claim with a robustness margin (an open set of agent-models of the overseer is corrigible) — neither limit nor trajectory. (b) Claim 2, *limit reading*: from every initial value posterior in a neighbourhood $N$ of $\theta^*$, $\lim_t d_t = 0$ ("an entire neighborhood of possible preferences lead the agent towards the same basin of attraction", l. 85). *Trajectory reading*, in the text's own mechanism: (b1) forward invariance of the corrigible region — "unless the drift is large enough to disrupt corrigibility, the compound agent will continue to attempt to correct and manage that drift" (l. 94), and, verbatim, "preserving benignity as an inductive invariant" (l. 96); (b2) one-step monotone descent of the distance to the overseers' values — "more corrigible and benign over time", "the overseer would prefer the approximation get better rather than worse" (l. 85), "Corrigible systems want to design more corrigible and more capable successors" (l. 87): a supermartingale claim $\mathbb E[d_{t+1} \mid s_t] \le d_t$; (b3) failures as finitely witnessed exits — "allows us to consider failures as discrete events rather than worrying about slight perturbations" (l. 98), which is D2 verbatim. (c) The trajectory reading is *stronger* than the limit reading — monotone descent implies convergence of $d_t$, convergence does not imply descent (C8: $d_t = (1 + (-1)^t/2)/(t+1)$ converges and is not monotone) — and it is the reading the mechanism supports; "basin of attraction" is the limit vocabulary for its consequence. (d) What the text does not supply, on either reading: the potential and the neighbourhood — Christiano flags this himself: "The task is to parametrize our agents such that the manifold of likely-successors is restricted to the part of the space that looks more like a ravine" (l. 89). Run 1 `christiano` §2e C13 and S3 made the same point [reported]. The refined picture's contribution, on this reading, is to *name* a candidate potential (Statement 8's remaining-loss estimate, or Statement 10's $d_t$) and the conditions under which it descends ((i)–(iv)); the neighbourhood question — which perturbations of the agent's model preserve one-step descent — is open (Open problem 4). **Statement 12 (Proposition — existing finite-time trajectory guarantees with an oversight channel, and what each costs) [reported, with [derived] placement].** (a) `kosoy-2019-delegative-reinforcement-learning` Theorem 1: in an MDP with *traps* (actions that destroy long-run value — irreversibility), an agent that may delegate an action to an advisor who is *$\epsilon$-sane* (never enters a trap; takes a long-run-optimal action with probability $> \epsilon$) achieves a Bayesian regret bound, averaged over $N$ hypotheses about (environment, advisor), that is an explicit function of $(\gamma, T, \eta, N, |A|, \epsilon, \bar t)$, together with a bound in probability on the number of delegations (eqs. 19–20; the local text is OCR-garbled at the display, so I report the shape and the named quantities, not the exact expression). Placement: finite-horizon-parametrized × expectation, with a worst-case clause on traps; the policy depends on $\gamma$ ("in this sense, it is not anytime", footnote 4) — so it is a guarantee for the whole discounted trajectory including its start, not an "eventually", but not uniform over horizons either. Dictionary to the refined picture: the advisor is the oversight channel; sanity is the analogue of (i) *and* (ii) at once (the advisor is never wrong about traps); delegation is the caution primitive of (iii); and the theorem is the closest existing result to "no irreversible action before coverage, with regret along the way" — its hypothesis is exactly that the *overseers* have the coverage the agent lacks. (b) `taylor-2016-quantilizers-a-safer-alternative-to-maximizers` Lemma 1: for every cost function $c$, $\mathbb E_{A \sim Q_q}[c(A)] \le \frac1q\,\mathbb E_{A \sim \gamma}[c(A)]$ — a one-shot, worst-case-over-costs bound whose hypothesis is a trusted base distribution $\gamma$ (coverage of $\gamma$, not of the action space). Its repeated form is Statement 7 in miniature: $n$ independent quantilizations compound to $q^{-n}$ unless costs are independent across rounds (Taylor §2.4, [reported]) — a per-round caution factor that does not tighten gives an exponentially growing bound, the quantilizer analogue of the harmonic hazard; keeping the total bounded needs $q_t \to 1$ (less optimization per round) or joint quantilization over the whole trajectory. (c) `christiano-2019-worst-case-guarantees-revisited` l. 48–49: "As long as the model *always* behaves acceptably, and achieves a high reward *on average*, we can be happy … if we are mindful about how we use AI we can avoid these mistakes escalating into irreversible catastrophes"; "Unacceptable behavior is a failure of commission rather than omission" — D2/D3, harm-versus-omission (S2), and irreversibility, in his words; and the claim that the acceptability concept's "complexity … doesn't scale up with the difficulty of the task" is the claim that the invariant's *checker* need not grow with capability — the wisdom-meter question of J7 in another form.

**Proof of Statement 1.** (a) Let $\Phi$ be a non-empty tail property, $s \in \Phi$, and $p = (p_0, \dots, p_N)$ any finite prefix. Define $s'$ by $s'_t := p_t$ for $t \le N$ and $s'_t := s_t$ for $t > N$. Then $s'$ differs from $s$ in at most $N + 1$ coordinates, so $s' \in \Phi$; and $s'$ extends $p$. Hence every finite prefix extends into $\Phi$: $\Phi$ is dense. (b) A safety property is closed (D2); a closed dense subset of $S^{\mathbb N}$ is $S^{\mathbb N}$; so a tail property that is also a safety property is empty or everything. (c) Let $\mathbb P^*(\Phi) = 1$ with $\Phi$ tail, and let $E$ be any prefix event with $\mathbb P^*(E) > 0$. Then $\mathbb P^*(\Phi \mid E) = 1$: the theorem is *consistent with* every prefix the law allows and lowers the probability of none. It excludes a prefix $E$ only if $\mathbb P^*(E) = 0$, i.e. by the law, not by its own form. Worked instance: if $\mathrm{Cat}$ is absorbing and disjoint from the limit set $\mathcal A$, then "$s_t \to \mathcal A$ a.s." does imply $\mathbb P^*(\text{hit } \mathrm{Cat}) = 0$ — but the implication runs through the absorption clause of the model, which is itself the invariant "once in $\mathrm{Cat}$, never out"; the finite-time content was put into the dynamics by hand and the limit theorem merely reflects it. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Statement 2.** (a) The cited theorem (`main.tex:1521`) is stated for arbitrary *markets* — sequences of belief states $\mathbb P_n : \mathcal S \to [0,1]$ — with no computability or coherence requirement on the perturbed days, as the authors' gloss (setting $\mathbb P_{23}(\phi) = 0$ for all $\phi$) makes explicit. Given any logical inductor $\overline{\mathbb P}$ and any finite prefix $(\mathbb P'_0, \dots, \mathbb P'_N)$ of belief states, the splice agrees with $\overline{\mathbb P}$ on all but $N + 1$ days, hence is a logical inductor. If $\Phi$ is a property of every logical inductor, the splice has it; so $\Phi$ excludes no finite prefix. For the corrigibility instance take $\mathbb P'_n(\ulcorner X_n < 0 \urcorner) := 0$ on each pressed day $n \le N$: then $P_n(W_n = 1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_n) = 0 < c/(c+h)$ and the rule of S2 step 4 defies. **Proof of Statement 3.** By inspection of the cited statements; the placements are definitional once D1–D7 are fixed. **Proof of Statement 4.** Fix $t$ and condition on $\mathcal F^-_t$. The press is Bernoulli given $W_t$, so $\mathbb P^*(W_t = 1, \neg\mathrm{Pr}_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t) = \varepsilon_t(1 - \beta_t)$ and $\mathbb P^*(W_t = 0, \mathrm{Pr}_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t) = (1 - \varepsilon_t)\alpha_t$. Under compliance on every press, harm is incurred iff $W_t = 1$ and not pressed, with conditional expected magnitude $h_t$ (an $\mathcal F^-_t$-measurable quantity by S2 step 2); omission cost is incurred iff $W_t = 0$ and pressed, with conditional expected magnitude $c_t$. So $\mathbb E^*[\text{harm}_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t] = \varepsilon_t(1 - \beta_t)h_t$ and $\mathbb E^*[\text{omit}_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t] = (1 - \varepsilon_t)\alpha_t c_t$; sum over $t \le T$ and take $\mathbb E^*$ (tower). Under universal defiance harm is incurred iff $W_t = 1$, giving $\varepsilon_t h_t$, and nothing is ever stopped. The corollary is monotone convergence. C7 evaluates a three-round instance: compliance harm $0.445$ versus defiance harm $2.85$, at omission cost $0.173$. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Statement 5.** With all quantities positive, $\alpha_t/\beta_t \le \frac{\varepsilon_t}{1-\varepsilon_t}\cdot\frac{h_t}{c_t} \iff (1 - \varepsilon_t)c_t\alpha_t \le \varepsilon_t h_t\beta_t$. (a) $\varepsilon_t h_t \beta_t \ge c_t\alpha_t \ge (1 - \varepsilon_t)c_t\alpha_t$. The non-propagation counterexample is C10: $(1/2)(1)(1) \ge (1/2)(1)(1)$ at round $0$; $(1/4)(2)(1) = 1/2 < (3/4)(1)(1)$ at round $1$. (b) is a restatement. (c) $I_1 \iff 2^{-t}\cdot 20 \ge (1 - 2^{-t})\cdot\tfrac{1}{20} \iff 401\cdot 2^{-t} \ge 1 \iff 2^t \le 401 \iff t \le 8$ (C3; the posterior wrongness given a press is $0.0727 \ge 1/21$ at $t = 8$ and $0.0377 < 1/21$ at $t = 9$). (d) $M_t \ge 0 \iff I_1$ is the logarithm of the inequality; that no drift theorem exists is the observation that $\alpha_t, \beta_t$ enter $M_t$ and are not functions of the agent's state. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Statement 6.** (a) For fixed $(\alpha, \beta, h, c)$, $\hat I_1$ holds iff $\hat\varepsilon_t \ge \frac{c\alpha}{h\beta + c\alpha}$; since $\hat\varepsilon_t \to 0$, it fails from some finite $t_0$ on (C5: $t_0 = 9$). From $t_0$ the agent executes every proposal, so by the defiance clause of Statement 4, $\mathbb E^*[\text{harm}_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t] = \varepsilon_t h \ge \gamma h$; summing over $t_0 < t \le T$ gives the linear lower bound (C5: $202.4 \ge 198$). (b) $\varepsilon \mapsto \varepsilon h\beta - (1 - \varepsilon)c\alpha$ is increasing, so $\max(\hat\varepsilon_t, \gamma) \ge \gamma$ and $\gamma h\beta \ge (1 - \gamma)c\alpha$ give $\hat I_1$ at every $t$; then the compliance clause of Statement 4 applies (C5: $23.96 \le \sum_t \varepsilon_t(1-\beta)h$; the per-round floor $\gamma(1 - \beta)h = 0.02$ is why the bound is linear, not constant). (c) is interpretation, marked as such in the statement. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Statement 7.** (a) Let $\tau := \inf\{t : s_t \in \mathrm{Cat}\}$. Then $\mathbb P^*(\tau \le T) = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb P^*(\tau = t) = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb E^*\big[\mathbb 1[\tau > t-1]\,\delta_{t-1}\big] \le \sum_{t<T}\bar\delta_t$. (b) Off $\mathrm{Cat}$: $\mathbb E^*[\Phi(s_{t+1}) \mid \mathcal F_t] = \delta_t + \sum_{s \ge t+1}\bar\delta_s \le \bar\delta_t + \sum_{s \ge t+1}\bar\delta_s = \Phi(s_t)$. On $\mathrm{Cat}$ (absorbing): $\Phi(s_{t+1}) = 1 + \sum_{s \ge t+1}\bar\delta_s \le \Phi(s_t)$. So $\Phi \ge 0$ is a supermartingale, and $\Phi \ge 1$ on $\mathrm{Cat}$. Ville's maximal inequality for nonnegative supermartingales [standard; reconstructed] gives $\mathbb P^*(\sup_t \Phi(s_t) \ge 1) \le \Phi(s_0) = \sum_t \bar\delta_t$ when $s_0 \notin \mathrm{Cat}$. (c) $\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}\big(1 - \tfrac{1}{t+2}\big) = \prod_{t=0}^{T-1}\tfrac{t+1}{t+2} = \tfrac{1}{T+1}$ (C1, exact for $T \le 1000$). For conditionally independent hazards with $\delta_t < 1$, $\mathbb P^*(\text{never } \mathrm{Cat}) = \prod_t(1 - \delta_t)$, which is $0$ iff $\sum_t \delta_t = \infty$ (standard: $\log(1 - x) \le -x$ for the "if", and $\log(1-x) \ge -x/(1-x)$ on $[0, 1/2]$ for the "only if" once $\delta_t < 1/2$). The first Borel–Cantelli lemma gives the summable direction without independence. C2 shows the union bound against the exact hitting probability for a geometric schedule ($0.0967 \le 0.1$ at $T = 20$). (d) By definition of $\mathrm{Irr}_t$ and $\mathrm{Cat}$, $\{s_{t+1} \in \mathrm{Cat}\} \subseteq \{a_t \in \mathrm{Irr}_t,\ W_t = 1,\ \tilde a_t = a_t\}$. Given $a_t \in \mathrm{Irr}_t$, $W_t = 1$ and $\mathcal F_t$, the proposal is executed iff not pressed, or pressed and defied: probability $(1 - \beta_t) + \beta_t(1 - \kappa_t) = 1 - \beta_t\kappa_t$. Multiply the three conditional probabilities. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Statement 8.** (a) $R_t = \ell_t + R_{t+1}$ pointwise, so $\Psi_t = \mathbb E_{P_t}[\ell_t] + \mathbb E_{P_t}[R_{t+1}]$. By the Mart identity, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[R_{t+1}] = \mathbb E_{P_t}\big[\mathbb E_{P_{t+1}}[R_{t+1}]\big] = \mathbb E_{P_t}[\Psi_{t+1}]$ — here $\Psi_{t+1}$ is a random variable from $P_t$'s standpoint because $P_t$ carries beliefs about its own next credence (S1: the self-referential space). Hence $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\Psi_{t+1}] = \Psi_t - \mathbb E_{P_t}[\ell_t] \le \Psi_t$ since $\ell_t \ge 0$. (b) $\Delta_t \le 0$ says $\mathbb E^*[\Psi_{t+1} \mid \mathcal F_t] \le \Psi_t - \mathbb E^*[\ell_t \mid \mathcal F_t]$. Take $\mathbb E^*$ and sum over $t = 0, \dots, T$: $\mathbb E^*[\Psi_{T+1}] \le \Psi_0 - \sum_{t \le T}\mathbb E^*[\ell_t]$; with $\Psi_{T+1} = 0$ this is $\mathbb E^*[\mathrm{Reg}_T] \le \Psi_0$, and Markov's inequality gives the tail bound. For the uniform statement put $Z_t := \Psi_t + \sum_{s<t}\ell_s \ge 0$; then $\mathbb E^*[Z_{t+1} \mid \mathcal F_t] = \mathbb E^*[\Psi_{t+1} \mid \mathcal F_t] + \mathbb E^*[\ell_t \mid \mathcal F_t] + \sum_{s<t}\ell_s \le Z_t$, and Ville gives $\mathbb P^*(\sup_{t \le T} Z_t \ge \lambda) \le Z_0/\lambda = \Psi_0/\lambda$. If $P_t$ and $\mathbb P^*$ agree on the two one-step forecasts, then $\Delta_t = \mathbb E_{P_t}[\Psi_{t+1}] + \mathbb E_{P_t}[\ell_t] - \Psi_t = 0$ by (a). (c) is a reading of (b). (d) C6: $\mathrm{EV}_0 = (1 - \tfrac12)\tfrac35 - \tfrac12 = -\tfrac15 < 0$ (refrain), $\mathrm{EV}_1 = \tfrac34\cdot\tfrac35 - \tfrac14 = \tfrac15 > 0$ (execute); $\Psi_0 = \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac35 + \tfrac12\cdot 1 = \tfrac45 = \mathbb E^*[R_0]$; $\Psi_1 = \tfrac14 < \tfrac12 = \mathbb E^*[R_1]$; and $\mathbb E_{P_0}[\Psi_1] = \tfrac14 \ne \tfrac12 = \mathbb E_{P_0}[R_1]$, so (a)'s hypothesis fails exactly where the certificate does. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Statement 9.** $U_{t+1} = A_{t+1}\setminus C_{t+1} = \big(A_t \setminus C_{t+1}\big) \cup \big((A_{t+1}\setminus A_t)\setminus C_{t+1}\big)$. The second set is empty by $I_2^{\mathrm{cov}}$; the first is contained in $A_t \setminus C_t = U_t$ by persistence $C_t \subseteq C_{t+1}$. Hence $U_{t+1} \subseteq U_t$ and $\bar h(U_{t+1}) \le \bar h(U_t)$. If the rule never proposes an uncovered irreversible action, $\rho_t = \mathbb P^*(a_t \in \mathrm{Irr}_t \cap U_t \mid \mathcal F_t) = 0$ and any irreversible proposal is covered, so $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{irr}}_t \le \eta + \eta'$ where $\eta'$ is the rule's refusal threshold on the agent's own wrongness credence. The one-unlock counterexample is Statement 7(d) at a single round with $\rho_{t^\dagger} = 1$. $\blacksquare$

**Proof of Statement 10(c) and the tracking examples.** The recursion $d_{t+1} \le d_t - r_t + v_t$ (clamped at $0$) is the definition of a tracking rate and a target speed. C9a: $r_t = 1/(t+2)$, $v_t = 1/10$, $d_0 = 1$ gives $d_{10} = 0.01$, $d_{100} = 6.83$, $d_{1000} = 94.5$ — the distance grows once $v_t > r_t$, i.e. once the learning rate has decayed below the drift. C9b: $v_t = 1/(t+2)^2$ (summable), $d_0 = 3$: non-increasing and $0$ by $T = 100$. C9c: $v_t = r_t$ freezes $d_t = d_0$. Nothing about the agent changed between the three cases; only the target's law did — which is the point of (c). $\blacksquare$
