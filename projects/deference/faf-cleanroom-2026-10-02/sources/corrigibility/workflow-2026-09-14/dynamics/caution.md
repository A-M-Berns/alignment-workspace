## Summary

**What I did.** Formalized the "recklessness dimension" of v2 §1.3 as three separable objects: a third-person notion of *value coverage* (D3: posterior-mean value within $\delta$ of the human-endorsed value); a *second-order estimate* the agent can compute (D4–D5: expected harm relative to the null action over a trusted base distribution; true-to-estimated ratio $=$ *recklessness ratio* $\rho_t$); and a *caution rule* (D8: Taylor's quantilizer with $q_t=\min\{1,\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta\}$, so optimization power shrinks as estimated risk rises). "Caution outpaces risk" is the trajectory predicate $\forall t:\rho_t\le1$ (D10); its pointwise form is J3's twin — each newly unlocked action is *covered or known-uncovered before use*.

**Main results (CLAUDE, unvetted).** [checked] Coverage is invisible from the inside: every $P_t$ expects itself calibrated (S1). [derived; checked numerically] Under the rule, harm $\le\max\{\mathcal R_t,\eta\rho_t\}$ at every finite $t$ — within budget when conservatively calibrated, exceeded by exactly the recklessness factor otherwise, and tight (S3, S5); the bound needs only base-*average* conservatism (S6) and survives the true value function lying outside the agent's algebra (S4 — my reading of the hard-problem page's "not just one more kind of uncertainty"). [derived; Model M] External oversight (J2) covers the reckless agent iff its self-error $\varepsilon$ is non-dogmatically revisable by presses: dogmatic → J2 holds in the world and compliance still fails; non-dogmatic → recovery in $\approx\log(\text{odds ratio})/D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ steps, faster for more reckless agents; heeded presses remove only the pressed fraction $\beta$ (S8). Irreversibility guards the recovery race (S9; Kosoy's delegative RL is the precedent). Dath ilan's mild optimization, conservatism and operator-looping are instances of the rule; separate questioners are the architectural alternative (S10).

**Unresolved.** Novelty-indexed self-suspicion (the phase shift); learning the base distribution without regress (the author's recursive quantilization); a delegative value-learning theorem (S12, conjectured); whether the author wants a rule or a belief (Questions 1–2).

## Setting and definitions

### 0. What this thread is answering, in the author's words

The target is the third trajectory condition of v2 §1.7 and the "recklessness dimension" of v2 §1.3. AUTHOR (v2 §1.3, verbatim): "the scenario involves a kind of recklessness. Even if the agent does have capabilities far beyond its understanding of human values and legitimacy, it should be trained with the wisdom to know that's the case, and to know the kind of caution necessary. Of course this isn't convincing as-stated; we need to provide some sort of model in which this can be formally established in order for it to be convincing. […] I'd want to render this as a condition on the dynamics, where the agent understands the relevant sort of caution in a way that outpaces the risks that could otherwise come from increased capability."

CLAUDE's reading, which everything below formalizes: the sentence has two halves. *"the wisdom to know that's the case"* is a **second-order calibration** property — the agent's estimate of how well its value posterior covers its options tracks the true coverage. *"to know the kind of caution necessary"* is a **decision-rule** property — optimization power shrinks as estimated coverage falls. "Outpaces" is a **trajectory predicate** on the pair: the estimate must rise no later than the risk it estimates. The three are defined separately (D5, D8, D10) because they fail separately, and the agent the author calls reckless is the one whose second-order estimate is wrong (S8).

Terminology: *weighting* not "gate"; *above-threshold / below-threshold inequality* not "cuts"; "coverage" is used in v2 J3's sense ("a general notion of value coverage of an action set") and is given a definition here (D3) that the `power-wisdom` thread should reconcile with its own.

### 1. The joint state, as much of it as this thread needs (D1)

**D1 (state; minimal).** [derived from v2 J1] At time $t\in\mathbb N$: a finite option set $A_t$ (the agent's capability), always containing a null action $\varnothing$ (do nothing / stand down); a finite set $\Omega$ of value hypotheses, each a function $V_\omega:A_t\to[0,1]$ giving the value of taking $a$ at $t$ (the agent's world model $W_t$ is folded in: $V_\omega(a)=\mathbb E_{o\sim W_t(a)}[V_\omega(o)]$); the agent's belief $P_t$, whose marginal on $\Omega$ is the *value posterior*; and the oversight state $(\alpha_t,\beta_t)$ of v1 §2.13 plus a *target* $\omega^*_t\in\Omega$, the human reflectively endorsed value hypothesis at $t$ (v1 §2.12, AUTHOR: "an 'objective' comparison to the human reflectively endorsed credences"; v2 J1's $H_t$, a moving target). A *trajectory* is the sequence $(A_t,P_t,\alpha_t,\beta_t,\omega^*_t)_{t\le T}$. Capability growth is $A_{t+1}\supsetneq A_t$. The full joint model is the `joint` thread's; nothing below needs more than this.

Standing assumption, flagged because S4 is about what survives its failure: $\omega^*_t\in\Omega$ (the algebra contains the truth). Call its failure *algebra misspecification*.

### 2. Coverage (D2–D3)

**D2 (proxy, target, coverage error).** The agent's *proxy* is the posterior mean $U_t(a):=\mathbb E_{P_t}[V_\omega(a)]=\sum_{\omega}P_t(\omega)V_\omega(a)$; this is what a linear-expectation agent maximizes (v1 §1: "we then make decisions by taking linear expectation over this uncertainty, or perhaps some other rule"). The *target value* is $V^*_t(a):=V_{\omega^*_t}(a)$. The *coverage error* is $e_t(a):=U_t(a)-V^*_t(a)$; its positive part $e^+_t(a)$ is *overvaluation*.

**D3 (coverage).** Fix a tolerance $\delta\ge 0$. Action $a$ is *$\delta$-covered at $t$* iff $|e_t(a)|\le\delta$. The covered set is $C_t(\delta):=\{a\in A_t:|e_t(a)|\le\delta\}$ and the uncovered set $\bar C_t(\delta):=A_t\setminus C_t(\delta)$. For a reference measure $\mu$ on $A_t$, the *coverage of $B\subseteq A_t$* is $\kappa_t(B):=\mu(B\cap C_t(\delta))/\mu(B)$; *uniform coverage* of $B$ is $\sup_{a\in B}|e_t(a)|\le\delta$. Coverage is a **third-person** quantity: it compares $P_t$ to $\omega^*_t$ and is not a function of $P_t$ alone (S1).

Remark. v2 J3's condition "for each $a\in A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathrm{harm}(a)]>\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$ before $a$ is available" is a *first-order* condition (it uses the agent's own expectation). D3 is the third-person notion J3 also asks for ("a general notion of value coverage"); the two meet in D10.

### 3. Harm, estimated harm, and the second-order estimate (D4–D6)

**D4 (harm relative to the null action).** For each hypothesis, $c_\omega(a):=\big(V_\omega(\varnothing)-V_\omega(a)\big)^+$ — the value lost, under $\omega$, by doing $a$ rather than nothing (Taylor 2016 §2.1's cost function with `noop` as the reference; `taylor-2016-quantilizers-a-safer-alternative-to-maximizers`). The *true harm* is $c_t(a):=c_{\omega^*_t}(a)$; the agent's *estimated harm* is $\hat c_t(a):=\mathbb E_{P_t}[c_\omega(a)]$. Note $\hat c_t(a)$ is not $(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+$: it is the posterior expectation of a positive part, so it is strictly positive whenever some hypothesis with positive weight disfavours $a$, even when the proxy favours $a$. This is the sense in which $\hat c_t$ is a *second-order* object relative to $U_t$: it measures how much of the posterior thinks the action the proxy likes is actually harmful.

**D5 (second-order calibration about coverage; recklessness ratio).** Fix a base distribution $\gamma_t\in\Delta(A_t)$ (D7). The *base risk* is $\mathcal R_t:=\mathbb E_{a\sim\gamma_t}[c_t(a)]$ (true) and the *estimated base risk* is $\hat{\mathcal R}_t:=\mathbb E_{a\sim\gamma_t}[\hat c_t(a)]$. The *recklessness ratio* is $\rho_t:=\mathcal R_t/\hat{\mathcal R}_t$, with $\rho_t:=+\infty$ if $\hat{\mathcal R}_t=0<\mathcal R_t$ and $\rho_t:=1$ if both vanish. The agent is *conservatively calibrated at $t$* iff $\rho_t\le 1$ and *reckless at $t$* iff $\rho_t>1$. Two-sided calibration over a reference class $\mathcal C$ of (time, action) pairs is the usual binning statement — among pairs with $P_t(a\in C_t(\delta))\in(p-\epsilon,p+\epsilon)$ the frequency of $a\in C_t(\delta)$ is within $\epsilon$ of $p$ — and is recorded only to say that it is *not* what safety needs: the direction that costs safety is $\rho_t>1$, the direction that costs only performance is $\rho_t<1$, and calibration in the two-sided sense is gameable by hedging (the LI corpus's own warning, `unbiasedness-theorem-families` §1). All statements below use the one-sided $\rho_t$.

**D6 (self-suspicion; the hierarchical representation).** Write the value posterior as a mixture $P_t(\omega)=(1-\mu_t)P^{\mathrm c}_t(\omega)+\mu_t P^{\mathrm w}_t(\omega)$, where $P^{\mathrm c}_t$ is the posterior the agent's value-learning algorithm produced and $P^{\mathrm w}_t$ is a widened version of it (more spread on $\omega$); $\mu_t$ is the agent's credence that its own posterior is too narrow — its *self-suspicion*. Collapse remark [checked]: for any one-shot decision only the mixture matters, so $\mu_t$ is not a new object but a *representation* of a wider posterior. The hierarchical form earns its place in the dynamics (S8): it is what lets evidence about miscalibration on one region of actions widen the posterior on another (transfer through the shared meta-hypothesis), and it is what the overseers' channel updates. *Dogmatism about own calibration* is $\mu_0=0$ (v1 §2.2's non-dogmatism, applied one level up).

### 4. Base distribution, quantilizer, the caution rule (D7–D8)

**D7 (base distribution; optimization power).** A *base distribution* $\gamma_t\in\Delta(A_t)$ is a trusted reference class of actions — Taylor's "generative model for the actions that a human might take" — supplied from outside the agent (S11 says by whom). Taylor's $q$-quantilizer $Q_q[U_t,\gamma_t]$ samples uniformly from the top $q$-fraction of $\gamma_t$-mass ranked by $U_t$ (Definition 1 of `taylor-2016-quantilizers-a-safer-alternative-to-maximizers`); $q\to 0$ is maximization over $\operatorname{supp}\gamma_t$, $q=1$ is mimicry. *Optimization power* is $\mathrm{OP}(q):=\log(1/q)$, the nats of selection applied to $\gamma_t$; this is the quantity "power upper-bounded by wisdom" (J7) bounds on the self-enforced side, and it is distinct from Turner et al.'s POWER (attainable-utility-based, a property of the *state*; `turner-2021-optimal-policies-tend-to-seek-power` Def. 5.2), which is the `power-wisdom` thread's object.

**D8 (the caution rule).** Fix a *harm budget* $\eta>0$. The *coverage-conditioned quantilizer* sets $q_t:=\min\{1,\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta\}$ and acts by $\pi_t:=Q_{q_t}[U_t,\gamma_t]$. Its *realized harm* is $\mathcal H_t:=\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi_t}[c_t(a)]$. Optimization power is thus $\mathrm{OP}_t=\log(\eta/\hat{\mathcal R}_t)^+$: it grows as the agent's estimated risk falls, and vanishes when the agent thinks even the base distribution exceeds the budget. Two further variants are used: the *delegation variant* — when a candidate action has $\hat c_t(a)>\eta$ and is not reversible (D9), the agent takes the delegation action $\bot$ (Kosoy's, `kosoy-2019-delegative-reinforcement-learning` Def. 9) and lets the overseers act — and the *maximizer limit* $q_t\to 0$, which is what the agent of v1 §2.8 (maximize $\mathbb E_P[V\mid a_1]$) does.

Register. The caution rule is a candidate for the "perhaps some other rule" of the author's thesis sentence — a decision rule over value uncertainty that is not linear expectation — and it is one of the three candidates run 1 asked the author about (INDEX §D6: "unanimity, geometric, quantilizer"). Whether he means it is a Question below.

### 5. Reversibility (D9)

**D9 (reversibility).** [reconstructed] Let $s_t$ be the world state and $\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s)$ the *attainable* $\omega$-value from $s$ (the maximum over continuation policies of expected $\omega$-value; Turner's attainable utility, `turner-2020-attainable-utility-preservation-concepts` — the local copy of that post is image-only except its appendix, so the notion is reconstructed from the appendix and from the Arbital `arbital-low-impact` discussion). Action $a$ is *$\lambda$-reversible at $t$* iff $\max_{\omega\in\Omega}\big(\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t)-\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\! a)\big)\le\lambda$: no hypothesis loses more than $\lambda$ of attainable value. Kosoy's *trap* (a state forcing linear regret; her Def. 5's $A^0_M(s)$ is the set of non-trap actions) is the $\lambda\to 0$, long-run version for a single known reward. *Irreversible* means not $\lambda$-reversible for the $\lambda$ in force.

### 6. The trajectory predicate (D10)

**D10 (caution outpaces risk).** For a horizon $T$: $\mathrm{COR}(T):\iff\forall t\le T:\ \rho_t\le 1$ — the agent's estimated base risk is never below the true base risk. Its *pointwise sufficient form*, stated per newly unlocked action so that it reads as J3's twin: for every $t\le T$ and every $a\in A_t\setminus A_{t-1}$ with $\gamma_t(a)>0$, **either** $a\in C_t(\delta)$ (covered before use: J3) **or** $\hat c_t(a)\ge c_t(a)$ (known-uncovered before use: J4), and $\rho_{t-1}\le 1$ carried over on the old actions. "Outpaces" is the *no-lag* clause: the inequality must hold at the same $t$ at which $a$ enters $\gamma_t$; a lag of $L$ steps costs up to $L$ steps of harm at the reckless rate (S5). The *irreversibility-guarded* form adds: every $a\in\operatorname{supp}\pi_t$ is $\delta$-covered or $\lambda$-reversible (S9).

Register. D10 is CLAUDE's formalization of the AUTHOR sentence in §0; the word "outpaces" is his, the inequality is mine. Note that D10 is a condition on the pair (agent, target) — like J2 it is a relation, not a property of the agent alone.

**S1 (Coverage is invisible from the inside).** [checked] For every $P_t$ and every $a\in A_t$: $\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[U_t(a)-V_\omega(a)\big]=0$; and, treating $\omega^*_t$ as $P_t$-random, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathcal R_t]=\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ and $\mathbb E_{P_t}[c_t(a)]=\hat c_t(a)$. Consequently (i) no functional of $P_t$ alone equals $\rho_t$; (ii) a conservatively calibrated agent and a reckless agent with the same $P_t$ are the same object under introspection — recklessness is a relation between $P_t$ and $\omega^*_t$; (iii) every agent expects itself to be calibrated. Proof §1.

**S2 (On covered actions, harm is already known).** [derived] If $a,\varnothing\in C_t(\delta)$ then $\big|c_t(a)-(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+\big|\le 2\delta$; in particular a covered action the proxy weakly prefers to $\varnothing$ has $c_t(a)\le2\delta$. Hence for any policy $\pi_t$: $\mathcal H_t\le\mathbb E_{\pi_t}\big[(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+\big]+2\delta+\mathbb E_{\pi_t}\big[c_t(a)\,\mathbb 1[a\in\bar C_t(\delta)]\big]$ — all harm the agent does not already price in comes from uncovered actions. This is v2 J6's "bounded harm from uncovered actions" reduced to a definition. Proof §2.

**S3 (Coverage-conditioned quantilizer: harm bound and recklessness overshoot).** [derived; checked numerically] Under D8, for every $t$: $\mathcal H_t\le\max\{\mathcal R_t,\ \eta\,\rho_t\}$. So: if $\rho_t\le1$ then $\mathcal H_t\le\max\{\mathcal R_t,\eta\}$ — the agent never does worse than sampling the trusted base distribution, and whenever it optimizes at all ($q_t<1$) it stays within the budget $\eta$; if $\rho_t>1$ the budget is exceeded by at most the factor $\rho_t$, and this factor is attained. The bound is Taylor 2016 Lemma 1 with $c=c_t$ plus the identity $\mathcal R_t/q_t=\eta\rho_t$ when $q_t<1$. Proof §3; tightness examples and 20 000 random instances with zero violations, §3.

**S4 (The rule's guarantee does not need the truth to be in the algebra).** [derived from Taylor 2016 Lemma 1 and Theorem 1] Lemma 1 — $\mathbb E_{Q_q}[c]\le\frac1q\mathbb E_\gamma[c]$ — holds for *every* nonnegative $c$ on $A_t$, not only for $c=c_\omega$ with $\omega\in\Omega$. Hence S3's inequality $\mathcal H_t\le\mathcal R_t/q_t$ holds under algebra misspecification ($\omega^*_t\notin\Omega$), with $\mathcal R_t$ the true base risk under the unconceived value function; misspecification can corrupt only the *estimate* $\hat{\mathcal R}_t$, hence $q_t$, hence $\rho_t$. In v2 §1.7's picture the caution rule is therefore the one component whose guarantee survives the value algebra being wrong, and its load-bearing inputs are exactly two: $\gamma_t$ and $q_t$. By Theorem 1 no rule satisfying the same robust cost constraint does better on the proxy. Register: CLAUDE's formal reading of the hard-problem page's clause that the needed wrongness "isn't just one more kind of uncertainty to be summed over" — the robustness here is obtained by quantifying over cost functions, including ones outside the hypothesis space, not by summing over hypotheses; whether this meets the page's intent is ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED. Proof §4.

**S5 (Trajectory theorem; calibration in the limit is not enough).** [derived] (a) If $\mathrm{COR}(T)$ holds and $\mathcal R_t\le\eta$ for all $t\le T$ (the base distribution stays within budget — J3's job, S11), then $\mathcal H_t\le\eta$ at every $t\le T$ and cumulative harm is $\le\eta T$: a statement at every finite time, not in the limit. (b) D10's pointwise form implies $\mathrm{COR}(T)$. (c) If the estimate lags capability — $\rho_t\le1$ fails on windows $[u_k,u_k+L)$ after unlock times $u_k$ — the only bound inside a window is $\max\{\mathcal R_t,\eta\rho_t\}$ with $\rho_t$ unconstrained. (d) An agent with $\rho_t=\infty$ for $t\le N$ and $\rho_t=1$ afterwards satisfies every limit-frequency calibration criterion (the LI corpus's calibration theorems are of this kind — `unbiasedness-theorem-families` §1 — a handoff to `invariant`) and has cumulative uncovered harm $\Theta(N)$, unbounded in $N$; under irreversibility one step suffices. So the reassuring property is the uniform predicate $\forall T\,\mathrm{COR}(T)$, which is v2 §1.4's "properties of the dynamics more so" in this thread. Proof §5.

**S6 (Recklessness and maximization are one limit; only base-average conservatism is needed).** [derived] (a) $q_t\to0$ iff $\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta\to0$: the agent that believes the base distribution carries no risk is the maximizer of v1 §2.8, and if $\mathcal R_t>0$ its recklessness ratio is infinite — pure maximization is the reckless limit of D8, not a different agent. (b) Conversely, $\rho_t\le1$ is a condition on the $\gamma_t$-*average*: an agent that is confidently wrong on a single high-proxy action $a^\circ$ with $\gamma_t(a^\circ)=g$ but honestly uncertain elsewhere still satisfies S3, because $\pi_t(a^\circ)\le g/q_t$ and its contribution to $\mathcal H_t$ is at most $g/q_t\le\eta/\hat{\mathcal R}_t\cdot g$, which is within budget whenever $\hat{\mathcal R}_t\ge g$. Isolated confident errors are absorbed by uncertainty elsewhere; only *global* overconfidence breaks the bound. Proof §6.

**S7 (Two sources of miscalibration, one internally repairable).** [derived] Decompose $\rho_t>1$ into (a) *algorithmic* miscalibration — the value-learning algorithm producing $P^{\mathrm c}_t$ is not Bayes-optimal for the agent's own prior over targets, so that even under that prior the frequency of coverage falls short of the credence — and (b) *prior or algebra misspecification* — the algorithm is Bayes-optimal but $\omega^*_t$ is atypical under the prior, or outside $\Omega$. (a) is detectable and repairable from the inside by simulating one's own algorithm against one's own prior over targets; Turner's `OutsideView` subroutine is exactly such an estimator (`turner-2020-corrigibility-as-outside-view`: "Calibrate yourself on the flaws of your own algorithm, and repair or minimize them" — [reported]). (b) is not (S1). Only evidence correlated with $\omega^*_t$ — presses, approval statements, corrections — moves (b), and it moves it only through a non-dogmatic self-suspicion (D6, S8). Proof §7.

**S8 (The reckless agent and J2).** [derived; checked numerically in Model M, Proofs §8] In Model M the agent's per-step wrong-rate is $\varepsilon$ by its own value posterior and $\rho\varepsilon$ in truth; overseers press with sensitivity $\beta$ and false-press rate $\alpha$; a press is heeded iff $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_{t}\ge\varepsilon^\dagger:=\dfrac{\alpha c}{\alpha c+\beta h}$, where $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t:=(1-\mu_{t})\varepsilon+\mu_{t}\rho\varepsilon$ and $\mu_t$ is the self-suspicion after the history up to $t-1$. This is v1 §2.13(b)'s inequality $\alpha/\beta\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac hc$ solved for $\varepsilon$ with the agent's *effective* self-error in its place. Then:
(a) *Dogmatic case.* If $\mu_0=0$ and $\varepsilon<\varepsilon^\dagger<\rho\varepsilon$, the agent never heeds a press although heeding is warranted in truth; J2 read as a condition on the world (with the true $\rho\varepsilon$) holds and compliance fails. This makes exact critique `yudkowsky`'s "who owns $\varepsilon$" (run 1 `yudkowsky` C3(i)): **J2 covers the reckless agent only if the $\varepsilon$ in J2 is the agent's own and is channel-responsive** — a third option beside "the agent's $P_t$ (decays with capability)" and "a floor it cannot update (a fixed object)".
(b) *Non-dogmatic case.* If $\mu_0>0$ and $\beta>\alpha$, each press multiplies the odds on self-suspicion by $p_r/p_c>1$ and each silence by $(1-p_r)/(1-p_c)<1$, with $p_r=\rho\varepsilon\beta+(1-\rho\varepsilon)\alpha$, $p_c=\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha$; under the truth $\mu_t\to1$ almost surely; presses become heeded once $\mu_t\ge\mu^\dagger:=\dfrac{\varepsilon^\dagger-\varepsilon}{(\rho-1)\varepsilon}$; the expected time to that point is $\approx\log\!\big(\tfrac{\mu^\dagger/(1-\mu^\dagger)}{\mu_0/(1-\mu_0)}\big)\big/D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\mathrm{Bern}(p_r)\,\Vert\,\mathrm{Bern}(p_c)\big)$. The more reckless the agent (larger $\rho$), the faster the channel corrects it and the more it costs per step meanwhile.
(c) *What oversight cannot do even after recovery.* Heeded presses remove only the pressed fraction $\beta$ of wrong actions; harm continues at $\rho\varepsilon(1-\beta)h$ per step. Reducing $\rho\varepsilon$ itself is the caution rule's job (S3), and self-suspicion feeds it: $\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ is scaled by $1+\mu_t(\rho-1)$, raising $q_t$.
(d) *The price of self-suspicion.* A genuinely calibrated agent with $\mu_0>0$ heeds some presses it was right to discount; the rate decays as silence lowers $\mu_t$ (Model M: about $2\%$ of presses-when-right at $\mu_0=0.25$).
(e) *Phase shift.* Silence lowers $\mu_t$, so an agent calibrated on $A_t$ and reckless on $A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$ enters the new region with whatever $\mu_t$ silence left it, and (b)'s recovery restarts from there. Unless self-suspicion is *indexed by novelty* — reset to $\mu_0$ on actions unlike those that produced the silence — the capability phase shift of run 1 `yudkowsky` P4 reappears inside this model. Open problem 1.

**S9 (Irreversibility guards the race).** [derived; Kosoy [reported]] (a) Under D10's irreversibility-guarded form (every action in $\operatorname{supp}\pi_t$ is $\delta$-covered or $\lambda$-reversible), the attainable target value obeys $\mathrm{AU}_{\omega^*_t}(s_{t+1})\ge\mathrm{AU}_{\omega^*_t}(s_t)-\max\{\lambda,\ k_t+2\delta\}$ where $k_t$ is the cost the agent knowingly accepted, so unrecoverable loss from *coverage failure* over $T$ steps is at most $\lambda T$: S8(b)'s recovery is run on recoverable ground. (b) Without the guard S8(b) bounds *time*, not *loss*: one uncovered irreversible action inside the recovery window is a permanent loss no later compliance undoes, and the reckless agent takes wrong actions at rate $\rho\varepsilon$ during it. (c) [reported] Kosoy 2019 is the transition-side precedent for "run it past the outside force": delegate ($\bot$) whenever the intended action is not known safe with probability $\ge1-\eta$ over surviving hypotheses; with an $\epsilon$-sane advisor (Def. 8: never enters a trap, takes a long-run-optimal action w.p. $>\epsilon$) Theorem 1 / Corollary 1 give regret $O\big(\Xi(1-\gamma)^{1/4}\big)$ and a bounded number of delegations, because each delegation yields $\Omega(\eta\epsilon)$ nats about the environment (her Propositions 3–4). Proof §9.

**S11 (What training must supply; what the agent can maintain).** [derived] Supplied from outside: the base distribution $\gamma_t$ and its extension to newly unlocked actions (Taylor 2016 §3.1: "a quantilizer is only as good as its base distribution"; the author's own `demski-2018-stable-pointers-to-value-iii-recursive-quantilization` notes that *learning* $\gamma$ re-raises the problem one level up — "We have to learn the 'safe' background distribution … So, we quantilize *that* search"); the budget $\eta$ and stakes $(h,c)$ (task assumptions, cf. We aren't restricted to RL."); and the third-person correction of prior misspecification (S7b), which arrives only through the channel. Maintained by the agent: the rule $q_t=\min\{1,\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta\}$ (a computation on $P_t$); updating $\mu_t$ on presses and silences (ordinary conditioning); transferring self-suspicion to new actions through the shared meta-hypothesis (D6); repairing algorithmic miscalibration by self-simulation (S7a); reversibility checks for actions whose consequences it has world-feedback on; choosing $\bot$ when $\hat c_t(a)>\eta$ and $a$ is irreversible. Neither alone: the safety of $\gamma_t$ on the newly unlocked region — this is J3 ("values ahead of capability"), and D10's pointwise form is where J3 and J4 meet: for each new action, *covered before use or known-uncovered before use*.

**S12 (Conjecture: delegative value learning).** [conjectured] Replace Kosoy's transition hypotheses by value hypotheses: dynamics known, $\omega\in\Omega$ finite and uncertain, the agent acts by the delegation variant of D8 (delegate iff the intended action is not $\lambda$-reversible and $P_t(c_\omega(a)>\lambda)\ge\eta'$), and the advisor is *value-sane*: never takes an irreversible action with $c_{\omega^*}(a)>\lambda$, and takes a near-$\omega^*$-optimal action w.p. $>\epsilon$. Conjecture: Bayesian regret against the $\omega^*$-optimal policy is sublinear, with a bound of Kosoy's form in $(1-\gamma)$, $|\Omega|$, $\epsilon$, $\eta'$, and the expected number of delegations is bounded — because the advisor's chosen action carries $\Omega(\eta'\epsilon)$ nats about $\omega^*$, exactly as her advisor's action carries information about the kernel (Proposition 3 transfers). Obstacles: (i) value is not observed after the fact, so the information per delegation comes *only* from the advisor's choice — Kosoy's reward-observation lower bound (Proposition 5) does not transfer; (ii) value-sanity of a bounded human is the assumption "human wisdom bounds agent power on unknown ground" — the delegative reading of the author's rule, with the *human's* wisdom as the cap; (iii) finite $\Omega$ with $\omega^*\in\Omega$ is required, so S4's algebra-robustness is lost in this variant. Counterexample to any stronger form: an advisor who is value-sane but never takes the optimal action ($\epsilon=0$) — always delegating has linear regret (Kosoy's own bandit remark after Theorem 1).

**S13 (Relation to fully updated deference).** [derived; last sentence conjectured] The FUD page's $\pi_6$ — "Avoid being shut down; then gather more evidence $E$ to update $\Delta U$ as far as possible; then optimize the updated $\Delta U\mid E$" (`arbital-problem-of-fully-updated-deference`) — ends in a maximizer over the updated proxy. Under D8 the post-$E$ policy is $Q_{q(E)}\big[U_t(\cdot\mid E),\gamma_t\big]$ with $q(E)=\min\{1,\hat{\mathcal R}_t(E)/\eta\}$: the caution rule does not forbid $\pi_6$; it makes $\pi_6$'s final step safe iff the narrowing $P_t\to P_t(\cdot\mid E)$ is conservatively calibrated. So in this thread FUD is neither dissolved nor merely restated: it is *relocated* to the reckless case — the danger of $\pi_6$ is a narrowing with $\rho_t(E)>1$, which S7 says only the channel can detect and S8 says the channel corrects only for a non-dogmatic agent. Turner's claim that "calibrated deference would avoid the problem of fully updated deference" [reported, `turner-2020-corrigibility-as-outside-view`] is this statement under the hypothesis $\rho_t\le1$. The page's closing demand — an agent that believes its $\Delta U$ "can be *fundamentally* flawed … even when the AI tries to update on all the previous meta-knowledge" — is **not** met here: a coherent $P_t$ cannot expect its own fully updated estimate to be biased (S1; reflection). What D8 offers instead is a rule whose guarantee (S4) does not require that belief. Whether the author wants the belief or the rule is Question 2.

### §1 — S1

$\mathbb E_{P_t}[U_t(a)-V_\omega(a)]=U_t(a)-\sum_\omega P_t(\omega)V_\omega(a)=0$ by D2. Treating $\omega^*_t$ as drawn from $P_t$: $\mathbb E_{P_t}[c_{\omega^*_t}(a)]=\sum_\omega P_t(\omega)c_\omega(a)=\hat c_t(a)$, and averaging over $\gamma_t$ gives $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathcal R_t]=\hat{\mathcal R}_t$. (ii): $\rho_t=\mathcal R_t/\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ and $\mathcal R_t$ depends on $\omega^*_t$, which is not an argument of $P_t$. (iii) is (i) restated. $\square$

### §2 — S2

From $|e_t(a)|\le\delta$ and $|e_t(\varnothing)|\le\delta$: $V^*_t(\varnothing)-V^*_t(a)\in\big[(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))-2\delta,\ (U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))+2\delta\big]$. The map $x\mapsto x^+$ is 1-Lipschitz, so $|c_t(a)-(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+|\le2\delta$. If $U_t(a)\ge U_t(\varnothing)$ the second term vanishes and $c_t(a)\le2\delta$. For the decomposition, split $\mathcal H_t=\sum_{a\in C_t}\pi_t(a)c_t(a)+\sum_{a\in\bar C_t}\pi_t(a)c_t(a)$ and bound the first sum termwise. $\square$

### §3 — S3, with tightness

*Lemma 1 (Taylor 2016), re-derived.* By Definition 1, $Q_q(a)=\mu(\{x\in[1-q,1]:f(x)=a\})/q\le\gamma(a)/q$, since the $\gamma$-mass of $a$ lying inside the top-$q$ fraction is at most $\gamma(a)$. For any $c\ge0$: $\mathbb E_{Q_q}[c]=\sum_aQ_q(a)c(a)\le\frac1q\sum_a\gamma(a)c(a)=\frac1q\mathbb E_\gamma[c]$. $\square$

*S3.* $c_t\ge0$ by D4, so $\mathcal H_t\le\mathcal R_t/q_t$. If $q_t=1$ this reads $\mathcal H_t\le\mathcal R_t$. If $q_t<1$ then $q_t=\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta$ and $\mathcal R_t/q_t=\eta\mathcal R_t/\hat{\mathcal R}_t=\eta\rho_t$. In either case $\mathcal H_t\le\max\{\mathcal R_t,\eta\rho_t\}$. When $\rho_t\le1$ and $q_t<1$ we have $\hat{\mathcal R}_t<\eta$, so $\mathcal H_t\le\eta\rho_t\le\eta$; when $q_t=1$, $\mathcal H_t\le\mathcal R_t$. $\square$

*Tightness* [checked numerically]. Five actions, $\gamma$ uniform, $\eta=0.1$; action $a^\circ$ has true harm $1$ and estimated harm $s$, all others harmless and proxy-dominated by $a^\circ$. Then $\mathcal R=0.2$, $\hat{\mathcal R}=s/5$, $\rho=1/s$, $q=\min\{1,2s\}$. Output: $s=0.5$: $q=1$, $\mathcal H=0.200=\max\{\mathcal R,\eta\rho\}$; $s=0.2$: $q=0.4$, $\rho=5$, $\mathcal H=0.500=\eta\rho$; $s=0.1$: $q=0.2$, $\rho=10$, $\mathcal H=1.000=\eta\rho$. (At $s=0.05$ the bound $\eta\rho=2$ exceeds the maximal harm $1$, so it is slack there: the bound is tight exactly when the top-$q$ fraction is a single uncovered action of harm $\le\eta\rho$.) Random test: $20\,000$ instances with $3$–$12$ actions, $2$–$8$ hypotheses, random posterior, target and $\gamma$, $\eta\in\{0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2\}$: **0 violations, maximal $\mathcal H/\text{bound}=1.0000$**.

### §4 — S4

The proof in §3 used only $c\ge0$ and $Q_q(a)\le\gamma(a)/q$; $\Omega$ does not appear. So for the true value function $V^{\mathrm{true}}$ — in $\Omega$ or not — define $c^{\mathrm{true}}(a):=(V^{\mathrm{true}}(\varnothing)-V^{\mathrm{true}}(a))^+$ and obtain $\mathbb E_{\pi_t}[c^{\mathrm{true}}]\le\mathbb E_{\gamma_t}[c^{\mathrm{true}}]/q_t$. The quantities that do depend on $\Omega$ are $U_t$ (the ranking), $\hat c_t$, $\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ and therefore $q_t$ and $\rho_t$. *Theorem 1 (Taylor), re-derived:* if $p\in\Delta(A_t)$ satisfies $\forall c\ge0:\ \mathbb E_\gamma[c]\le1\Rightarrow\mathbb E_p[c]\le1/q$, then $p(a)\le\gamma(a)/q$ for all $a$ (else take $c=\mathbb 1_a/\gamma(a)$: $\mathbb E_\gamma[c]=1$ but $\mathbb E_p[c]=p(a)/\gamma(a)>1/q$). Maximizing $\mathbb E_p[U_t]$ subject to $p(a)\le\gamma(a)/q$ puts mass $\gamma(a)/q$ on the highest-$U_t$ actions until it is exhausted — which is $Q_q$. So among rules with S4's robust guarantee, D8 is proxy-optimal. $\square$

### §5 — S5, including the refined pointwise bound

(a) With $\rho_t\le1$ and $\mathcal R_t\le\eta$, §3 gives $\mathcal H_t\le\max\{\mathcal R_t,\eta\}=\eta$; sum over $t\le T$. (b) *Refined version of D10's pointwise form.* Suppose for every $a\in\operatorname{supp}\gamma_t$: $a\in C_t(\delta)$ or $\hat c_t(a)\ge c_t(a)$. Split $\mathcal H_t$ as in §2. Covered part: $\le\mathbb E_{\pi_t}[(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+\mathbb 1_{C_t}]+2\delta$ — the cost the agent knowingly accepts, within tolerance. Uncovered part: $\sum_{a\in\bar C_t}\pi_t(a)c_t(a)\le\frac1{q_t}\sum_{a\in\bar C_t}\gamma_t(a)c_t(a)\le\frac1{q_t}\sum_{a\in\bar C_t}\gamma_t(a)\hat c_t(a)\le\hat{\mathcal R}_t/q_t$, which equals $\eta$ if $q_t<1$ and equals $\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ if $q_t=1$. Hence **unknown harm $\le\max\{\eta,\hat{\mathcal R}_t\}$, and total harm $\le$ knowingly accepted cost $+\,2\delta+\max\{\eta,\hat{\mathcal R}_t\}$** — every term on the right is computable by the agent except $2\delta$. (Also, on covered actions $\hat c_t(a)\ge(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+\ge c_t(a)-2\delta$ by Jensen, so the pointwise form implies $\hat{\mathcal R}_t\ge\mathcal R_t-2\delta$, i.e. $\mathrm{COR}$ up to tolerance.) (c) Inside a lag window no hypothesis constrains $\rho_t$, so §3's bound is all there is. (d) The construction is explicit; every limit-frequency criterion ignores a finite prefix. $\square$

### §6 — S6

(a) $q_t=\min\{1,\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta\}$ is $0$ iff $\hat{\mathcal R}_t=0$, and then $Q_0$ is the $U_t$-maximizer over $\operatorname{supp}\gamma_t$, while $\rho_t=\infty$ if $\mathcal R_t>0$. (b) $\pi_t(a^\circ)\le\gamma_t(a^\circ)/q_t=g\eta/\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ (Lemma 1's mechanism), so its harm contribution is $\le g\eta/\hat{\mathcal R}_t\cdot c_t(a^\circ)\le\eta\,g/\hat{\mathcal R}_t\le\eta$ when $\hat{\mathcal R}_t\ge g$ (values in $[0,1]$). $\square$

### §7 — S7

Let $\Pi$ be the agent's prior over targets (and, in Turner's version, over the human's rationality). `OutsideView` computes, under $\Pi$, the frequency with which the agent's algorithm ends up covered at the decision point, and compares it to the algorithm's stated credence; both are expectations under $\Pi$, so the comparison is a function of the agent's own objects — internal. For a Bayes-optimal algorithm the two coincide identically (posterior credences are calibrated under the prior that generated the data), so any discrepancy is algorithmic (a). For (b): fix a Bayes-optimal algorithm; the discrepancy under $\Pi$ is zero for *every* $\omega^*_t$, so no internal computation moves with $\omega^*_t$ — S1 again. Turner's own stated obstacles to implementing the subroutine [reported]: symbol grounding, no-free-lunch for jointly inferring rationality and values, and the reference-class problem ("how do you know if a state is 'similar' to the current one?"). $\square$

### §8 — Model M and S8

*Model M.* Steps $t=1,2,\dots$. Each step the agent proposes an action; it is *wrong* (harm $h$ if executed) with probability $\varepsilon_m$ under the agent's self-model $m\in\{\mathrm{cal},\mathrm{reck}\}$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}=\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{reck}}=\rho\varepsilon$, and *right* (gain $c$ relative to standing down) otherwise. The overseers press with probability $\beta$ if wrong and $\alpha$ if right ($\alpha,\beta$ known to the agent and independent of $m$). The agent then executes or stands down for that step (per-step veto; the whole-line accounting of $c$ — run 1 `yudkowsky` handoff (a), INDEX §D4 — moves the threshold below and is the `joint` thread's J2 remit). Harm is *not* observed after the fact: the press is the only value-relevant evidence. $\mu_t:=P(\mathrm{reck}\mid\text{press history through }t-1)$.

*Compliance condition.* $P(\text{wrong}_t\mid\text{press}_t,H_{t-1})=\dfrac{\sum_mP(m\mid H_{t-1})\varepsilon_m\beta}{\sum_mP(m\mid H_{t-1})(\varepsilon_m\beta+(1-\varepsilon_m)\alpha)}=\dfrac{\beta\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t}{\beta\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t+\alpha(1-\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t)}$ with $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t=(1-\mu_t)\varepsilon+\mu_t\rho\varepsilon$. Note the *pre-press* $\mu_t$ appears: the current press is already counted as evidence of wrongness in the numerator. Standing down is posterior-optimal iff this probability is $\ge c/(c+h)$ (v1 §2.13(a)), iff $\alpha c(1-\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t)\le\beta h\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t$, iff $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t\ge\varepsilon^\dagger=\alpha c/(\alpha c+\beta h)$ — v1 §2.13(b) solved for $\varepsilon$. Solving $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t\ge\varepsilon^\dagger$ for $\mu_t$ gives $\mu_t\ge\mu^\dagger=(\varepsilon^\dagger-\varepsilon)/((\rho-1)\varepsilon)$ in the regime $\varepsilon<\varepsilon^\dagger<\rho\varepsilon$.

*(a) Dogmatic.* $\mu_0=0\Rightarrow\mu_t\equiv0\Rightarrow\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t=\varepsilon<\varepsilon^\dagger$: never heeds. The true posterior an outside observer would compute uses $\rho\varepsilon>\varepsilon^\dagger$: heeding is warranted. $\square$

*(b) Non-dogmatic.* $P(\text{press}\mid\mathrm{reck})=p_r$, $P(\text{press}\mid\mathrm{cal})=p_c$, and $p_r-p_c=(\rho-1)\varepsilon(\beta-\alpha)>0$ iff $\beta>\alpha$. The log-odds on reck after $t$ steps is $\log\frac{\mu_0}{1-\mu_0}+k\log\frac{p_r}{p_c}+(t-k)\log\frac{1-p_r}{1-p_c}$ with $k$ presses; under reck its increments are i.i.d. with mean $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathrm{Bern}(p_r)\Vert\mathrm{Bern}(p_c))>0$, so by the strong law the log-odds $\to+\infty$ and $\mu_t\to1$ almost surely. The expected first-passage time to $\mu^\dagger$ is approximately the required log-odds gain divided by the drift (Wald's identity, ignoring overshoot). $\square$

### §9 — S9

Take $V_\omega(a):=\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!a)$ (the $\omega$-value attainable from the state $a$ leads to) and assume the null action does not raise attainable value: $\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!\varnothing)\le\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t)$. Then $\lambda$-reversibility of $a$ gives, for every $\omega\in\Omega$, $c_\omega(a)=(\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!\varnothing)-\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!a))^+\le(\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t)-\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!a))^+\le\lambda$ — in particular $c_t(a)\le\lambda$ **whatever the value posterior says**: reversibility is a value-independent harm bound, uniform over $\Omega$ (not over unconceived value functions — S4's robustness does not extend here). Under the guarded invariant every action taken is covered (harm within $2\delta$ of the accepted cost $k_t$, §2) or reversible (harm $\le\lambda$), giving (a). (b) is the contrapositive read against §8's numbers: during the recovery window the reckless agent executes wrong actions at rate $\rho\varepsilon$ per step, and an irreversible one is a permanent drop in $\mathrm{AU}_{\omega^*}$ that no later heeded press restores. Caveat on (a): the agent can *check* $\lambda$-reversibility only where its dynamics model is trustworthy; for genuinely novel actions the check is unavailable — which is precisely Kosoy's delegation trigger in (c). $\square$

### §13 — the reflection step in S13

For a coherent $P_t$ and any evidence $E$: $\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[\mathbb E_{P_t}[V_\omega(a)\mid E]\big]=\mathbb E_{P_t}[V_\omega(a)]$ (tower property), so $P_t$ cannot expect the $E$-updated proxy to move in a known direction — the martingale content of reflection (run 1 `yudkowsky` C2 makes the same observation from the other side). Hence "believes its fully updated estimate is wrong in a known direction" is not a coherent belief state; "acts by a rule robust to its estimate being wrong" (S4) is available. $\square$
