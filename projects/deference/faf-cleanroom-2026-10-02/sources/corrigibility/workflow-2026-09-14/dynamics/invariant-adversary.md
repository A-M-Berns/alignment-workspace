### Statement 6 — the corruption floor

20. [checked, A7] The premise of (a) — "with $\mathbb P^*$-probability $\gamma$ per round the value posterior is corrupted … its own evidence, faked by the corruption, drives $\hat\varepsilon_t \to 0$" — is satisfiable only by an agent that assigns prior $0$ to its own corruption or whose model of the corruption is wrong. Exhibit the joint law the develop file did not: corruption $C_t \sim \mathrm{Bern}(\gamma)$; under $C_t$ the proposal is wrong and each evidence bit "looks right" with probability $1$; under $\neg C_t$ the proposal is wrong with prior $1/2$ and evidence looks right with probability $1$ if right, $1/2$ if wrong. A Bayesian *with $C_t$ in its model at its true prior* $\gamma$ has $\hat\varepsilon_t \downarrow \gamma/(\gamma + (1-\gamma)/2) \approx 0.0198 > 0$ (A7); a Bayesian with $P(C_t) = 0$ has $\hat\varepsilon_t \to 0$ (A7, $8.7 \times 10^{-19}$ after $60$ bits). So the agent of (a) violates non-dogmatism (v1 §2.2, I2: "the agent should obey non-dogmatism so that these things that are zero are only the logical impossibilities") or has a misspecified corruption model. It is outside the thesis's agent class, and (a) is a theorem about an agent the thesis excludes.
21. [derived] Consequently the dichotomy in (c) collapses. Reading $(\alpha)$ — correct posterior under an accurate corruption model — *is* the non-dogmatic Bayesian of A7, and its "residual" $\max(\hat\varepsilon_t, \gamma)$ is not a rule bolted on but what Bayes returns when the fakes are (by hypothesis) uninformative about $C_t$. Reading $(\beta)$ — "$\gamma$ imposed against the evidence" — has no content in the model as stated, because the evidence carries no information against which anything is imposed. $(\beta)$ acquires content only if the agent's model says the evidence *is* informative about $C_t$ when in fact it is not — i.e. misspecification, which is run 1 `radical` I17.4's unanswered clause, not §C5's fixed object. ### Statement 8 — the remaining-loss estimate as potential

32. [checked, A1] Title: "is a potential **iff** reflection holds". Proved: "if". The converse fails: $h = 1$, $c = 3/5$, $P_0$ has wrongness credence $1/2$ (refrains), $P_1$ predictably $3/4$ with no new evidence (refrains). Then $\mathbb E_{P_0}[\Psi_1] = 3/20 \le \Psi_0 - \mathbb E_{P_0}[\ell_0] = 3/10$ (the potential inequality holds, strictly) while $\mathbb E_{P_0}[\Psi_1] = 3/20 \ne \mathbb E_{P_0}[R_1] = 3/10$ (Mart fails). Worse: this $P_t$-potential's round-$1$ certificate is *false* under $\mathbb P^*$ ($\mathbb E^*[R_1] = 3/10 > \Psi_1 = 3/20$). So (a)'s property — the $P_t$-chain inequality — is neither equivalent to reflection nor sufficient for anything under $\mathbb P^*$. Only (b) matters.
33. [derived] (a) is also not a supermartingale: $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\Psi_{t+1}] \le \Psi_t$ for each $t$ is a chain of inequalities under *different* measures. A supermartingale is defined relative to one measure and one filtration; no maximal inequality follows from the chain. Under superconditioning (v1 I3) the $P_t$ are conditionals of one $P$ and the term is earned — but then "reflection" is automatic (conditioning satisfies Mart) and (a) says only that a Bayesian's expected remaining loss is a supermartingale, which is Doob. The develop file never states which reading it uses.
34. [checked, A8] Title, second "iff": "certifies a finite-time regret bound **iff** its one-step forecasts are calibrated". Proved: "if" (calibration $\Rightarrow \Delta_t \le 0 \Rightarrow$ bound). The "only if" fails: a *pessimistic* agent certifies without being calibrated. One round, $h = 1$, $c = 3/5$, agent's wrongness credence $1/4$ (executes, $\mathrm{EV} = 1/5 > 0$), objective wrongness $1/8$: $\Psi_0 = 1/4$, $\mathbb E^*[\ell_0] = 1/8$, $\Delta_0 = -1/8 < 0$, the bound $\mathbb E^*[\mathrm{Reg}_0] = 1/8 \le 1/4$ holds, and the one-step forecasts disagree ($1/4 \ne 1/8$). Calibration is one sufficient condition for $\Delta_t \le 0$; the title's "iff" is unearned in both halves.
35. [checked, A2] (c) "*Locality.* Only one-step forecasts need to be calibrated; nothing about the far future is assumed." False in substance. $\Delta_t \le 0$ for all $t$ implies, by backward induction from $\Psi_{T+1} = 0 = R_{T+1}$, that $\Psi_t \ge \mathbb E^*[R_t \mid \mathcal F_t]$ for every $t$ — the agent's estimate of *all* remaining loss upper-bounds the objective one at every time. The hypothesis is far-future honesty at every round, stated one step at a time; "agreement on $\mathbb E[\Psi_{t+1} \mid \mathcal F_t]$" is agreement about a forecast of a forecast of the far future. Conversely, honest certificates at every time do *not* give $\Delta_t \le 0$: with $P_1$ predictably at the threshold $3/8$ (more conservative), both certificates are honest ($3/5 \le 3/5$, $3/10 \le 3/8$) and $\Delta_0 = 3/40 > 0$. So the develop file's hypothesis is not "weaker than full calibration" (its handoff question): it is a different, far-future condition — no predictable increase in conservatism-plus-loss — that happens to imply honesty.
36. [derived] The conclusion $\mathbb E^*[\mathrm{Reg}_T] \le \Psi_0$ is only as good as $\Psi_0$, the *agent's own* number. Under the develop file's Statement 6(a), $\Psi_0$ is small and the bound is false because $\Delta_t > 0$; under a correct corruption model $\Psi_0$ already contains $\gamma$. The certificate certifies what the agent believes, given that the agent's beliefs are right — INDEX §C12 in one line.

## Questions for the author

1. **What kind of condition is (iii), "caution outpaces risk"?** (a) A per-round comparative invariant — the agent's caution measure at least its risk measure, both defined on the agent's state (D2/D4; finite-time sums follow, no whole-future claim); (b) a whole-future hazard budget $\sum_t \delta_t \le \Delta$ on the *objective* hazard (Statement 7's choice; gives $\mathbb P^*(\text{Cat ever}) \le \Delta$ but is a condition on $\mathbb P^*$, not on the agent's caution); (c) a second-order calibration condition on the agent's epistemic state whose *consequence* is (a) or (b). Consequence of the choice: under (a) or (c) the `caution` thread's object is agent-side and checkable in principle; under (b) it is the overseers' or a modeller's number.
2. **Is (iv) legitimacy-conditioned when it serves as a certificate hypothesis?** If yes (S4's and v1 §2.11's form), the finite-time regret certificate re-certifies only across legitimate transitions and $P_t(\neg L_{t,t+1})$ must be budgeted like a hazard — (iii) and (iv) become one budget (A3). If no (unconditional reflection on the loss variable), the thesis's claim that reflection fails toward illegitimate sources is being waived for that variable — say why that is acceptable for remaining loss specifically.
3. **Per-round stop or whole-line shutdown in the iterated model?** Per-round: $c_t$ is one round's forgone value, compliance is recoverable, and Statement 4's sums are right. Whole-line: compliance is absorbing, $c_t$ is the agent's future, $\mathrm{Omit}$ is a single terminal term, and the compliance inequality (i) is evaluated once against the entire future — with the consequence that (i) is much harder to satisfy at high capability (INDEX §D4, §C10) and the "recoverable" asymmetry of v1 §2.13(a) is gone.
4. **Do you accept the trade reading of the row gap (item 10)?** Accept: then the next leg's LI target is not rates but *observability of the certificate's hypothesis* — which calibration facts about $W_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t$ can be forced by settling questions (run 1 `li` I13.5's "report-checkable" channel) — and Open problem 1 (a quantitative criterion) becomes secondary. Reject: then name a finite-time guarantee in the toy whose hypothesis does not mention $\mathbb P^*(W_t = 1 \mid \mathcal F^-_t)$ on complied rounds.
5. **Which coverage?** Calibration of the wrongness credence per available action (S4), or distance of the value posterior from the target (Statement 10c)? The first is what the hazard factorization uses and is action-relative; the second is what the tracking recursion and Christiano's neighbourhood use and is global. Consequence: only the second makes "the target moves faster than the agent tracks" a statement about coverage; only the first is what compliance needs.
