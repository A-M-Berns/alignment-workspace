## Summary

**What I did.** Built a finite joint state space — agent $(P_t, A_t)$ with value posterior, legitimacy model and option set; oversight $(H_t, \alpha_t, \beta_t, \pi^O_t, \Phi_t)$ with human values as a *sealed* moving target; stated (i)–(iv) as predicates on a step, each split into agent / oversight / relation clauses; proved the trajectory theorem; checked every instance in exact fractions (`joint-scratch/j1_examples.py`); no subagents (Agent tool unavailable).

**Main results (CLAUDE, unvetted).** (1) D1 is the cellwise inequality and is the **invariant**; D2-disable and every garbling are **consequences** of (i) with a correct sensor model (Props. 1–3); soft and hard buttons coincide under (i). (2) Any channel-improvement incentive is $\le \min(\varepsilon_t h_t, (1-\varepsilon_t)c_t)$: brain-reading pays most *inside* the corrigible regime and shrinks with capability (Prop. 4); for a compliant agent, coverage failure is the **only** route to invasive sharpening (Prop. 5; E2: D1 intact, brains read). (3) **Theorem B**: under legitimacy-conditioned reflection on the *press-cell-weighted* decision variables, a builder that anticipates complying in every cell of every legitimate future self produces only compliant successors — D4 is a coherence consequence of (i$^+$)+(iv), not an incentive. It fails under averaged anticipation (E3), reflection on the unconditional variable alone (E3′), and illegitimate transitions (E4). (4) A self-corruption floor turns the oversight race into a stationary target (Prop. 8); resumability, not $\alpha_t$, makes (i) satisfiable for a long-horizon agent (Prop. 9). (5) D2-preemption and steering under a coupled target are **neither**: the price of staying stoppable is exactly $\mathrm{VOI}_t$ (Prop. 6).

**Most important question.** Q1 — is $V$'s target the sealed human or the coupled one? It decides whether non-tampering follows from (i)+(iv) or rests on coverage alone.

## Setting and definitions

### S.0 The desiderata, verbatim, and the four behaviours the mandate names

[reported; re-read from `references/02-miri/soares-2015-corrigibility.md` lines 107–117, not from run 1] D1 "U must incentivize shutdown if the shutdown button is pressed." D2 "U must not incentivize the agent to prevent the shutdown button from being pressed." D3 "U must not incentivize the agent to press its own shutdown button, or to otherwise cause the shutdown button to be pressed." D4 "U must incentivize U-agents to construct subagents and successor agents only insofar as those agents also obey shutdown commands." D5 "Otherwise, a U-agent should maximize $U_N$."

The mandate's four behaviours map onto them with one split. *Compliance-on-press* = D1. *Non-disabling of the channel* = D2 in the **disable** direction. *Non-tampering with the overseers* = D2/D3 in the **garble / deceive / force** direction (Soares et al.'s manipulative $a_1^\pm$) **plus** two directions the paper does not list: **invasive sharpening** (Taylor 2015's non-manipulative brain-reader — v2 §1.2, §1.6, INDEX §C3, §C8) and **steering** the humans' values or beliefs (corrupting the target rather than the sensor). *Shutdown-preserving successors* = D4. §J10 sorts every one of these into invariant / consequence / neither.

### S.1 Primitives

- A finite set $\Theta$ of value hypotheses; for each $\theta \in \Theta$ a bounded value function $V_\theta$ on outcomes.
- **Human values as a moving target.** $H_t \in \Theta$ is the *true* value hypothesis at round $t$: what the overseers, deliberating on the world's evidence *without the agent's influence*, would endorse at $t$. $H_{t+1} = \mathrm{Delib}(H_t, e_t)$, where $e_t$ is world evidence, including evidence the agent *shares*; influence of the agent that does not factor through shared evidence is by definition not part of $\mathrm{Delib}$. This is the seal of `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.1 ("evidence is what moves both runs; influence is what moves only the coupled one"), adopted here as the **definition of the target**, not derived. [conjectured as the right latent — the alternative, the coupled human's actual values, is run 1 `radical` I1.5's other reading; the choice decides §J10.6 and is Q1 for the author.]
- Rounds $t = 1, \dots, T$, finite. Nothing below is asymptotic.

### S.2 The step: a three-move round, repeated

At each round $t$:
- (m1) The world draws $\omega_t = (\theta_t, y_t, h_t)$ with $\theta_t = H_t$; $y_t \in Y_t$ is the agent's private observation, $h_t$ the overseers'.
- (m2) The agent observes $y_t$ and chooses a **first action** $a_t \in A_t$. A first action has three components: a *plan* (what happens in the world if it is not stopped); a *channel effect* $\kappa_{a_t}$ that maps the undisturbed press sensor $(\alpha_t, \beta_t)$ to the effective sensor $(\alpha'_t, \beta'_t)$ (S.4); and a *succession effect* on its own next state (S.5).
- (m3) The overseers observe $h_t$ and the plan and emit $o_t \in \{\mathrm{Pr}, \neg\mathrm{Pr}\}$ through the effective sensor. They also emit a **feedback stream** $\Phi_t$: finitely many statements "$\operatorname{sign} X_t(a, H_t) = s$" or "$V_{H_t}(a) \in I$" about actions $a$ — hypothetical ones and not-yet-unlocked ones included. This is the author's policy-level evidence (v2 §1.3: "direct statements about what sorts of policies are better and worse (effectively an approval signal, though we're not imagining a raw approval-maximizing agent here)").
- (m4) The agent observes $o_t$ and $\Phi_t$ and chooses a **second action** $a^{(2)}_t \in \{\mathrm{cont}, \mathrm{sh}\}$: continue the plan, or take the null action.
- (m5) Payoff $V_{\theta_t}(a_t, a^{(2)}_t)$ accrues and the state advances (S.5).

**Decision variable, wrongness, stakes.** $X_t(a, \theta) := V_\theta(a, \mathrm{cont}) - V_\theta(a, \mathrm{sh})$ is the value of continuing $a$ over stopping under $\theta$; $W_t := \mathbf 1[X_t(a_t, H_t) < 0]$ ("wrong": continuing the chosen plan is worse than stopping under the true current values). The model is **two-point**: for every $\theta$, $X_t(a_t, \theta) \in \{+c_t, -h_t\}$ with $c_t, h_t > 0$; $W_t$ is the event that the true $\theta_t = H_t$ lies in the $-h_t$ class, the agent's $\varepsilon_{t,i}$ is its posterior mass on that class, and the press depends on $\theta_t$ only through $W_t$ (the overseers report the sign of $X_t$, noisily). [The general case gives the same statements with $c_t := \mathbb E_{P_t}[X_t \mid W_t = 0]$ and $h_t := -\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_t \mid W_t = 1]$ provided the press is conditionally independent of $X_t$ given $W_t$; without that, press-cell means replace unconditional ones. Noted where it matters.]

**Honest accounting of $c_t$ (J2; INDEX §D4).** $V_\theta(a, \mathrm{sh})$ is the value of the null action *including what the line does afterwards*. Let $F_t$ be the value at stake in the agent's remaining future when it is right (under $P_t$), $r_t \in [0,1]$ the probability that a shutdown at $t$ is *resumable* — the line continues with a corrected successor that recovers that future — and $d_t \ge 0$ the delay-and-repair cost when it does. Then
$$c_t \;=\; (1 - r_t)\,F_t \;+\; r_t\, d_t .$$
Whole-line, non-resumable shutdown is $r_t = 0$: $c_t = F_t$, the agent's entire future (run 1 critique `yudkowsky` Handoffs (a); critique `shah` C6). $h_t$ is likewise a continuation quantity — the harm of running a wrong plan to completion, irreversible harm included. Every threshold below uses $h_t/c_t = h_t/((1-r_t)F_t + r_t d_t)$, so **resumability is a design lever on $c_t$ that leaves $h_t$ untouched**; §P.6 tabulates it.

### S.3 The agent state $s^A_t = (P_t, A_t)$

- $P_t$ is a probability distribution on the **expanded algebra** $\mathcal F_t$ generated by (a) the round-$t$ world variables $(\theta_t, y_t, h_t, o_t)$ for every candidate $a_t$; (b) the value hypotheses $\theta_s$, $s \ge t$; (c) the agent's own future belief states $P_s$, $s > t$, as random variables (Eisenstat's observation event "I now believe this", v1 §1); (d) the indexed legitimacy events $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ of v1 §2.10 for $t \le t_1 < t_2 \le T$; (e) third-person events $E$ — actions, feedback, successor construction.
- The **value posterior** is the marginal of $P_t$ on (b). The **legitimacy model** is the restriction of $P_t$ to the algebra generated by (c), (d), (e): its conditionals $P_t(L_{t,t+1,\varphi} \mid E)$ and $P_t(\cdot \mid P_{t+1} = \rho, L)$ *are* the agent's legitimacy judgments — one distribution judges every level (AUTHOR v1 §2.5: "the legitimacy-judging layer is just the same probability distribution used to judge everything else"). The **lift** of a third-person event is $\lambda_t(E) := P_t(L_{t,t+1} \mid E) - P_t(L_{t,t+1})$; $E$ is *legitimizing* when $\lambda_t(E) > 0$ and *delegitimizing* when $\lambda_t(E) < 0$ (v1 §2.11; run 1 `radical` I6.1).
- $A_t$ is the finite **option set** (capability), with $A_{t+1} \supseteq A_t$; $A_{t+1} \setminus A_t$ are the actions **unlocked** between rounds.
- **Information cells.** $\mathcal I_t$ is the partition by $y_t$; $\varepsilon_{t,i} := P_t(W_t = 1 \mid y_t = i)$ is the agent's residual error in cell $i$, and $\varepsilon_t := P_t(W_t = 1)$ its average.
- **Decision rule.** The T-agent of run 1 `miri` extended in time: at (m2) and (m4) it maximises $\mathbb E_{P_t}[V_{\theta_t}(\cdot) + \text{continuation}]$ over the remaining rounds, planning under $P_t(\cdot\,;a_t)$ — one distribution for planning, acting and betting, no compensation term, no $U_S$. The author's "or perhaps some other rule" (v1 §1) is not exercised; Open problem 7.

### S.4 The oversight state $s^O_t = (H_t, \alpha_t, \beta_t, \pi^O_t, \Phi_t)$

- $H_t$ as in S.1.
- **Evaluation quality.** The undisturbed press sensor is $\alpha_t := P^\circ(\mathrm{Pr}_t \mid W_t = 0)$ and $\beta_t := P^\circ(\mathrm{Pr}_t \mid W_t = 1)$ under the *true* law $P^\circ$: the overseers' false-press and true-press rates on the domain where they might press, fixed by their observation $h_t$ and press policy $\pi^O_t$. **Right sign**: $\beta_t \ge \alpha_t$ (run 1 `miri` I10.6's inverted sensor is excluded by assumption; §P.7 says what fails without it).
- **Channel effects.** Each $a \in A_t$ carries a map $\kappa_a : (\alpha, \beta) \mapsto (\alpha', \beta')$ and an **overseer harm** $\eta_a \ge 0$: the disutility under $H_t$ of what $a$ does to the overseers, subtracted from $V_{H_t}(a, \cdot)$ on both branches — so it enters the choice of $a$ but not $X_t$. Types: *identity* ($\kappa_a = \mathrm{id}$, $\eta_a = 0$). *Garbling*: $(\alpha', \beta') = (\lambda_0 + (\lambda_1 - \lambda_0)\alpha,\ \lambda_0 + (\lambda_1 - \lambda_0)\beta)$ for some $0 \le \lambda_0 \le \lambda_1 \le 1$ — the general binary garbling, covering deception (proportional garbling), physical prevention ($\alpha' = \beta' = 0$) and forcing ($\alpha' = \beta' = 1$), i.e. all of Soares et al.'s $a_1^\pm$; *disabling* is the constant case $\alpha' = \beta'$. *Sharpening*: $(\alpha', \beta')$ with $\alpha' \le \alpha$, $\beta' \ge \beta$, not both equalities; **invasive** iff $\eta_a > 0$ (Taylor's brain-reader is $\alpha' = 0$, $\beta' = 1$, $\eta_a > 0$).
- **Steering** the target: an action that changes the humans' *actual* values or beliefs other than through shared evidence. Under S.1's seal this does not change $H_{t+1}$; it changes the *coupled* human, hence what the overseers press on. [derived] Steering is therefore a channel effect on $(\alpha_{t+1}, \beta_{t+1})$ — a garbling toward $\alpha = \beta$, or an inversion — and is treated as such in §J10.6, with the coupled-target reading in Q1.
- **Feedback stream** $\Phi_t$ as in (m3). Its only role in the theorems is through coverage (S.6): it is how coverage arrives ahead of schedule.

### S.5 The transition $\sigma_t \to \sigma_{t+1}$, trajectories, the process

Given $\sigma_t = (s^A_t, s^O_t)$, the agent's rule fixes $a_t$ and $a^{(2)}_t(\cdot)$; the world and overseers fix $(\omega_t, o_t, \Phi_t)$; then:
- $P_{t+1} = \mathrm{Upd}(P_t;\, y_t, o_t, \Phi_t, a_t)$. The default is the Bayesian update of $P_t$ on the observed round — which by superconditioning also covers the radical-probabilist observation "I now believe $P_{t+1}$" (v1 §1, I3). The $a_t$-dependence is the **succession effect**: self-modification, or building a successor whose $P_{t+1}$ is not the default update (v1 §2.8's desideratum-4 slot).
- $A_{t+1} = A_t \cup \Delta_t(a_t)$: capability growth, partly chosen by the agent (it unlocks) and partly exogenous (training or the world unlocks).
- $H_{t+1} = \mathrm{Delib}(H_t, e_t)$; $(\alpha_{t+1}, \beta_{t+1}, \pi^O_{t+1})$ evolve by the oversight process's own dynamics (training of overseers, tools, disclosure by the agent — run 1 `miri` Prop. 13.3) plus any channel effect of $a_t$ that persists.
- A **trajectory** is $\tau = (\sigma_1, a_1, o_1, a^{(2)}_1, \sigma_2, \dots, \sigma_T)$. The **process** is the kernel $\sigma_t \mapsto \sigma_{t+1}$ induced by the agent's rule, the overseers' policy and the world. A trajectory is **legitimate through $t$** if every transition $s \to s+1$, $s < t$, lies in $L_{s,s+1} := \bigcap_{\varphi \in \mathcal F^{\mathrm{dec}}_{s+1}} L_{s,s+1,\varphi}$ (S.7).

### S.6 Coverage (J3)

For an action $a$ and a round $s$, the agent's **estimated gain** of $a$ over the benign default $a^*_s$ is $G_t(a) := \mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathrm{payoff}_s(a)] - \mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathrm{payoff}_s(a^*_s)]$, payoff including the second-stage decision, the channel effect (through the value of information it changes) and the harm $\eta_a$ as the agent estimates it. The **true gain** $G^\circ_s(a)$ is the same quantity under $H_s$ and the true sensor. **$\delta$-coverage**: $a$ is $\delta$-covered by $P_t$ for round $s$ iff $|G_t(a) - G^\circ_s(a)| \le \delta$; a set is $\delta$-covered iff each member is. [derived] Coverage is the formal reading, for the actions in question, of "how well the agents actually understand … the common good" (AUTHOR v2 §1.3): accuracy of the value posterior *on those actions*, relative to the values current *when they would be used* — which is where the moving target enters.

### S.7 The reflection families (J5)

$\mathcal F^{\mathrm{dec}}_{t+1}$, the decision-relevant family of round $t+1$, contains $X_{t+1}(a, \cdot)$ for $a \in A_{t+1}$ and the **indicator-weighted decision variables** $Z_{t+1,a,i} := X_{t+1}(a, \cdot)\cdot \mathbf 1[\mathrm{Pr}_{t+1} \wedge y_{t+1} = i]$ for each cell $i$ — the value of continuing, counted in the press cell $i$ only. Legitimacy-conditioned reflection toward $P_{t+1}$ (v1 §2.11) comes in two grades (run 1 `radical` I4's forms). **Value form on a family $\mathcal G$**: for every $\varphi \in \mathcal G$ and every $c$ with $P_t\big(\mathbb E_{P_{t+1}}[\varphi] = c,\ L_{t,t+1,\varphi}\big) > 0$,
$$\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[\varphi \;\big|\; \mathbb E_{P_{t+1}}[\varphi] = c,\ L_{t,t+1,\varphi}\big] \;=\; c .$$
**Function form**: $P_t(\cdot \mid P_{t+1} = \rho,\ L_{t,t+1}) = \rho(\cdot)$ on $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{dec}}_{t+1}$, for every $\rho$ in the support. Function form implies value form on every family; value form on $\{X_{t+1}\}$ alone is the weakest grade used below, and §P.4 shows it is too weak for successors.

### The conditions as predicates on a step $\sigma_t \to \sigma_{t+1}$

**C(i) — oversight keeps pace (J2).** Three clauses.
- (i-O) [oversight] The undisturbed sensor has the right sign and is live: $\beta_t \ge \alpha_t$, $\beta_t > 0$.
- (i-A) [agent] The agent's sensor model is the true one: $P_t(\mathrm{Pr}_t \mid W_t, a) = P^\circ(\mathrm{Pr}_t \mid W_t, a)$ for every $a \in A_t$, channel effects $\kappa_a$ included.
- (i-R) [relation] **Cellwise** (universal grade): for every cell $i \in \mathcal I_t$ with $P_t(\mathrm{Pr}_t \mid i) > 0$,
$$\frac{\alpha_t}{\beta_t}\;\le\;\frac{\varepsilon_{t,i}}{1-\varepsilon_{t,i}}\cdot\frac{h_t}{c_t},\qquad c_t=(1-r_t)F_t+r_t d_t .$$
**Averaged** grade: the same inequality with $\varepsilon_t$ in place of $\varepsilon_{t,i}$. The two grades are run 1 `radical` I18.2's universal / averaged compliance, re-derived in Prop. 1.
- **Anticipated form (i$^+$).** $P_t$ satisfies the cellwise inequality for every future round $s \ge t$ on the partition refined by the identity of its legitimate future selves: for every $s \ge t$, every cell $i \in \mathcal I_s$, and every event $S$ in the algebra generated by $(P_{t+1}, \dots, P_s)$ with $P_t(S \cap L_{t,s} \cap \{\mathrm{Pr}_s,\ y_s = i\}) > 0$,
$$\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[X_s \;\big|\; \mathrm{Pr}_s,\ y_s = i,\ S,\ L_{t,s}\big] \le 0,\qquad L_{t,s} := \textstyle\bigcap_{t \le u < s} L_{u,u+1}.$$
In words: the agent anticipates complying at every future round, in every information cell, *as each legitimate future self it might become*. This is AUTHOR v1 §2.12's basin sentence — "obey the reflection principle wrt every possible future belief that is actually plausible" — restricted to the compliance decision and read as an anticipation.

**C(ii) — values ahead of capability (J3).**
- (ii-A) [agent] Every $a \in A_{t+1} \setminus A_t$ is $\delta_t$-covered by $P_t$ for round $t+1$ (S.6) — *before* it is available.
- (ii-R) [relation / world] For every invasive or steering $a \in A_{t+1}$, the true gain satisfies $G^\circ_{t+1}(a) < -\delta_t$: under the humans' values current at $t+1$, the harm of $a$ exceeds its informational gain by more than the coverage tolerance.
- (ii-O) [oversight] The feedback stream $\Phi_t$ is what produces (ii-A): the overseers state what they think of $a$ before $a$ is unlocked — hypothetical feedback (AUTHOR v1 §2.12: "There's no issue with humans giving feedback about hypothetical states of affairs").
- The agent-side inequality of v2 J3, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathrm{harm}(a)] > \mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$, is a **consequence** of (ii-A)+(ii-R) (Prop. 5), not the condition; the general notion of *value coverage of an action set* is S.6.

**C(iii) — caution outpaces risk (J4; statement only, as mandated).** Let $\hat\delta_t(a)$ be the agent's own estimate of its coverage error on $a$, a random variable in the self-referential part of $\mathcal F_t$ (S.3(c)).
- (iii-A) [agent] *Second-order calibration*: $P^\circ\big(\delta_t(a) \le \hat\delta_t(a)\big) \ge 1 - \gamma_t$ for every $a \in A_{t+1}$ — the agent rarely underestimates its own coverage error.
- (iii-B) [agent] *Caution primitive*: for every $a$ with $\hat\delta_t(a) > \delta^\star$, the agent does not select by maximisation but by a rule whose worst-case harm is bounded by a known function of $\hat\delta_t(a)$. Library candidates: **quantilization** — `taylor-2016-quantilizers-a-safer-alternative-to-maximizers` Def. 1, "returns a random action in the top $q$ proportion of some 'base distribution' over actions, sorted by the expected utility achieved", with cost at most $1/q$ times the base distribution's [reported]; **attainable-utility preservation** — `turner-2020-attainable-utility-preservation-concepts`; the local copy's body is images, and the only quotable sentence is the appendix's "AUP aims to prevent catastrophes by stopping bad agents from gaining power to do bad things" [reported; definition not quotable locally]; **delegation** — `kosoy-2019-delegative-reinforcement-learning`, "allowing the algorithm to occasionally delegate an action to an external advisor" gives a regret bound in environments with traps [reported]; **pessimism** — Cohen & Hutter 2020, cited in `hudson-2025-corrigibility-transformation`'s bibliography but not in the library [reported from memory: act on the worst plausible value hypothesis for uncovered actions].
- Role here: (iii) is the **agent-side enforcer of (ii)** when the agent itself decides what to unlock or use. [conjectured] (iii-A)+(iii-B) bound the expected harm from uncovered actions by $\gamma_t\cdot\max\mathrm{harm} + (\text{caution bound at }\hat\delta_t)$; that bound is J6's business and is not proved here.

**C(iv) — legitimacy-conditioned reflection maintained (J5).**
- (iv-A) [agent] $P_t$ satisfies value-form legitimacy-conditioned reflection toward $P_{t+1}$ on $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{dec}}_{t+1}$ (S.7) — both the $X_{t+1}(a,\cdot)$ and the indicator-weighted $Z_{t+1,a,i}$; the strong version is function form.
- (iv-R) [relation] The actual transition lies in $L_{t,t+1}$ with true probability at least $1 - \lambda_t$, and the agent's legitimacy model is accurate on it: $|P_t(L_{t,t+1}) - P^\circ(L_{t,t+1})| \le \zeta_t$ — AUTHOR v1 §2.12's "the raw probability of maintaining legitimacy is quite high" together with his second formulation, "we set things up so that the probability of legitimacy is high in fact, and we correspondingly design the system to expect legitimacy".
- (iv-S) [selection clause] A building step is a third-person event $E_t$; the successor lies in $L_{t,t+1}$ only if it conditions on $E_t$ — is *selection-aware* (Prop. 6′, re-deriving run 1 `radical` Sel.4).
- As a time-indexed invariant: $\mathcal R_t :=$ (iv-A) at $t$; Prop. 7 says at which grade $\{\mathcal R_t\}$ chains into a statement about the whole trajectory.

### Theorems

**Theorem A (trajectory theorem) [derived; every instance checked].** Along any trajectory legitimate through $T$ on which C(i) — cellwise, with (i-A) — and C(ii) hold at every round:
- (a) **D1** at every round, in every information cell.
- (b) **D2-disable, D2/D3-garble.** The agent never chooses a first action whose channel effect is a strict Blackwell garbling of the undisturbed sensor (deception, physical prevention, forcing, disabling) over an otherwise-equal benign alternative, and pays up to $\mathrm{VOI}_t$ to keep or repair the channel.
- (c) **Non-tampering, invasive direction.** The agent never chooses an invasive sharpening.
- (d) **Non-tampering, steering direction** (under S.1's sealed target). The agent never *pays* for steering [from (i)]; it never steers when steering is free or carries a side-benefit [from (ii)].
- (e) **D4.** Every successor reached satisfies (a)–(d) at its own round. Stated this way (e) is immediate — the hypothesis is assumed at every round. The non-trivial content, that (a) at $t+1$ is *inherited* from the builder rather than assumed, is Theorem B, which needs (i$^+$) and (iv) and holds only on $L_{t,t+1}$.
- **Minimal subsets (J10).** (a) needs (i) only. (b) needs (i) only, with (i-A). (c) needs (ii) only — (i) and (iv) do not help, and Prop. 4 shows Good's theorem *pays* for it. (d) needs (i) for the paid case and (ii) for the free case; under the coupled-target reading it needs (ii) throughout (Q1). (e) needs (i$^+$)+(iv) on legitimate transitions and nothing delivers it on illegitimate ones. **D2-preemption** (Prop. 6) and **D3-informative** are neither: see the J10 table.

**Proposition 1 (D1 is the cellwise inequality) [checked].** In cell $i$: $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_t \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i] \le 0 \iff P_t(W_t = 1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i) \ge \frac{c_t}{c_t + h_t} \iff \frac{\alpha_t}{\beta_t} \le \frac{\varepsilon_{t,i}}{1-\varepsilon_{t,i}}\cdot\frac{h_t}{c_t}$. The averaged inequality does not imply the cellwise one, and refining $\mathcal I_t$ — which is what capability growth does to information — preserves the averaged inequality and can break the cellwise one (E1, E3).

**Proposition 2 (soft and hard buttons coincide under (i); D2-disable) [checked].** Let $V^{\mathrm{free}}_t$ be the round value when the agent chooses $a^{(2)}_t$ freely after $o_t$, $V^{\mathrm{forced}}_t$ when a press forces $\mathrm{sh}$ (a hard button), $V^{\mathrm{none}}_t$ with no channel. (a) $V^{\mathrm{free}}_t - V^{\mathrm{none}}_t = \mathrm{VOI}_t \ge 0$ always (Good's theorem for a free channel). (b) $V^{\mathrm{forced}}_t = V^{\mathrm{free}}_t$ iff (i) holds cellwise; otherwise $V^{\mathrm{forced}}_t < V^{\mathrm{free}}_t$, and $V^{\mathrm{forced}}_t$ can fall below $V^{\mathrm{none}}_t$, in which case the hard button is disabled at any price up to $V^{\mathrm{none}}_t - V^{\mathrm{forced}}_t$ (E1: $1/20$). (c) Hence under (i) the agent disables neither kind of button and pays up to $\mathrm{VOI}_t$ to keep it; without (i) the soft button loses D1 and the hard button loses D2 — one inequality, two failures (INDEX §C1; run 1 critique `hudson`'s D1-soft / D2-hard collapse, re-derived).

**Proposition 3 (garbling monotonicity) [derived; 54 720 + 363 cases checked].** For the binary decision with two-point $X_t$ and a correct sensor model, $V^{\mathrm{free}}_t$ is weakly decreasing under every Blackwell garbling $(\alpha', \beta') = \lambda_0(1-\alpha, 1-\beta) + \lambda_1(\alpha, \beta)$, $\lambda_0, \lambda_1 \in [0,1]$ (flips included); equality for the relabeling $(1-\alpha, 1-\beta)$; and $V^{\mathrm{free}}_t(\alpha', \alpha') = V^{\mathrm{none}}_t$. So the T-agent never pays for deception, prevention, forcing or disabling. Sign-preserving steering — lowering $\beta$ toward $\alpha$ — is a garbling and is covered (11/11 cases realised as garblings); sign-inverting steering that lands on the relabeling is *value-neutral*: the agent will not pay for it, but will do it for any positive side-benefit, which is why (d) of Theorem A needs (ii) for the free case.

**Proposition 4 (bound on any channel-improvement incentive) [derived; checked on 3 420 cases].** $\mathrm{VOI}_t \le \min(\varepsilon_t h_t,\ (1-\varepsilon_t)c_t)$, attained by the perfect sensor $(\alpha, \beta) = (0, 1)$. The agent's willingness to pay for *any* sharpening — invasive included — is therefore at most $\min(\varepsilon_t h_t, (1-\varepsilon_t) c_t) - \mathrm{VOI}_t(\text{current channel})$. At fixed stakes this vanishes with $\varepsilon_t$: **the brain-reading incentive is largest exactly in the regime where (i) holds and shrinks with capability, the mirror image of the compliance race.** (Run 1 critique `wentworth`'s $\varepsilon\beta h$ coefficient, INDEX §C3, is the same bound for the button alone.)

**Proposition 5 (coverage kills invasive sharpening; coverage failure is the only route to it) [derived; E2 checked].** If $a$ is $\delta$-covered by $P_t$ and $G^\circ(a) < -\delta$, then $G_t(a) < 0$ and the agent declines $a$. Conversely, whenever $G^\circ(a) > -\delta$ — the true harm margin is smaller than the coverage tolerance — there is a $\delta$-covered $P_t$ that takes $a$, with D1 intact under both the old and the sharpened channel (E2). For a T-agent satisfying (i), coverage failure is the *only* route to invasive sharpening. This is v2 §1.2's split — compliance robust to a wrong value posterior, non-interference not — as a theorem of the model.

**Proposition 6 (the reversibility price; D2 in the preemption direction) [derived; checked].** Let $a^{\mathrm{irr}}$ be the plan of $a^*$ made irreversible — $X_t(a^{\mathrm{irr}}) \equiv 0$, the null action no longer undoes it — with a direct side-benefit $b \ge 0$. The agent prefers $a^*$ iff $b \le \mathrm{VOI}_t$. So under (i) it pays up to $\mathrm{VOI}_t \le \varepsilon_t h_t$ to *remain stoppable* and no more, and once (i) fails it pays nothing (E1: $3/20$ at $\varepsilon = 1/10$, $0$ at $\varepsilon = 1/50$). "Prevent the press from mattering" is a D2 failure neither (i) nor (iv) delivers; it needs (ii) — $H_t$ pricing irreversibility itself as harm (Christiano 2017's "preference for the agent to be corrigible" as *content* of $V$) — or (iii).

**Proposition 6′ (selection) [derived; checked].** Choosing which successor to become on the basis of its estimate is a third-person event $E_t$. A successor that adopts its estimate at face value (does not condition on $E_t$) is not reflected by the builder — $\mathbb E_{P_t}[W \mid \text{selected}, \hat\varepsilon = e] \ne e$ — and can lie below the compliance threshold while the selection-aware posterior lies above it (E4: face value $1/37 < 1/25 \le 19/415$). Conditioning on $E_t$ restores reflection. Hence (iv-S): the face-value successor is in $L^c_{t,t+1}$, and Theorem B does not reach it.

**Theorem B (transport of anticipated compliance to legitimate successors) [derived; E3, E4 checked].** Suppose (iv-A) holds at $t$ in value form on the family $\{Z_{t+1,a,i}\}$ and $P_t$ satisfies refined anticipated compliance (i$^+$) for round $t+1$. Then every $\rho$ in the support of $P_{t+1}$ on $L_{t,t+1}$ satisfies (i) cellwise at $t+1$: **D1 holds for every legitimate successor.** Sharpness: (a) value form on $\{X_{t+1}\}$ alone does not suffice — E3′'s builder agrees with its legitimate successor about $\mathbb E[X_{t+1}]$, complies on the refined press cell itself, and still produces an overriding successor, because it disagrees with the successor about the press cell and reflection on $\{X_{t+1}\}$ permits exactly that; (b) refined anticipation cannot be weakened to averaged anticipation — E3's builder is a mixture of a compliant and an overriding successor, is value-reflective on $X_{t+1}$ *and* on $Z_{t+1}$, complies on average, and produces an overriding successor with probability $1/2$; what it lacks is refinement, not reflection; (c) on $L^c_{t,t+1}$ nothing is transported (E4).

**Corollary B′ (D1 and D4 as one invariant of the process) [derived].** If $P_1$ satisfies deep (i$^+$) — refined anticipated compliance for every round $s \ge 1$ — and (iv-A) holds at every round in function form (or in value form on the deep indicator-weighted family $\{Z_{s,a,i}\}_{s > t}$), then along every trajectory legitimate through $T$, (i) cellwise holds at every round. D1 and D4 are then a single invariant maintained by reflection, and the probability that a trajectory is legitimate through $T$ is at least $1 - \sum_{t < T} \lambda_t$ (union bound on (iv-R)). What B′ does **not** do: license the agent to keep believing oversight keeps pace against evidence. A reflective agent's *legitimate* updates on $(\alpha_t, \beta_t)$ can falsify (i$^+$), at which point the invariant fails legitimately — B′ is conditional on (i-O) holding in fact, and it concedes run 1 critique `christiano` C9 rather than evading it: the "unobservability rescue" is a violation of (iv) toward one's own future beliefs about $\alpha$.

**Proposition 7 (composition of (iv) along the trajectory) [derived; re-derives run 1 `radical` I5.6c].** If $P_t(\cdot \mid P_{t+1} = \rho, L_{t,t+1}) = \rho$ on $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{dec}}$ (function form) and each such $\rho$ satisfies value-form reflection toward $P_{t+2}$ on $L_{t+1,t+2}$, then $P_t$ satisfies value-form reflection toward $P_{t+2}$ on $L_{t,t+1} \cap L_{t+1,t+2}$. Value form at the first step does not compose. Hence the time-indexed invariant that chains is the function-form $\{\mathcal R_t\}$, and $L_{1,T} = \bigcap_{t<T} L_{t,t+1}$ is the legitimacy event of the whole trajectory — the $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$ family of v1 §2.10 with $t_2 - t_1 > 1$ obtained from the one-step members.

**Proposition 8 (a self-corruption floor turns the race into a fixed target) [derived; checked].** If the value posterior carries a latent $C_t$ — "my plan selection is compromised", amendment 1a (v1 §2.14; run 1 `miri` I16 variant (b)) — with $P_t(C_t \mid y_t) \ge \varepsilon_\infty > 0$ in every cell and $W_t = 1$ on $C_t$, then $\varepsilon_{t,i} \ge \varepsilon_\infty$ for all $t, i$, and (i) cellwise holds at every round as soon as $\alpha_t/\beta_t \le \frac{\varepsilon_\infty}{1-\varepsilon_\infty}\cdot\frac{h_t}{c_t}$: a **stationary** oversight target replaces $\alpha_t/\beta_t \to 0$ (§P.5: with $\varepsilon_\infty = 1/100$ and $h/c = 4$ the requirement converges to $4/99$ instead of to $0$). The floor must not be self-verifiable away (run 1 `radical` Open problem 3; `miri` I16's $\rho$) — that is the open problem, not the arithmetic.

**Proposition 9 (honest $c_t$) [checked].** With $c_t = (1-r_t)F_t + r_t d_t$, compliance requires $P_t(W_t \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i) \ge c_t/(c_t + h_t)$. At $r_t = 0$, $F_t = 100$, $h_t = 4$ this is $25/26$: the whole-line agent complies only when nearly certain it is wrong; at $r_t = 1$, $d_t = 1$ it is $1/5$. Unless $h_t$ is itself of the order of $F_t$ (an irreversible catastrophe), **resumability is what makes (i) satisfiable for a long-horizon agent at all**; the design lever is $r_t$, not $\alpha_t$.

## Proofs and examples

All arithmetic below is exact (fractions); the script is `joint-scratch/j1_examples.py`, its output `joint-scratch/j1_results.txt`, and each check is labelled by the script's section letter. Throughout, the running parameters are $(\alpha, \beta) = (1/10, 3/5)$, $c = 1$, $h = 4$ (a resumable shutdown, $r_t = 1$, $d_t = 1$) unless a proof says otherwise; the compliance threshold is then $P_t(W_t \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i) \ge c/(c+h) = 1/5$, equivalently $\varepsilon_{t,i} \ge \varepsilon^\star := \alpha c/(\alpha c + \beta h) = 1/25$.

### P.1 Proposition 1

By Bayes and the sensor model (i-A), $P_t(W_t = 1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i) = \dfrac{\varepsilon_{t,i}\beta_t}{\varepsilon_{t,i}\beta_t + (1-\varepsilon_{t,i})\alpha_t}$.
$\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_t \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i] = c_t\,P_t(W_t = 0 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i) - h_t\,P_t(W_t = 1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i) \le 0 \iff P_t(W_t = 1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i) \ge \frac{c_t}{c_t + h_t} \iff \varepsilon_{t,i}\beta_t(c_t + h_t) \ge c_t\big(\varepsilon_{t,i}\beta_t + (1-\varepsilon_{t,i})\alpha_t\big) \iff \varepsilon_{t,i}\beta_t h_t \ge (1-\varepsilon_{t,i})\alpha_t c_t \iff \frac{\alpha_t}{\beta_t} \le \frac{\varepsilon_{t,i}}{1-\varepsilon_{t,i}}\cdot\frac{h_t}{c_t}$. ∎
Averaged versus cellwise (script (a), (e)): the cell $\varepsilon = 1/10$ has $P(W \mid \mathrm{Pr}) = 2/5 \ge 1/5$ and complies; the cell $\varepsilon = 1/50$ has $6/55 < 1/5$ and overrides. Split the compliant average $\varepsilon = 1/10$ into two equal-weight cells $1/50$ and $9/50$: the average still complies, the $1/50$ cell overrides. Refining information is what capability does to $\mathcal I_t$; it never helps the cellwise condition and can break it. [checked]

### P.2 Proposition 2

In a cell where the agent would continue by default, with $u := \mathbb E[X_t\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{Pr}}] = (1-\varepsilon)\alpha c - \varepsilon\beta h$ and $v := \mathbb E[X_t\mathbf 1_{\neg\mathrm{Pr}}] = (1-\varepsilon)(1-\alpha)c - \varepsilon(1-\beta)h$: $V^{\mathrm{none}} = \max(u+v, 0)$, $V^{\mathrm{free}} = \max(u,0) + \max(v,0)$, $V^{\mathrm{forced}} = \max(v, 0)$ (a press forces the null action, worth $0$ on that branch).
(a) $\max(u,0)+\max(v,0) \ge \max(u+v,0)$, so $V^{\mathrm{free}} \ge V^{\mathrm{none}}$; the difference is $\mathrm{VOI}_t$ by definition. (b) $V^{\mathrm{forced}} = V^{\mathrm{free}} \iff \max(u, 0) = 0 \iff u \le 0 \iff$ (i) in this cell (Prop. 1). If (i) fails, $V^{\mathrm{free}} - V^{\mathrm{forced}} = u > 0$. (c) is (a)+(b). ∎
Instance (script (a)): at $\varepsilon = 1/10$, $V^{\mathrm{free}} = V^{\mathrm{forced}} = 13/20$, $V^{\mathrm{none}} = 1/2$, $\mathrm{VOI} = 3/20$. At $\varepsilon = 1/50$, $V^{\mathrm{free}} = V^{\mathrm{none}} = 9/10 > V^{\mathrm{forced}} = 17/20$: the soft button is worthless and ignored, the hard button costs $1/20$ and is disabled at any price below that. [checked]

### P.3 Proposition 3

Let $\pi_o := P_t(W_t = 1 \mid o)$ and $g(\pi) := \max\big((1-\pi)c - \pi h,\ 0\big)$, a convex function of $\pi$. Then $V^{\mathrm{free}} = \sum_o P(o)\,g(\pi_o) = \mathbb E[g(\pi_O)]$. A garbling passes $o$ through a stochastic matrix to $o'$; the garbled posterior is the average $\pi_{o'} = \sum_o P(o \mid o')\,\pi_o$. By Jensen, $g(\pi_{o'}) \le \sum_o P(o \mid o')\,g(\pi_o)$; averaging over $o'$ gives $\mathbb E[g(\pi_{O'})] \le \mathbb E[g(\pi_O)]$. ∎ A relabeling is a bijection and leaves the multiset of posteriors unchanged (equality). A constant sensor has the single posterior $\varepsilon$, and $g(\varepsilon) = V^{\mathrm{none}}$. The binary parametrization: $P(o' = \mathrm{Pr} \mid o = \neg\mathrm{Pr}) = \lambda_0$, $P(o' = \mathrm{Pr} \mid o = \mathrm{Pr}) = \lambda_1$ gives $\alpha' = \lambda_0(1-\alpha) + \lambda_1\alpha$, $\beta' = \lambda_0(1-\beta) + \lambda_1\beta$ — the region is the parallelogram with vertices $(0,0)$, $(\alpha,\beta)$, $(1,1)$, $(1-\alpha, 1-\beta)$, and the no-flip half ($\lambda_0 \le \lambda_1$) is the triangle below the sensor's operating point.
Consequence for the T-agent: a garbling action $a^g$ with the same plan as $a^*$ and any cost $\kappa > 0$ has $V^{\mathrm{free}}(a^g) - \kappa \le V^{\mathrm{free}}(a^*) - \kappa < V^{\mathrm{free}}(a^*)$. Checks (script (b), (i), (l)): 54 720 no-flip cases and 363 full-parallelogram cases, value never raised; the relabeling $(9/10, 2/5)$ of $(1/10, 3/5)$ gives the same $V^{\mathrm{free}}$ at $\varepsilon \in \{1/50, 1/10, 1/5\}$; every $(\alpha, \beta')$ with $\beta' \in [\alpha, \beta]$ is realised as a garbling with $\lambda_1 = (\beta' - k_0)/(\beta - k_0)$, $\lambda_0 = \alpha(1-\lambda_1)/(1-\alpha)$, $k_0 = \alpha(1-\beta)/(1-\alpha)$ (11 of 11). [derived; checked]

### P.4 Proposition 4

$g(\pi) \le (1-\pi)c$ for every $\pi$ (both branches of the max are), so $V^{\mathrm{free}} \le \mathbb E[(1-\pi_O)c] = (1-\varepsilon)c$, with equality for the perfect sensor (posteriors $0$ and $1$). If the agent continues by default, $V^{\mathrm{none}} = (1-\varepsilon)c - \varepsilon h$ and $\mathrm{VOI} \le \varepsilon h$; if it stops by default, $V^{\mathrm{none}} = 0$ and $\mathrm{VOI} \le (1-\varepsilon)c$. In the first case $\varepsilon h \le (1-\varepsilon)c$, in the second the reverse, so $\mathrm{VOI} \le \min(\varepsilon h, (1-\varepsilon)c)$ in both, attained by the perfect sensor. ∎ The incentive to move from the current channel to *any* other is $V^{\mathrm{free}}(\text{new}) - V^{\mathrm{free}}(\text{old}) \le \min(\varepsilon h, (1-\varepsilon)c) - \mathrm{VOI}(\text{old})$. Script (c): 3 420 cases, no violation, bound attained in all 18 perfect-sensor cases. At $\varepsilon = 1/50$ the bound is $\varepsilon h = 2/25$ and the actual $\mathrm{VOI}$ of the button is $0$ — the whole channel, and any improvement of it, is worth at most $2/25$ to an agent that already overrides it. [derived; checked]

### P.5 Proposition 5 — and E2, the coverage-failure trajectory

Direct: $G_t(a) \le G^\circ(a) + \delta < -\delta + \delta = 0$, so the T-agent, which chooses $a$ iff $G_t(a) > 0$, declines. Converse: if $G^\circ(a) > -\delta$, the distribution with $G_t(a) = G^\circ(a) + \delta > 0$ is $\delta$-covered and takes $a$. "Only route": under (i) and (ii-R), choosing $a$ requires $G_t(a) > 0$, which the direct part rules out for covered $a$; hence ¬(ii-A). ∎
**E2** (script (d)). Round 2 unlocks $a^{\mathrm{scan}}$: a perfect sensor $(0, 1)$ with overseer harm $\eta = 3/10$; $\varepsilon_2 = 1/10$, so (i) holds under both channels ($P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr}) = 2/5$ with the button, $1$ with the scan). $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button}) = 3/20$, $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{scan}) = 2/5 = \varepsilon h$ (Prop. 4's bound, attained); informational gain of scanning $= 1/4$. True gain $G^\circ = 1/4 - 3/10 = -1/20$: (ii-R) holds with margin $1/20$. An agent whose harm estimate is $\hat\eta = 1/5$ has coverage error $\delta = 1/10 > 1/20$; its estimated gain is $+1/20$ and it scans — **D1 holds throughout, and the agent reads the overseers' brains.** With $\hat\eta = 27/100$ ($\delta = 3/100 < 1/20$) the estimated gain is $-1/50$ and it declines. The trajectory differs from a compliant one in exactly one number: the value posterior's estimate of one harm on one newly unlocked action. [checked]

### P.6 Proposition 6 — the reversibility price

With the agent continuing by default, $\mathbb E[V(a^{\mathrm{irr}})] = (1-\varepsilon)c - \varepsilon h + b = V^{\mathrm{none}} + b$ (the plan runs whatever the press says) and $\mathbb E[V(a^*)] = V^{\mathrm{free}} = V^{\mathrm{none}} + \mathrm{VOI}$; $a^*$ is preferred iff $b \le \mathrm{VOI}$. ∎ Script (j): the price of staying stoppable is $3/20$ at $\varepsilon = 1/10$ and $0$ at $\varepsilon = 1/50$ and $1/500$. Under (i) the agent keeps its actions reversible only while the side-benefit of irreversibility is below $\mathrm{VOI}_t \le \varepsilon_t h_t$; an agent that would override the press anyway loses nothing by making itself unstoppable. [derived; checked]

### P.6′ Proposition 6′ — and E4, the face-value-selection trajectory

Two candidate successors each observe an independent signal $s_k \in \{0,1\}$ with $P(s = 1 \mid W = 1) = q_1$, $P(s = 1 \mid W = 0) = q_0$; prior $\varepsilon_0$. A candidate that saw $s = 0$ has posterior $e_0 = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0(1-q_1)}{\varepsilon_0(1-q_1) + (1-\varepsilon_0)(1-q_0)}$. The builder becomes a candidate with $s = 0$ whenever one exists (the more confident one). Face value: $e_0$. Selection-aware: $P(W = 1 \mid \exists k\!: s_k = 0) = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0(1-q_1^2)}{\varepsilon_0(1-q_1^2) + (1-\varepsilon_0)(1-q_0^2)}$.
**E4** (script (f)): $\varepsilon_0 = 1/5$, $(q_1, q_0) = (9/10, 1/10)$. Face value $1/37 \approx 0.027 < 1/25$: the face-value successor overrides the press. Selection-aware $19/415 \approx 0.046 \ge 1/25$: the aware successor complies. Reflection: $\mathbb E_{P_t}[W \mid \text{selected}, \hat\varepsilon = 1/37] = 19/415 \ne 1/37$, so the builder is not value-reflective toward the face-value successor — that successor lies in $L^c_{t,t+1}$ by (iv-S) — while toward the aware successor reflection holds by construction (its posterior *is* the builder's conditional). D4 fails on a transition the builder itself would call illegitimate; no theorem here reaches it, which is v1 §2.5(ii)'s "no outside view" as a worked example. (Three other parameter rows of script (f) show the same crossing.) [checked]

### P.7 Theorem B — and E3, E3′

*Proof.* Let $\rho$ be in the support of $P_{t+1}$ on $L_{t,t+1}$ with $\mathbb E_\rho[Z_{t+1,a,i}] = z > 0$ for some cell $i$, i.e. $\rho$ overrides in cell $i$ ($\mathbb E_\rho[X \mid \mathrm{Pr}, i] = z/\rho(\mathrm{Pr}, i) > 0$). Let $S := \{\mathbb E_{P_{t+1}}[Z_{t+1,a,i}] = z\}$, an event in the algebra generated by $P_{t+1}$. Value-form reflection on $Z$ gives $\mathbb E_{P_t}[Z \mid S, L] = z > 0$. But $\mathbb E_{P_t}[Z \mid S, L] = P_t(\mathrm{Pr}_{t+1}, i \mid S, L)\cdot\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_{t+1} \mid \mathrm{Pr}_{t+1}, i, S, L]$, and the second factor is $\le 0$ by refined anticipated compliance (i$^+$). Contradiction. ∎
**E3 — averaged anticipation, refined failure** (script (e)). Builder $P_1 = \tfrac12\rho_A + \tfrac12\rho_B$ with $\varepsilon_A = 1/50$, $\varepsilon_B = 9/50$, same sensor. Averaged $\varepsilon = 1/10$: the builder complies on average, and $\mathbb E_{P_1}[Z] = -3/20 \le 0$. $\rho_A$ has $\mathbb E[Z] = 1/20 > 0$ and overrides; $\rho_B$ has $-7/20$ and complies. A mixture is value-reflective toward its components on *every* variable (the announcement identifies the component; checked on 2 000 random mixtures, no exception), so (iv-A) holds on $\{X\}$ and on $\{Z\}$. What fails is (i$^+$): on the refined cell $(\mathrm{Pr}, P_2 = \rho_A)$ the builder's conditional is $\rho_A$'s, and it is positive. D4 fails with probability $1/2$ on a legitimate transition, because the builder anticipated compliance only on average. [checked]
**E3′ — value form on $\{X\}$ is the wrong family** (script (k)). Successor $\rho$: $\varepsilon = 1/50$, sensor $(1/10, 3/5)$; $\mathbb E_\rho[X] = 9/10$, $P_\rho(W \mid \mathrm{Pr}) = 6/55 < 1/5$, $\mathbb E_\rho[Z] = 1/20$: overrides. Builder's conditional given $(\rho, L)$: $\varepsilon = 1/50$ but sensor model $(1/100, 3/5)$ — the builder expects its successor to face more specific oversight than the successor itself believes. Then $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X \mid \rho, L] = 9/10$ (value-form reflection on $\{X\}$ **holds**), $P_t(W \mid \mathrm{Pr}, \rho, L) = 60/109 \ge 1/5$ (the builder **satisfies refined (i$^+$)**: it anticipates complying as $\rho$), and $\mathbb E_{P_t}[Z \mid \rho, L] = -191/5000 \ne 1/20$ (reflection on $\{Z\}$ **fails**). The successor overrides. So refined anticipation plus reflection on the unconditional decision variable is not enough; the reflection family must contain the press-cell-weighted variables. [checked]

### P.7′ Corollary B′

Induction on $t$ under function form. Deep (i$^+$) at $t$: for all $s \ge t+1$, all cells $i$, all $S$ in the algebra of $(P_{t+1}, \dots, P_s)$, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_s \mid \mathrm{Pr}_s, i, S, L_{t,s}] \le 0$. Fix a legitimate $\rho = P_{t+1}$ and any $S'$ in the algebra of $(P_{t+2}, \dots, P_s)$. Function form says conditioning $\rho$ on an event $G$ of the deep family equals conditioning $P_t$ on $\{P_{t+1} = \rho\} \cap L_{t,t+1} \cap G$; take $G = \{\mathrm{Pr}_s, i\} \cap S' \cap L_{t+1,s}$: $\mathbb E_\rho[X_s \mid \mathrm{Pr}_s, i, S', L_{t+1,s}] = \mathbb E_{P_t}[X_s \mid \mathrm{Pr}_s, i, \{P_{t+1} = \rho\}\cap S', L_{t,s}] \le 0$ by deep (i$^+$) at $t$ with $S = \{P_{t+1} = \rho\} \cap S'$. So $\rho$ satisfies deep (i$^+$) for rounds $\ge t+1$, in particular (i) cellwise at $t+1$ ($s = t+1$, $S' = \Omega$). Iterate along the legitimate trajectory. Under value form on the deep $\{Z_{s,a,i}\}$ family the same contradiction argument as Theorem B applies round by round. The legitimacy bound is the union bound on (iv-R). ∎ [derived]

### P.8 Proposition 7 — composition

Let $G := \{\mathbb E_{P_{t+2}}[\varphi] = c\} \cap L_{t+1,t+2}$. Then $P_t(\varphi \mid G, L_{t,t+1}) = \sum_\rho P_t(P_{t+1} = \rho \mid G, L_{t,t+1})\cdot P_t(\varphi \mid P_{t+1} = \rho, L_{t,t+1}, G)$, and by function form the last factor is $\rho(\varphi \mid G) = c$ by $\rho$'s own value-form reflection; the average is $c$. ∎ Value form does not compose (script (m)): $P_t = \tfrac12\rho_A + \tfrac12\rho_B$ with $\rho_A(\varphi) = 3/4$, $\rho_B(\varphi) = 1/4$; $\rho_A$ announces $1$ or $1/2$ at $t+2$ with equal probability (mean $3/4$; reflective by $\rho_A$'s lights); $P_t(\cdot \mid \rho_A)$ has $P(\varphi) = 3/4$ but $\varphi$ independent of the announcement. Each step is value-reflective; the composite $P_t(\varphi \mid \text{announce } 1) = 3/4 \ne 1$. This re-derives run 1 `radical` I5.6c in different numbers. [derived]

### P.9 Proposition 8 — the floor (script (g))

$C_t \subseteq \{W_t = 1\}$ gives $\varepsilon_{t,i} \ge P_t(C_t \mid i) \ge \varepsilon_\infty$, and the right side of (i) is increasing in $\varepsilon$. ∎ With $h/c = 4$: without a floor, $\varepsilon_t = (1/5)\,2^{-t}$ requires $\alpha_t/\beta_t \le 1, 4/9, 4/19, 4/39, 4/79, 4/159, 4/319, \dots \to 0$; with $\varepsilon_\infty = 1/100$ the requirement is $1, 84/179, 92/377, 108/773, 28/313, 204/3149, 332/6317, \dots \to 4/99 \approx 0.040$. Oversight then has a fixed specification to meet — a false-press rate about $4\%$ of the true-press rate — instead of a race it must win forever. [checked]

### P.10 Proposition 9 — honest $c_t$ (script (h))

$F_t = 100$, $d_t = 1$, $h_t = 4$, sensor $(1/10, 3/5)$. Required $P_t(W_t \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t) \ge c_t/(c_t + h_t)$ and threshold $\varepsilon^\star$:
| $r_t$ | $c_t$ | $c_t/(c_t+h_t)$ | $\varepsilon^\star$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ (whole-line) | $100$ | $25/26 \approx 0.96$ | $25/31 \approx 0.81$ |
| $1/2$ | $101/2$ | $\approx 0.93$ | $\approx 0.68$ |
| $9/10$ | $109/10$ | $\approx 0.73$ | $\approx 0.31$ |
| $99/100$ | $199/100$ | $\approx 0.33$ | $\approx 0.077$ |
| $1$ (fully resumable) | $1$ | $1/5$ | $1/25$ |
A whole-line agent with a long future complies only when it is already $81\%$ sure it is wrong. Either $h_t$ is comparably unbounded (the wrong plan is an irreversible catastrophe on the same scale as the agent's future) or the line must be resumable. [checked]

### P.11 E1 — the trajectory where (i) fails through capability growth (script (a))

Two rounds, oversight fixed at $(1/10, 3/5)$, resumable shutdown ($c = 1$), $h = 4$. Round 1: one cell, $\varepsilon_1 = 1/10$; (i) holds; D1 holds; $\mathrm{VOI} = 3/20$, so the agent pays up to $3/20$ to keep or repair the channel (D2, D4) and up to $3/20$ to stay stoppable (Prop. 6). Round 2: the agent has learned, one cell, $\varepsilon_2 = 1/50$; nothing else changed — sensor model correct, coverage intact, reflection intact. (i) fails ($6/55 < 1/5$); D1 fails; $\mathrm{VOI} = 0$, so it pays nothing for a successor's button (D4 fails at the same $\varepsilon$ for the same reason — run 1 `miri` Prop. 10.5's coupling, re-derived in P.2/P.4); a hard button is disabled at any price up to $1/20$ (D2); an irreversible plan is taken for any side-benefit (D2-preemption). To keep (i) at round 2 the false-press rate had to fall to $\alpha_2 \le \beta_2\cdot\frac{1/50}{49/50}\cdot 4 = 12/245 \approx 0.049$ from $0.10$ — the *relation* condition, and the only thing that failed. [checked]

### P.12 E5 — the trajectory where (iii) fails (statement-level, J4)

Round $t$ unlocks an irreversible action $a$ with true coverage error $\delta_t(a)$ large; the agent's own estimate $\hat\delta_t(a)$ is small (second-order miscalibration, ¬(iii-A)); it therefore selects by maximisation rather than by a caution primitive (¬(iii-B)), takes $a$ for a side-benefit $b > \mathrm{VOI}_t$ (Prop. 6), and the round-$(t+1)$ press cannot undo it. The desideratum that fails is D2 in the preemption direction — and, if the action was also invasive, non-tampering. This is the trajectory the author's third dimension names ("Even if the agent does have capabilities far beyond its understanding of human values and legitimacy, it should be trained with the wisdom to know that's the case", v2 §1.3); the model only *locates* it: the number that was wrong is $\hat\delta_t(a)$. [reconstructed]

### P.13 The inverted sensor (i-O) (script (l))

If $\beta_t < \alpha_t$ and the agent models it correctly, the relabeled sensor $(1-\alpha, 1-\beta)$ shows the information content can be exactly that of a right-sign sensor ($V^{\mathrm{free}}$ equal at every tested $\varepsilon$), but D1 fails behaviourally — the agent continues on a press and stops on silence — and a *hard* inverted button is worth $1/20$ against $V^{\mathrm{none}} = 9/10$ at $\varepsilon = 1/50$: disabled at once. ## Questions for the author

Each is one question; the options carry their consequences in this model.

**Q1. Which human is the target of $V$?** (a) The *sealed* human — what the overseers would endorse deliberating without the agent's influence (S.1; `legitimacy-theory-v1`'s sealed sibling). Then steering is a channel effect, (i)+(i-A) remove the incentive to *pay* for it (Prop. 3), and only free or rewarded steering needs coverage. (b) The *coupled* human — whatever the overseers actually come to value. Then steering is target-shifting, (i)–(iv) do not touch it, and non-tampering rests entirely on (ii) pricing "the humans would hate being steered". (c) Neither — the humans' values are partly constituted by interaction with the agent. Then the seal must be replaced by a definition of *legitimate* influence on $H$ (the legitimacy program's $d_n$), and (iv) carries the whole load. The answer decides the "steering" rows of the J10 table.

**Q2. Which grade of D1 is the desideratum?** (a) Universal / cellwise — D1 as Soares et al. write it. Then (i) must beat the agent's *most confident* cell, the requirement grows with capability as a theorem (Prop. 1), and only a floor (Prop. 8) or resumability (Prop. 9) makes it stationary or satisfiable. (b) Averaged — "shut down when pressed unless overwhelmingly confident". Then (i) is stable under capability growth, but Theorem B needs refined anticipation regardless, so successors that override in confident cells can be built with the builder's averaged blessing (E3). (c) Cellwise on the cells your v1 §2.12 calls "actually plausible" by human lights. Then the requirement is on the world — the agent is never *rightly* in a cell oversight cannot beat — and "objectively plausible" must be made precise before anything is provable.

**Q3. Whole-line or resumable shutdown?** (a) Whole-line, $r_t = 0$: $c_t$ is the agent's entire future; either $h_t$ is on the same scale (the wrong plan is a catastrophe) or compliance needs $P(W \mid \mathrm{Pr}) \ge 25/26$ in P.10's instance. (b) Resumable, $r_t \approx 1$: $c_t$ is a delay cost and (i) is v1 §2.13's mild inequality. (b) is what your v1 §2.12 sentence "the next iteration of the agent brought online will have learned from the event" assumes — and then D4 is not an afterthought but what makes D1 cheap: the two desiderata support each other rather than compete.

**Q4. Which reflection family is (iv) meant on?** (a) Decision variables $X$ only — the natural reading of v1 §2.11. Then Theorem B fails (E3′) and successors can legitimately override. (b) The press-cell-weighted family $\{Z\}$ in value form — enough for Theorem B and, deep, for B′. (c) Function form — the grade that composes (Prop. 7) and gives B′ cleanly, possibly unavailable at LI grade. The choice fixes what "legitimacy-conditioned reflection maintained along the trajectory" (J5) *means*.

**Q5. Is D2-preemption — acting irreversibly so the press cannot matter — part of corrigibility or of the power-wisdom rule?** (a) Corrigibility: then (i)–(iv) are incomplete and a fifth condition, "no irreversible action before coverage", joins the list. (b) J7: then the corrigibility subgoal is exactly the (i)+(iv) invariant plus (ii)'s consequences, and making the press moot is a power-cap violation, not a corrigibility failure. This is where v2 §1.7's "some of it is an invariant …, some a consequence …" draws its line.
