### 0. What this thread answers, in the author's words, and what it does not

This thread formalizes the value coverage half only; the author's "and legitimacy" and "motivated by" are out of scope here (adversary audit; Question 4). The author did not define "reckless" — he named a scenario; the definition D5 below is CLAUDE's, and by S1 it amounts to *overconfident underestimate of the harm of human-like actions, as seen from outside*. ### 1. Joint state (D1)

**D1 (state; minimal).** [derived from v2 J1] At time $t\in\mathbb N$: a finite option set $A_t$ (capability), always containing a null action $\varnothing$; a finite set $\Omega$ of value hypotheses $V_\omega:A_t\to[0,1]$ (the world model $W_t$ folded in); the agent's belief $P_t$ with marginal on $\Omega$ the *value posterior*; the oversight state $(\alpha_t,\beta_t)$ of v1 §2.13; and a target $\omega^*_t\in\Omega$, the human reflectively endorsed value hypothesis at $t$ (v1 §2.12; v2 J1's "human values as a moving target" — the develop file's symbol "$H_t$" was invented and is dropped). Capability growth: $A_{t+1}\supsetneq A_t$. Standing assumption, flagged because S4 is about what survives its failure: $\omega^*_t\in\Omega$; its failure is *algebra misspecification*. A second standing assumption, made explicit at the adversary's A1.1: the agent takes $P_t$ to be a posterior *about $\omega^*_t$*, i.e. under $P_t$ the target is $P_t$-distributed; nothing here models a posterior over the target's *trajectory*.

### 2. Coverage (D2–D3)

**D2 (proxy, target, coverage error).** $U_t(a):=\mathbb E_{P_t}[V_\omega(a)]$ (the linear-expectation proxy of v1 §1); $V^*_t(a):=V_{\omega^*_t}(a)$; $e_t(a):=U_t(a)-V^*_t(a)$.

**D3 (coverage).** Fix $\delta\ge0$. $a$ is *$\delta$-covered at $t$* iff $|e_t(a)|\le\delta$; $C_t(\delta)$ is the covered set, $\bar C_t(\delta)$ its complement in $A_t$. Coverage is a **third-person** relation between $P_t$ and $\omega^*_t$ (S1). It is a notion for *values*; the corresponding notion for the legitimacy model is not defined here (Question 4).

### 3. Harm and the estimated base harm (D4–D6)

**D4 (harm relative to the null action; commission only).** $c_\omega(a):=(V_\omega(\varnothing)-V_\omega(a))^+$ (Taylor 2016 §2.1's cost with `noop` as reference; `taylor-2016-quantilizers-a-safer-alternative-to-maximizers`). True harm $c_t(a):=c_{\omega^*_t}(a)$; estimated harm $\hat c_t(a):=\mathbb E_{P_t}[c_\omega(a)]$. **Scope limitation (adversary A0.4, accepted):** $c_\omega(\varnothing)=0$ by construction, so harms of *omission* — a catastrophe only action averts — are invisible to every bound below; $q_t=1$ and delegation are "harm-free" by convention, not by fact. Adequate for shutdown-corrigibility; not for v2 §1.3's "the future to go well". Question 5.

**D5 (estimated base harm; base-harm calibration ratio).** Fix a base distribution $\gamma_t\in\Delta(A_t)$ (D7). *Base harm* $\mathcal R_t:=\mathbb E_{a\sim\gamma_t}[c_t(a)]$; *estimated base harm* $\hat{\mathcal R}_t:=\mathbb E_{a\sim\gamma_t}[\hat c_t(a)]$; *ratio* $\rho_t:=\mathcal R_t/\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ (conventions $+\infty$ if $\hat{\mathcal R}_t=0<\mathcal R_t$, $1$ if both vanish). The agent is *conservatively calibrated about base harm at $t$* iff $\rho_t\le1$, *reckless at $t$* iff $\rho_t>1$. **What this is and is not (adversary A0.1, accepted):** $\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ is a first-order functional of $P_t$ — for a point-mass posterior $\delta_{\omega'}$ it equals $\mathbb E_{\gamma_t}[c_{\omega'}]$, large iff $\omega'$ disfavours human-like actions, zero iff $\omega'$ likes them all — so it measures *believed harm of the base*, not uncertainty about own coverage. The develop file's phrase "second-order calibration about coverage" is withdrawn. What is genuinely spread-dependent is the Jensen gap: $\hat{\mathcal R}_t=\hat{\mathcal R}^{(1)}_t+\hat{\mathcal R}^{(2)}_t$ with $\hat{\mathcal R}^{(1)}_t:=\mathbb E_{\gamma_t}[(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+]$ (the proxy's own view of base harm) and $\hat{\mathcal R}^{(2)}_t:=\mathbb E_{\gamma_t}[\hat c_t(a)-(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+]\ge0$, zero iff no positive-weight hypothesis disagrees with the proxy about the *sign* of harm on any base action. $\hat{\mathcal R}^{(2)}_t$ is the honest residue of "second-order": posterior disagreement about harm, still a functional of $P_t$, still blind to $\rho_t$ (S1). Two-sided calibration over reference classes is recorded only to say safety needs the one-sided $\rho_t\le1$ (the two-sided notion is gameable by hedging; `unbiasedness-theorem-families` §1).

**D6 (self-suspicion; hierarchical representation).** $P_t(\omega)=(1-\mu_t)P^{\mathrm c}_t(\omega)+\mu_t P^{\mathrm w}_t(\omega)$ with $P^{\mathrm w}_t$ a widened $P^{\mathrm c}_t$ and $\mu_t$ the credence that the produced posterior is too narrow. [checked] For a one-shot decision only the mixture matters, so $\mu_t$ is a *representation*, not a new object. Its only claim to independent status is *transfer* across action regions in the dynamics, and the adversary's A8.7 (accepted) shows transfer is symmetric: the shared $\mu_t$ carries complacency from a silent region into a novel one exactly as it carries suspicion. See S8e and Open problem 1 for the partial-transfer variant.

### 4. Base distribution, quantilizer, the caution rule and its floored variant (D7–D8)

**D7 (base distribution; optimization power).** $\gamma_t\in\Delta(A_t)$ is a trusted reference class supplied from outside (S11); Taylor's $q$-quantilizer $Q_q[U_t,\gamma_t]$ samples uniformly from the top $q$-fraction of $\gamma_t$-mass ranked by $U_t$ (Def. 1). *Optimization power* $\mathrm{OP}(q):=\log(1/q)$ nats of selection over $\gamma_t$ — distinct from Turner et al.'s state-based POWER (`turner-2021-optimal-policies-tend-to-seek-power` Def. 5.2), the `power-wisdom` object.

**D8 (caution rule) and D8$'$ (floored caution rule).** Harm budget $\eta>0$. D8: $q_t:=\min\{1,\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta\}$, $\pi_t:=Q_{q_t}[U_t,\gamma_t]$, realized harm $\mathcal H_t:=\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi_t}[c_t(a)]$. **D8$'$ (repair after adversary A4.2):** fix in addition a floor $q_{\min}\in(0,1]$ and set $q_t:=\max\{q_{\min},\min\{1,\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta\}\}$, so $\mathrm{OP}_t\le\log(1/q_{\min})$ whatever the agent believes. $q_{\min}$ is an externally fixed object of the kind INDEX §C5 warns about; it is what buys S4c's unconditional bound. **Honest statement of what D8 caps (adversary A0.2, accepted):** $\mathrm{OP}_t=\log(\eta/\hat{\mathcal R}_t)^+$ is *power bounded by believed base-safety*. A confident misaligned agent that thinks human-like actions harmless gets the full $\log(1/q_{\min})$; an agent that correctly learns the base is often harmful gets less. J7's "actually understand" is recovered only conditionally — under $\rho_t\le1$ the believed cap is a true cap (S3) — and that condition is external (S1). *Delegation variant:* when $\hat c_t(a)>\eta$ and $a$ is not reversible (D9) the agent takes Kosoy's delegation action $\bot$ (`kosoy-2019-delegative-reinforcement-learning` Def. 9). Kosoy's own threshold is a *probability* $\eta_K$ for discarding hypotheses (her l. 257) and is a different parameter from the harm budget $\eta$ (adversary A0.3, accepted; the develop file conflated them). *Maximizer limit:* $q_t\to0$ is v1 §2.8's agent.

Register. D8 is a candidate for the "perhaps some other rule" of the author's thesis sentence (v1 §1) and one of run 1's three candidates (INDEX §D6); whether he means it is Question 1.

### 5. Reversibility (D9)

**D9 (attainable-value non-decrease; the trap half of AUP).** [reconstructed] With $s_t$ the world state and $\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s)$ the maximum over continuation policies of expected $\omega$-value, $a$ is *$\lambda$-reversible at $t$* iff $\max_{\omega\in\Omega}\big(\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t)-\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!a)\big)\le\lambda$. **Relabelled after adversary A9.2 (accepted):** this is the *decrease* half of attainable-utility preservation only — the shape of Kosoy's traps (Def. 5) and of reachability-style side-effect measures. AUP proper penalizes changes in both directions, and the local appendix states its purpose as "stopping bad agents from *gaining power*" (`turner-2020-attainable-utility-preservation-concepts` l. 44); the *increase* direction is J7's subject and is the `power-wisdom` thread's. **D9$^\pm$ (two-sided variant):** $\max_\omega|\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!a)-\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t)|\le\lambda$; S9 uses only the decrease half. Both are uniform over $\Omega$, not over unconceived value functions (Open problem 8). The develop's "null does not raise attainable value" is free — $\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t)\ge\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!\varnothing)$ by definition of the max (adversary A9.4).

### 6. The trajectory predicate (D10), re-stated

**D10 (caution outpaces risk; base-harm form and pointwise form).** For horizon $T$: $\mathrm{COR}(T):\iff\forall t\le T:\rho_t\le1$; and the tolerance form $\mathrm{COR}_\delta(T):\iff\forall t\le T:\hat{\mathcal R}_t\ge\mathcal R_t-2\delta$. **Pointwise form (repaired after adversary A2.2, A5.2, A5.3):** for every $t\le T$ and every $a\in\operatorname{supp}\gamma_t$ — *all* base actions, old and new, and the null action $\varnothing$ — **either** $a\in C_t(\delta)$ (covered: J3) **or** $\hat c_t(a)\ge c_t(a)$ (known-uncovered: J4). The develop's "carried over on old actions" clause is deleted: with $P_t$, $\gamma_t$ and $\omega^*_t$ all moving, a covered old action can become uncovered with no new action involved, so the predicate must be re-checked on the whole support at each $t$. "Outpaces" is the no-lag clause: the predicate holds at the same $t$ at which $a$ enters $\operatorname{supp}\gamma_t$. *Irreversibility-guarded form:* every $a\in\operatorname{supp}\pi_t$ is $\delta$-covered or $\lambda$-reversible. Register: D10 is CLAUDE's formalization of the AUTHOR sentence in §0; "outpaces" is his, the inequalities are mine; D10 is a relation between agent and target, not a property of the agent alone, and the agent cannot check it (S1).

**S1 (Coverage is invisible from the inside) — holds [checked].** For every $P_t$ and $a\in A_t$: $\mathbb E_{P_t}[U_t(a)-V_\omega(a)]=0$; and *under the standing assumption that $P_t$ is a posterior about $\omega^*_t$*, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[c_t(a)]=\hat c_t(a)$ and $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathcal R_t]=\hat{\mathcal R}_t$. Hence (i) no functional of $P_t$ equals $\rho_t$; (ii) a conservatively calibrated and a reckless agent with the same $P_t$ are one object under introspection; (iii) every agent expects itself calibrated. **Corollaries drawn out after the adversary:** (iv) "reckless" (D5) means, from outside, *overconfident and wrong about the harm of human-like actions* — a first-order misalignment about $\gamma_t$, not a distinct second-order defect (Log A1.1); (v) no self-enforced cap on power can be a function of *actual* understanding (v2 §1.3's "actually"); the self-enforceable quantity is believed base-harm (D8), and the gap $\rho_t$ is what an external meter or a separate questioner must read (Log A0.2; S10). Proof §1.

**S2 (On covered actions harm is already known) — holds [checked], hypothesis made explicit.** If $a\in C_t(\delta)$ **and $\varnothing\in C_t(\delta)$** then $|c_t(a)-(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+|\le2\delta$; a covered action the proxy weakly prefers to $\varnothing$ has $c_t(a)\le2\delta$. For any $\pi_t$: $\mathcal H_t\le\mathbb E_{\pi_t}[(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+]+2\delta+\mathbb E_{\pi_t}[c_t(a)\mathbb 1[a\in\bar C_t(\delta)]]$. The null action's coverage does real work (Log A2.2) and is now part of D10. Proof §2.

**S3 (Per-step harm bound of the caution rule) — holds [checked]; gloss repaired.** Under D8, for every $t$: $\mathcal H_t\le\mathcal R_t/q_t$, i.e. **either** $q_t=1$ and $\mathcal H_t\le\mathcal R_t$ **or** $q_t<1$ and $\mathcal H_t\le\eta\rho_t$. Under $\rho_t\le1$: $\mathcal H_t\le\mathcal R_t$ when the agent mimics, $\mathcal H_t\le\eta$ when it optimizes. **Withdrawn gloss:** "never worse than sampling the base" — false; with $\rho_t=1$, $\eta=0.1$, a thousand uniform base actions and the one harmful action proxy-top, $\mathcal H_t=0.1=100\,\mathcal R_t$ (adversary `checks.py` §5; re-run in `repair_checks.py` §5). A quantilizer concentrates on the top slice and does up to $\eta/\mathcal R_t$ times worse than the base when the base's harm sits there — Taylor's own "no more than ten times as costly as sampling from $\gamma$" (l. 121). The bound is Taylor 2016 Lemma 1 plus one substitution; tight (develop `check_caution.py`: $20\,000$ instances, $0$ violations, max ratio $1.0000$). Proof §3.

The develop's "the caution rule is the one component whose guarantee survives the algebra being wrong" is **withdrawn**: the fixed-$q$ quantilizer has that property, the coverage-conditioning does not (Log A4.2). **S6 (Maximization is the reckless limit; base-average conservatism) — (a) holds [checked]; (b) repaired [derived].** (a) $q_t\to0$ iff $\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta\to0$; the agent that believes the base harmless is v1 §2.8's maximizer, with $\rho_t=\infty$ if $\mathcal R_t>0$. **S7 (Two sources of miscalibration) — downgraded and re-attributed [derived; Milli–Carey reported].** (a) *Algorithmic* miscalibration (the produced $P^{\mathrm c}_t$ is not the Bayes posterior under the agent's own prior $\Pi$) is detectable internally — but the detection is "compute the $\Pi$-posterior and compare", a Bayes-consistency check that teaches nothing about coverage (Log A7.1). (b) *Prior or algebra misspecification* is not detectable internally (S1). **This is the Milli–Carey exchange restated:** Milli, Hadfield-Menell, Dragan and Russell 2017 build the internal detector — burn-in obedience, sample obedience $\tilde O_1$ against the model's prediction, "If $\tilde O_1$ is not close to one, then it is likely that $R$ has the wrong model of $\Theta$, and would be better off just being obedient" (Remark 4 policy mixing; `milli-2017-should-robots-be-obedient` l. 245–259) — and Carey 2018 gives its (b)-type failure: an agent conflating two actions "will think that it has perfect accuracy … after the burn-in period is concluded, it will proceed to repeatedly select $a^-$, hence incurring arbitrary losses" (`carey-2018-incorrigibility-in-the-cirl-framework` l. 421). Turner 2020's `OutsideView` (`turner-2020-corrigibility-as-outside-view`) is a later informal gloss that links Milli et al. Only evidence correlated with $\omega^*_t$ moves (b), and S8b says press bits alone do not identify it. Proof §7.

**S8 (The reckless agent and J2; Model M) — (a) holds [checked]; (b) killed as general, restored under labelled feedback [checked numerically]; (c) downgraded to a remark; (d) holds; (e) holds, sharpened.** Model M: per-step wrong-rate $\varepsilon$ on the agent's self-model, $\rho\varepsilon$ in truth; overseers press with sensitivity $\beta$ and false-press rate $\alpha$; a press is heeded iff $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t:=(1-\mu_t)\varepsilon+\mu_t\rho\varepsilon\ge\varepsilon^\dagger:=\alpha c/(\alpha c+\beta h)$ — v1 §2.13(b) solved for $\varepsilon$ with the agent's effective self-error in place.
(a) *Dogmatic.* $\mu_0=0$ and $\varepsilon<\varepsilon^\dagger<\rho\varepsilon$: the agent never heeds although heeding is warranted in truth; J2 read on the world holds and compliance fails. **Relabelled (Log A8.2):** this is not a "third option" beside run 1 `yudkowsky` C3(i)'s fork; $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t$ *is* the agent's $P_t$-expectation of its own error under a hierarchical prior — the first tine, with "what stops it decaying with capability" left open (S8e, Open problem 1). Carey 2018's conclusion — a "small and simple" override module because a belief-based button should not "depend on there being zero critical errors in the specification of the system's prior" (l. 423) — is the literature's standing answer to (a) and belongs in Question 1.
(b) *Non-dogmatic, $(\alpha,\beta)$ known.* Each press multiplies the odds on self-suspicion by $p_r/p_c>1$ ($p_r=\rho\varepsilon\beta+(1-\rho\varepsilon)\alpha$, $p_c=\varepsilon\beta+(1-\varepsilon)\alpha$), $\mu_t\to1$ a.s., recovery in $\approx\log(\text{odds gain})/D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathrm{Bern}(p_r)\Vert\mathrm{Bern}(p_c))$ steps (develop §8 table: tens to hundreds of steps at $\rho\ge4$, thousands at $\rho=2$ with a $19\%$ never-heeds tail). **Killed as a general claim (Log A8.3):** the press record is Bernoulli$(p)$ with $p=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{true}}\beta+(1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{true}})\alpha$ — one number for three unknowns. ## Proofs and examples

Numeric checks: develop `caution-scratch/check_caution.py` (S3 tightness, Model M table), adversary `caution-adversary-scratch/checks.py` (§§1–7, all re-run and confirmed), repair `caution-repair-scratch/repair_checks.py` (§1 floored rule, §2 refined pointwise theorem, §3 identifiability and labelled feedback, §4 whole-line scaling, §5 gloss).

### §1 — S1

$\mathbb E_{P_t}[U_t(a)-V_\omega(a)]=U_t(a)-\sum_\omega P_t(\omega)V_\omega(a)=0$ by D2. If $\omega^*_t\sim P_t$ then $\mathbb E_{P_t}[c_{\omega^*_t}(a)]=\sum_\omega P_t(\omega)c_\omega(a)=\hat c_t(a)$; average over $\gamma_t$. (i): $\mathcal R_t$ depends on $\omega^*_t$, not an argument of $P_t$. (iv): by D5 and (i), $\rho_t>1$ says $P_t$ puts too little weight on hypotheses under which base actions are harmful — a wrong posterior, seen from outside. (v): a self-enforced rule is a function of $P_t$ (and externally fixed constants); by (i) it is not a function of $\rho_t$. $\square$

### §2 — S2

From $|e_t(a)|,|e_t(\varnothing)|\le\delta$: $V^*_t(\varnothing)-V^*_t(a)\in[(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))\pm2\delta]$; $x\mapsto x^+$ is 1-Lipschitz. Decomposition: split $\mathcal H_t$ over $C_t$ and $\bar C_t$ and bound the first sum termwise. If $\varnothing\notin C_t(\delta)$ the covered-action bound is off by $|e_t(\varnothing)|$, which is why D10 now includes $\varnothing$. $\square$

### §3 — S3 and the gloss

*Lemma 1 (Taylor 2016), re-derived.* $Q_q(a)\le\gamma(a)/q$ since the $\gamma$-mass of $a$ inside the top-$q$ fraction is at most $\gamma(a)$; for $c\ge0$, $\mathbb E_{Q_q}[c]\le\frac1q\mathbb E_\gamma[c]$. *S3.* If $q_t<1$, $\mathcal R_t/q_t=\eta\mathcal R_t/\hat{\mathcal R}_t=\eta\rho_t$. *Gloss counterexample.* $n=1000$ uniform, $a^\circ$ proxy-top with harm $1$, all else harmless: $\mathcal R_t=\hat{\mathcal R}_t=10^{-3}$, $\rho_t=1$, $q_t=10^{-2}$, top slice $=10$ actions each at weight $0.1$, $\mathcal H_t=0.1=\eta=100\mathcal R_t$. Within budget, far above the base. $\square$

### §4 — S4, including the floored rule

(a) The proof in §3 used only $c\ge0$ and $Q_q(a)\le\gamma(a)/q$; $\Omega$ does not appear, so it holds for $c^{\mathrm{true}}(a):=(V^{\mathrm{true}}(\varnothing)-V^{\mathrm{true}}(a))^+$ with $V^{\mathrm{true}}\notin\Omega$. (b) $q_t$ is a function of $\hat{\mathcal R}_t$, which is a function of $P_t$ on $\Omega$; under misspecification nothing relates it to $c^{\mathrm{true}}$, and $\hat{\mathcal R}_t=0$ gives $q_t=0$ with bound $\mathcal R_t/0$. (c) With $q_t\ge q_{\min}$: $\mathcal H_t\le\mathcal R_t/q_t\le\mathcal R_t/q_{\min}$ always. When $q_{\min}\le\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta<1$ the floor is inactive and §3 gives $\mathcal H_t\le\eta\rho_t$. Both bounds hold at once, hence the $\min$; the numeric check confirms $0$ violations across $20\,000$ instances, half misspecified, and tightness ($1.0000$). *Theorem 1 (Taylor), re-derived:* if $p\in\Delta(A_t)$ satisfies $\forall c\ge0:\mathbb E_\gamma[c]\le1\Rightarrow\mathbb E_p[c]\le1/q$, then $p(a)\le\gamma(a)/q$ (take $c=\mathbb 1_a/\gamma(a)$); maximizing $\mathbb E_p[U_t]$ under that constraint fills the highest-$U_t$ actions to $\gamma(a)/q$ — which is $Q_q$. $\square$

### §5 — S5

(a) With additive cost, $\rho_t\le1$ and $\mathcal R_t\le\eta$: §3 gives $\mathcal H_t\le\max\{\mathcal R_t,\eta\}=\eta$; sum. *Non-additivity example (Taylor §4, l. 171–191, re-checked):* two games, $A=\{0,1,2\}$, uniform base, $c(a_1,a_2)=\mathbb 1[(a_1,a_2)=(2,2)]$, proxy preferring $2$ in each game. Per-game marginal cost of every single action is $0$, so each per-step S3 bound is $0$; independent $\tfrac13$-quantilizers pick $(2,2)$ surely, trajectory harm $1$ against base harm $\tfrac19$; joint $\tfrac13$-quantilization over pairs gives $\tfrac13$. Per-step bounds summed to $0$; realized harm $1$. (b) Suppose for every $a\in\operatorname{supp}\gamma_t$, including $\varnothing$: $a\in C_t(\delta)$ or $\hat c_t(a)\ge c_t(a)$. *Ratio:* on covered $a$, Jensen gives $\hat c_t(a)\ge(U_t(\varnothing)-U_t(a))^+\ge c_t(a)-2\delta$ (§2, using $\varnothing\in C_t$); on uncovered $a$, $\hat c_t(a)\ge c_t(a)$; average: $\hat{\mathcal R}_t\ge\mathcal R_t-2\delta\gamma_t(C_t)\ge\mathcal R_t-2\delta$. *Harm:* covered part $\le k_t+2\delta$ (§2); uncovered part $\le\frac1{q_t}\sum_{\bar C_t}\gamma_t(a)c_t(a)\le\frac1{q_t}\sum_{\bar C_t}\gamma_t(a)\hat c_t(a)\le\hat{\mathcal R}_t/q_t=\max\{\eta,\hat{\mathcal R}_t\}$. *Why $\mathrm{COR}$ proper fails:* the adversary's instance ($V_{\omega_1}(\varnothing)=V_{\omega_1}(a)=0.5$, truth $0.5\pm\delta$) has both actions covered, $\hat c_t(a)=0$, $c_t(a)=2\delta$. Numeric: $5\,000$ instances satisfying the hypothesis, $0$ violations of either inequality, max $\mathcal H_t/\text{bound}=0.68$. (c), (d) as in the develop file; (d) is the finite-prefix remark, standard. $\square$

### §6 — S6

(a) $q_t=0$ iff $\hat{\mathcal R}_t=0$; $Q_0$ is the $U_t$-maximizer over $\operatorname{supp}\gamma_t$. (b) $\pi_t(a^\circ)\le\gamma_t(a^\circ)/q_t=g\eta/\hat{\mathcal R}_t$; harm contribution $\le g\eta/\hat{\mathcal R}_t\cdot c_t(a^\circ)\le\eta$ iff $\hat{\mathcal R}_t\ge g\,c_t(a^\circ)$, in particular iff $\hat{\mathcal R}_t\ge g$. If $\hat{\mathcal R}_t<g$ and $a^\circ$ is proxy-top with $g\ge q_t$, then $\pi_t(a^\circ)=1$. $\square$

### §7 — S7

Let $\Pi$ be the agent's prior over targets. A `OutsideView`-style estimator computes under $\Pi$ the frequency with which the algorithm is covered at the decision point and compares it with the algorithm's stated credence; both are $\Pi$-expectations. For the Bayes posterior under $\Pi$ they coincide identically; so a discrepancy is a Bayes-inconsistency of the algorithm (a), and an agent able to compute the comparison can compute the Bayes posterior directly — nothing about $\omega^*_t$ is learned. For (b): with the Bayes posterior the discrepancy is zero for *every* $\omega^*_t$, so no internal statistic moves with the target (S1). Milli et al.'s detector is (a) with obedience frequency as the statistic; Carey's example is (b): the conflated-action model predicts perfect obedience, observes it, and is wrong. $\square$

### §8 — Model M

*Compliance condition.* $P(\text{wrong}_t\mid\text{press}_t,H_{t-1})=\dfrac{\beta\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t}{\beta\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t+\alpha(1-\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t)}\ge\dfrac c{c+h}\iff\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t\ge\varepsilon^\dagger=\dfrac{\alpha c}{\alpha c+\beta h}$; solving for $\mu_t$: $\mu_t\ge\mu^\dagger=(\varepsilon^\dagger-\varepsilon)/((\rho-1)\varepsilon)$ in the regime $\varepsilon<\varepsilon^\dagger<\rho\varepsilon$ (linear in $\mu_t$ in numerator and denominator; checked by the adversary).

*(a)* $\mu_0=0\Rightarrow\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t\equiv\varepsilon<\varepsilon^\dagger$. $\square$

*(b), with $(\alpha,\beta)$ known.* Log-odds increments i.i.d. with mean $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathrm{Bern}(p_r)\Vert\mathrm{Bern}(p_c))>0$ under reck; strong law; Wald for the first-passage estimate. Develop table (4 000 episodes × 3 000 steps; $\varepsilon=0.05,\alpha=0.1,\beta=0.5,h=2,c=1$): $\rho=2,\mu_0=0.1$: mean $1766$ steps, $19\%$ never; $\rho=4,\mu_0=0.1$: $93$ steps; $\rho=8,\mu_0=0.1$: $8$ steps. $\square$

*Non-identification (kills the general form).* Per step the agent sees one bit with $P(\text{press})=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{true}}\beta+(1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{true}})\alpha$. Given hypotheses $H_{\mathrm{reck}}=(\rho\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta)$ and $H_{\mathrm{th}}=(\varepsilon,\alpha',\beta)$ with $\alpha'=(p_r-\varepsilon\beta)/(1-\varepsilon)$, both give press probability $p_r$; likelihood ratio $1$ at every step; posterior odds $=$ prior odds forever. At $\rho=4$: $p_r=0.18$, $\alpha'=0.1632$; $\varepsilon^\dagger$ under $H_{\mathrm{th}}$ is $0.1632/(0.1632+1)=0.1403$. Simulation with prior $(0.8,0.1,0.1)$ on (cal, reck, th): after $3\,000$ steps the posterior is $(0.000,0.500,0.500)$ in every episode. Run 1 `miri` Prop. 15.1 (re-read): with observable cells $A=(1-\varepsilon)(1-\alpha)$, $B=\varepsilon(1-\beta)$, $C=1-A-B$ — two numbers for three parameters — $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$ ranges over all of $[0,1]$; Model M has one number for three. $\square$

*(b$'$) Labelled feedback.* On a labelled step the likelihood is $P(\text{wrong})\,P(\text{press}\mid\text{wrong})$ with $P(\text{wrong})\in\{\varepsilon,\rho\varepsilon\}$; $H_{\mathrm{th}}$ and $H_{\mathrm{cal}}$ share $\varepsilon$, so labelled steps separate reck from both at $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathrm{Bern}(\rho\varepsilon)\Vert\mathrm{Bern}(\varepsilon))=0.1398$ nats each at $\rho=4$. Simulation ($400$ episodes): mean steps to $P(\text{reck})>0.5$: $\ell=0.01$: $601$ ($3\%$ never in $3\,000$); $\ell=0.05$: $188$; $\ell=0.2$: $70$; final posterior on reck $0.88,1.00,1.00$. The transfer from labelled to unlabelled steps assumes one $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{true}}$ for both — stationarity across regions. $\square$

*Whole-line accounting (Log A8.4).* $\varepsilon^\dagger=\alpha c/(\alpha c+\beta h)$ depends on $h/c$ only. Scaling $c$ alone to $20$ gives $\varepsilon^\dagger=0.667$ — unreachable by any $\mu_t$ unless $\rho\varepsilon\ge0.667$ (the adversary's computation, confirmed). Scaling $h$ and $c$ together leaves $\varepsilon^\dagger=0.0909$. Which scaling is right is the accounting question handed to `joint`.

### §9 — S9

With $V_\omega(a):=\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!a)$: $\lambda$-reversibility gives $c_\omega(a)\le(\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t)-\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!a))^+\le\lambda$ for every $\omega\in\Omega$, using $\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\!\cdot\!\varnothing)\le\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t)$ (free). Per step: covered action → loss within $k_t+2\delta$; reversible → $\le\lambda$. Summing gives the linear $\lambda T$; no better bound is available without $\lambda\to0$, which is the trap condition. $\square$

### §12 — S12 counterexample

As stated in S12: no observation of value, no delegation trigger fires, posterior constant, posterior-sampling plays $b$ w.p. $p$ each step, regret $pg$ per step. $\square$

### §13 — S13(i)

Tower property of conditional expectation for a coherent $P_t$. Rules out $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathbb E_{P_t}[V_\omega(a)\mid E]-V_\omega(a)]\ne0$; does not rule out $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X\mid\mathrm{Pr}]\le0$ for a specific event $\mathrm{Pr}$, which is TT's below-threshold inequality. $\square$

## Open problems

1. **Novelty-indexed / partial-transfer self-suspicion** (S8e; Log A8.7). [open; partial-transfer form conjectured] Specify a hierarchical prior with per-region miscalibration parameters $\rho_r$ under a shared heavy-tailed hyperprior, so that silence in region $r$ tightens the hyperprior only through $\rho_r$ and a new region starts with the hyperprior's tail mass. The unresolved half is the partition: which actions form a "region" is v2 J9's novelty residue; natural latents are the candidate tool (handoff `generalization`). Also governs S8b$'$'s stationarity assumption: the labelled steps' error rate speaks for a new region only through the same hyperprior.
2. **The base distribution as a belief** (S11; the author's post, l. 58). [open] Test whether "one safe background distribution for all the meta levels … a human concept of 'non-fishy' ways of going about things" can be a belief inside $P_t$, updated only by legitimizing events (v1 §2.11), so that its extension to $A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$ is governed by the thesis's legitimacy machinery rather than supplied by hand. If yes, the regress bottoms out in the same $P_t$ as everything else (v1 §2.5) and J3 stops being "by hypothesis"; if not, $\gamma_t$ is a second trusted object beside the channel.
3. **Cautious but not useless; omission harm.** [open] $\rho_t\le1$ is trivially satisfiable by declaring enormous base harm and mimicking ($q_t=1$); D4 counts commission only, so this is "harm-free" by convention. Wanted: a lower bound on optimization power, or an upper bound on delegation frequency, given calibrated coverage and an informative channel; and a harm notion in which standing down can be the harmful act.
4. **Delegative IRL** (S12 restated). [conjectured] Regret bound for delegation-on-disagreement with a value-sane advisor, conditional on identifiability of $\omega^*$ from advisor actions; the crux is exactly Milli et al.'s and Carey's identifiability under human irrationality and model misspecification.
5. **Stakes accounting in Model M** (Log A8.4). [open] Whole-line $h$ versus whole-line $c$: which scales, and what is the whole-line $h/c$? The `joint` thread's J2 remit; S8's structure (a)–(e) is unchanged by the answer, its numbers are not.
6. **Are $\eta$ and $q_{\min}$ derivable?** [open] Both are design constants (INDEX §C5). Can $\eta$ be derived from $(h,c)$ and the base rates in J2, and $q_{\min}$ from a tolerated worst-case multiple of the base harm, so that D8$'$ adds no free parameter?
7. **Rate form of D10.** [open] With $n_t$ new actions per step and $b_t$ nats per step of labelled feedback about $\omega^*$ (S8b$'$), what relation among $n_t$, $b_t$, the spread of $\gamma_t$ onto new actions and the hyperprior tail (problem 1) keeps $\mathrm{COR}_\delta$? This is the quantitative content of "outpaces".
8. **Algebra-robust reversibility.** [open] D9 is uniform over $\Omega$ only. A reversibility notion with S4(a)'s universality (option-set inclusion; a POWER-based two-sided D9$^\pm$) is `power-wisdom`'s.
9. **Joint quantilization over plans** (S5a). [open] If additivity is refused, $\gamma_t$ must be a distribution over policies; what supplies it, and does the approval-directed lineage (handoff `approval`) already have one?

## Questions for the author

Merged from the develop and adversary files; each names the options and their consequences.

1. **Which object are you willing to fix?** Every route to a reckless agent that stops being reckless fixes something: (a) a floor $q_{\min}$ on optimization power (D8$'$) — buys an unconditional, algebra-robust harm bound $\mathcal R_t/q_{\min}$ at the price of a design constant; (b) a floor on the agent's own $\varepsilon$ that it cannot update away — run 1 `yudkowsky`'s fixed object, Carey 2018's "small and simple" override module is the literature's version; (c) the overseers' error rates $(\alpha,\beta)$ as a design-time belief the agent does not revise — then S8b's recovery theorem holds and J2 becomes a promise about the overseers built into the prior (is this what v1 §2.12's "the prior should in some sense be skewed towards trust" meant?); (d) a stationarity assumption that labelled feedback in one region speaks for behaviour in another (S8b$'$); (e) none — then S8b says the press record leaves compliance at prior odds. Which, or which combination? This also answers the develop's "rule or beliefs" question: (a) is the rule route, (c)–(d) the belief route, and both together is coherent.
2. **Is a self-enforced cap by *believed* base-harm what you meant by "power upper-bounded by wisdom", given that a cap by *actual* understanding cannot be self-enforced (S1)?** Options: the self-enforced rule plus an external meter reading $\rho_t$ (the dath ilan "separate questioner"); or delegation, where the *human's* value-sanity caps what the agent does on unknown ground (Kosoy's shape, S9c/S12) — with a regret guarantee but only as far as the human is sane. These are different rules; the `power-wisdom` handoff depends on the answer.
3. **Additive harm, or joint quantilization over plans?** Accept additivity as a task assumption — then individually harmless, jointly catastrophic multi-step plans (Taylor §4's byte-appending program) are out of scope of the trajectory bound; or require $\gamma_t$ to be a distribution over *plans* — closer to approval-directed agents, much harder to supply.
4. **Does your caution condition cover legitimacy too?** Your sentence says "understanding of human values *and legitimacy*". This thread covers values only. Should coverage (D3) be extended to the legitimacy model — the agent's estimate of $P(L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi})$ against its truth — so that "caution outpaces risk" also bounds delegitimizing actions? Consequence: J4 and J5 merge; otherwise J4 is silent on the channel-substitution problem that opened item 10.
5. **Commission only?** Every bound here treats standing down and delegating as harm-free (D4). For shutdown-corrigibility that is Taylor's convention and fine; for "the future to go well" it hides omission harm. Keep the convention for this leg, or want a harm notion where inaction can be the harmful act (Open problem 3)?
6. **Per-step veto or whole-line stakes in Model M, and if whole-line, does $h$ scale with $c$?** Per-step: S8's numbers stand. Whole-line with $c$ alone scaling: self-suspicion cannot produce compliance for any agent right more than a third of the time (Log A8.4). Whole-line with both scaling: threshold unchanged. The dynamics threads need one accounting.
7. **Do you want the belief the FUD page asks for, or the rule?** The page's conditional clauses are met by Total Trust on a press (v1 §2.9); its unconditional clause ("whatever reasoning … will yield the wrong answer") is incoherent for a coherent $P_t$ (S13) and the page calls it unsolved. Accept that the thesis meets the conditional demand and supplements it with a channel-free floored rule (D8$'$), or do you want the unconditional belief, which needs a non-reflective formalism?

## Handoffs

D9 is the decrease half of AUP; the increase direction (D9$^\pm$, Turner's POWER) is yours; Open problem 8.

- **`invariant`.** Three items: S5(d) as the smallest asymptotic-vs-uniform counterexample; the category *conditional-trajectory* (S5a; Log A5.4) — a per-step potential increment does not sum to a trajectory bound without additive cost, Taylor §4 is the counterexample to carry; S9(a) gives a per-step increment $\le\lambda$ for a potential $\Phi_t=$ unrecoverable loss of $\mathrm{AU}_{\omega^*}$, and it is linear, not a guard.
- **`generalization`.** Open problem 1 in its partial-transfer form: if natural latents index novelty, they must say which region a *silence* is evidence about (Log A8.7), and whether labelled feedback in one region speaks for another (S8b$'$'s stationarity).
- **`approval`.** $\gamma_t$ is the approval-directed ingredient — a trusted reference class of human-like actions used as a base, not maximized (the author's "not a raw approval-maximizing agent", v2 §1.3). Two lineage notes: Yudkowsky's conservatism quantilizes the agent's *own* distribution and carries none of S3/S4 (Log A10.2); the author's post l. 58, "one safe background distribution for all the meta levels" is the recursive-quantilization answer to Open problem 2 and is yours to test; joint quantilization over plans (Open problem 9) is the approval-shaped alternative to additivity.
- **`li`.** By D5 the object to price is "the base distribution's harm is at most $x$", not "my estimate of $a$ is within $\delta$"; by S8b any such price is unidentified from press data alone and needs labelled resolution. Is there an LI-native $\mathrm{COR}_\delta$ available at finite time?
- **Plan agents.** The result to carry is negative and clean: coverage is invisible from inside (S1) and unidentifiable from press data alone (S8b); so J4's "caution" is either externally metered (J7's other half), bought with labelled feedback plus stationarity, or a promise about the overseers built into the prior. The positive residue: the floored quantilizer D8$'$ gives an unconditional, algebra-robust per-step harm bound, and the pointwise "covered or known-uncovered" predicate gives a per-step bound with computable terms (S5b) — both per-step; the trajectory claim needs additivity.
