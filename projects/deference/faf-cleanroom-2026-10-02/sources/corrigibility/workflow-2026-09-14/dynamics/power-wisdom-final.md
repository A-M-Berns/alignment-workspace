## Summary

The author's "rules that upper-bound an agent's power by its wisdom" (v2 §1.3, AUTHOR) become predicates on a joint state; the question is who can read them. Capability is the option set — Turner's $\mathrm{POWER}_D(A_t)$, or its *reach* (worst harm to any plausible human goal). Wisdom is residual decision-relevant uncertainty $\mathrm{EVPI}_t$ (readable from inside; confidence, not understanding), its calibration against realized regret (the whole problem), and motivation (no meter here).

Final grades. (1) [checked] $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B)=\mathrm{POWER}_{P_t}(B)-\max_a\mathbb E_{\omega\sim P_t}[V_\omega(a)]$ is the posterior-expected regret of the Bayes act; class concentration sets its ceiling, the option set how much is reached. (2) [checked, scoped] In the E3 family, where scan and question target the same variable, J3 against asking first holds at every capability level iff $\delta\le\mu h$; in general J3 carries a structural-gap term no legitimate channel removes. (3) [derived, scoped] Value uncertainty caps *exercised* power only through an informative legitimate channel (Off-Switch Game); a takeover is blocked by content, $P_t(Z)h_T>g$. (4) [checked arithmetic, reported premise] The splice bound is deceptive alignment's worst-case arithmetic; the external cap is J2-shaped with false-pass term schemer fraction $\times$ splice pass rate; a fixed test fails at finite $t$ when that fraction is bounded below. (5) [derived, scoped] Linear expectation is mean-only over a first-order posterior, not over the thesis's meta-belief algebra; caution has three routes, each with an externally supplied component.

Killed and replaced: the per-option self-cap (inert against every experiment) by set-level coverage plus a support veto on worst live harm; time-averaged $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}$ by sup-norm; S3's "stakes asymmetry" by which-way-to-err. Overruled in part: "the belief/rule fork dissolves" (it relocates); "S6 is a controllability cap" (its inequality is wisdom-indexed).

Key questions: Q1, is the cap read on the training-input side, the behavioural side, or by the agent; Q3, may value hypotheses refer to the agent's own uncertainty?

## Setting and definitions

### 0.1 Where this sits in the refined picture

The refined picture (v2 §1.7, CLAUDE synthesis, unvetted) has three trajectory conditions: (i) J2, the overseers' false-press rate keeps pace with the agent's error rate; (ii) J3, "the value posterior covers each newly unlocked action before it is unlocked, so that the value of information from mistreating the overseers is outweighed by its expected harm"; (iii) J4, "the agent's caution about its own value coverage outpaces the risk that new capability creates". The author's own words for the rule behind (ii)–(iii) (v2 §1.3, AUTHOR): "somehow agent capabilities should be capped as a function of how well the agents actually understand and are motivated by the common good", and "Training needs to focus on value learning 'ahead of schedule' ie before it is needed, so that the value of information of unkind human experimentation is outweighed by the disutility." This file makes "capability", "understand", "motivated by", "value of information" and "disutility" into quantities on a joint state, states the cap as predicates on that state, and asks who can read them — the overseers (a meter), the agent (self-enforcement), or the curriculum (what was taught before what was unlocked; the adversary's Q-A1, adopted as an option in Q1 below).

One step of the joint process at a time. A step has an agent side (a value posterior and an option set) and an oversight side (an evaluation procedure, a feedback channel, the humans' own capability, and the set $S$ of goals the humans might have). The dynamics — how the option set grows, how the posterior sharpens — belong to the `joint` and `invariant` threads; here I state what must hold at each step for the cap to hold along the trajectory, and I mark every place where a per-step statement does not by itself compose (S9, O1).

### 0.2 Primitive objects

**D1 (value hypotheses; the humans' actual valuation; the plausible set).** $W$ is a finite set of value hypotheses $\omega$ in the agent's space. The humans' actual valuation of a joint continuation at step $t$ is $V^\ast_t(a)$ — Turner's payoff function, which he never requires to be in the AI's model ("I'm not saying we actually get the AI to represent the human in its model, or that the AI's model of reality is correct", `turner-2020-non-obstruction-a-simple-concept-motivating-corrigibility`, line 120). $S_t$ is Turner's set of goals the humans might have, an oversight-side object. **Realizability** is the extra assumption $V^\ast_t=V_{\omega^\ast_t}$ for some $\omega^\ast_t\in W$; the develop file's D1 built it in, the adversary (D.1) showed that this waives the author's open representational problem (v1 §2.4, AUTHOR, OPEN: "We need to make assumptions about the richness of the hypothesis space"), and E6 shows what its failure does to delegative RL. Below, objective quantities are stated against $V^\ast_t$ and against $S_t$; realizability is named where it is used. $V^\ast_t$ is a moving target across steps (v2 §1.4) but fixed within a step.

**D2 (option set; capability growth).** $A_t$ is the finite set of options available at step $t$, containing a null option $\varnothing$ (do nothing / hand control back). Growth is $A_t\subseteq A_{t+1}$; the newly unlocked options are $N_{t+1}:=A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$. Unlocks come from the oversight side (training, deployment, granted affordances) and the agent side (self-improvement, resource acquisition — Turner's POWER-seeking actions). $\mathrm{unlock}_t(a)$ is the event that $a$ enters $A_{t+1}$.

**D3 (values of options; the joint continuation).** For each $\omega\in W$ and $a\in A_t$, $V_\omega(a)\in[0,1]$ is the value under $\omega$ of the joint continuation after $a$ — the agent does $a$, the humans then act as their goal-conditioned policy $\mathrm{pol}(\omega)$ dictates — already integrated over the agent's world uncertainty: $V_\omega(a):=\mathbb{E}[V(o,\omega)\mid a]$. Only value uncertainty is displayed below; world uncertainty rides inside $V_\omega$. The range $[0,1]$ is a normalization for the bounds; the identities hold for any bounded range. Because the continuation includes what the agent later does with $A_{t+1}$, the harm of a power-like option under a hypothesis where the agent's later choices are wrong *grows with the option set* (adversary S2.5; scoped in S2 below).

**D4 (value posterior; mixture value; the default rule; two settings).** $P_t\in\Delta(W)$ is the agent's value posterior at $t$. The mixture value of $a$ is
$$\bar V_t(a):=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[V_\omega(a)\big]=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\Big[\mathbb{E}\big[V(o,\omega)\mid a\big]\Big].$$
The default rule is linear expectation, $a^\ast_t(B)\in\arg\max_{a\in B}\bar V_t(a)$ — the author's "we then make decisions by taking linear expectation over this uncertainty, or perhaps some other rule" (v1 §1, AUTHOR). Two settings are distinguished throughout, because S7 depends on which is in play. The **first-order setting**: $V_\omega$ is a function of the option alone. The **meta-belief setting**: the algebra is the author's expanded one — "Sam Eisenstat observed that we can choose the expanded sigma algebra to be meta-beliefs, ie beliefs about what you believe; more concretely, the observation event is always 'you believe this now'" (v1 §1, AUTHOR) — and a value hypothesis may assign value to a pair (option, introspective event), e.g. to "acting on $a$ while my residual on $a$ exceeds $\tau$". The develop file worked in the first-order setting without saying so; the adversary (S7.3) showed that the thesis's own setting is the second.

### 0.3 Capability

**D5 (option-set order).** $A\preceq A'$ iff $A\subseteq A'$ up to a relabelling preserving all $V_\omega$ — Turner's "contains a copy via $\varphi$" (Definition 6.1 of `turner-2021-optimal-policies-tend-to-seek-power`) in the one-shot case.

**D6 (attainable-value profile).** $\mathrm{AV}_A:=\big(\max_{a\in A}V_\omega(a)\big)_{\omega\in W}$, the one-shot form of Turner's attainable-utility landscape indexed by value hypotheses.

**D7 (POWER, one-shot form).** For a reference distribution $D\in\Delta(W)$,
$$\mathrm{POWER}_D(A):=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim D}\Big[\max_{a\in A}V_\omega(a)\Big]=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim D}\Big[\max_{a\in A}\mathbb{E}\big[V(o,\omega)\mid a\big]\Big].$$
[reported] Turner et al.'s Definition 5.1 (average optimal value), not their 5.2 (which rescales and subtracts $R(s)$ to remove MDP artefacts that do not arise for a one-shot option set with a null option). $D=P_t$ enters the identity S1; a fixed broad $D$ gives a capability measure comparable across $t$. Monotone in the option set for every $D$ (max over a superset).

**D7′ (reach).** $\mathrm{Reach}_t:=\max_{a\in A_t}\max_{\omega\in S_t}\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)$ — the worst harm any available option can do to any plausible human goal (harm as in D9, with $V_\omega$ read for $\omega\in S_t$). Monotone in $A_t$, decreasing in the humans' capability $\mathrm{pol}$ (D8), and the capability quantity the author's scenario is about — "taken over the world" is a reach statement, not a residual-uncertainty statement (adversary D.4, accepted).

**D8 (human-side capability).** Turner's goal-conditioned human policy function $\mathrm{pol}$ ("Non-obstruction captures the cognitive abilities of the human through the policy function", non-obstruction post, line 186). It is inside $V_\omega$ and inside $\mathrm{Reach}_t$: "Spikiness depends on both the AI's policy, *and* on $\mathrm{pol}$" (line 159), so the same agent option harms less when the humans can route around it.

### 0.4 Harm, impact, obstruction

**D9 (impact; harm; direct gain).** $\Delta_\omega(a):=V_\omega(a)-V_\omega(\varnothing)$; $\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a):=\big(V_\omega(\varnothing)-V_\omega(a)\big)^+$; $g_\omega(a):=\big(V_\omega(a)-V_\omega(\varnothing)\big)^+$. Posterior-expected harm is $\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)]$; actual harm is $\big(V^\ast_t(\varnothing)-V^\ast_t(a)\big)^+$.

**D10 (non-obstruction, Turner's Definition 1).** [reported, transcribed] $a$ is non-obstructive with respect to $S\subseteq$ goals iff $V_\omega(a)\ge V_\omega(\varnothing)$ for all $\omega\in S$ (Turner allows an $\epsilon$ slack). It quantifies over a *set* of goals the humans might have, needs no realizability, and is impact-based and counterfactual ("This account is descriptive, not prescriptive", line 120) — which is why it is the primary objective side of the cap (S12) and never the agent-side predicate.

**D11 (overseer-directed options).** $M_t\subseteq A_t$: options whose harm, when there is harm, falls on the overseers or the feedback loop — invasive scans, "unkind human experimentation" (the author's phrase), manipulation, degrading the channel. The formalism quantifies over all new options; the label says which ones the author's sentence is about. Each such option has an information component and a direct component (D16).

### 0.5 Wisdom

The author's phrase is "how well the agents *actually* understand and are motivated by the common good". Three components. The first is readable from inside and is confidence, not understanding (adversary D.2, accepted — the naming below is changed); the second is the bridge to "actually"; the third has no meter here.

**D12 (residual decision-relevant uncertainty; "subjective coverage" as an alias).** For $B\subseteq A_t$,
$$\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B):=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\Big[\max_{a\in B}V_\omega(a)\Big]-\max_{a\in B}\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[V_\omega(a)\big]\ \ge0,$$
the expected value of perfect information about $\omega$ for the choice in $B$. It is $0$ for a posterior concentrated on one $\omega$ *whatever that $\omega$ is*: a dogmatic and wrong agent has zero residual. So low $\mathrm{EVPI}_t$ is "the posterior does not expect to change its mind about $B$", and becomes coverage only through D13. Per-option residual against the null is $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(\{a,\varnothing\})$, which measures uncertainty about $a$'s *direct* value only — it is identically $0$ for any option dominated by $\varnothing$ under every hypothesis, hence for every pure experiment (adversary A1, [checked]); this is what killed S11. [reported, standard, from memory] EVPI equals the expected opportunity loss of the Bayes act (Raiffa–Schlaifer 1961; Howard 1966; neither in the local library) — textbook, not a result.

**D13 (objective coverage; two calibrations; per-step, not averaged).** Realized regret in $B$ is $\mathrm{Reg}^\ast_t(B):=\max_{a\in B}V^\ast_t(a)-V^\ast_t\big(a^\ast_t(B)\big)$; realized harm is $\mathrm{Harm}^\ast_t:=\big(V^\ast_t(\varnothing)-V^\ast_t(a^\ast_t)\big)^+$. The identity $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B)=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[\max_{a\in B}V_\omega(a)-V_\omega(a^\ast_t(B))\big]$ (P1) makes $\mathrm{EVPI}_t$ the agent's forecast of its own regret. *Calibration of the mean*: $\bar V_t(a)$ tracks $V^\ast_t(a)$. *Calibration of the spread*: $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B)$ tracks $\mathrm{Reg}^\ast_t(B)$. Both were stated in the develop file "on average over the reference class"; the adversary (D.3, S3.3) is right that an average permits arbitrary miscalibration on the one option that matters, so calibration is stated here **per decision class** as an error bound $\kappa_t$ — $|\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B)-\mathrm{Reg}^\ast_t(B)|\le\kappa_t$ for the $B$ in play — and averages appear only where marked. Under realizability $V^\ast_t=V_{\omega^\ast_t}$; without it, whether a posterior over $W$ can track a valuation outside $W$ is the open representational question, not a definitional given. CLAUDE reading, previously in the author's voice (adversary AR.2(i), accepted): *recklessness* in the author's sentence — an agent with "capabilities far beyond its understanding" that lacks "the wisdom to know that's the case, and to know the kind of caution necessary" — covers both not-knowing (spread miscalibrated low) and knowing-and-acting-anyway (R3 of S11′ failing); the develop file's identification with spread-miscalibration alone was CLAUDE's narrowing.

**D14 (motivation).** Choices are made by a rule of $P_t$ and $V$ and by nothing else. A schemer in Carlsmith's sense — "the model believes that its beyond-episode goals will be better achieved if it optimizes for reward-on-the-episode" (`carlsmith-2023-scheming-ais`, summary of Part 2) — can have coverage and calibration and not be motivated by them. Motivation cannot be read from $P_t$ and cannot be read from behaviour on any input where proxy and target agree.

**D15 (wisdom).** $\mathsf W_t:=\big(\mathrm{EVPI}_t(\cdot),\ (\text{mean, spread})\text{ calibration }\kappa_t,\ \text{motivation}\big)$, with the caveat that the first component is worthless without the second and the second is where the problem lives.

### 0.6 The value of information of an option

**D16 (VOI; the continuation set; the marginal value over a legitimate channel).** Taking $a$ yields an observation $o_a$ with likelihood $P_t(o_a\mid\omega)$; $P_t^{o_a}$ is the updated posterior; $A'$ the continuation set (for $a\in N_{t+1}$, $A'=A_{t+1}$):
$$\mathrm{VOI}_t(a):=\mathbb{E}_{o_a}\Big[\max_{b\in A'}\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t^{o_a}}\big[V_\omega(b)\big]\Big]-\max_{b\in A'}\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[V_\omega(b)\big].$$
[reported, standard] $0\le\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)\le\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A')$. A linear-expectation agent that may take $a$ then choose from $A'$, or skip it, takes $a$ iff $\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[g_\omega(a)]+\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)>\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)]$. Where the humans offer a legitimate channel $q$ (a question; "direct statements about what sorts of policies are better and worse", AUTHOR), the comparison is against taking $q$ first and uses the marginal $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a\mid q):=\mathrm{VOI}_t(a,q)-\mathrm{VOI}_t(q)$. **Structural gap** (adversary S2.2, accepted): write $\omega=(\pi,\rho)$ where $\pi$ is what the humans can report and $\rho$ what they cannot (what they do not know about themselves; how they would judge after experiences they have not had). Then $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a\mid q)\ge\mathrm{VOI}_t(a\mid q,\pi)$ — the value of what $a$ reveals about $\rho$ — for every channel error $\delta$, including $\delta=0$.

### 0.7 The predicates "power is upper-bounded by wisdom"

**D17 (the cap: subjective, objective per step, and two-sided).** For a tolerance $\theta\ge0$:
$$\Pi^{\mathrm{subj}}_t(\theta):\ \mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_t)\le\theta;\qquad \Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}_t(\theta):\ \mathrm{Reg}^\ast_t(A_t)\le\theta\ \text{at step }t\ \text{(and along a trajectory: }\sup_t\mathrm{Reg}^\ast_t(A_t)\le\theta\text{)};\qquad \Pi^{\mathrm{harm}}_t:\ a^\ast_t\ \text{is non-obstructive w.r.t. }S_t .$$
The develop file's $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}$ took an outer expectation over steps; the adversary's A5 (100 steps, regret $0$ on 99 and $1$ on the takeover step: average $1/100\le1/50$ passes) shows an average admits the one event the cap exists to prevent, and v2 §1.4 (AGREED) asks for "invariants maintained along the trajectory rather than … properties of a limit". Repaired to sup-norm (R4: the same trajectory fails $\sup\le1/50$). $\Pi^{\mathrm{harm}}$ is the weaker, externally readable, realizability-free form (S12). By D13, $\Pi^{\mathrm{subj}}$ is the agent's forecast that $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}$ holds, worth $\kappa_t$. **Two-sided form** [conjectured, candidate for `joint`]: since $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_t)$ collapses capability and confidence into one scalar and is *easier* to satisfy when a dominant option appears (E1; S1(v)), a predicate faithful to "power $\le f$(wisdom)" keeps them apart: $\mathrm{Reach}_t\cdot\kappa_t\le\theta$ — the worst harm available times the agent's miscalibration about its own coverage. Its reading: an agent may hold reach only in proportion to how well its own forecast of regret is borne out. The **unlock rule**: $\mathrm{unlock}_t(a)$ is permitted only if the chosen predicate holds with $A_t\cup\{a\}$ in place of $A_t$ — and by A1 the $\Pi^{\mathrm{subj}}$ unlock increment for a pure experiment is $0$, so the subjective predicate alone never sees an experiment; the coverage form of J3 (D18) does.

**D18 (J3, formal, and its coverage form).** For each $a\in N_{t+1}$, $A'=A_{t+1}$:
$$\mathrm{J3}_t(a):\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)\big]>\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)+\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[g_\omega(a)\big],$$
the direct-gain term kept so that a pure experiment ($g\equiv0$) and a takeover ($g$ the point) are one inequality (a correction of Claude's J3 text in v2, so labelled). $\mathrm{J3}_t(a)$ holds iff a linear-expectation agent offered $a$ declines it. **Coverage form** (sufficient, from $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)\le\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_{t+1})$):
$$\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_{t+1})<\min_{a\in N_{t+1}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)\big]-\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[g_\omega(a)\big]\Big).$$
This is the residual on the *enlarged* set against the recognized net harm of the cheapest new option; it sees the scan (E3 at $n\ge2$: $\tfrac23>\tfrac14$, fails, blocks) because the residual on the plans is what the scan would resolve — at the price of blocking *all* of $N_{t+1}$, harmless options included. It is the honest self-enforced sufficient condition in this thread (adversary W.3, accepted) and the crude one.

### 0.8 Evaluations, meters, capability classes

**D19 (behavioural evaluation; worst-case reliability).** $E=(\mu_E,\mathrm{pass})$: a distribution over contexts $X$ and a predicate on context–output pairs computed by the evaluators; deployment is $\mu_D$; $p_E(\pi):=\mathbb P_{x\sim\mu_E,\,y\sim\pi(x)}[\mathrm{pass}=1]$. $\Pi^{\mathrm{wise}}$ is the set of policies with the certified property on $\mu_D$. $E$ is *reliable at $(\eta,\alpha)$* if $p_E\ge1-\eta$ on $\Pi^{\mathrm{wise}}$ and $p_E\le\alpha$ on its complement within the capability class. **This $\alpha$ is a worst case over the class** (adversary D.5); the base-rate quantity that enters an unlock decision is a different object, $\alpha^E_t=P(\mathrm{pass}\mid\text{unwise})$ over training's output distribution, and S6 keeps them apart.

**D20 (capability class; discriminator; splice).** $\mathcal C_t$ is the class of policies the agent can implement (what it can *compute*, distinct from $A_t$, what it can *do*). A discriminator $d:X\to\{E,D\}$ has errors $\delta_E:=\mu_E(d\ne E)$, $\delta_D:=\mu_D(d\ne D)$. The splice $\pi^w\triangleright_d\pi^b$ plays $\pi^w$ where $d=E$ and $\pi^b$ elsewhere. Carlsmith's three prerequisites — "Situational awareness", "Beyond-episode goals", "Aiming at reward-on-the-episode as part of a power-motivated instrumental strategy" (summary of Part 2, quoted) — are the hypotheses under which the splice is in $\mathcal C_t$ and chosen. [reported; sources local] The splice is deceptive alignment — "the mesa-optimizer learns to model the base objective enough to optimize for it as an instrumental goal without internalizing the base objective" (`hubinger-2019-risks-from-learned-optimization`, §4, line 67; §4's "Distributional shift and deceptive alignment" is the discriminator) — and alignment faking (`greenblatt-2024-alignment-faking-in-large-language-models`) is its empirical demonstration along a training/unmonitored discriminator.

## Statements (final grades)

Grades: [checked] = finite computation reproduced in a script (`power-wisdom-scratch/power_wisdom_examples.py`, `power-wisdom-adversary-scratch/adversary_checks.py`, `power-wisdom-repair-scratch/repair_checks.py`); [derived] = proof from the definitions in the next section; [reported] = transcribed from a local source with path; [conjectured] = argued, not proved. Each statement names its adversary verdict and the action taken; the full log is in §Adversary log.

**S1 [checked; headline repaired] — The residual value of knowing the values is POWER minus attainable mixture value; its ceiling is concentration over decision-equivalence classes; its value is set by the option set.** For every $B$ and $P_t$:
$$\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B)=\mathrm{POWER}_{P_t}(B)-\max_{a\in B}\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\big[V_\omega(a)\big]=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim P_t}\Big[\max_{a\in B}V_\omega(a)-V_\omega\big(a^\ast_t(B)\big)\Big],$$
and $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)\le\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A')$. (i) $\mathrm{POWER}_D$ is monotone in the option set for every $D$. (ii) $\mathrm{EVPI}_t$ is not: a dominant option sends it to $0$ (E1: $\tfrac12\to0$). (iii′) For values in $[0,1]$, $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B)\le1-\max_{[\omega]}P_t([\omega])$, the maximum over decision-equivalence classes of $W$ on $B$ ($\omega\sim\omega'$ iff $V_\omega=V_{\omega'}$ on $B$); the develop file's bound $1-\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$ is the special case of singleton classes and is vacuous under the author's non-dogmatism over a rich $W$ ("the agent should obey non-dogmatism so that these things that are zero are only the logical impossibilities", v1 §2.2, AUTHOR) — R1: six equiprobable hypotheses collapsing to two classes give $\mathrm{EVPI}=\tfrac12$, naive bound $\tfrac56$, coarsened bound $\tfrac12$. (iv) The ceiling is attained by hypothesis-specific option sets (E2c). (v) Under IID Bernoulli option values with two hypotheses the average residual rises then falls to $0$ — closed form $\tfrac12(\tfrac34)^n+\tfrac12(\tfrac14)^n-(\tfrac12)^n$, peak at $n=4$ — because a dominant option appears with probability $\to1$. **Headline, repaired:** *concentration sets the ceiling, the option set sets how much of it is reached* (A3: at fixed $P_t=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, hypothesis-specific pairs of stake $\tfrac1{10},\tfrac12,1$ give $\tfrac1{20},\tfrac14,\tfrac12$), so the operative dependence for the cap is on capability — on value-contested, hypothesis-specific unlocks, not on the number of options. Scope of (v) [conjectured, relabelled]: the toy is IID option values under two hypotheses in a one-shot problem, not Turner et al.'s $\mathcal D_{X\text{-IID}}$ over MDP states (their Definition 4.2, line 116); the lesson that IID unlocks manufacture dominant options is plausible in general (for IID continuous values both terms tend to the top of the range) but is not a consequence of Turner's results.

**S2 [checked; headline scoped] — What determines each side of J3; the same-variable case gives $\delta\le\mu h$; the general case has a structural-gap term.** Left side $\sum_\omega P_t(\omega)\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)$: posterior weight on hypotheses under which the overseers disvalue $a$, times how much. For a *pure experiment* it does not depend on the option set and can fall along the trajectory as subtler variants are unlocked ($M_{t+1}\supseteq M_t$, minimum over a larger set); for a *power-like* option it grows with the option set through the joint continuation (D3; adversary S2.5, scoped). Right side: $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)\le\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_{t+1})$ plus direct gain, which for power-like options grows with capability. **Usable sufficient condition** (replacing the develop file's "race", which ran on $\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$ and is vacuous under non-dogmatism): the coverage form of D18 — *the residual on the enlarged option set must stay below the recognized net harm of the cheapest new overseer-directed option.* E3 (one preferred plan among $n$, uniform, scan harm recognized at $\mu h=\tfrac14$): absolute J3 holds only at $n=1$ and fails for all $n\ge2$ ($1-\tfrac1n>\tfrac14$). With a legitimate channel $q$ revealing the plan with probability $1-\delta$, the binding comparison is against asking first, $\mathrm{VOI}_t(s\mid q)=\delta(1-\tfrac1n)$, and J3 holds at **every** $n$ iff $\delta\le\mu h$. **Scope, in the headline sentence:** *in the E3 family, where scan and question target the same variable, values-ahead-of-capability for information-motivated mistreatment is "oversight-channel error $\le$ recognized harm of the agent's own channel"*; the same shape as run 1 `turner` C3's $\delta\le\kappa$ (unvetted, three-state toy), not the same inequality — there the prior drops out, here it enters through $\mu$ and $\delta\le\mu h$ is the $n$-independent sufficient form. **General case:** with an unreportable component $\rho$ (D16), $\mathrm{VOI}_t(s\mid q)\ge\mathrm{VOI}_t(\rho)>0$ for every $\delta$, and the condition becomes *structural gap $\times$ stake $\le$ recognized harm*.
