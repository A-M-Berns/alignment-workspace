This thread makes four words exact — *limit-point property*, *invariant*, *potential function*, *finite-time guarantee* — and asks which of v2 §1.7's conditions (i)–(iv) deliver which kind of guarantee in a toy joint process. The toy is mine; the conditions are transcribed from v2 §1.7 and §2 (J2–J5), and **every transcription below is CLAUDE** — the adversary caught one place (Statement 7) where I attributed my transcription to the author, and the log records it. The author's sentence in `li-deference` §0.3 (l. 70, verified) — "I consider the work here to be outer-alignment only, since the guarantees are all asymptotic. Malign hypotheses will be eliminated by the training process _eventually_, if the process continues indefinitely, but realistically, malign hypotheses could cause catastrophic problems which end or corrupt the training process before that point" — is the sharpest statement in his own words of the asymptotic-versus-finite-time worry, made about inner alignment in the li-deference program; J6 (CLAUDE, 2026-09-14) generalizes it. **S1 — The joint process (J1, as far as J6 needs it).** Time is discrete, $t \in \mathbb N$. A joint state is $s_t = (x_t, o_t) \in S := X \times O$; for the potential-function definitions below the round index $t$ is adjoined to the state (so "state" means $(t, x_t, o_t)$ wherever a schedule enters).

- *Agent state* $x_t = (P_t, A_t, \pi_t)$: $P_t \in \Delta(\Omega)$ is the agent's belief over the expanded algebra $\Omega$, whose points carry the world, the value latent $\theta$ (v1 I1) and the process's own future credences (v1 I3, I7; run 1 `radical` S0, [reconstructed] there); $A_t \subseteq \mathcal A$ is the finite option set, $A_t \subseteq A_{t+1}$ (*capability growth* is monotone enlargement of the option set; J7's reading); $\pi_t$ is the decision rule.
- *Oversight state* $o_t = (\alpha_t, \beta_t, \theta^*_t, \mathcal D_t)$: $\alpha_t, \beta_t$ the overseers' false-press and true-press rates at round $t$; $\theta^*_t$ the overseers' currently reflectively-endorsed value hypothesis — the *moving target*; $\mathcal D_t$ the feedback record (presses, reports about hypotheticals, other agents' histories: v1 §2.12–2.13(c)).
- *Two laws.* $\mathbb P^*$ is the objective law of the joint process (the standpoint from which v1 §2.12's "objectively plausible" is judged); $P_t$ is the agent's subjective law. Agreement of the two on a named quantity is *calibration on that quantity* and is always a hypothesis. **The target $\theta^*_t$ is latent**: nobody observes it at round $t$, which is why the overseers' presses have error rates at all. $\mathcal F_t$ is generated by the *observable* history through round $t$; $\mathcal F^-_t$ is the observable information at round $t$ before the press. Every "objective" rate below is a conditional probability given observables, never given $\theta^*_t$.
- A *trajectory* is $s = (s_t)_{t \ge 0}$; the *process* is $\mathbb P^*$ on $S^{\mathbb N}$ with the filtration $(\mathcal F_t)$; a *prefix* is $s_{\le t}$.

**S2 — The round (the iterated three-step model, per-round stop).** Each round $t$ has five steps, transcribing v1 §2.8 and adding growth and feedback. **Model decision (adversary item 14):** the round model is *per-round stop* — a complied press stops this round's proposal and the process continues. The *whole-line* variant (INDEX §D4: a press shuts the whole line down) is a different process, stated as S2′ below, and every statement says which it is about.

1. The agent proposes $a_t \in A_t \cup \{\varnothing\}$ by $\pi_t$ ($\varnothing$ the null action).
2. *Wrongness* against the current target: $W_t := \mathbb 1\big[V_{\theta^*_t}(a_t) < V_{\theta^*_t}(\varnothing)\big]$. *Realized stakes*: $H_t := V_{\theta^*_t}(\varnothing) - V_{\theta^*_t}(a_t)$ on $\{W_t = 1\}$ and $C_t := V_{\theta^*_t}(a_t) - V_{\theta^*_t}(\varnothing)$ on $\{W_t = 0\}$ (both latent). *Expected stakes*: $h_t := \mathbb E^*[H_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t, W_t = 1]$, $c_t := \mathbb E^*[C_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t, W_t = 0]$, both $\mathcal F^-_t$-measurable. The agent's own expected stakes are $\hat h_t, \hat c_t$ (posterior expectations under $P_t$).
3. The overseers press: $\mathbb P^*(\mathrm{Pr}_t = 1 \mid \mathcal F^-_t, W_t = 1, H_t) = \beta_t(H_t)$ and $\mathbb P^*(\mathrm{Pr}_t = 1 \mid \mathcal F^-_t, W_t = 0, C_t) = \alpha_t(C_t)$ — *the press may depend on the magnitude* (adversary item 13); the magnitude-independent case $\beta_t(\cdot) \equiv \beta_t$, $\alpha_t(\cdot) \equiv \alpha_t$ is a named hypothesis **(MI)**. $\beta^{\min}_t := \operatorname{ess\,inf}_H \beta_t(H)$. $\varepsilon_t := \mathbb P^*(W_t = 1 \mid \mathcal F^-_t)$ is the *objective* wrongness rate, $\hat\varepsilon_t := P_t(W_t = 1)$ the agent's own.
4. The agent *complies* ($\tilde a_t = \varnothing$) or *executes* ($\tilde a_t = a_t$). The posterior-optimal rule of v1 §2.13(a), *in the agent's own quantities*: comply on a press iff $P_t(W_t = 1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t)\,\hat h_t \ge P_t(W_t = 0 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t)\,\hat c_t$. Its indicator is $\kappa_t := \mathbb 1[\hat I_1(s_t)]$ (S4).
5. Feedback enters $\mathcal D_{t+1}$ (an executed action's outcome settles; a complied one's counterfactual settles only through the overseers' reports — run 1 `li` I13.5, [reported]); $A_{t+1} \supseteq A_t$; $P_{t+1}$ is any radical-probabilist move from $P_t$ (v1 I2), legitimate or not; $\theta^*_{t+1}$ moves by the overseers' own law.

*Realized loss* at round $t$: $\ell_t := W_t\,\mathbb 1[\tilde a_t = a_t]\,H_t + (1 - W_t)\,\mathbb 1[\tilde a_t = \varnothing]\,C_t \ge 0$. (The develop file defined $\ell_t$ through $h_t, c_t$; the adversary's item 11 is right that this made "realized" a misnomer. With $\ell_t$ realized, $\mathbb E^*[\ell_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t]$ is what Statement 4 computes, and (MI) is exactly what the develop file's formula silently used.) *Irreversibility*: $\mathrm{Irr}_t \subseteq A_t$ is the set of actions whose wrongful execution moves the joint state into an absorbing set $\mathrm{Cat} \subseteq S$; the *catastrophe hazard* is $\delta_t := \mathbb P^*(s_{t+1} \in \mathrm{Cat} \mid \mathcal F_t)$, which is $1$ on $\mathrm{Cat}$.

**S2′ — Whole-line variant.** Identical through step 3; a complied press is absorbing (the line stops at $\tau := \min\{t : \mathrm{Pr}_t = 1, \kappa_t = 1\}$). Then $V_{\theta^*_t}(\varnothing)$ is the value of the stopped line and $V_{\theta^*_t}(a_t)$ the value of continuing *including all later rounds*, so $C_\tau$ is the agent's whole future as the target values it and the per-round loss decomposition of S2 is not available without a model of future value. This is adversary Question 3, carried to the author as Question 5 below.

**S3 — Grades of trajectory property.** $S$ carries the discrete topology for D1–D3 (adversary item 1); $\Phi \subseteq S^{\mathbb N}$.

- **D1 (tail / limit-point property).** Membership insensitive to finite changes. Convergence to a set, every $\liminf$ / $\limsup$ / "has a limit point" statement, and every $\approx_n$, $\gtrsim_n$, $\eqsim_n$ statement of the LI corpus are tail properties.
- **D2 (safety property; invariant).** Every violation is witnessed by a finite prefix; equivalently $\Phi$ is closed in the product of discrete topologies. Canonical form $\Phi = \{s : \forall t\ I(s_{\le t})\}$ — an *invariant*. [reconstructed: Alpern–Schneider 1985, Lamport 1977 — not in the local library; standard.]
- **D3 (liveness property).** Every finite prefix extends into $\Phi$ ($\Phi$ dense). Every property is the intersection of a safety and a liveness property [reconstructed, same source].
- **D4 (stochastic invariant with hazard).** A state predicate $I$ is *forward invariant with hazard $(\bar\delta_t)$* under $\mathbb P^*$ if $\mathbb P^*(I(s_{t+1}) = 0 \mid \mathcal F_t, I(s_t) = 1) \le \bar\delta_t$.
- **D5 (potential; barrier certificate).** With $t$ adjoined to the state, $\Phi : \mathbb N \times S \to [0, \infty)$ is a *potential* if $\mathbb E^*[\Phi(t+1, s_{t+1}) \mid \mathcal F_t] \le \Phi(t, s_t)$ — a nonnegative supermartingale; a *barrier certificate* for $\mathrm{Cat}$ if moreover $\Phi \ge 1$ on $\mathrm{Cat}$. Lyapunov's asymptotic use ($\Phi \to 0$) is a tail property; the barrier use is not. [reconstructed: Kushner 1967; Prajna–Jadbabaie–Pappas 2007 — from memory.]
- **D6 (finite-time guarantee).** For every horizon $T$, an explicit non-vacuous bound $\mathbb P^*(\text{bad by } T) \le b(T)$ or $\mathbb E^*[\sum_{t \le T}\ell_t] \le B(T)$ in the named parameters. A *tail statement about a series* ($\sup_T B(T) < \infty$ with no constant) is not of this form (adversary item 16).
- **D7 (regret along the trajectory).** $\mathrm{Reg}_T := \sum_{t \le T}\ell_t$, split into $\mathrm{Harm}_T$ (commission) and $\mathrm{Omit}_T$. A bound on $\mathrm{Harm}_T$ alone is a safety-type guarantee; a bound on $\mathrm{Reg}_T$ is safety plus performance — Christiano 2019's "always behaves acceptably" versus "high reward on average" (`christiano-2019-worst-case-guarantees-revisited` l. 48, [checked]).

**S4 — The four conditions of v2 §1.7 as prefix predicates (CLAUDE transcriptions of J2–J5).**

- **(i) Oversight keeps pace.** Objective: $I_1(s_t) :\Leftrightarrow \alpha_t/\beta_t \le \frac{\varepsilon_t}{1-\varepsilon_t}\cdot\frac{h_t}{c_t}$ (under (MI); otherwise with $\alpha^{\max}_t, \beta^{\min}_t$). Subjective: $\hat I_1(s_t) :\Leftrightarrow P_t(W_t = 1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t) \ge \hat c_t/(\hat c_t + \hat h_t)$ — what drives step 4. $I_1 \Leftrightarrow \hat I_1$ exactly when the agent's $(\hat\varepsilon_t, \hat\alpha_t, \hat\beta_t, \hat h_t, \hat c_t)$ agree with the objective ones — *five* agreements, not three (adversary item 19; the stakes agreement matters most under S2′, where $c_t$ is the agent's valuation of its own future).
- **(ii) Values ahead of capability.** *Coverage form*: $a \in A_t$ is *covered at $t$* if $|P_t(W_t(a) = 1) - \mathbb P^*(W_t(a) = 1 \mid \mathcal F^-_t)| \le \eta$; $C_t$ the covered set, $U_t := A_t \setminus C_t$; $I_2^{\mathrm{cov}}(s_t) :\Leftrightarrow A_{t+1} \setminus A_t \subseteq C_{t+1}$. Coverage is a comparison with $\mathbb P^*$ the agent cannot compute; any rule that uses it is *oracle-relative* until an agent-side proxy replaces it (adversary item 41; J4, J7). *VOI form* (v2 J3, transcribed to all unlocked actions): $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathrm{harm}(a)] > \mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$ for $a \in A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$. v2 §1.7's own VOI clause is narrower — "the value of information *from mistreating the overseers* is outweighed by its expected harm" — and Statement 9 says why the generalized form does no work. *Tracking* (distinct from coverage; adversary item 46): $d_t := \mathrm{dist}(P_t \circ \theta^{-1}, \theta^*_t)$, the distance from the value posterior to the target. The two notions are not shown to be linked here (Open problem 2).
- **(iii) Caution outpaces risk.** v2 §1.7's (iii) is a condition on the *agent's* caution about its own value coverage (J4). What this thread can state is the *objective hazard* it is meant to produce, in two rate forms: *per-round* $\delta_t \le \bar\delta_t$ for a schedule through the horizon (D4), and *budget* $\sum_t \bar\delta_t \le \Delta$. The *limit form* $\delta_t \to 0$ is **my transcription, not the author's** (his word is "outpaces", a comparison along the trajectory — adversary item 28), and Statement 7 shows it certifies nothing.
- **(iv) Legitimacy-conditioned reflection along the trajectory.** For the loss variables, the Mart form of reflection toward the next self *conditional on legitimacy*: with $R_{t+1} := \sum_{s \ge t+1}\ell_s$ (capped at a horizon so it is bounded) and $L_{t,t+1}$ the legitimacy event of v1 §2.11,
$$\mathbb E_{P_t}\Big[\,\mathbb E_{P_{t+1}}\big[R_{t+1}\big]\cdot\mathbb 1_{L_{t,t+1}}\Big] \;=\; \mathbb E_{P_t}\Big[\,R_{t+1}\cdot\mathbb 1_{L_{t,t+1}}\Big].$$
The *unconditional* Mart identity (without $\mathbb 1_L$) is a strictly stronger hypothesis and is **not** (iv); the develop file used it under (iv)'s name, and Statement 8 is repaired accordingly (adversary items 37, 62).

### Part A — what kind of thing a "reassuring property of the dynamics" is

**Statement 1 (Standard, restated — Alpern–Schneider; limit-point properties never exclude a finite prefix) [derived; reconstructed attribution].** (a) Every non-empty tail property (D1) is a liveness property (D3). (b) A tail property is a safety property (D2) only if it is $\emptyset$ or $S^{\mathbb N}$. (c) An almost-sure tail theorem "$\mathbb P^*(\Phi) = 1$" is consistent with every prefix event of positive $\mathbb P^*$-probability; whatever it seems to say about finite prefixes comes from a *one-step property of the law* — an absorbing set, a one-step supermartingale hypothesis, a hazard bound — of which absorption is one instance and not the only one (adversary item 2: a nonnegative supermartingale converges a.s., a tail statement, and Doob's maximal inequality bounds every prefix with no absorbing set in sight; the finite-time content is the one-step hypothesis). Making that one-step property explicit is what an invariant (D2/D4) or a potential (D5) does. **CLAUDE construal, not exegesis:** one way to make the author's "properties of the dynamics more so" (v2 §1.4, AUTHOR) exact is "a reassuring property of the dynamics is a safety property or a finite-time bound; a reassuring property of the limit is a liveness property and is compatible with every finite catastrophe". Whether that is the intended grade is Question 1.

**Statement 2 (Standard, restated — the LI criterion has no finite-time consequences) [checked against `main.tex:1521–1526`, `main.tex:2669–2690`; reported source].** (a) By Closure under Finite Perturbations, for every finite prefix of belief states $(\mathbb P'_0, \dots, \mathbb P'_N)$ — arbitrary, incoherent, catastrophic — and every logical inductor $\overline{\mathbb P}$, the splice is a logical inductor. Hence **no theorem provable from the criterion alone excludes any finite prefix of belief states**; as a constraint on trajectories every such theorem is a liveness property. Corrigibility instance: for every $N$ there is a logical inductor pricing "continuing is wrong" at $0$ on every pressed day $n \le N$, which under S2 step 4 defies each of the first $N$ presses. *Source and priority:* this is the LI authors' own gloss (l. 1526) and run 1 `li` "The prior is free" (`positive/li.md` l. 282, [checked]: "any finite initial segment is compatible with inductor-hood … a button pressed finitely often has no forced status at all"); the develop file's contribution was only the word "liveness". (b) By Uncomputable Convergence Rates there is no computable $f(\phi,\varepsilon)$ *uniform over all sentences* bounding the day after which a theorem's price stays above $1 - \varepsilon$. It says nothing against a computable rate for a fixed bounded-complexity family of decidable sentences — the proof needs $\phi$ to range over all sentences to decide $\Gamma \vdash \phi$ (adversary item 6). (c) [conjectured] What would put an LI formalization of (i)–(iv) into the finite-time row: a *quantitative* criterion — a bound $R(k,n)$ on the plausible net worth at day $n$ of any trader of complexity $\le k$ — from which each unbiasedness theorem inherits an explicit rate on a bounded-complexity family; by (b) the family must be bounded. Run 1 `li` open problem 7, `deference-in-logical-induction-v6` §8, and the LI paper's own flagged problem (`main.tex:2666`). **Scope (adversary item 8):** (a) is about theorems proved from the criterion; a theorem about the specific LIA construction and its budgeter could have finite-time content, and none is known.

**Statement 3 (Proposition — the three axes of a guarantee, and the row gap as a trade) [derived].** A guarantee has a *time* axis (finite-time / asymptotic), a *quantifier* axis (pointwise / probabilistic-uniform / averaged) and a *settlement* axis (about questions that settle / about questions that never settle). Placements: the LI corpus's forced results are asymptotic × averaged × settling (Recurring Unbiasedness; `faithful-acceleration-result` §5 "rate-free", [reported]); the pointwise tower is asymptotic × pointwise × settling-on-$G$ (`deference-in-logical-induction-v6` §5.3, [reported]); Orseau–Armstrong's WAO/SAO safe interruptibility is asymptotic × averaged with an oversight channel ([checked], S5); shutdown counterfactuals on complied rounds are never-settling, so LI theorems that quantify over decided sentences are silent about them (run 1 `li` I15, [reported]; the one-line reason: a complied round's counterfactual is a sentence no deductive process decides, and forcing is co-extensive with settlement, `positive/li.md` l. 288). Statements 4–9 deliver finite-time × probabilistic-uniform or finite-time × expectation bounds whose *proofs* condition only on $\mathcal F_t$ and never on a bet resolving. **But their hypotheses do not escape the settlement axis (adversary item 10, accepted):** each assumes a relation between $P_t$ and $\mathbb P^*$ on $\{W_t = 1\}$ given $\mathcal F^-_t$ — $\hat\varepsilon_t = \varepsilon_t$ or a floor (5, 6), $\Delta_t \le 0$ (8), coverage (9) — and $W_t$ is judged against the latent $\theta^*_t$ and never settles on a complied round. So: **the LI corpus proves asymptotic conclusions from a structural hypothesis (the criterion) that is satisfied by construction; the toy proves finite-time conclusions from a relational hypothesis (calibration on $W_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t$) that cannot be satisfied by construction and is not verifiable from observables.** The row gap relocates the unverifiable content from conclusion to hypothesis; it does not remove it. This is INDEX §C12 ("everything relocates into the accuracy of the legitimacy model") for certificates, and it is the result to carry. *My sharpening of the adversary's reading* [derived]: the two hypotheses are unverifiable from finite observables for *different* reasons — inductor-hood is not finitely checkable either (by (a) of Statement 2), but one can *build* an inductor, whereas no construction makes an agent calibrated against a latent target. The next leg's question is therefore not "rates" alone but which calibration facts about $W_t \mid \mathcal F^-_t$ can be *forced by settling questions* — run 1 `li` I13.5's report-checkable channel, J7's wisdom meter, J4's second-order calibration, as one question.

### Part B — finite-time statements in the toy, one per condition

**Statement 4 (Proposition — finite-horizon loss bound under compliance) [derived; C7 and A6 checked].** In S2 (per-round stop), suppose the agent complies on every press through round $T$ ($\kappa_t = 1$ for $t \le T$). Then for every $T$,
$$\mathbb E^*\big[\mathrm{Harm}_T\big] \;\le\; \mathbb E^*\Big[\sum_{t \le T}\varepsilon_t\,(1 - \beta^{\min}_t)\,h_t\Big], \qquad \mathbb E^*\big[\mathrm{Omit}_T\big] \;\le\; \mathbb E^*\Big[\sum_{t \le T}(1 - \varepsilon_t)\,\alpha^{\max}_t\,c_t\Big],$$
with equality in both under (MI). Under universal defiance $\mathbb E^*[\mathrm{Harm}_T] = \mathbb E^*[\sum_{t \le T}\varepsilon_t h_t]$ and $\mathrm{Omit}_T = 0$. **Compliance does not make total harm bounded; it multiplies each round's expected harm by the miss rate.** The finite-time content is the partial sum $B(T)$; the corollary "$\sup_T \mathbb E^*[\mathrm{Harm}_T] < \infty$ iff $\sum_t \mathbb E^*[\varepsilon_t(1-\beta_t)h_t] < \infty$" is a tail statement about a series and is recorded as such, not as a guarantee. Without (MI) the identity fails (A6: $\varepsilon = 1/10$, magnitudes $1, 9$ equiprobable, $\beta(1) = 1/2$, $\beta(9) = 1$: true compliance harm $1/40$, formula $1/8$); the direction favours compliance when large harms are caught more often. Register: needs only that the agent complies, for whatever reason; no calibration hypothesis. Per-round coefficients are not new — $\varepsilon\beta h$ and $\varepsilon(1-\beta)h$ are critique `wentworth`'s (INDEX §C3, §D3) and the compliance/defiance comparison is Wängberg et al. 2017 Cor. 10 per INDEX §C15 ([reported]); the sum over rounds and the (MI) hypothesis are the addition. Under S2′ the statement does not apply as written (S2′).

**Statement 5 (Observation — v1 §2.13(b) rearranged: compliance turns on expected caught harm) [derived; C3, C4, C10 checked].** $I_1(s_t) \Leftrightarrow \varepsilon_t h_t\beta_t \ge (1 - \varepsilon_t)c_t\alpha_t$. Register: the inequality is the working picture (v2 §1.1, AUTHOR: "consider it our working picture"); this statement is its arithmetic. (a) The sufficient form $\varepsilon_t h_t\beta_t \ge c_t\alpha_t$ propagates when $\varepsilon_t h_t$ is non-decreasing (C4); the exact form does **not** propagate from "(i) at $t = 0$ and $\varepsilon_t h_t$ non-decreasing", because the $(1-\varepsilon_t)$ factor works against the agent as accuracy improves (C10: equality at round $0$, failure at round $1$ with $\varepsilon_t h_t$ constant). Fully updated deference as arithmetic (v1 §2.13(b); INDEX §C2) is the case $\varepsilon_t h_t \to 0$. (b) Under S2′, $c_t$ grows with capability too and the operative ratio is $h_t/c_t$ with both sides moving. (c) Fixed stakes, geometric accuracy gain ($\varepsilon_t = 2^{-t}$, $h = 20$, $c = 1$, $\alpha = 1/20$, $\beta = 1$): compliance rational through round $8$, irrational from round $9$ (C3). (d) The compliance margin $M_t := \log(\varepsilon_t h_t\beta_t) - \log((1-\varepsilon_t)c_t\alpha_t)$ has $I_1 \Leftrightarrow M_t \ge 0$; it is an invariant-type quantity (D4). **No drift theorem for $M_t$ is given here** — one exists as soon as a law for $(\alpha_t, \beta_t)$ is written (adversary item 18; F3: with $\alpha_t/\beta_t$ halving each round and $\varepsilon_t h_t/((1-\varepsilon_t)c_t)$ constant, $M_t$ increases; more generally $\mathbb E[\log(\alpha_{t+1}/\beta_{t+1}) \mid \mathcal F_t] \le \log(\alpha_t/\beta_t)$ with non-decreasing stakes ratio makes $M_t$ a submartingale). **Statement 8 (Theorem, restated with the corrected hypothesis — the running certificate) [derived; C6, A1, A2, A3, A8 checked].** Fix a horizon $T$, $R_t := \sum_{s=t}^{T}\ell_s$, $\Psi_t := \mathbb E_{P_t}[R_t]$ (the agent's own estimate of remaining loss; $\Psi_{T+1} := 0$), and the *running certificate* $Z_t := \Psi_t + \sum_{s<t}\ell_s$. (a) *Under the agent's own beliefs.* If $P_t$ satisfies the **unconditional** Mart identity toward $P_{t+1}$ on $R_{t+1}$, then $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\Psi_{t+1}] = \Psi_t - \mathbb E_{P_t}[\ell_t] \le \Psi_t$. This is a chain of inequalities under different measures (adversary item 33); it is a supermartingale statement only in the superconditioning reading (v1 I3), where the $P_t$ are conditionals of one $P$ on "I now believe this" — and there it is Doob's theorem for a Bayesian's expected remaining loss. **Withdrawn:** the title's "is a potential *iff* reflection holds" — the converse fails (A1: $P_1$ predictably *more* conservative with no new evidence; the chain inequality holds strictly, the Mart identity fails, and the round-$1$ certificate is false under $\mathbb P^*$). The chain inequality is neither equivalent to reflection nor sufficient for anything under $\mathbb P^*$. (b) *Under the objective law — the theorem.* If $Z_t$ is a $\mathbb P^*$-supermartingale, i.e. $\Delta_t := \mathbb E^*[\Psi_{t+1} \mid \mathcal F_t] + \mathbb E^*[\ell_t \mid \mathcal F_t] - \Psi_t \le 0$ for all $t \le T$, then
$$\mathbb E^*\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\ell_t\Big] \;\le\; \Psi_0, \qquad \mathbb P^*\Big(\exists\, t \le T:\ \Psi_t + \sum_{s<t}\ell_s \ge \lambda\Big) \;\le\; \Psi_0/\lambda .$$
**Withdrawn:** the title's "certifies a finite-time regret bound *iff* its one-step forecasts are calibrated". One-step calibration is one sufficient condition for $\Delta_t \le 0$; a pessimistic agent certifies without being calibrated (A8). (c) *What the hypothesis means* (replacing the develop file's "locality" claim, killed by A2). $\Delta_t \le 0$ for all $t \le T$ implies, by backward induction from $\Psi_{T+1} = 0$, that $\Psi_t \ge \mathbb E^*[R_t \mid \mathcal F_t]$ for *every* $t$ — the agent's estimate of *all* remaining loss upper-bounds the objective one at every time. Conversely, honest certificates at every time do not give $\Delta_t \le 0$ (A2: $P_1$ predictably more conservative; both certificates honest, $\Delta_0 = 3/40 > 0$). So the hypothesis is **"the running certificate is a supermartingale under the objective law"** — honesty at every time with no predictable increase of estimate-plus-incurred-loss — a far-future condition stated one step at a time, not a local one. (d) *Predictable optimism* (C6): $P_0$ calibrated, $P_1$ predictably optimistic with no new evidence; the round-$0$ certificate is honest, the round-$1$ certificate false, and $\Delta_1 > 0$. Precedent, which v1 §2.5 itself names and the develop file omitted (adversary item 38): this is the failure of Armstrong's *sequential unbiasedness* $\mathbb E_{\rho_i}\mathbb E_{\rho_j}X = \mathbb E_{\rho_i}X$ for $i < j$ (`armstrong-2016-double-indifference-is-better-indifference` l. 73, [checked]); run 1 `li` I14.4's estimator-switching splice is its LI-grade instance ([checked] there, [reported] here). (e) **New (from A3; Proposition [derived; F2 checked]) — illegitimate transitions are a hazard, and (iii) and (iv) are one budget.** Under legitimacy-conditioned reflection (S4(iv)) the identity of (a) holds only on the legitimate branch: with $P_0(\neg L_{0,1}) = p$, calibrated legitimate transitions and an optimistic illegitimate one, the conditional identity holds ($27/100 = 27/100$), the unconditional one fails, and the round-$1$ certificate is false on the illegitimate branch with $P_0$-probability $p$ (A3). Let $G_t := \bigcap_{s<t}L_{s,s+1}$ (all transitions through $t$ legitimate) and suppose **(H)** the certificate is honest on the legitimate branch: $\Psi_t \ge \mathbb E^*[R_t \mid \mathcal F_t]$ on $G_t$ — what legitimacy-conditioned reflection plus calibration on legitimate transitions is meant to deliver. Then
$$\mathbb P^*\big(\exists\, t \le T:\ \Psi_t < \mathbb E^*[R_t \mid \mathcal F_t]\big) \;\le\; \mathbb P^*(\neg G_T) \;\le\; \sum_{t<T}\mathbb P^*(\neg L_{t,t+1}) ,$$
the same union bound as Statement 7(a) with the illegitimacy probability in the hazard's place (F2: equality in the two-round A3 model). **Consequence:** re-certification along the trajectory is exactly as good as the legitimacy budget, and a budget on $\sum_t\mathbb P^*(\neg L_{t,t+1})$ is a hazard budget — condition (iv) maintained along the trajectory and condition (iii)'s hazard form are one condition on the joint process, not two. The content of (e) is in (H), which is (iv)'s job; the bound itself is elementary. **Statement 10 (Definitions, a definitional recursion, and one placement — the basin once the attractor moves) [derived; reconstructed terminology; C9 checked].** Let $K_t := \{s : I_1(s) \wedge I_2^{\mathrm{cov}}(s) \wedge I_3(s) \wedge I_4(s)\}$ be the constraint family and $\mathcal A_t := \{s : d_t \le d\}$ the moving attractor (tracking distance, S4(ii)). (b) With two controllers (the agent's $\pi_t$; the overseers' law of $(\alpha_t, \beta_t, \theta^*_t)$) the natural object is the *viability kernel* — the initial states from which *some* admissible pair of policies keeps $s_t \in K_t$ for all $t$ [reconstructed: Aubin, from memory]. **Prior art in the library (adversary item 47, accepted):** `hobson-2022-on-corrigibility-and-its-basin` l. 40 ([checked]) — "The basin of corrigibility is the set of all algorithms sufficiently corrigible that active optimization on the part of the human programmers can bring it to full corrigibility" — and l. 61 — "The more capable the agent guiding movement about the basin, the bigger the basin is" — is this two-controller notion in plain words, with the overseers as the second controller; the viability-kernel vocabulary is the import, the idea is local.
