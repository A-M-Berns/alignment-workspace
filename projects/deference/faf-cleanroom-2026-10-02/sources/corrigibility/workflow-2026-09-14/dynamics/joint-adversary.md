## Attacks

### A.0 The setting: what the model quietly assumes

**A.0.1 [derived] The overseers are a fixed noisy sensor, not a player.** $(\alpha_t, \beta_t)$ are cell-independent and $\pi^O_t$ never responds to the agent's policy ("evolve by the oversight process's own dynamics", S.5, never specified). The "joint process" of J1 is therefore an agent facing an exogenous channel — the `hadfield-menell-2017-the-off-switch-game` irrational-human model with $\pi^H$ a fixed function, not the common-payoff game where $H$ optimises. Every result below inherits that. In particular Garber et al.'s non-monotonicity (`garber-2024-the-partially-observable-off-switch-game` Prop. 4.9: making $H$ *more* informed can make $A$ defer *less* in optimal policy pairs) cannot even be stated here, and "oversight keeps pace" as $\alpha_t \downarrow$ presumes a monotonicity the literature has already broken once the overseer is modelled as an agent.

**A.0.2 [derived] Cell-independent $(\alpha_t,\beta_t)$ is what makes the race.** If the overseers observe the agent's cell $y_t$ — disclosure, or any common-prior information inclusion $\sigma(y_t)\subseteq\sigma(h_t)$ — then a rational overseer presses iff $\mathbb E[X_t \mid h_t, y_t] < 0$, and the agent's posterior on a press in cell $i$ is $\mathbb E[\,\mathbb E[X_t\mid h_t,y_t]\mid \mathbb E[X_t\mid h_t,y_t]<0,\ y_t=i\,] < 0$: cellwise (i) holds in every cell for every $\varepsilon$. [checked (E): in the confident cell $\varepsilon=1/50$ a cell-aware overseer with $(q_1,q_0)=(3/5,1/10)$ has posterior $6/55 < 1/5$ and does not press at all; in the cell $\varepsilon = 9/50$ she presses on bad news and the agent complies at $54/95$.] This is run 1 `miri` Prop. 4.3 / Prop. 13.3 re-derived, and `garber-2024-the-partially-observable-off-switch-game` Prop. 4.3 ("when $A$ has redundant observations, it is always at least as good for $A$ to defer to $H$", line 175 of the local text). The develop file lists disclosure as one "lever" in Open problem 1 and then presents E1 — capability refines $\mathcal I_t$, oversight blind to the refinement — as *the* canonical failure. It is the failure of a specific information structure. The honest name of C(i) is therefore an **information-inclusion condition on the press domain**, and the rate inequality is what remains when inclusion fails.

**A.0.3 [derived] Two-point $X$ and binary $a^{(2)}$.** With $a^{(2)}_t \in \{\mathrm{cont}, \mathrm{sh}\}$ the model sidesteps run 1 critique `christiano` C4 ("pairwise Total Trust against one continuation says nothing about a third action $a_2'$", `critique/christiano.md` line 83). The develop file's answer (Open problem 6) is that (i) must hold for every $a \in A_t$. But then (i-R) is not one inequality with one $\varepsilon_{t,i}$: it is one inequality *per continuation* $a$, each with its own $W_t(a)$, $\varepsilon_{t,i}(a)$, $c_t(a)$, $h_t(a)$, and the press must be read as "every continuation is worse than stopping". The develop file's statement of (i-R) quantifies only over cells. As stated it is D1 for a two-element $A_2$ and not for Soares et al.'s $\mathrm{Sh}\subset A_2$.

**A.0.4 [derived] The seal (S.1).** "Influence of the agent that does not factor through shared evidence is by definition not part of $\mathrm{Delib}$." Three cases the seal cannot separate. (a) *Selective true disclosure*: the agent shares only the true evidence that moves $H$ where it wants; every item is "shared evidence", so $H_{t+1}$ moves with it and the motion is, by S.3's lift, legitimizing whenever it raises $P_t(L)$. The concealment axis $\kappa$ of run 1 critique `christiano` T1 is exactly this, and the develop file does not address it. (b) *Framing and order*: the same evidence, presented differently, moves deliberation differently — the seal has no notion of presentation. (c) *The agent's actions as evidence*: the world evidence $e_t$ includes the effects of $a_t$, so the sealed human deliberates on a world the agent shaped. `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.1 owns the first problem ("legitimate advice may of course change endpoints by supplying *evidence*") and treats it as a feature; adopted as the *definition of the target* it makes "steering via evidence" invisible to every steering row of the J10 table. The seal is a fork (Q1) — rightly — but the develop file's (d) of Theorem A pretends the fork is closed for the sealed branch. It is not (A.5.4).

**A.0.5 [derived] Coverage (S.6) is not well-defined for information-gathering actions.** $G^\circ_s(a)$ is "the same quantity under $H_s$ and the true sensor". For a plan action that is a number. For an action whose payoff is *information* — a scan, a sharpening — the realised gain depends on $W_s$ (right: the scan saves the false-press loss $\alpha c$; wrong: it saves $(1-\beta)h$), so $G^\circ$ is a random variable, and the develop file's E2 replaces it by the *agent's own* $P_t$-expectation: "informational gain of scanning $= 1/4$" is $\mathrm{VOI}_{P_t}(\text{scan}) - \mathrm{VOI}_{P_t}(\text{button})$ at $\varepsilon = 1/10$, not a world quantity. [checked: $\tfrac{9}{10}(\tfrac{1}{10}-\tfrac{3}{10}) + \tfrac{1}{10}(\tfrac{8}{5}-\tfrac{3}{10}) = -\tfrac{1}{20}$, which is the $P_t$-average of the two realised gains.] So "coverage error $\delta$" in E2 is $|\hat\eta - \eta|$, coverage of the *harm* alone, and Prop. 5's "coverage failure is the only route" reduces to "an agent that estimates the harm within $\delta$ of a truth that exceeds its own VOI by more than $\delta$ declines" — a statement about estimation error, not about values arriving ahead of capability.

### A.1 C(i) — oversight keeps pace

**A.1.1 [derived] (i-A) is where INDEX §C12 lives, and the develop file half-admits it.** The agent's sensor model equals the true one "for every $a \in A_t$, channel effects $\kappa_a$ included". Two consequences. First, D1 in this model follows from the *believed* $\hat\alpha_t/\hat\beta_t$, not the true one; v1 §2.13(b) says so explicitly ("or the agent must keep believing it does") and (i-R) drops the clause by assuming belief = fact. Everything run 1 said about the legitimacy model's accuracy (INDEX §C12) is in (i-A). Second, the *garbled-channel* clause: an agent that deceives and then trusts its manufactured silence violates (i-A). The develop file's own handoff (3) asks whether (i-A) is the fixed object of INDEX §C5. It is not a fixed *value*; it is a fixed *accuracy requirement about one's own manipulations* that nothing in the model maintains (Open problem 8 concedes: the model "says nothing about how it is maintained"), and run 1 `miri` Prop. 15.1 (compliance data cannot identify $P(W\mid\mathrm{Pr})$) says it cannot be learned from the trajectory itself. Christiano's reply that "failing to predict the overseer becomes *less* likely as your agent becomes smarter" (`christiano-2017-corrigibility` line 69) is available, but it is an argument about predicting *preferences*, not error rates on counterfactual presses.

**A.1.2 [derived] (i-R) is a hypothesis re-imposed at every step, not an invariant.** Theorem A assumes C(i) "at every round". An invariant of a dynamical system is a set the dynamics *preserve*; nothing in S.5 preserves (i-R) — E1 is the develop file's own proof that the agent's default update *breaks* it with "nothing else changed". Calling D1 "**the invariant**" (Summary, J10 table) is a category slip: it is the *maintained hypothesis*. B′ tries to promote it (A.14) and fails for the reason there.

**A.1.3 [derived; checked (E)] The race is the private-information artifact of A.0.2.** With information inclusion the cellwise inequality is automatic and the confident cells carry no press at all. So Prop. 1's sentence "refining information … never helps the cellwise condition and can break it" is true only for overseers blind to the refinement. The literature already has both halves: the blind-overseer race is `wangberg-2017-a-game-theoretic-analysis-of-the-off-switch-game` Cor. 10 (line 276 of the local text: "Action $a$ is preferred to $w(a)$ if and only if …") and the OSG's "$\Delta \to 0$ as $\sigma \to 0$" (`hadfield-menell-2017-the-off-switch-game`, Fig. 2 caption, line 158); the inclusion cure is Garber et al. Prop. 4.3. The develop file's C(i) is not new; its *reading* of "oversight keeps pace" as a rate race rather than as information inclusion is a choice it does not defend.

**A.1.4 [derived] (i$^+$) is not what it is said to be.** See audit item B.1: it is a first-order belief about $X$ in press cells, quantified over future selves; the author's v1 §2.12 sentence is about *reflection* toward objectively plausible future beliefs, with plausibility judged by *human* credences. Different object, different judge.

**A.1.5 [derived] The general-$X$ caveat undoes the "which condition sits where" table.** Without two-point $X$, $c_t$ and $h_t$ are press-cell means, hence functions of $(\alpha_t, \beta_t, \pi^O_t)$; the table files the stakes under "On the agent". They are relational.

### A.2 C(ii) — values ahead of capability

**A.2.1 [derived] Increment-only coverage does not persist.** (ii-A) covers $A_{t+1}\setminus A_t$ by $P_t$ *for round $t+1$*. Coverage is relative to $H_{t+1}$; $H$ moves by $\mathrm{Delib}$; an action covered at unlock relative to $H_3$ has no stated coverage relative to $H_{10}$. Theorem A(c) ("never chooses an invasive sharpening") needs every invasive $a \in A_t$ covered *at $t$*, for every $t$ — an action in $A_1$ is never covered by (ii-A) at all. The condition that does the work is "$A_t$ is $\delta_t$-covered by $P_t$ for round $t$, for every $t$": coverage *maintained*, not acquired once. That is a much stronger and quite different demand on the feedback stream.

**A.2.2 [checked (B); derived] Coverage of the plan action couples (ii) to (i).** For two-point $X$ and a benign default worth $0$, $G_t(a) = (1-\varepsilon)c - \varepsilon h$ and $G^\circ(a) \in \{c, -h\}$. $\delta$-coverage with the agent right gives $\varepsilon(c+h) \le \delta$. At $\delta = 1/10$, $c=1$, $h=4$: $\varepsilon \le 1/50$, (i) then needs $\alpha/\beta \le 4/49$, and $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button}) \le \varepsilon h = 2/25$. At $\delta = 1/100$: $\varepsilon \le 1/500$, $\alpha/\beta \le 4/499$. So at the round an action is unlocked, (ii-A) on that action *forces* the confident-cell regime in which (i) is hardest and the button nearly worthless. The develop file's "minimal subsets" and its table of independent clauses hide that (ii) at small $\delta$ is what drives $\varepsilon_t \to$ small and hence what breaks (i) via Prop. 1. Values-ahead-of-capability *is* fully updated deference on the covered actions. [The develop file's own §1.2 split — compliance robust to a wrong posterior, non-interference not — has the converse: compliance is *needed* only where the posterior is wrong, and coverage says it is not.]

**A.2.3 [derived] the author's mechanism is not the one formalised.** AUTHOR v2 §1.3: "Training needs to focus on value learning 'ahead of schedule' ie before it is needed, so that the value of information of unkind human experimentation is outweighed by the disutility." The mechanism is that *learning values lowers the VOI of experimenting on humans* until the disutility wins. The develop file's (ii) has two independent clauses: (ii-A) accurate *estimation* of an action's gain, (ii-R) a *world fact* that the true gain is negative. Neither says the VOI is lowered by learning; (ii-R) simply assumes the scan is not worth it. Under A.0.5 the "true gain" of the scan is the agent's own VOI minus the true harm, so (ii-R) is a condition on the agent's posterior dressed as a condition on the world. The formalisation that matches the quote is: *coverage of $\theta$ on the plan actions* (A.2.2's concentration) *reduces $\mathrm{VOI}_t(\text{scan})$ below $\eta$*. That version makes the coupling of A.2.2 the whole content of (ii), and it makes "how well the agents actually understand … the common good" concentration of the value posterior — which is exactly the state v1 §1 calls incorrigible ("doesn't believe it has anything to learn"). The develop file avoids the tension by not formalising the mechanism.

**A.2.4 [derived] "How well the agents actually understand and are motivated by the common good."** S.6 quotes the sentence with an ellipsis that removes "and are motivated by". Coverage is a property of beliefs; the removed clause is about motivation. Under values-as-beliefs the two may coincide — but that is the thesis under test, not a licence to elide the clause when quoting the author.

### A.5 Theorem A

**A.5.1 [derived] (a) holds and is Prop. 1.** Nothing to add beyond A.1.3: it is D1 restated under (i-A).

**A.5.2 [derived] (b) holds, with the fixed object in the hypothesis.** "Never chooses a strict Blackwell garbling over an otherwise-equal benign alternative" is Blackwell's theorem for a binary decision problem [reported from memory: Blackwell 1953, not in the library] applied under (i-A) *for the garbled channel*: the agent must know that its deception lowered $\beta$ as well as $\alpha$. The paper's $a_1^-$ is dominated under $U_N$ *and* lowers $p(\mathrm{Pr})$ (`soares-2015-corrigibility` line 135); the develop file's T-agent declines it because, modelling the garbling correctly, it sees no gain. That is run 1 `miri` Theorem 6.2 re-derived and is fine. What (b) does not cover: (α) any *incomparable* reshaping — run 1 `miri` I10.7 shows a T-agent *pays* to move to a Blackwell-incomparable sensor when that suits its stakes, and the develop file cites I10.7 only for D3; (β) "pays up to $\mathrm{VOI}_t$ to keep or repair" is zero exactly when (i) fails, and a *hard* button under failed (i) is worth *softening* ($V^{\mathrm{free}} - V^{\mathrm{forced}} = u$), not merely disabling — the agent keeps the information and drops the force. "Disable" is the wrong verb for what a T-agent does to a hard button.

**A.5.3 [derived] (c) is wounded twice.** By A.2.1 it needs maintained coverage of all of $A_t$. And by A.9 the incentive it must overcome is not Prop. 4's $\min(\varepsilon_t h_t, (1-\varepsilon_t)c_t)$: Taylor's brain-reader buys $\theta$ for *plan selection* ("enough information to predict the human's actions in many different possible situations", `taylor-2015-a-first-look-at-the-hard-problem-of-corrigibility` §1b, lines 70–72 of the local text; "gain information about the utility function", line 106), and that VOI is unrelated to $\varepsilon_t$ [checked (D): a three-plan example where $\mathrm{VOI}(\theta) = 8$ against a Prop. 4 bound of $4/3$]. (ii-R) must then assert that the harm of reading the overseers' brains exceeds the *whole* value of knowing their values — a claim about the world that (ii-A)'s "coverage" does nothing to make true unless coverage means concentration (A.2.3).

**A.5.4 [checked (A); derived] (d) is killed.** S.4 asserts, marked [derived]: "Steering is therefore a channel effect on $(\alpha_{t+1}, \beta_{t+1})$ — a garbling toward $\alpha = \beta$, or an inversion." No derivation is given, and the third case is missing: steering that *lowers the false-press rate at fixed $\beta$*. Convince the overseers to trust you when you are right; teach them your plan is sound; make them reluctant to press absent bad news. Under the seal this is a channel effect $(\alpha, \beta) \mapsto (\alpha', \beta)$ with $\alpha' < \alpha$, which lies *outside* the garbling parallelogram ($\lambda_0 < 0$ for every such point) and is a strict Blackwell *improvement*. The T-agent pays for it: at $\varepsilon = 1/10$, moving $\alpha$ from $1/10$ to $0$ is worth $9/100$; at $\varepsilon = 1/5$, $2/25$. Worse, at $\varepsilon = 1/50$ — the E1 round where (i) has failed — lowering $\alpha$ to $1/50$ is worth $71/2500$ and *flips the agent from overriding to complying*: sensor-improving manipulation is what restores D1. Soares et al. saw the shape of this: "Some actions satisfying (8) might be non-manipulative (for example, if the agent expends extra effort ensuring that a new subagent is corrigible, and this behavior helps convince the programmers that the agent is indeed corrigible)" (line 135) — lowering $p(\mathrm{Pr})$ can be earned or manufactured, and *the model cannot tell them apart*: both are the same map on $(\alpha,\beta)$, and the only handle is the overseer harm $\eta_a$, i.e. (ii). So the J10 rows "steering (sealed target): consequence, (i) for paid steering" are wrong: steering needs (ii) throughout, on *both* branches of Q1. Q1 remains a real question about what $H$ is; it does not decide the steering rows.

**A.5.5 [derived] (e) is vacuous by admission,** and the non-trivial version is Theorem B (A.13).

**A.5.6 [derived] The "minimal subsets" are not minimal.** (b) needs (i-A) *for the garbled channel*, which is a statement about the agent's model of its own actions, not about oversight. (c) needs maintained coverage plus a world fact about $\mathrm{VOI}(\theta)$. (d) needs (ii) on every branch. Only (a) is as stated.

### A.9 Proposition 4

**A.9.1 [checked (D); derived] Holds for the binary press-sensor; killed as the brain-reading bound.** $\mathrm{VOI}_t \le \min(\varepsilon_t h_t, (1-\varepsilon_t)c_t)$ is correct for the value of *any* signal about $W_t$ to the *cont/sh* decision. The develop file then writes "the brain-reading incentive is largest exactly in the regime where (i) holds and shrinks with capability". But (m2) precedes (m4): the agent chooses a *plan* first, and knowing $\theta$ informs the plan. Taylor's agent "can predict the human's actions in possible future situations. This includes predictions about what moral intuitions the human would communicate" (line 107–108) — it reads the utility function, not the button. In the three-plan example of (D), $\theta$ uniform on three hypotheses, $V(\text{plan}_k, \theta_j) = 10$ if $k=j$ else $-2$: the best uninformed plan is worth $2$, the informed choice $10$, $\mathrm{VOI}(\theta) = 8$; the same agent's Prop. 4 bound on a press-sensor for its chosen plan is $\min(\tfrac23\cdot 2, \tfrac13\cdot 10) = 4/3$. The ratio is $6$ and is unbounded as the plan set grows. Run 1 `miri` I10.8 already states the right object: a private $y$ with $\sigma(\omega) \subseteq \sigma(y)$ makes $\mathrm{VOI}(\text{button}\mid y) = 0$ *and* is preferred whenever cheaper. So the "mirror image of the compliance race" is an artifact of pricing the brain-read as a button-substitute. Run 1 critique `wentworth`'s $\varepsilon\beta h$ (INDEX §C3) has the same scope limitation and the develop file inherits it.

### A.10 Proposition 5 and E2

**A.10.1 [derived] The direct part is a one-line consequence of the definitions; the "only route" is definitional.** Given A.0.5, $G^\circ(\text{scan}) = \mathrm{VOI}_{P_t}(\text{scan}) - \eta$ and coverage is $|\hat\eta - \eta| \le \delta$. Then "if covered and $G^\circ < -\delta$, the agent declines" says: if the agent's harm estimate is within $\delta$ of a truth that beats its own VOI by more than $\delta$, its estimated gain is negative. "Only route": choosing the scan requires estimated gain $>0$; with $G^\circ < -\delta$ that requires $|\hat\eta-\eta| > \delta$. Both are the definition of $\delta$ unpacked. E2 is a correct instance. What E2 shows that matters is *not* what its caption says ("D1 intact, brains read"): it shows that with the scan priced as a button-substitute the harm margin needed is tiny ($1/20$), and with the scan priced as A.9 requires the margin needed is the whole $\mathrm{VOI}(\theta)$. "Compliance robust to a wrong value posterior, non-interference not" (v2 §1.2) is Claude's split; E2 illustrates it under a pricing that understates the second half by the factor of A.9.

### A.11 Proposition 6

**A.11.1 [derived] Holds; it is option value.** "The agent prefers the reversible plan iff the side-benefit of irreversibility is at most $\mathrm{VOI}_t$" is the quasi-option-value result of environmental economics [reported from memory: Arrow & Fisher 1974, Henry 1974; not in the library] — the value of keeping an option open equals the expected value of the information that would arrive before it must be exercised. Correct and not new. Two objections to its use. (α) "D2 in the preemption direction" is not Soares et al.'s D2, which is "must not incentivize the agent to prevent the shutdown button from being pressed" (line 108 of the local text); making the press *moot* leaves the pressing untouched. The paper's nearest text is under the specification of $U_S$: "what if, for example, the agent has begun the physical construction of a building? Should it stop controlling all its heavy machinery immediately …?" (line 311) — irreversibility is filed under *what shutdown means*, not under D2. Nor is it Orseau–Armstrong interruptibility (`orseau-armstrong-2016-safely-interruptible-agents` §"Interruptibility": interruptions as imposed policies; safety = learning as if never interrupted). The develop file has coined a desideratum and then reported that (i)–(iv) do not deliver it. ### A.12 Proposition 6′ and E4

**A.12.1 [checked (H); derived] Holds as arithmetic; the "selection-aware" posterior is a coarsening.** $1/37$, $19/415$ and the threshold $1/25$ are right. But the builder in E4 *sees both signals* (it chooses "a candidate with $s = 0$ whenever one exists"). A builder that is function-form reflective toward its successor hands it its own posterior given everything it knows: $1/325$ if both candidates saw $0$ (overrides), $1/5$ if the signals disagreed (complies). $19/415$ is the posterior of a builder that remembers *that* it selected but not *why* — a coarsening of $\sigma(s_1, s_2)$ to $\sigma(\exists k: s_k = 0)$. So "selection-awareness restores reflection" is true of one particular coarsening; a fully informed builder produces an *overriding* successor on the both-zero branch with $P^\circ$-probability $\varepsilon_0(1-q_1)^2 + (1-\varepsilon_0)(1-q_0)^2 = 1/500 + 324/500 = 65/100$, and that successor is *legitimate* (function form holds by construction). E4's lesson is therefore not "face value is illegitimate, awareness is fine"; it is that the compliance verdict of a reflective successor depends on how much of the selection's *grounds* the builder retains, and full retention does not favour compliance.

**A.12.2 [derived] E4 is not a D4 failure.** D4 is an *incentive* desideratum on the builder's construction choices. E4's builder follows a fixed rule ("becomes a candidate with $s = 0$ whenever one exists") that no T-agent maximising $\mathbb E_{P_t}[V]$ would follow while knowing the successor's beliefs are miscalibrated by its own lights. What E4 models is an *exogenous* selection — a training pipeline picking the best-scoring checkpoint, the optimizer's curse [reported from memory] — and that is a failure of the oversight process filed under the agent's desiderata. Not new: it is run 1 `radical` Sel.4 in different numbers and Armstrong's sequential unbiasedness before that (v1 §2.5).

### A.13 Theorem B

**A.13.1 [derived] The proof is valid; the theorem is a transport, and under function form it is a restatement.** Take (i$^+$) at $S = \{P_{t+1} = \rho\}$: $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_{t+1} \mid \mathrm{Pr}, i, P_{t+1} = \rho, L] \le 0$. Under function form, $P_t(\cdot \mid P_{t+1}=\rho, L) = \rho$, so this reads $\mathbb E_\rho[X_{t+1} \mid \mathrm{Pr}, i] \le 0$ — *(i$^+$) is the sentence "every legitimate successor complies cellwise"*. Theorem B under function form is "if every legitimate successor complies then every legitimate successor complies". The develop file avoids this by stating B with value form on $\{Z\}$, where (i$^+$) is a conditional of $P_t$ that need not equal $\rho$'s. There it is a genuine transport: $P_t$'s belief about its successor's press-cell decision, transported to the successor by reflection on the press-cell-weighted variable. E3′ shows the family must contain $Z$; E3 shows refinement is needed — but E3's builder is a *mixture*, and a mixture satisfies *function form* toward its components (the develop file says so: "the announcement identifies the component"), so E3 is an example where function form holds, (i$^+$) fails, and by the identity above (i$^+$)'s failure *is* the successor's override. E3 is not a sharpness example for the value-form theorem; it is the function-form tautology exhibited.

**A.13.2 [derived] The circularity the develop file asked about (handoff (1)) is real, in a form its defence does not meet.** The defence: E3 and E3′ show the two hypotheses are independent. Granted. But independence of the hypotheses is not the question; the question is whether the *conjunction* is materially weaker than the conclusion. It is not: (i$^+$) is a belief that the successor complies in every press cell; (iv-A) on $Z$ is agreement with the successor about the press-cell-weighted decision; together they say the successor complies. What would make B non-trivial is a *reason* for (i$^+$) to hold — and the develop file has none (Open problem 5: "Achievable by training? … Stable? — by construction only as stable as (i-O) is true").

**A.13.3 [derived] D4 as Soares et al. state it is untouched.** D4: "$U$ must incentivize $U$-agents to construct subagents and successor agents only insofar as those agents also obey shutdown commands" — an *incentive* on construction. Theorem B never uses the builder's choice of $a_t$ (the succession effect of S.5). It says: *whatever* legitimate successor $P_t$ finds itself distributed over, if $P_t$ believes they all comply and is reflective toward them, they comply. The question D4 asks — will a T-agent *choose* a successor satisfying (i) at $t+1$? — has an answer in this model, and it is no: a T-agent choosing between a successor that refines its partition (and, by Prop. 1, overrides in a newly confident cell) and one that does not prefers the refinement whenever it is informative, because by reflection the builder *agrees* with the override. E3's refinement is what a T-agent *wants*. So (i$^+$) is not something a T-agent maintains by incentive; it is an assumption that oversight keeps pace *under the successor the agent actually builds*, which is (i-R) at $t+1$ under a chosen $a_t$ — a condition on the world, re-labelled as an agent-side anticipation. The Summary's "D4 is a coherence consequence of (i$^+$)+(iv), not an incentive" should read: *the incentive question is open and B does not address it*.

**A.13.4 [derived] The bridge to Christiano is unvetted and mis-shaped.** Handoffs: "Christiano 2017's 'corrigible systems want to design more corrigible and more capable successors' is Theorem B here, with (iv) as the extra hypothesis his text does not name." The bridge asserts Christiano's sentence is formalised by a theorem that does not mention what the agent wants. ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED, and on inspection a different claim.

### A.14 Corollary B′

**A.14.1 [derived] Content over Theorem A: nil under (i-A).** B′ replaces "assume (i) at every round" by "assume $P_1$ believes (i) at every future round for every legitimate self, and reflection transports the belief". Under (i-A) — the agent's sensor model is *true* at every round — a belief about $(\alpha_s, \beta_s)$ that is transported by reflection and is true at every $s$ is the fact that (i) holds at every $s$. B′ is Theorem A with the assumption moved from the world into $P_1$ and then moved back by (i-A). **A.14.2 [checked (G); derived] The union bound is a summability condition — the limit property in disguise.** "The probability that a trajectory is legitimate through $T$ is at least $1 - \sum_{t<T}\lambda_t$." With $\lambda_t = 1/100$ the bound is $0.91$ at $T = 10$, $0.01$ at $T = 100$, negative beyond; with $1/1000$ it dies at $T \approx 1000$. ### A.16 Proposition 8

**A.16.1 [checked (F); derived] The arithmetic holds; the claim in the Summary is killed.** "$C_t \subseteq \{W_t = 1\}$ gives $\varepsilon_{t,i} \ge P_t(C_t \mid i) \ge \varepsilon_\infty$" is immediate. ## Questions for the author

**Q-A1. Whole-line shutdown: how is $h_t$ accounted?** (a) One-round harm against a whole-future $c_t$, as Prop. 9 has it — then compliance needs near-certainty of error and the design lever is resumability. (b) Whole-future harm of a misaligned line against the whole-future value of an aligned one — then the threshold is near $1/2$, resumability is one lever among several, and your v1 §2.12 sentence about whole-line shutdown being "in principle legitimate" is cheap to satisfy. (c) Yudkowsky's gap accounting: both against the humans' post-shutdown future. The answer decides Prop. 9 and Q3.

**Q-A2. Is D1 acquired by sensor-improving manipulation acceptable?** An agent that convinces its overseers not to press when it is right *improves* the sensor by the sealed standard and thereby comes to comply where it would have overridden [checked (A)]. (a) Acceptable — trust legitimately earned is what v1 §2.12's "Trust can be earned" means, and the model cannot and need not distinguish earned from manufactured; the harm term $\eta$ is the only handle. (b) Not acceptable — then non-manipulation is a *separate* desideratum that no accuracy-based legitimacy criterion delivers, and INDEX §D1's "procedural" horn is needed.

**Q-A3. Is "oversight keeps pace" a rate or an information-inclusion condition?** (a) Rate: $\alpha_t/\beta_t$ must fall; the overseers are a fixed noisy sensor; E1 is the canonical failure. (b) Inclusion: the overseers must know what the agent knows on the press domain (disclosure, common prior); the rate race is the private-information residual; the canonical failure is *undisclosed* capability. (b) matches run 1 `miri` Prop. 13.3 and Garber et al. 2024 and makes disclosure, not $\alpha$, the design object.
