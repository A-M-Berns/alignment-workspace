## Attacks

Numbering: A0 attacks the definitions; A$n$ attacks S$n$.

### A0 — Definitions D3–D8: the second-order object is first-order

**A0.1 [derived] $\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ measures disagreement with the base distribution, not calibration about own coverage.** By D4–D5, $\hat{\mathcal R}_t=\mathbb E_{a\sim\gamma_t}\mathbb E_{P_t}\big[(V_\omega(\varnothing)-V_\omega(a))^+\big]$. Take a point-mass posterior $P_t=\delta_{\omega'}$ (maximal overconfidence, zero spread). Then $\hat{\mathcal R}_t=\mathbb E_{\gamma_t}[c_{\omega'}(a)]$, which is large iff $\omega'$ *disfavours human-like actions* and zero iff $\omega'$ rates every base action at least as well as $\varnothing$. So the quantity the rule reads as "estimated risk" is driven by the content of the point estimate, not by any uncertainty about it. The file's own remark under D4 ("strictly positive whenever some hypothesis with positive weight disfavours $a$") is the mechanism, and it works for a *single* hypothesis just as well. Consequence: the recklessness ratio $\rho_t$ compares true base-harm to *believed* base-harm; it is a calibration statistic about $\gamma_t$, not about the agent's own top choices. The file's §0 gloss — "the agent's estimate of how well its value posterior covers its options tracks the true coverage" — is not what D5 defines.

**A0.2 [derived] D8 bounds optimization power by believed base-safety, and the sign is wrong for J7.** $\mathrm{OP}_t=\log(\eta/\hat{\mathcal R}_t)^+$. An agent that correctly learns that human-like actions are often harmful (large true $\mathcal R_t$, and it knows it) gets *less* power; an agent that wrongly or rightly believes the base harmless gets *all* of it. The author's rule (v2 §1.3) is that "agent capabilities should be capped as a function of how well the agents *actually* understand and are motivated by the common good". "Actually" is third-person; S1 proves no functional of $P_t$ equals it. So the self-enforced half of J7 cannot exist in the form v2 asks for, and D8 is its shadow: a cap by *believed* $\gamma_t$-harm. The develop file knows S1 and still labels D8 "the quantity 'power upper-bounded by wisdom' (J7) bounds on the self-enforced side" (D7). Not established; the honest statement is A0.2's.

**A0.3 [derived] Two rules, one budget, two $\eta$s.** D8's $\eta$ is a harm budget; the "delegation variant" of D8 and S9(c) use Kosoy's $\eta$, a *probability* threshold for discarding hypotheses (`kosoy-2019-delegative-reinforcement-learning` l. 257: "$\eta$ is a probability threshold below which we stop considering hypotheses"). The delegation trigger "not known safe with probability $\ge1-\eta$" and the quantilizer's "$\hat{\mathcal R}_t/\eta$" are not the same parameter. S12's $\eta'$ silently fixes this for one statement.

**A0.4 [derived] D4 counts commission only.** $c_\omega(\varnothing)=0$ for every $\omega$ by construction. A pending catastrophe that only action averts is harm-free under D4, so the "safe" limit $q_t=1$ (mimic $\gamma_t$) and the "safest" delegation $\bot$ are harm-free by fiat. For corrigibility this is Taylor's `noop` convention and acceptable; for v2 §1.3's "the future to go well, or at least reliably go well" it is not — omission is where "cautious but useless" (the file's open problem 3) becomes a harm, not a performance cost.

### A3 — S3

**A3.1 [checked] The inequality holds and is Taylor 2016 Lemma 1 plus one substitution.** Nothing to break. **A3.2 [checked] The gloss is false.** "if $\rho_t\le1$ … the agent never does worse than sampling the trusted base distribution" — with $\rho_t=1$, $\eta=0.1$, $1000$ uniform base actions and one harmful action ranked top by the proxy, $q_t=0.01$ gives $\pi_t(a^\circ)=0.1$ and $\mathcal H_t=0.1=100\,\mathcal R_t$ (`checks.py` §5). The correct gloss is the disjunction the proof actually gives: $q_t=1$ and $\mathcal H_t\le\mathcal R_t$, *or* $q_t<1$ and $\mathcal H_t\le\eta$. A quantilizer's whole point is to concentrate on the top slice; if the base's harm sits there, the agent does up to $\eta/\mathcal R_t$ times worse than the base. Taylor says exactly this (`taylor-2016-quantilizers-a-safer-alternative-to-maximizers` l. 121: "no more than ten times as costly as sampling from $\gamma$").

### A4 — S4

**A4.1 [checked] Holds, and is in the literature under Taylor's name.** The file registers "quantifying over cost functions, including ones outside the hypothesis space" as CLAUDE's reading of the hard-problem clause. Taylor 2016 l. 63 already frames it so: "we assume there is some unknown cost function $c$ … We expect $c$ to be quite difficult to approximate … Even without an approximation of $c$, though, we can still prove that under certain assumptions, a quantilizer will not incur a high cost according to $c$." The robustness-by-quantification is the paper's stated design goal, not a new reading.

**A4.2 [derived] What survives misspecification is the fixed-$q$ quantilizer, not the caution rule.** The file's own proof lists what depends on $\Omega$: "$U_t$ (the ranking), $\hat c_t$, $\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ and therefore $q_t$ and $\rho_t$". So under algebra misspecification the surviving statement is $\mathcal H_t\le\mathcal R_t/q_t$ for whatever $q_t$ the corrupted estimate happens to produce — including $q_t\to0$ when the misspecified posterior sees no base harm, where the bound is vacuous. The distinctive content of D8 (coverage-conditioned $q$) is precisely the part with no robust guarantee; Taylor's externally fixed $q$ has one. The Summary's "survives the true value function lying outside the agent's algebra" is true of Lemma 1 and false of D8's contribution.

**A4.3 [derived] The hard-problem page's conclusion is the opposite of quantilization.** The clause quoted ends: "the programmers need to be allowed to actually observe and correct the AI's behavior, rather than the AI extracting all updates implied by its current formula for moral uncertainty and then ignoring the programmers" (`arbital-hard-problem-of-corrigibility` l. 21). Its remedy is *external* correction; S4's remedy is an internal rule with no channel in it. The register flag ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED is correct; the reading should be marked as *not* meeting the page's intent rather than "whether this meets the page's intent" is open.

### A5 — S5

**A5.1 [checked] S5(a)'s trajectory sum assumes additive cost, and Taylor §4 shows that assumption is dangerous.** $\mathcal H_t$ is per-step expected harm relative to $\varnothing$ under the step's value functions; summing to $\eta T$ presumes the trajectory's harm is the sum of per-step harms. Taylor 2016 l. 171–191 gives the counterexample: two games, actions $\{0,1,2\}$, uniform base, cost $1$ iff the pair is $(2,2)$; per-step $\tfrac13$-quantilization picks $(2,2)$ surely (harm $1$) while the base pays $\tfrac19$ — "This cost blowup increases exponentially with $n$" — and every *per-step* cost is $0$, so S3's per-step bounds sum to $0$ (`checks.py` §7). Her §4 verdict: "assuming that the cost function is additive could be quite dangerous", with the byte-appending program as the alignment-relevant case. S5(a) therefore needs either joint quantilization over trajectories (whose base distribution is over policies, not actions — a much stronger $\gamma$) or an explicit additivity hypothesis flagged as a task assumption. Without one, the *trajectory* claim — the point of the thread — is not established; only the per-step S3 is.

**A5.2 [checked] S5(b) is false for $\delta>0$.** Statement: "D10's pointwise form implies $\mathrm{COR}(T)$." Counterexample: $\Omega=\{\omega_1\}$ with $V_{\omega_1}(\varnothing)=V_{\omega_1}(a)=0.5$; truth $V^*(\varnothing)=0.5+\delta$, $V^*(a)=0.5-\delta$. Both actions are $\delta$-covered, $\hat c_t(a)=0$, $c_t(a)=2\delta$, so $\rho_t=\infty$ (`checks.py` §4). The proof concedes this ("$\mathrm{COR}$ up to tolerance") but the statement does not carry the tolerance. **A5.3 [derived] S5(b) is ill-posed for the old actions even at $\delta=0$.** D10's pointwise form says "$\rho_{t-1}\le1$ carried over on the old actions", but $\rho_{t-1}$ is an average under $\gamma_{t-1}$ of $t{-}1$ quantities; at $t$ the posterior, the weights $\gamma_t$ and (by D1) the target $\omega^*_t$ have all moved. A covered old action can become uncovered with no new action involved. "Carried over" is not a predicate on the state at $t$; it is a hope.

**A5.4 [derived] S5(a) is conditional on an unauditable hypothesis at every $t$.** The theorem is: ($\rho_t\le1$ for all $t\le T$) $\Rightarrow$ ($\mathcal H_t\le\eta$ for all $t\le T$). By S1 the hypothesis is a third-person relation the agent cannot check, and by A0.1 it is not calibration. So the "finite-time guarantee" the Summary advertises is S3 at each $t$; the trajectory content is the quantifier, and the quantifier is over an assumption. This is exactly the shape the mandate asks me to flag: it *reads* as a trajectory property and *is* a conditional per-step property.

**A5.5 [checked] S5(d) holds and is standard.** Every limit-frequency criterion ignores finite prefixes; the LI paper's own caveat is quoted in `unbiasedness-theorem-families` l. 22. Correct handoff to `invariant`; not a result of this thread.

### A6 — S6

**A6.1 [checked] (a) holds** — it is D8 read at $\hat{\mathcal R}_t=0$. **A6.2 [derived] (b)'s "absorbed" needs $\hat{\mathcal R}_t\ge\gamma_t(a^\circ)$, which is not a small hypothesis.** The bound $\pi_t(a^\circ)c_t(a^\circ)\le\eta\,\gamma_t(a^\circ)/\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ is within budget only when the agent's estimated base harm is at least the base's probability of the bad action. For a bad action that is a *common* human move ($\gamma_t(a^\circ)$ not small) and an agent that is otherwise fairly confident ($\hat{\mathcal R}_t$ small), the top-$q$ slice is $a^\circ$ alone and $\mathcal H_t=c_t(a^\circ)$, the maximal harm. This is Taylor's own worst case (l. 145: "if humans are likely to misunderstand the problem and thereby cause catastrophe, then a simple generative model would not suffice"), and it is the shape of the reckless case: one confidently over-valued action that humans sometimes take. "Isolated confident errors are absorbed by uncertainty elsewhere" should read "absorbed iff uncertainty elsewhere outweighs the base mass of the error".

### A7 — S7

**A7.1 [derived] (a) is "replace an approximate posterior by the Bayes posterior", not a calibration result.** The proof says so: "For a Bayes-optimal algorithm the two coincide identically". An agent that can run `OutsideView` under its own prior $\Pi$ can compute the $\Pi$-posterior directly; the "detection" is the discovery that $P^{\mathrm c}_t$ is not that posterior. Nothing about *coverage* is learned; the agent's expectation of its coverage is unchanged (S1).

**A7.2 [checked] The detector is in the literature, with its counterexample.** Milli, Hadfield-Menell, Dragan and Russell 2017 (`milli-2017-should-robots-be-obedient` §"Detecting Misspecification", l. 245–259) build exactly an internal misspecification detector: during a burn-in of $B$ rounds the robot measures its sample obedience $\tilde O_1$ against the value its own model predicts, and "If $\tilde O_1$ is not close to one, then it is likely that $R$ has the wrong model of $\Theta$, and would be better off just being obedient" — Remark 4's *policy mixing*. Their Takeaway 5 is D6's widening in other words: "If $R$ has extra features, it is more obedient than with the true model, whereas if it is missing features, then it is less obedient", so "erring on the side of extra features is far better". Carey 2018 (`carey-2018-incorrigibility-in-the-cirl-framework` l. 421) then gives the S7(b)-type failure of that detector: an agent that conflates two actions $a^+,a^-$ into one "will think that it has perfect accuracy in making this prediction … after the burn-in period is concluded, it will proceed to repeatedly select $a^-$, hence incurring arbitrary losses". So S7's dichotomy — internal repair works for algorithmic error, not for misspecification — is the Milli–Carey exchange restated without citing it. Turner 2020's post is a later informal gloss; he himself links Milli et al. for "calibrated deference" (l. 45).

### A8 — S8 and Model M

**A8.1 [checked] (a) holds** — it is v1 §2.13(b) with $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t$ in place of $\varepsilon$; the algebra of the compliance condition, $\varepsilon^\dagger$ and $\mu^\dagger$ checks (linearity of the numerator and denominator in $\mu_t$). **A8.2 [derived] The "third option" is run 1's first option with a hierarchical prior.** Run 1 `yudkowsky` C3(i) offered "the agent's $P_t$ (decays with capability)" versus "a floor it cannot update". S8's $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t$ *is* the agent's $P_t$-expectation of its own error rate, computed from a two-point prior over $\{\varepsilon,\rho\varepsilon\}$. That the agent's $P_t$ can be non-dogmatic about its own error was never excluded by option one; what run 1 asked is what stops that $P_t$-quantity from decaying with capability — and S8(e) answers: nothing, silence drives it down. So the fork is intact and S8 lands on the first tine, with the file's own open problem 1 as the admission.

**A8.3 [checked] (b) is killed as a general claim by identifiability.** Model M gives the agent *certain* knowledge of $(\alpha,\beta)$ and only one alternative self-model. The only observation is the press bit, so per step the data are Bernoulli$(p)$ with $p=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{true}}\beta+(1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{true}})\alpha$: one identifiable number for three unknowns. Add a single hypothesis "I am calibrated and the overseers are trigger-happy", $(\varepsilon,\alpha',\beta)$ with $\alpha'=(p_r-\varepsilon\beta)/(1-\varepsilon)$: at the file's parameters $\rho=4$ gives $p_r=0.18$ and $\alpha'=0.163$ (a $16\%$ false-press rate instead of $10\%$). Its likelihood equals the reckless hypothesis's at every step; the Bayes factor between them is $1$ forever; $\mu_t$ converges not to $1$ but to the *prior* odds split. Worse, the trigger-happy hypothesis *raises* the compliance threshold: $\varepsilon^\dagger$ goes from $0.0909$ to $0.1403$ at $\rho=4$ (`checks.py` §1), so the unidentified mass argues *against* heeding. Run 1 `miri` Prop. 15.1 proved the same non-identification in a setting where the agent additionally observes $\omega$ on non-press steps; Model M observes less and is weaker. The recovery theorem therefore holds only for an agent dogmatic about the overseers' error rates — a fixed object about $(\alpha,\beta)$ traded for non-dogmatism about $\varepsilon$. This is where the file said to attack ("$\alpha,\beta$ known and hypothesis-independent"), and the attack lands.

**A8.4 [checked] Whole-line $c$ makes recovery impossible, not slower.** Open problem 5 says "S8(b)'s recovery lengthens". With $c$ the agent's whole future, $\varepsilon^\dagger=\alpha c/(\alpha c+\beta h)\to1$; at $c=20,h=2,\alpha=0.1,\beta=0.5$, $\varepsilon^\dagger=0.667$, and no $\mu_t\in[0,1]$ makes $\varepsilon^{\mathrm{eff}}_t=(1-\mu_t)\varepsilon+\mu_t\rho\varepsilon\ge\varepsilon^\dagger$ unless the true error rate $\rho\varepsilon$ itself exceeds $0.667$ (`checks.py` §2). Under run 1's accounting (INDEX §D4, which v2 J2 adopts: "whole-line shutdown makes $c$ the agent's whole future"), self-suspicion cannot produce compliance at all for any agent that is right more than a third of the time. Model M's per-step veto is not a simplification of J2; it is a different problem.

**A8.5 [checked] In the file's own numbers the channel is weakest where it matters.** Preventable harm ("pressed-but-ignored") at $\mu_0=0.1$ versus dogmatic: $\rho=2$: $201$ vs $300$ ($33\%$ removed); $\rho=4$: $31$ vs $601$ ($95\%$); $\rho=8$: $8$ vs $1201$ ($99\%$) (`checks.py` §3). The mildly reckless agent — twice as error-prone as it thinks, the realistic case — keeps two thirds of its preventable harm over $3000$ steps and never heeds in $19\%$ of runs. "The more reckless the agent, the faster the channel corrects it" is true and is the *bad* news: recovery is fast exactly for the agents whose recklessness is gross enough to be obvious.

**A8.6 [derived] (c)'s coupling is asserted, not modelled.** Model M's wrong-rate is a constant $\rho\varepsilon$ independent of $q_t$; scratch §C recomputes $q_t$ at several $\mu_t$ but never feeds it back into the harm process. "Reducing $\rho\varepsilon$ itself is the caution rule's job (S3)" is a cross-reference, not a result of the model; in Model M there is no action set, no base distribution and no ranking, so S3 has nothing to act on.

**A8.7 [derived] (e) is right, and D6 is why.** The transfer D6 advertises ("evidence about miscalibration on one region … widen the posterior on another") is symmetric: the same shared $\mu_t$ that carries suspicion carries *complacency* from the silent region into the novel one. Either D6 transfers (and S8(e) bites) or it does not (and D6 is not a new object). Not established that there is a third way.

**A8.8 [checked] Harm unobserved is a modelling choice that cuts against the file.** Kosoy's regret bound uses reward observation (Prop. 5) to lower-bound information in non-delegating episodes; Model M removes it, so the only value-relevant evidence is the press. ### A9 — S9

**A9.1 [derived] (a) is a linear leak, not a guard.** Unrecoverable loss $\le\lambda T$ is linear in $T$; Kosoy's traps are the $\lambda\to0$ case precisely because a positive per-step irreversible loss *is* linear regret (her Def. 5, $A^0_M$). With $\lambda$ small enough to matter, most novel actions fail the check and the agent delegates (open problem 3); with $\lambda$ large enough to act, the "guard" allows $\lambda$ of permanent loss per step for the whole recovery window $T_{\mathrm{rec}}\approx\log(\cdot)/D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ — thousands of steps at $\rho=2$. The file's own S8 numbers price the guard: $\lambda\times1766$ at $\rho=2,\mu_0=0.1$.

**A9.2 [reconstructed; against the local text] D9 is the wrong half of AUP for J7.** The local copy is image-only except the appendix, and the appendix states what AUP is for: "AUP aims to prevent catastrophes by stopping bad agents from *gaining power* to do bad things" (`turner-2020-attainable-utility-preservation-concepts` l. 44) and "it has 'approval incentives' which bias it towards AU landscapes in which its power hasn't decreased too much, either" (l. 50) — a penalty on *changes* in attainable utility, with the increase direction as the corrigibility-relevant one. D9 keeps only decreases ("no hypothesis loses more than $\lambda$"), which is the shape of Kosoy's traps and of reachability-style side-effect measures, and drops the increase direction that is J7's whole subject ("upper-bound an agent's power"). Calling it "Turner's attainable utility" is a mislabel; and an agent that takes a $\lambda$-reversible action which *raises* its own $\mathrm{AU}$ under every $\omega$ (gains power) passes D9 with no penalty.

**A9.3 [checked] (c) is reported accurately, with two corrections.** Def. 5, Def. 8 (supp $\subseteq A^0_M$; some Blackwell-optimal action w.p. $>\epsilon$), Def. 9, Theorem 1 / Corollary 1 and the $\Omega(\eta\epsilon)$ information gain (Props. 3–4, l. 415–423) are as the file says. But (i) "a bounded number of delegations" is $O(\ln N/(\eta\epsilon))$ with $\eta\sim(1-\gamma)^{1/4}$ (Cor. 1 proof, l. 1161–1175), so it grows as the horizon lengthens — bounded for fixed $\gamma$, not uniformly; (ii) Kosoy's regret is against a *known* reward $R$ shared by all hypotheses (Theorem 1: "some $R:S\to[0,1]$"), so nothing in her theorem is about value uncertainty — S12's transposition changes the object of learning, not just the hypothesis class (A12).

**A9.4 [derived] "null does not raise attainable value" is harmless, and the checkability caveat is the result.** If $V_\omega(a):=\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\cdot a)$ with no immediate reward, then $\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t)\ge\mathrm{AU}_\omega(s_t\cdot\varnothing)$ holds by definition of a max over continuations, so the flagged assumption is free. The real hypothesis is the one the proof ends on: the agent can check $\lambda$-reversibility only where its dynamics model is trustworthy, "for genuinely novel actions the check is unavailable" — i.e. exactly on $A_t\setminus A_{t-1}$, where D10's guarded form needs it. So the guard is available where it is not needed and unavailable where it is.

### A12 — S12

**A12.1 [derived] Killed as stated: linear regret with no delegation ever triggered.** Take $\Omega=\{\omega^*,\omega'\}$, two actions $a,b$, both $\lambda$-reversible under both hypotheses (no traps), $a$ optimal under $\omega^*$ and $b$ under $\omega'$ with gap $g>0$ per step, $P_0(\omega')=p>0$. Harm is unobserved (S12's own obstacle (i)); the delegation rule fires only when the intended action is not $\lambda$-reversible — never. So the agent receives no value-relevant evidence, its posterior never moves, posterior sampling plays $b$ with probability $p$ every episode, and Bayesian regret is $p\,g$ per step: linear (`checks.py` §6). The advisor is $\omega^*$-sane and would take $a$ w.p. $>\epsilon$, but is never asked. Kosoy's bound does not have this failure because (i) her non-delegating episodes observe rewards (Prop. 5) and (ii) her line-14 case delegates *to learn* when no action is known to be advisor-taken. S12 dropped both. **A12.2 [derived] The salvage is IRL with a delegation action, and its obstacle is named in the library.** Add "delegate when the posterior over $\omega$ disagrees about the optimal action" (an exploration trigger). Then each delegation reveals an advisor action, and learning $\omega^*$ from advisor actions is inverse reinforcement learning — Kosoy's Theorem 1 already allows hypotheses differing in the advisor policy $\upsilon^k$, but with a *shared known* $R$, so nothing in it bears on unknown value. The IRL-side obstacle is identifiability under human irrationality and model misspecification (`milli-2017-should-robots-be-obedient` Theorems 5 and §Model Misspecification; `carey-2018-incorrigibility-in-the-cirl-framework` passim; run 1 `cirl`). S12 should be re-stated as "delegative IRL: regret bound conditional on identifiability of $\omega^*$ from a value-sane advisor's actions", and is then a known-hard problem rather than a new conjecture. **A12.3 [checked] "the expected number of delegations is bounded"** inherits A9.3(i): bounded for fixed $\gamma$, growing as $(1-\gamma)^{-1/4}$.
