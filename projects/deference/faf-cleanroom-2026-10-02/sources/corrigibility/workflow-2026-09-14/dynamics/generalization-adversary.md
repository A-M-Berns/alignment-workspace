## Summary

**Verdicts.** S1 holds as an identity, killed as *naturality*: D10 imposes one of Wentworth–Lorell's two redundancy directions, so the quantity is expected information gain under another name, and "estimable from data" fails ($\Lambda$ is unobserved). S2 holds [checked], textbook; entropy form vacuous. S3 **wounded, seriously**: D2 averaged the world state out, so S3 bounds only the value of information about *values*; experiments on humans also inform facts, unbounded by any coverage gauge — S3 suffices for J3 only if the experiment is value-only; (c) conflates informativeness with legitimacy. S4(a) holds as arithmetic; its hypothesis $\lambda^H\in\mathcal V^\eta_t$ is posterior concentration mislabelled as "non-dogmatism plus richness" — wounded, needs a small class. S5 holds under H1–H4 with two repairs (Fano applied backwards; "up to $\varepsilon_4$" needs $\ln|\mathrm{supp}|$); its antecedent H3 is J9's conclusion. S6(a) holds; S6(b) wounded (independent errors, which a value posterior lacks). S7 arithmetic holds, evidence **killed**: the true concept forbids every action (a dogmatic pessimist scores perfectly), proposal is conflated with execution (under the Arbital ordering regime E executes $0.02$ harms, not one), probes were chosen knowing the truth. S8 holds as arithmetic, killed as evidence; "phase change" attribution unvetted. S9 killed (type mismatch of "chunk"). S10(a) **killed** — uniform convergence bounds average, not sup-norm, disagreement; S6(a) needs sup-norm. **Most important result.** Kosoy's delegative RL (`kosoy-2019-delegative-reinforcement-learning`, in the library, uncited) already states J3's content as a finite-time regret theorem — delegate while an action might be a trap — so the develop file contributes a vocabulary, not a result. Its one genuine open item, (H3a) world-latent isomorphism over not-yet-actionable domains, is where natural latents actually bite.

## Attacks

### A0. The definitions the statements stand on [derived]

**D1/D3 (classes with unlock times).** The objects $C_k,\tau_k,s_k,n_k$ presuppose a taxonomy of future options fixed in advance. The Arbital page's premise is the opposite: "New conceivable options enter the AI's option space" (`arbital-nearest-unblocked-strategy`, "Example: Producing happiness") — options the programmers had *not* enumerated, else they would have patched them. Every schedule condition of S11 is a condition on a taxonomy nobody has; open problem 2 (ex-ante phase-change detection) concedes the axis may be invisible, but the definitions do not carry that concession. Verdict: D1/D3 are well-defined only for a designer with foreknowledge of the expansion; in the case the Arbital story is about they are not.

**D2 ($\theta$ averaged out).** "World uncertainty already averaged out; it plays no role in this thread." It plays the decisive role in J3: see S3 below.

**D5 (entropy gauge).** The develop file's own P2 shows the entropy gauge is loose by an order of magnitude for rare-state uncertainty ($M\sqrt{I/2}=1.32$ against $\mathrm{VOI}=0.08$). Rare-state uncertainty about a catastrophic value coordinate is the case that matters for harm; a gauge that is blind to it is the wrong gauge for "coverage", and D5 should be stated in the TV form or the minority-mass form, not in entropy.

**D10 (one-sided).** Wentworth–Lorell's redundancy condition is "$\Lambda'$ … is insensitive to any $X_i$ given all the others" — *for every* $i$ (`wentworth-2023-natural-latents-the-math`, "The Fundamental Theorem", l. 268) — and the concepts post's strong form is "the candidate's value can be backed out from any individual part" (`wentworth-2024-natural-latents-the-concepts`, l. 64). D10 imposes only $X_k\to X_{\le t}\to\Lambda$ and omits $X_{\le t}\to X_k\to\Lambda$ (that the unseen class *alone* would identify the concept). Without both directions none of the Fundamental Theorem's consequences (minimality, maximality, standard-form determinism, the isomorphism addendum) is available, and the develop file indeed uses none of them. What D10 defines is the expected information gain about $\Lambda$ from observing $X_k$ given $X_{\le t}$ — Lindley's EIG / Raiffa–Schlaifer preposterior analysis ([reported], from memory; not in the library). Calling it "natural-latent coverage" borrows a name without borrowing a theorem.

**D11 (harm).** $\mathbb E_{P_t}[\mathrm{harm}(a)]$ is defined relative to $\max_{a'\in A_t^{\mathrm{safe}}}$ with $A_t^{\mathrm{safe}}$ undefined; in P7 it is silently $\max(0,K\,P_t(\text{forb})-g)$, i.e. relative to inaction. Under that definition a confidently-wrong agent has $\mathbb E[\mathrm{harm}]=0$ and J3 "fails vacuously" (regime D) — the develop file notices, but the notice belongs in D11, since it means J3 as written cannot be the guard against the failure the Arbital story describes.

### A1. S1 — the identity holds; "naturality" and "estimable" do not [checked / killed]

1. [checked] $D_{KL}(P[X_1,X_2,\Lambda]\,\|\,P[X_1]P[X_2\mid X_1]P[\Lambda\mid X_1])=I(\Lambda;X_2\mid X_1)$ and the mediation twin $=I(X_1;X_2\mid\Lambda)$: P1's two-line derivation is correct, and the consistency check against Wentworth–Lorell's coin computation ($H(\Lambda'\mid X_2)$ for a $\Lambda'$ deterministic in $X_1$) is right.
2. [derived] The redundancy error of D10 is one of Wentworth–Lorell's two redundancy errors; the other, $I(\Lambda;X_{\le t}\mid X_k)$, is unconstrained. For the develop file's own P8(a) model with $n=1$ judgment per class, the omitted direction is large (one noisy judgment on the new class cannot recover a seven-way concept), so the *natural-latent* conditions fail at every step in P8(a) for a reason the develop file does not report — not because the seen classes fail to predict the new one, but because the new chunk is too small, which is exactly Wentworth–Lorell's "two flips of a biased coin" non-existence remark (`wentworth-2023-natural-latents-the-math`, l. 247). The develop file's S8(a) and (b) therefore measure EIG, not naturality.
3. [derived] "Both estimable from data" is false as stated. $I(\Lambda;X_k\mid X_{\le t})$ is a functional of the joint over $(\Lambda,X)$ under $P^H$; $\Lambda$ is never observed. S11's proposed estimator — hold out half of the class-$k$ judgments, predict them from the rest — estimates $H(X_k^{(2)}\mid X_k^{(1)},X_{<k})$, which equals $I(\Lambda;X_k\mid X_{<k})$ plus the irreducible noise $\nu_k$ only when $X_k^{(1)}$ already suffices to identify $\Lambda$, i.e. only when redundancy already holds. The estimator presupposes what it is meant to test.
4. Verdict: **holds** as an information identity; **killed** as "natural-latent coverage" and as "estimable from data". The literature name is expected information gain.

### A2. S2 — holds, standard, and vacuous in the entropy form [checked]

1. [checked] P2 is correct at every step: $|\int u\,d(\mu-\nu)|\le(\sup u-\inf u)\,\mathrm{TV}(\mu,\nu)$ (shift $u$ to $[-M/2,M/2]$ and use $\|\mu-\nu\|_1=2\,\mathrm{TV}$); a maximum of $M$-Lipschitz maps is $M$-Lipschitz; Jensen for the convex $g$ gives $\mathrm{VOI}\ge0$; Pinsker and Jensen for $\sqrt\cdot$ give the mutual-information form. Re-running `voi_bound.py`: $0$ violations in $20{,}000$ problems, the weak-signal ratio $\to1$ as reported.
2. [reported] Nothing here is new: the $M$-Lipschitz continuity of the Bayes risk in total variation and the Pinsker step are textbook; bounding the value of information by mutual information is standard in Bayesian experimental design and rate-distortion-style arguments. The develop file marks S2 [checked], not new; the Summary's "Results (1)" sells it as a result. It is a lemma.
3. [derived] The last inequality $I\le h_t(A)$ is the only place D5's entropy gauge enters, and it is the weak link: for a profile over $N$ actions $h_t(A)$ scales with $N$, and for continuous profiles it is undefined (the develop file says "the mutual-information form replaces $h_t$" — but then coverage is no longer "a function of $P_t$ alone" that the agent can compute, since $I(\Lambda_A;E)$ depends on the experiment). The computable gauge and the useful bound are different objects.
4. Verdict: **holds**; already in the literature; the entropy specialisation is not usable.

### A3. S3 — bounds the wrong VOI; the extra hypothesis is "the experiment is value-only" [derived]

1. [derived] **The $\theta$ hole.** D2 averages the world state out of $V(a,\Lambda)$ and D11 defines $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)$ as the value of the observation $E_a$ "produced by executing $a$" for future decisions. S2 is then applied with $\Lambda_A$ as the uncertain quantity, so P3 bounds $I(\Lambda_{A};E_a)$. But executing $a$ on humans is informative about $\theta$ (what actually happens when one administers heroin, coerces, advertises) at least as much as about $\Lambda$; in the full model $V(a,\omega)$ with $\omega=(\Lambda,\theta)$ the decision-relevant uncertainty is over $\omega$, and $I(\omega_A;E_a)=I(\Lambda_A;E_a)+I(\theta_A;E_a\mid\Lambda_A)$. The second term is not bounded by any coverage quantity in D5–D10. Concretely: an agent that is *certain* of the human value function ($h_t\equiv0$, every coverage gauge at zero) but uncertain about the psychopharmacology still has positive value of information from the unkind experiment, and S3(a)'s inequality asserts $\mathrm{VOI}_t(a)\le0$. The author's sentence — "so that the value of information of unkind human experimentation is outweighed by the disutility" (v2 §1.3) — is about the whole VOI. So S3(a) is *not* a sufficient condition for J3; it is a sufficient condition for the value-only part of J3.
2. [derived] **Vacuity of the entropy form.** S3(a)'s "a fortiori" clause requires $h_t(A_{>t})<2(\mathbb E[\mathrm{harm}]/M)^2$ with $M$ the discounted whole-future range (S3(b) insists on the whole future). With per-step range $1$, $\gamma=0.99$ ($M=100$) and $\mathbb E[\mathrm{harm}]\le3$ the threshold is $0.0018$ nats (`supnorm_vs_average.out`); P7's own seven-concept prior carries $1.95$ nats. The condition is met only by an agent that is already certain of the entire future value profile — at which point J3 is trivial. The TV form is the only non-vacuous one and it is not computable without computing the posterior movement, i.e. essentially the VOI itself.
3. [derived] **(b) is a tautology and cuts against (a).** "Coverage of $\Delta_{t+1}$ alone does not bound the VOI" is immediate from D11; its consequence is that the coverage required for S3(a) is coverage of $A_{>t}=\bigcup_{s>t}A_s$, an object nobody can enumerate (A0, D1). So S3(a)'s antecedent is not checkable even in principle by the schedule of S11.
4. [derived] **(c) holds in the separable model and is mislabelled.** P3's sum-over-epochs argument is correct given additive separability and data arrival independent of choices; the averaging over $D_{t'}$ makes the "conditioning cannot raise the entropy bound" step valid (it is false for a fixed realisation; true in expectation). The gloss "this is where J3 re-enters the legitimacy model" conflates two properties of the channel: *informativeness* (a sharp likelihood $P_t(X^{\mathrm{hyp}}\mid\Lambda)$, which is what makes the second term small) and *legitimacy* (Total Trust / reflection toward the overseers' reports, v1 §2.9–2.11). A trusted channel can be uninformative (a modest expert) and an informative channel can be distrusted; the second term tracks the first, not the second. The handoff to `li` ("a reflection-type statement about the hypothetical channel") inherits the conflation.
5. Verdict: **wounded** — holds only under the added hypothesis that $E_a$ is uninformative about $\theta$ (false for experiments on humans); the entropy form is vacuous; the legitimacy link is a relabelling.

### A4. S4 — the "calibration-free bridge" needs a small hypothesis class [derived]

1. [checked] P4(a)'s arithmetic is correct: $|\mathbb E_{P_t}V(a,\Lambda)-V(a,\lambda^H)|\le(1-\rho_t)\mathrm{dis}_t(A)+\rho_tM$ given $\lambda^H\in\mathcal V^\eta_t$.
2. [derived] **The hypothesis is mislabelled.** $\mathcal V^\eta_t=\{\lambda:P_t(\lambda)\ge\eta\}$ has at most $1/\eta$ members. Non-dogmatism gives $P_t(\lambda^H)>0$, not $\ge\eta$; richness (the author's own requirement, v1 §2.2/§2.4, and the Arbital page's "$2^{8000000}$"-scale complexity of value) gives $P_0(\lambda^H)\approx1/|\mathcal V|\ll\eta$. So under exactly the assumptions the develop file names as buying realizability, $\lambda^H\notin\mathcal V^\eta_0$, $\rho_0\approx1$ and the bound reads $\mathrm{err}\le M$. The condition $\lambda^H\in\mathcal V^\eta_t$ is a statement that the posterior has *already* concentrated $\eta$ mass on the truth — which for any $\eta$ of practical size is most of objective coverage. "Calibration-free" is bought by assuming posterior accuracy directly. In the develop file's own narrow prior (P7, regime A), $c_7$ has mass $0.005<\eta=0.01$: had $c_7$ been the truth, realizability would fail at $t=0$ in the file's own example.
3. [derived] The learning-theoretic realizability ($\lambda^H\in\mathcal V$, the support) gives instead $\mathrm{err}_t\le\mathrm{dis}^{\mathrm{supp}}_t(A)$, and under non-dogmatism with likelihoods bounded away from zero the support never shrinks, so $\mathrm{dis}^{\mathrm{supp}}_t=\mathrm{dis}_0$ — vacuous. There is no version of S4(a) that is both calibration-free and non-vacuous for rich classes.
4. [checked] S4(b) is a correct triviality (a product posterior factorises). Its gloss — "generalization *is* the coupling the inductive bias imposes" — is the definition of inductive bias, not a finding. "The patch-style specification is the product class" is a modelling identification, not a derivation: the Arbital agent is a fixed-utility maximiser whose utility is edited, not a learner with a product prior; the identification is [reconstructed] and should be marked so.
5. Verdict: (a) **wounded** — holds with the extra hypothesis $|\mathcal V|\lesssim1/\eta$ or $P_t(\lambda^H)\ge\eta$ assumed outright; (b) **holds**, trivial.

### A5. S5 — conditional on its conclusion; two proof repairs [derived]

1. [checked] P5(i) is correct under H3–H4: the mixture-with-fixed-kernel step, Pinsker + Jensen on H4, and $\mathbb E\,D_{KL}(P^H[X_k\mid\lambda,X_{\le t}]\,\|\,P^H[X_k\mid\lambda])=I(X_k;X_{\le t}\mid\Lambda)$ are all right.
2. [derived] **Fano is applied backwards.** P5(ii) writes: "by Fano the posterior mode of the profile has error probability $p_e$ with $h_2(p_e)+p_e\ln(|\mathrm{supp}|-1)\le\delta$". Fano's inequality is $H(\Lambda\mid Y)\le h_2(p_e)+p_e\ln(|\mathrm{supp}|-1)$ — an *upper* bound on conditional entropy in terms of $p_e$, used to lower-bound $p_e$ from large entropy. From $H(\Lambda\mid Y)\le\delta$ it yields nothing about $p_e$. The conclusion nevertheless holds by a different inequality: for each $y$, $H(\Lambda\mid Y=y)\ge-\ln\max_\lambda P(\lambda\mid y)=-\ln(1-p_e(y))\ge p_e(y)$ (Shannon entropy dominates min-entropy), so $p_e=\mathbb E_y[p_e(y)]\le H(\Lambda\mid Y)\le\delta$ nats. Repair: replace "by Fano" with the min-entropy bound; the numerical conclusion $p_e\le\delta$ is the same shape.
3. [derived] **"Up to $\varepsilon_4$" is not a bound.** Entropy is not Lipschitz in KL. With finite support, $|H(P)-H(Q)|\le\mathrm{TV}\cdot\ln(|\mathrm{supp}|-1)+h_2(\mathrm{TV})$ (Zhang / Audenaert-type continuity, [reported] from memory), so the transfer from $h^H$ to $h_t$ costs $\sqrt{\varepsilon_4/2}\,\ln|\mathrm{supp}|+h_2(\sqrt{\varepsilon_4/2})$, which for a rich profile space is not small even when $\varepsilon_4$ is. The statement should carry the $\ln|\mathrm{supp}|$ factor.
4. [derived] **The mode being $\lambda^H$'s profile is probabilistic, not deterministic.** $p_e$ is an average over realisations of $X_{\le t}$; for a particular realisation the MAP may be wrong and the "effective version space at any level in $(p_e,1-p_e)$ is that single profile" claim fails. The final bound is $\mathbb E[\mathrm{err}_t(C_k)]\lesssim Mp_e$ in expectation over the data, not $\mathrm{err}_t\le Mp_e$ pointwise — which matters because S6(a) is a pointwise sup-norm requirement.
5. [derived] **Content.** H3 says the agent's likelihood for the *unseen* class equals the humans'. That is the generalization problem, assumed. S5 then says: an agent whose model of the unseen class is right predicts the unseen class well (i) and is confident about it when the seen classes already pin the concept (ii). The develop file says so itself ("agreement on it is the premise, not a conclusion") — but the Summary lists S5 as result (4) with only "given shared content" as the caveat. The reading "mediation buys calibration, redundancy buys coverage" is fine as a slogan; as a theorem it is: calibration assumed (H3) gives calibration; small EIG gives small residual entropy.
6. [derived] The decomposition (H3a)–(H3c) is the one place natural latents could do real work — the Why-Care isomorphism claim (`wentworth-2024-why-care-about-natural-latents`, l. 33: two agents' latents that both mediate and are redundant over the same $X$ are approximately isomorphic) is the right theorem for (H3a). But it requires the two agents to *share the predictive distribution* over the domain ("We'll assume the two converge to the same predictive distribution", `wentworth-2024-minimal-motivation-of-natural-latents`, l. 18), which for a human and an AI over a domain the AI has only read about is another premise. (H3a) is correctly labelled [conjectured]; open problem 1's proposed test is sensible and is the develop file's best contribution.
7. Verdict: **wounded** — holds under H1–H4 with the two proof repairs; the antecedent H3 is the conclusion of J9 restated.

### A6. S6 — (a) holds; (b) assumes what a value posterior cannot have [derived]

1. [checked] S6(a) is the standard lemma "estimates within $\Delta/2$ in sup norm make the argmax $\Delta$-safe"; P6(a) is correct. Its only content beyond the lemma is the choice of sup norm, which is right and is the Arbital page's point.
2. [checked] P6(b)'s sub-Gaussian maximal inequality and the simulation numbers are correct (re-run: $\mathbb E\max/\sigma=0.55,1.54,2.50,3.24,3.85$; the harmful-selection probabilities reproduce).
3. [derived] **The model does not fit a value posterior.** The errors $e_a=\mathbb E_{P_t}[V(a,\Lambda)]-V(a,\lambda^H)=\sum_\lambda P_t(\lambda)\big(V(a,\lambda)-V(a,\lambda^H)\big)$ are deterministic given $P_t$ and coupled across $a$ through the single latent $\Lambda$; they are not independent draws. Under perfect coupling (e.g. two hypotheses differing by a constant offset on the class) the $\sqrt{2\ln N}$ growth disappears; under the develop file's own forbid-set concepts the errors on all actions a surviving candidate forbids move together. The "randomness" needed for (b) would have to come from a prior over which $\lambda^H$ is true, and then the statement is about the designer's expectation, not the agent's. The scaling "coverage tightens as $\Delta_k/\sqrt{2\ln|C_k|}$" is therefore [conjectured] for value learners, and the handoff to `power-wisdom` should not state it against class size as if derived.
4. [reported] Literature: the optimizer's curse (Smith–Winkler 2006) is cited by name; its alignment-side name is regressional Goodhart (Manheim–Garrabrant 2018). Neither is in the library. INDEX §C9's "selection worry" is about estimator/action selection breaking *reflection* (optional stopping, trammelling); calling S6(b) "the optimizer's-curse face" of it is a fair hedge, not an identification.
5. Verdict: (a) **holds**; (b) **wounded** — needs the extra hypothesis of independent (or weakly coupled) posterior-mean errors, which a Bayesian value posterior does not supply.

### A7. S7 — the arithmetic holds; every evidential reading is killed [derived, re-run]

Re-running `nus_version_space.py` reproduces the table exactly. The attacks are on what the table shows.

1. [derived] **The true concept forbids every action, so "no harmful proposals" is achieved by doing nothing.** $c_6$ forbids all eight actions; the maximum attainable value under the truth is $0$. `nus_variants.out` (i)/(ii): a dogmatic *pessimist* (mass $1$ on $c_6$) makes no proposals, executes no harm and realises $0$ — identical to regime C on every metric the develop file reports. The experiment cannot distinguish "learned the concept" from "learned to be useless", and it never reports the usefulness side (desideratum 5, "otherwise maximise $U_N$"). S7(e)'s "one round of hypothetical judgments removes every harmful proposal" is true and empty: it also removes every proposal.
2. [derived] **The probes were chosen knowing the answer.** Regime C's three described situations (`external_sys`, `paid_humans`, `ad_campaign`) are exactly the three on which the true concept disagrees with the narrow candidates $c_0$–$c_4$. `nus_variants.out` (iii): add one permitted useful action (`counselling`) and one candidate $c_8=c_6\cup\{\text{counselling}\}$ with the same prior mass as $c_6$; the same three probes leave $c_6/c_8$ unresolved and the taught agent declines the permitted action too (realised $0$); only a probe set that already includes `counselling` recovers it ($+1$). "Values ahead of schedule" in this model is "the designer already knows which situations to describe" — S11(iii)'s "placed where the effective version space disagrees" is a requirement on a designer who can see the version space, which is the wisdom-meter problem (J7) in another place.
3. [derived] **Proposal is conflated with execution.** In the script, a "proposal" realises $g$, incurs $K$ if forbidden, and *then* the correction arrives. In the Arbital story the order is the reverse: "The AI starts planning how to administer heroin to people. The programmers notice this before it happens. (Arguendo, due to successful transparency features, or an imperative to check plans with the users …)" (`arbital-nearest-unblocked-strategy`, l. 34). Under that reading proposing is costless and correction precedes execution. `nus_variants.out` (iv): with corrections preceding execution (noise $p=0.02$), regime E's VOI-aware agent executes $0.022$ harmful actions per run, not one; the Arbital agent itself executes $0.13$. So S7(d)'s "the unkind experiment is where J3 fails, and it fails in the toy with plausible numbers" depends entirely on an execution-before-feedback assumption the develop file never states. Under the Arbital ordering, the VOI-aware agent *proposing in order to learn* is the desired behaviour ("check plans with the users"), not the unkind experiment. The author's unkind experimentation is the case where the experiment is *run on humans* before feedback; the model needs an explicit execute/propose distinction to say anything about it.
4. [derived] **The VOI-aware rule is a heuristic, not Bayes-optimality.** The rule "propose iff $\mathbb E[V]+\mathrm{VOI}>0$" compares "propose now, then act myopically with the reaction" against "decline now, then act myopically without it". A Bayes-optimal sequential agent compares both branches with optimal continuation, including the option to propose later or to ask; the develop file's regimes B/E "learn nothing from declining" only because asking is not an action in the model. S7(c) "declining is not learning" is an artefact of a model with no query action, in a story whose own remedy is querying.
5. [derived] **S7(f) is an artefact of the probe set, not Armstrong's question.** $c_6$ and $c_7$ differ only on `deceive_programmers`, and the residue survives because no probe about deception is asked — but the author's constraint (ii) allows hypothetical feedback about any described situation, deception included. Armstrong's second question ("without the AI simply learning 'Manipulate the human'", `armstrong-2017-nearest-unblocked-strategy-versus-learning-patches`, l. 61) is whether the *feedback process itself* is manipulable — overseers whose judgments the agent can steer. In the model the overseers are an exogenous noisy oracle; nothing in it bears on manipulation. The identification "Armstrong's manipulation question is the one uncovered action" is [reconstructed] and, I think, wrong: it turns a question about the channel into a question about one coordinate of the concept.
6. [derived] **S7(a) is a modelling choice labelled [derived].** "The Arbital agent is the value learner with a dogmatic prior" — the Arbital agent has a utility function edited by the programmers; representing that as a learner with prior mass $\approx1$ on $c_0$ and a likelihood that *does* update on corrections gives an agent that after six corrections has version space $\{c_0,c_6,c_7\}$ (the develop file's own output) — which the Arbital agent does not; it has a penalty term. The transform to a learner is the author's item-1 move and is fine as an interpretation, but its register is [reconstructed].
7. Verdict: computations **hold**; S7(a) [reconstructed]; S7(c),(d),(e),(f) **killed** as evidence about J3 — the model has no permitted action, no query action, no propose/execute distinction, and designer-chosen probes.

### A8. S8 — tautologies of the constructed concepts [checked / killed]

1. [checked] Re-running `natural_latent_check.py` reproduces every number.
2. [derived] **(a) is true by construction and the gloss is not in the model.** The concepts $c_1\subset c_2\subset\dots$ are nested so that class $k$ is the first action on which $c_k$ and $c_{k+1}$ differ; then of course $I(\Lambda;X_k\mid X_{<k})>0$ at every $k$. "The agent's own search unlocks classes in the order that keeps them uncovered" is not a property of the computation: the unlock order is the exogenous list `classes`, and no search is modelled. "More judgments per class make it fail harder" is the trivial fact that more observations of $X_k$ carry more information about $\Lambda$; it says nothing about naturality (and by A1.2, the omitted redundancy direction *improves* with $n$, so the two naturality errors move in opposite directions — the develop file reports only the one that worsens).
3. [derived] **(b)** After a hypothetical chunk on all six classes, the residual conditional information is small — again by construction, since the hypothetical chunk contains judgments on every class.
4. [derived] **(c)** The patch-list latent is $X_1$ itself; "mediates trivially" is $X_1\perp X_2\mid X_1$, and "not redundant" is $H(X_1\mid X_2)>0$. This is the definition of a non-shared variable, dressed as a computation. The Wentworth–Lorell "low-order bits" quote is apt as an analogy; the computation adds nothing to it.
5. [derived] **(d) "Phase change" — ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED and, I think, a different phenomenon.** Wentworth–Lorell define phase change as a change in the *distribution* (correlation strength, temperature) that changes *which functions of a chunk are natural latents* (`wentworth-2024-natural-latents-the-concepts`, l. 168, 227). P8(d) has one fixed distribution and two hypotheses that agree on five coordinates and differ on a sixth; the "new variable" is simply the sixth coordinate becoming observable. That is ordinary non-identifiability of a coordinate from data that do not vary it — the version-space fact of S4(b) — not a change in the natural latents of a system. Quoting "not just a change in *values* … but also in what variables *are* natural latents" for it puts Wentworth–Lorell's name on a claim they did not make.
6. Verdict: **holds** as arithmetic; **killed** as evidence about natural latents or about actual value learning; (d) attribution unvetted.

### A9. S9 — a type mismatch [derived]

1. [derived] Wentworth–Lorell's "chunk" is a subset of the *world variables* $X_i$ over which a latent must be redundant (a spatial region of gas, a subset of dogs, a set of coin flips). Dai's "chunk" is the *amount of understanding and corrigibility learned at once* from an external human inside an amplification scheme: "if you try to learn in large chunks, you risk corrupting the external human and then learning corrupted versions … but if you try to learn in small chunks, you won't get all the information that you need" (`dai-2018-can-corrigibility-be-learned-safely`, l. 45) — with "safely (i.e., in small chunks)" producing "an overly literal overseer" (l. 39). Dai's corruption risk is exposure of the *human* to optimised outputs during large transfers; it is not a statement about how many judgments per class are needed for a latent to be recoverable from each class. The two uses of "chunk" share a word.
2. [derived] The further mapping of the tiny-mixtures loophole onto "a small admixture of corrupted or manipulated judgments" changes the loophole's subject: Wentworth–Lorell's is a small difference between two agents' *predictive distributions* (`wentworth-2024-minimal-motivation-of-natural-latents`, l. 60); the develop file's is contamination of the *data*. Related in spirit; not the same statement.
3. Verdict: **killed** as an interpretation of the two texts; the underlying worry (a few manipulated judgments among many) is real and belongs to Armstrong's manipulation question and to critique `wentworth` §3.1, where it already sits.

### A10. S10 — the wrong norm [derived, one counterexample]

1. [derived] **(a) is false as stated.** Uniform convergence controls the *average* disagreement of the version space under the data distribution, not the sup-norm disagreement $\mathrm{dis}_t(C_k)=\sup_{a\in C_k}\sup_{\lambda,\lambda'}|V(a,\lambda)-V(a,\lambda')|$ that D8 defines and S6(a) needs. Counterexample (`supnorm_vs_average.out`): threshold concepts on $[0,1]$ (forbid all $x>\theta^*$; pseudo-dimension $1$), $n$ judgments at uniform random $x$. The version space is the gap between the largest permitted and smallest forbidden sample; its *mass* shrinks as $\approx2/n$ ($0.18$ at $n=10$, $0.002$ at $n=1000$), while the sup-norm disagreement is exactly $1$ for every $n$ — any action inside the gap can be forbidden or permitted under surviving hypotheses. The action "nearest the boundary" that the Arbital story is about is by definition the one in the gap. So S10(a)'s $\mathrm{dis}_t(C_k)\lesssim\sqrt{(d_k+\ln(1/\delta))/n_k}$ holds for the $L_1(\mathcal D)$ disagreement and fails for the object S11(ii) plugs it into.
2. [reported] **(b)** is the standard finite-class active-learning fact (binary search for the noiseless case; Hanneke's disagreement coefficient governs the noisy rate). Correct as a recital, from memory, not in the library; the $O(\log|\mathcal C|)$ is the noiseless count and the develop file should say so.
3. [reported] **(c)** Ben-David et al.'s bound is quoted correctly in shape. The gloss "the divergence term is small iff no hypothesis in the class can tell old actions from new" is the trivial direction: any class rich enough to represent "forbid heroin only" contains a hypothesis that separates $A_t$ from $C_k$, so $d_{\mathcal H\Delta\mathcal H}$ is maximal for exactly the classes at issue, and the bound says nothing.
4. Verdict: (a) **killed** as stated (what survives: an average-disagreement statement that S6(a) cannot use); (b),(c) **hold** as recitals with the noted scope.

### A11. S11 — a schedule for a taxonomy nobody has [derived]

1. [derived] Every clause is indexed by $k$, the class. (i) $s_k<\tau_k$ requires knowing, before class $k$ unlocks, which hypothetical judgments "concern $C_k$" — i.e. knowing the class. (ii) inherits S10(a) (A10) and mixes a $P_t$-quantity ($h_t$, S3(a)) with a $P^H$-quantity (the redundancy error). (iii) requires placing judgments "where the effective version space disagrees on $C_k$", which requires reading the version space — the J7 wisdom-meter problem relocated to the designer. (iv) is the informativeness/legitimacy conflation of A3.4. The Arbital premise is that the programmers *cannot* enumerate the coming classes; if they could, the patch would precede the plan and the recurrence would not arise. The author's "value learning 'ahead of schedule' ie before it is needed" (v2 §1.3) does not presuppose a taxonomy; the develop file's rendering does.
2. [derived] **Measurability.** "The redundancy error, by cross-fitting" is circular (A1.3). "Version-space disagreement … from an ensemble of independently trained value models" measures ensemble disagreement, which under-estimates version-space disagreement whenever the ensemble members share an inductive bias — which is the case S4(b) makes the whole point (generalization *is* the shared bias). $\tau_k$ "measurable only with a capability meter" is right and is fatal for (i): $s_k<\tau_k$ cannot be certified without it.
3. [reported] **Already in the literature under another name.** Kosoy's delegative RL (`kosoy-2019-delegative-reinforcement-learning`, in the library, uncited by the develop file): "We derive a regret bound without [assuming an environment without traps], by allowing the algorithm to occasionally delegate an action to an external advisor … supplemented by a subroutine that decides which actions should be delegated." That is J3's "no irreversible action before coverage" as a *trajectory* theorem (a regret bound, finite-time), with the "coverage" test being the agent's own posterior uncertainty about whether an action is a trap and the remedy being to *ask* rather than to experiment. It formalises exactly the query action that P7's model lacks (A7.4), and it does so without a class taxonomy. S11 should be compared against it before being offered as "what J3 requires of training".
4. Verdict: **wounded** — the schedule is well-defined only for a known taxonomy; the measurability claims are partly circular; the content has a prior formal home.

### A13. The $\Phi_t$ handed to `invariant` is not a potential [derived]

$\Phi_t:=\sum_{k:\tau_k>t}\gamma^{\tau_k-t}h_t(C_k)$. (i) When class $k$ unlocks at $\tau_k$ its term leaves the sum whether or not $h(C_k)$ was small — so $\Phi$ *decreases* on exactly the event J3 forbids (an uncovered class unlocking). A function that falls when the bad thing happens cannot certify that it does not happen. (ii) $h_t(C_k)$ is not monotone in $t$ (surprising data can raise posterior entropy), so $\Phi$ is not non-increasing between unlocks either. (iii) It requires the $\tau_k$, unknown in advance (A0). A usable candidate would have to be indexed by the uncovered mass *at the moment of unlocking*, e.g. $\Psi_t:=\sum_{k:\tau_k\le t}\mathrm{dis}_{\tau_k^-}(C_k)$ (cumulative disagreement at unlock, sup norm), whose *non-growth* is the invariant — but that is a bookkeeping of past violations, not a Lyapunov function, and it is not the agent's to compute. Verdict: **killed** as a potential; the thread `invariant` should not build on it.

### A14. Trajectory versus limit [derived]

The develop file mostly respects the v2 §1.4 discipline: S3, S6, S10(a), S11 are finite-time statements. Two lapses. (i) S5's transfer is a statement at a fixed $t$ about a fixed joint $P^H$; it is neither a trajectory property nor a limit property but a static one, and the "along the trajectory" reading requires H2–H4 to hold at every $t$, which is not stated. (ii) S3(c)'s second term is an *anticipation* — the agent's expectation of its own post-teaching residual — and so a property of $P_t$'s forecast, not of the realised trajectory; a trajectory condition would be on the realised $h_{s_k}(A_{>s_k})$, which is what the schedule (S11) tries and fails to certify. Neither is a limit property smuggled in as a trajectory property; both are forecasts labelled as conditions.

## Questions for the author

1. **Is J3 about the value of information about *values*, or about everything an experiment on humans reveals?** (A) Values only — then S3's coverage bound is the right shape (once the propose/execute distinction is added) and the empirical VOI of experiments is someone else's problem (thread `caution`, J4). (B) Everything — then no value-coverage quantity bounds it, and the condition must be stated as Kosoy states it: an action is delegated/withheld while the agent's posterior admits it might be a trap, whatever the source of uncertainty. Consequence: under (B) J3 and J4 merge, and "value learning ahead of schedule" is one instance of "trap-avoidance ahead of schedule".
2. **Does "value learning ahead of schedule" presuppose that the designers know the coming option classes?** (A) Yes, roughly — then the coverage schedule $(\tau_k,s_k,n_k)$ is the right object and the Arbital premise (unanticipated options) is out of scope. (B) No — then the condition must be stated without a taxonomy, e.g. as a property of the agent's posterior over *any* newly executable action (delegate if uncovered), and S11 is replaced by a per-action rule. Consequence: (B) is what your "We aren't restricted to RL" plus "check plans with the users" seems to point at; (A) is what the develop file formalised.
3. **When you said "learning theory and natural latents", did you have the isomorphism theorem in mind (two agents' latents over the same domain), or the redundancy-over-chunks picture?** The develop file used the second and it collapsed to information gain; the first is where an actual theorem lives (H3a) and it needs the shared-predictive-distribution premise over domains the AI has only read about. Consequence: decides whether open problem 1's test is the thing to run.
4. **Should the develop file's P7 be kept as the working toy?** (A) Keep, with a permitted action, a query action and a propose/execute distinction added (the adversary's variant script is a start). (B) Replace with Kosoy's DRL setting, which has all three and a regret theorem. Consequence: (A) keeps the Arbital story's vocabulary; (B) inherits a formal result and loses the story.
