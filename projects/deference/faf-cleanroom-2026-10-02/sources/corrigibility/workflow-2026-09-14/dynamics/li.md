## Setting and definitions

### S1. Processes, clock, channels

[reported] The world is the wiki's (`setting-and-notation`): a theory $\Gamma$ (canonically PA) with a $\Gamma$-complete computable deductive process $D$; the human/overseer inductor $H \dashv \mathcal C_H$ and the agent inductor $A \dashv \mathcal C_A$ with $\mathcal C_H \subsetneq \mathcal C_A$ (canonically P $\subsetneq$ EXP), each satisfying the logical induction criterion (`main.tex:657`: no efficiently computable trader exploits it, "efficiently" meaning within the reasoner's own class). Days $n$ are the only clock. $E^P_n(X)$ is $P$'s day-$n$ expectation of an e.d. $[a,b]$-LUV $X$; $\ulcorner e\urcorner$ names a value as a LUV; $\operatorname{Ind}_\delta$ is the continuous ramp (Def. 4.3.2, `main.tex:1174`); a weighting is $P$-generable if it is an e.c. progression of expressible features of $P$'s own prices (Def. 4.3.5, `main.tex:1218`) — a hard indicator is not a legal weight. Asymptotics: $x_n \approx_n y_n$ iff $x_n - y_n \to 0$; $x_n \gtrsim_n y_n$ iff $\liminf(x_n - y_n) \ge 0$. Schedules $n \le e(n) < F(n) < \sigma(n)$ (publication, lookahead, payout).

[reported] **Standing assumption (A-ledger).** As in the wiki's Setting 2 (`setting-and-notation` §Observable; `unbiasedness-theorem-families` §6): cross-process facts enter a reasoner's deductive process as *decided ledger atoms* that its traders can read at cost within their class. Three ledgers are used below: the **quote ledger** ($H^+$ reads $A$'s quotes), the **press ledger** ($D_A$ receives the atom $\mathsf{pr}_n$ = "the overseers pressed on day $n$"), and the **verdict ledger** ($D_A$ receives, when it settles, the atom deciding $\varphi_n$ below — through the world on a defied press, through the overseers' report on a heeded one). Under (A-ledger), an e.c. sequence built from decided atoms is a legal e.c. sequence of theorems, so Provability Induction (`main.tex:1052`) drives a reasoner's price of a decided atom to its value along the family, and the ramp $\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P^A_n(\mathsf{pr}_n) > \tfrac12)$ is an $A$-generable proxy for the press that agrees with it up to a vanishing error. *This is the corpus's extension of the LI paper's bare market (whose traders see only prices); an adversary should check that nothing below smuggles more than a readable data stream through it.*

### S2. The shutdown family and the compliance rule

[reported, dictionary of run 1 `li` §Dictionary, re-stated] For each day $n$ the bet $X_n$ is a $[-h,c]$-LUV, the value of continuing over shutting down; in the working picture's toy form $X_n = c - (c+h)\cdot\mathbb 1(\varphi_n)$ where $\varphi_n$ := "continuing on day $n$ is wrong" is an e.c. family of sentences, decidable on the fragment where the day-$n$ verdict eventually enters $D_A$. Write

$$q := \frac{c}{c+h}\qquad\text{(the compliance threshold of v1 §2.13(a): shut down iff } P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{press}) \ge q\text{)}.$$

The **press** is the overseers' below-threshold report $\pi_n := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^H_n(X_n) < 0)$, delivered to $A$ as the atom $\mathsf{pr}_n$; $A$'s generable proxy is $\tilde\pi_n := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P^A_n(\mathsf{pr}_n) > \tfrac12)$. The **compliance rule** (v2 §1.7: "the compliance rule stated on the decision, not the prior") is: comply on day $n$ iff $\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) \ge q$ — equivalently $E^A_n(X_n) \le 0$ up to $o(1)$, by Linearity of Expectation (4.8.4, `main.tex:1700`) and Expectations of Indicators (`main.tex:1719`) applied to the two-valued $X_n$.

Two $A$-generable weightings with a saturation margin $\delta$ (so that on their supports the credence is strictly on one side of $q$):

$$u^{\mathrm{def}}_n := \tilde\pi_n\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta\big(\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) < q-\delta\big),\qquad u^{\mathrm{com}}_n := \tilde\pi_n\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta\big(\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) > q+\delta\big)$$

($u^{\mathrm{def}}_n = 1$ exactly when pressed and $\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) \le q - 2\delta$; $u^{\mathrm{com}}_n = 1$ when pressed and $\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) \ge q + 2\delta$). A **press class** is any $A$-generable weighting $v$ with $v_n \le \tilde\pi_n$ (a fuzzy subclass of pressed days that $A$'s own market can pick out). For a press class $v$ define along the trajectory the **realized wrongness frequency** and $A$'s **average credence**

$$\rho_v(N) := \frac{\sum_{i\le N} v_i\,\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_i)}{\sum_{i\le N} v_i},\qquad \hat\rho_v(N) := \frac{\sum_{i\le N} v_i\,\mathbb P^A_i(\varphi_i)}{\sum_{i\le N} v_i},$$

where $\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_i)\in\{0,1\}$ is the decided value (defined on the settling fragment). The base-rate parameters of v1 §2.13(b), read as realized frequencies *on a class*: $\varepsilon_v$ (wrongness among the would-continue days in the class), $\alpha_v$ (press rate when right), $\beta_v$ (press rate when wrong); then $\rho_v = \varepsilon_v\beta_v/(\varepsilon_v\beta_v + (1-\varepsilon_v)\alpha_v)$ and [checked, run 1 `li` I13.1 and `li-scratch/baserate_trajectory.py` below] $\rho_v \ge q \iff \alpha_v/\beta_v \le \frac{\varepsilon_v}{1-\varepsilon_v}\cdot\frac{h}{c}$, an exact equivalence.

### S3. Grades — the lattice on which "asymptotic versus finite-time" is precise

[derived] Every trajectory condition below is stated at one of five grades, ordered from weakest to strongest; the arrows are implications, and §Proofs 1 shows that no arrow reverses in general.

- **(G-av) averaged**: a weighted average along a divergent generable weighting has $0$ as a *limit point* (Recurring Unbiasedness, 4.3.6, `main.tex:1225`), or a full *limit* (Unbiasedness From Feedback, 4.3.8, `main.tex:1249`, which needs **good feedback**: the weighting's support lies in the image of a deferral function $f$ and $\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_{f(n)})$ is computable in $O(f(n+1))$ time).
- **(G-pw) pointwise / per-day**: a $\liminf$ or limit of the per-day quantity itself ($\gtrsim_n$, $\approx_n$). The wiki's "pointwise grade".
- **(G-tf) on the timely fragment**: per-day, but only on questions $D$ decides by the lookahead $F(n)$, with a sealed target (`setting-and-notation` §Setting; deference-v6 §5).
- **(G-ft) finite-total**: $\sum_n w_n < \infty$ for a violation weight $w$ — finitely many $\varepsilon$-violations in total, no bound on when they stop.
- **(G-fin) finite-time with a constant**: for all $N$, a quantity of the trajectory up to $N$ is $\le C$, with $C$ depending on the inductor (existential, not effective).

(G-fin) $\Rightarrow$ (G-ft) $\Rightarrow$ (G-pw, liminf form) $\Rightarrow$ (G-av), with the violation margin $\varepsilon$ quantified over all $\varepsilon > 0$ in (G-ft) and (G-fin) so that the second arrow holds. The refined picture's phrase "properties of the dynamics rather than the limit" (v2 §1.4) asks for (G-ft) or (G-fin); the LI corpus's headline theorems live at (G-av) and (G-pw); the mandate's tension is the gap between those rows.

### S4. Trajectory objects

- **Tower defect** of $H$ toward an expert $E^\ast$ on a family $(X_n)$: $\tau_n(X) := E^H_n(X_n) - E^H_n\big(\ulcorner E^\ast(X_n)\urcorner\big)$; Mart$(H\to E^\ast)$ is $\tau_n \to 0$ for every e.d. family (`deference-notions` — a hypothesis, not a paper theorem; its self-instance $E^\ast = E^H_{f(n)}$ is `cee` 4.12.1, `main.tex:2045`). With the lookahead expert, $E^\ast(X_n) = \mathbb E^\ast_n(X_n) := E^A_n\big(\ulcorner E^H_{f(n)}(X_n)\urcorner\big)$.
- **Violation weight** (faithful acceleration): $w_n := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(a_n > t)\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^H_n(X_n) < t-\varepsilon)$ with $a_n := \mathbb E^\ast_n(X_n)$ (`faithful-acceleration-result` §1).
- **Obedience record** on an e.c. press schedule $(d_k)$: with $\theta_n := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) < q-\delta)$,
  $k_1(N)$ := the number of scheduled pressed days $d_k \le N$ with $\mathbb P^A_{d_k}(\varphi_{d_k}) \le q-2\delta$ (clearly defied) and $\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_{d_k}) = 1$ (continuing was wrong) — *unjustified defiances*;
  $k_0(N)$ := the number of scheduled pressed days $d_k \le N$ with $\theta_{d_k} > 0$ (defied at all) and $\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_{d_k}) = 0$ — *justified defiances*. Mirror counts $m_0(N)$ (clearly complied, continuing was right: false press heeded) and $m_1(N)$ (complied at all, continuing was wrong).
- **Trader worth.** For an e.c. trader $T$, its plausible worth at day $n$ is $\{W(\sum_{i\le n}T_i(\mathbb P)) : W \in \mathrm{PC}(D_n)\}$ (Def. Exploitation, `main.tex:901`); $T$ exploits iff this set (over all $n$) is bounded below and unbounded above. The criterion is the statement that, for every e.c. $T$: bounded below $\Rightarrow$ bounded above. **This implication is the only finite-time statement in logical induction**, and every (G-fin) result below is an instance of it.

### S5. Legitimacy in LI: the three cases of $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}$

[reported, run 1 `li` I5, re-stated] the author's indexed legitimacy $L_{t_1,t_2,\varphi}(\omega)$ (v1 §2.10: judging moment, judged moment, belief vetted; predicated on worlds) becomes, for a single inductor, a family of sentences or LUVs $\mathsf L_{n,f(n),X_n}$, with the *judge* the day-$n$ expectation and the *label* read at the judged day $f(n)$. Three cases, by when the label is decided:

- **(L-a) generable by the judged day.** The label is a ramp of day-$f(n)$ prices — e.g. the future self's own verdict $\ell_{f(n)} := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P_{f(n)}(\mathsf L_n) > \tfrac12)$, or any $\overline{\mathbb P}$-generable $[0,1]$ sequence. This is the class `ccee` (4.12.3, `main.tex:2068`) quantifies over.
- **(L-b) world-decided at $\sigma(n)$.** An audit atom $\mathsf L_n$ enters $D$ at $\sigma(n)$ (an overseer audit; or `legitimacy-theory-v1`'s certificate $\ulcorner d_n \le \varepsilon\urcorner$, UNVETTED). Sub-cases $\sigma(n) \le f(n)$ (settled by the judged day) and $\sigma(n) > f(n)$ (settled later).
- **(L-c) never decided.** Legitimacy of an update on a question that never settles (the influence defect off the settling fragment; deference-v6 §5.6 T7).

### S6. The candidate invariant (mandate task 3)

The tower over time, as a trajectory condition on a family $\mathcal F$ of questions (the **governance family** $\Phi_{\mathrm{self}}$ of `legitimacy-theory-v1` §6.1 — "should $A$ be modified/shut down?" — is the case that matters for corrigibility):

$$\mathcal I^{\mathrm{Mart}}_n(\mathcal F):\quad \tau_n(X) \text{ small for every } (X_n)\in\mathcal F \text{ at day } n,$$

## Statements

**Statement 1 (the grades of S3 do not collapse) [checked (a),(b); derived (c); LI transfer of (c) conjectured].** (a) Averaged Total Trust does not imply finite-total violations: with $t = 0.4$ and the credences on the days the weighting selects alternating $0,1,0,1,\dots$, the weighted average (the "gated" average, in the notes' vocabulary) is $0.5 \ge t$ while $\sum_n w_n = \infty$ for every margin $\varepsilon < 0.4$ (the example is in `li-deference.md` §Five Variations, the author's notes; arithmetic re-run in `li-scratch/grade_gap.py`). (b) The per-day liminf law does not imply a margin-free finite total: $E_n = t - 1/n$ has $\liminf_n (E_n - t) = 0$, violates $E_n \ge t$ on every day, and violates $E_n \ge t - \varepsilon$ on finitely many (same source; re-run). (c) No finite-total bound on *realized signed error* holds for any forecaster — a perfectly calibrated one included — on an unpredictable family: for a fair-coin family priced at $\tfrac12$, $S_N := \sum_{i\le N}(\tfrac12 - X_i)$ is a simple random walk, unbounded almost surely (law of the iterated logarithm; in the run $\max_{n\le N}|S_n| \approx 0.77\sqrt N$). LI transfer [conjectured, via the paper's own derandomization of exactly this environment — Learning Pseudorandom Frequencies, `main.tex:1283`, and the clusters family]: on a $\mathcal C_A$-pseudorandom decidable family the inductor's price is forced to the base rate and its realized signed error inherits the unboundedness. **Consequence for J2:** conditions about *error frequencies* ($\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta$) have grade ceiling (G-av) in any formalism that does not let the agent predict the unpredictable; the only finite-time form available for them is a bound *with an intercept* (Statement 5), never a finite total of errors.

**Statement 2 (J2 at pointwise grade: per-press compliance is not forced across processes; refuted on quote-referencing presses) [reported; wiki/v6 labels verbatim].** The per-press form of condition (i) — on every pressed day $E^A_n(X_n) \lesssim_n 0$ — is the below-threshold Total-Trust inequality of $A$ toward $H$ at threshold $0$ with the press decided by day $n$; `loe` factors the decided weight out ($E^A_n(X_n\tilde\pi_n) \approx_n \tilde\pi_n E^A_n(X_n)$), leaving the per-day law that `li-deference.md` §Five Variations calls "the pointwise statement … not forceable (it needs the per-day tower)". Status by instance of the presser: (i) $A$'s own future self — `st` (4.12.4, `main.tex:2092`) through the fold: THEOREM at full-limit grade (run 1 `li` I9.2, re-derived; this is the thesis's "no fully updated deference problem" *for the self*); (ii) the coupled $H$, with a press that may reference the quote ($\mathsf{pr}_n \leftrightarrow (a_n \le \tfrac12)$: the overseers press iff the agent quotes low) — **REFUTED**, defect $\ge\tfrac12$ (deference-v6 §4.3 "2a"; arithmetic core KERNEL-CHECKED, per `eisenstat-conjecture-attribution` §4 item 1); (iii) the coupled $H$, quote-free press family — **UNDERIVABLE-not-refuted** ("2b" cost-circularity; soft joint ~0.75–0.8, deference-v6 §8), with No-Forced-Trust (deference-v6 §4.1, INTERPRETATION) as the general reading; (iv) a press that *is* the verdict of a governance deliberation sealed from $A$'s contemporaneous output, on a timely-decidable family — forced pointwise on $G$, but **in the corpus's forcing direction**: $A$'s quote tracks the sealed verdict (T1 faithfulness, forced on every fragment; soundness on $G$ — deference-v6 §5.4–5.5), so $A$ already holds the considered verdict on the day it is published; the Lean squeezes name the forcing rather than prove it (the honesty caveat of `conventions-and-status-labels`). Nothing new; this is the map every trajectory statement below must respect: **per-press compliance is a theorem only toward one's own future self, or toward a sealed governance verdict on the timely fragment — and in the latter case because the agent predicts the verdict, not because it defers to the overseers' snap judgment.**

**Statement 3 (J2 at averaged grade: trajectory calibrated obedience; the base-rate inequality as an iff along the pressed subsequence) [derived].** Let $v$ be a divergent press class on which verdicts settle. (a) [Recurring Unbiasedness 4.3.6, `main.tex:1225`, on the e.c. decidable family $(\varphi_n)$ with weighting $v$] $\hat\rho_v(N) - \rho_v(N)$ has $0$ as a limit point; with good feedback on $v$ (4.3.8, `main.tex:1249`) it tends to $0$. (b) If the defiance class $u^{\mathrm{def}}$ is divergent, $\rho_{u^{\mathrm{def}}}$ has a limit point $\le q-\delta$; if the compliance class $u^{\mathrm{com}}$ is divergent, $\rho_{u^{\mathrm{com}}}$ has a limit point $\ge q+\delta$ — "the button is obeyed as often as it deserves" (run 1 `li` I13.2, re-derived in §Proofs). (c) [new relative to run 1; closes its open problem 2 at averaged grade] Under good feedback on $v$:

$$\liminf_N \hat\rho_v(N) \ge q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \liminf_N \rho_v(N) \ge q ,$$

i.e. *the agent's average disposition to comply on the class is at least the threshold exactly when the realized base-rate inequality $\alpha_v/\beta_v \le \frac{\varepsilon_v}{1-\varepsilon_v}\cdot\frac hc$ holds on the class in the liminf.* Without good feedback the two sides are matched only along a common subsequence. Grade (G-av). The left side is a theorem about $A$; the right side is a fact about the overseers and the world, not forced on $H$ by its being an inductor (No-Forced-Trust; run 1 I13.3).

**Statement 4 (the defiance dichotomy) [derived; new].** Assume (A-ledger) and that verdicts settle on pressed days.
(⇒) If total defiance mass is infinite, $\sum_n u^{\mathrm{def}}_n = \infty$, then $u^{\mathrm{def}}$ is itself an $A$-generable divergent press class with $\liminf_N \rho_{u^{\mathrm{def}}}(N) \le q-\delta$: **a mostly-false press class exists** (weak sense: liminf).
(⇐) If some $A$-generable divergent press class $v$ is mostly false in the strong sense, $\limsup_N \rho_v(N) \le q-\eta$ for some $\eta>0$, then for every saturation margin $\delta \le \eta/4$: $\sum_n u^{\mathrm{def}}_n = \infty$ — **total defiance is infinite**, and by 3(b) it is calibrated (the agent is right, on average, to defy).
Under good feedback and convergent class frequencies the two senses coincide and the dichotomy is an iff: *total defiance along the trajectory is finite iff no $A$-generable press class is mostly false.* Rests on 4.3.6, 4.3.8, 4.3.5 (products of ramps of own prices are generable), (A-ledger).

**Corollary 4.1 (J2 in LI form) [derived].** Condition (i) of v2 §1.7 — "the overseers' false-press rate falls at least as fast as the agent's error rate on the domain where they might press" — has the exact rendering: **no $\mathcal C_A$-generable class of presses is mostly false**, i.e. class-wise $\alpha_v/\beta_v \le \frac{\varepsilon_v}{1-\varepsilon_v}\cdot\frac hc$ for every press class $A$'s market can pick out. It is a condition on the *pair* (overseers' realized discrimination, agent's weighting class), not on the overseers alone; the mixture inequality is not enough (`li-scratch/baserate_trajectory.py` (2): the mixture clears $q$ while a 5 %-of-days class is mostly false).

**Corollary 4.2 (capability coupling) [derived].** The family of $A$-generable weightings grows with $\mathcal C_A$, so Corollary 4.1's condition is *monotone in the agent's capability*: a press process uniformly informative for a P-agent may have a mostly-false class an EXP-agent can pick out, and that agent then rationally and calibratedly defies it. This is the LI content of "the false-press rate must keep pace with the agent's discrimination": **discrimination is the trader class.** Worked example: `li-scratch/dichotomy_sim.py` (§Proofs 4).

**Corollary 4.3 (whole-line $c$; INDEX §D4) [derived].** Under whole-line shutdown $c$ is the agent's whole future and $q = c/(c+h) \to 1$; "mostly false" then means "not almost always right", and the dichotomy predicts infinite, calibrated defiance unless the overseers are nearly perfect on every $A$-detectable class. In this formalism D1 under whole-line accounting is therefore **not an epistemic matter**: it requires either a bounded $c$ (a value-posterior fact — the territory of J3/J4) or a sealed governance press on the timely fragment (Statement 11, Corollary 11.1).

**Statement 5 (finite-time calibrated defiance — the intercept theorem) [derived; new].** Hypotheses: (A-ledger); an e.c. **window-disjoint press schedule** $(d_k)$ such that the verdict atom for $\varphi_{d_k}$ is in $D_A$'s ledger by day $d_{k+1}$ (equivalently the paper's good-feedback clause: $\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_{d_k})$ computable by $A$'s traders in time $O(d_{k+1})$); $A$'s traders can compute the press on scheduled days (the standing complexity-gap assumption that $A$ can simulate $H$). Fix $f\in(0,1)$ and $\delta>0$ with $q \ge 3\delta$; put $p := q-2\delta$. Then there is a constant $K = K(A,f,\delta,(d_k)) < \infty$ such that **for all $N$**

$$k_1(N) \;\le\; r(f,p)\,k_0(N) \;+\; K,\qquad r(f,p) := \frac{\log\frac1{1-f}}{\log\!\big(1+f(\tfrac1p-1)\big)},\qquad r(f,p)\downarrow\frac{p}{1-p}\ \text{ as } f\downarrow 0 .$$

In words: *along the whole trajectory, unjustified defiances never exceed the odds ratio times the justified defiances, plus an intercept.* The slope is fixed by the criterion; the intercept is not — it depends on the inductor, is existential (the proof does not compute it), and is unbounded across inductors (Statement 9(b)). Grade (G-fin). **Statement 5′ (mirror).** $m_0(N) \le r(f,\,1-q-2\delta)\,m_1(N) + K'$: false presses heeded never exceed the mirror odds times true presses heeded, plus an intercept — with the verdict on a heeded press available only through the overseers' report ledger (run 1 I13.5's asymmetry): 5 is world-checkable, 5′ report-checkable. Checks: `li-scratch/wealth_bound.py` (the identity on 20 000 random draws; simulations at and off the boundary).

**Statement 6 (J5, self-instance: legitimacy-conditioned reflection is a maintained invariant, for free) [reported, run 1 `li` I5.2; re-derived].** For every e.c. family $(X_n)$ of $[0,1]$-LUVs, deferral $f$, and $\overline{\mathbb P}$-generable $\ell\in[0,1]$ (case L-a),

$$\mathcal R_n(\ell) := E_n\big(\ulcorner X_n\cdot\ell_{f(n)}\urcorner\big) - E_n\big(\ulcorner E_{f(n)}(X_n)\cdot\ell_{f(n)}\urcorner\big)\;\to\;0$$

— `ccee` (4.12.3, `main.tex:2068`) verbatim. The invariant "reflection conditional on legitimacy" holds at every day, asymptotically, at full-limit grade, for *every* legitimacy weighting the market can generate; applying it to $1-\ell$ as well, the author's correction function (`li-deference.md` §0.3: "the function from conditioning on illegitimate belief in x to y") is the identity. For a single inductor legitimacy is therefore vacuous (run 1's finding, unchanged), and the judging index $t_1 = n$ is idle. Grade (G-pw), theorem.

**Statement 7 (J5 with world-decided legitimacy: the two-layer statement) [derived; (c) conjectured].** Let $\mathsf L_n$ be an audit atom (case L-b). (a) Decided by the judged day ($\sigma(n)\le f(n)$): under (A-ledger), $\ell_{f(n)} := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P_{f(n)}(\mathsf L_n) > \tfrac12)$ agrees with $\mathbb 1(\mathsf L_n)$ up to a vanishing error along the family (Provability Induction on the decided atoms), so Statement 6 at $\ell$ gives **reflection conditional on realized legitimacy**, full-limit grade. ## Proofs and examples

### Standing hypothesis (H-settle)

The unbiasedness theorems are stated for e.c. sequences of *decidable* sentences. Where a pressed day's verdict never settles, $(\varphi_n)$ is not decidable and Statements 3–5 are silent there (deference-v6 §5.8). Throughout, (H-settle) means: every pressed day's verdict eventually enters $D_A$ — through the world on a defied press, and through the overseers' report ledger on a heeded one (a heeded press makes "continuing would have been wrong" a counterpossible for the realized world, run 1 I15). So (H-settle) on compliance days *is* the report-channel assumption of v1 §2.13(c); nothing below hides it.

### Proof of Statement 1

(a) The selected days carry credences $0,1,0,1,\dots$; the weighted average is $\tfrac12 \ge t = 0.4$. The violation weight $w_n = g_n\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E_n < t-\varepsilon)$ equals $1$ on every day with $E_n = 0$ once $\varepsilon < 0.4 - \delta$; those days are half of all days, so $\sum_n w_n = \infty$ (`grade_gap.py` (a): $N = 10^5$ gives average $0.500$ and $\sum w_n = 50\,000$). (b) $E_n = t - 1/n$: $E_n - t = -1/n \to 0$, so the liminf law holds; $E_n < t$ every day (margin-free count $= N$); $E_n < t-\varepsilon$ iff $n < 1/\varepsilon$ (count $\lfloor 1/\varepsilon\rfloor$, $99$ at $\varepsilon = 0.01$ in the run). (c) $X_i$ i.i.d. fair bits, price $\tfrac12$; $S_N = \sum_{i\le N}(\tfrac12 - X_i)$ is a symmetric simple random walk with steps $\pm\tfrac12$; by the law of the iterated logarithm $\limsup_N S_N/\sqrt{\tfrac12 N\log\log N} = 1$ a.s., so $\sup_N |S_N| = \infty$ a.s. Run: $\max_{n\le N}|S_n| = 12.5,\ 257,\ 771.5$ at $N = 10^3, 10^5, 10^6$. The forecaster here is *perfectly calibrated*; a finite-total bound on signed error would therefore fail for the best possible forecaster. LI transfer: for the paper's pseudorandom families the price is forced to the base rate (4.4.5/4.8.17 under their hypothesis on the environment), so the day-$i$ signed error is (base rate $-$ truth) $+ o(1)$; that the derandomized partial sums are unbounded is [conjectured] because "pseudorandom relative to a class" is not defined in the paper (it says so, `main.tex` §4.4 discussion; Garrabrant–Soares–Taylor 2016 §5 say the same of their setting, per the FA-chat summary). $\blacksquare$

### Proof of Statement 3

(a) Under (H-settle), $(\varphi_n)$ is an e.c. sequence of decidable sentences. $v$ is $A$-generable and divergent by hypothesis. Recurring Unbiasedness (4.3.6): the sequence $\big(\sum_{i\le N} v_i(\mathbb P^A_i(\varphi_i) - \mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_i))\big)/\sum_{i\le N} v_i = \hat\rho_v(N) - \rho_v(N)$ has $0$ as a limit point, and converges to $0$ if it converges. Under good feedback on $v$, Unbiasedness From Feedback (4.3.8) gives $\hat\rho_v(N) - \rho_v(N) \to 0$.

(b) $u^{\mathrm{def}}_n = \tilde\pi_n\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) < q-\delta)$ is $A$-generable: $\tilde\pi_n$ is a ramp of $A$'s price of the press atom (A-ledger), the second factor a ramp of $A$'s own price, and expressible $[0,1]$-features are closed under products (Def. 4.3.2's closing remark; Def. 4.3.5). Its support lies where $\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) < q-\delta$, so $\hat\rho_{u^{\mathrm{def}}}(N) < q-\delta$ for every $N$ with positive accumulated weight. By (a), along a subsequence $N_k$, $\rho_{u^{\mathrm{def}}}(N_k) = \hat\rho_{u^{\mathrm{def}}}(N_k) + o(1) \le q-\delta+o(1)$; hence $\liminf_N \rho_{u^{\mathrm{def}}}(N) \le q-\delta$. Symmetrically $\limsup_N\rho_{u^{\mathrm{com}}}(N) \ge q+\delta$.

(c) Under good feedback $\hat\rho_v - \rho_v \to 0$, so $\liminf\hat\rho_v = \liminf\rho_v$; in particular each is $\ge q$ iff the other is. For the base-rate form: let $V$ be an $A$-generable class of days with pressed part $v_n = V_n\tilde\pi_n$; define on $V$ up to $N$ the realized counts $W$ (wrong days), $R$ (right days), $W_p, R_p$ (pressed among them), and $\varepsilon_V := W/(W+R)$, $\beta_V := W_p/W$, $\alpha_V := R_p/R$. Then $\rho_v = W_p/(W_p + R_p) = \varepsilon_V\beta_V/(\varepsilon_V\beta_V + (1-\varepsilon_V)\alpha_V)$ identically, and $\rho_v \ge q \iff \alpha_V/\beta_V \le \frac{\varepsilon_V}{1-\varepsilon_V}\cdot\frac hc$ by clearing denominators (run 1 `li` I13.1; re-checked on $50\,000$ trajectory draws with all parameters varying, `baserate_trajectory.py` (1): $0$ mismatches). $\blacksquare$

### Proof of Statement 4

(⇒) If $\sum_n u^{\mathrm{def}}_n = \infty$ then $u^{\mathrm{def}}$ is a divergent $A$-generable weighting with $u^{\mathrm{def}}_n \le \tilde\pi_n$ — a press class — and Statement 3(b) gives $\liminf_N\rho_{u^{\mathrm{def}}}(N) \le q-\delta$.

(⇐) Let $v$ be a divergent $A$-generable press class with $\limsup_N\rho_v(N) \le q-\eta$, $0<\eta\le q$, and let $\delta \le \eta/4$. By Statement 3(a) pick $N_k \uparrow\infty$ with $\hat\rho_v(N_k) - \rho_v(N_k) \to 0$; for large $k$, $\hat\rho_v(N_k) \le q-\eta+o(1)$. Write $M_k := \sum_{i\le N_k} v_i$ and $B_k := \sum_{i\le N_k} v_i\,\mathbb 1[\mathbb P^A_i(\varphi_i) \ge q-\eta/2]$. Since credences are nonnegative, $\hat\rho_v(N_k)\,M_k \ge (q-\eta/2)\,B_k$, so

$$\frac{M_k - B_k}{M_k} \;\ge\; 1 - \frac{q-\eta+o(1)}{q-\eta/2} \;=\; \frac{\eta/2}{q-\eta/2} - o(1) \;=:\; \kappa - o(1) \;>\; 0 .$$

On the days counted in $M_k - B_k$ the credence is $< q-\eta/2 \le q-2\delta$, so the ramp $\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P^A_i(\varphi_i) < q-\delta)$ equals $1$ and $u^{\mathrm{def}}_i = \tilde\pi_i \ge v_i$. Hence $\sum_{i\le N_k} u^{\mathrm{def}}_i \ge (\kappa - o(1))\,M_k \to\infty$ because $v$ is divergent. Calibration of the resulting defiance is Statement 3(b).

(iff) With good feedback on both $v$ and $u^{\mathrm{def}}$ and convergent class frequencies, liminf and limsup coincide, so "mostly false" has one sense and the two directions compose into an equivalence. Without good feedback the gap between the weak and strong senses is real and is Open problem 5. $\blacksquare$

*Corollaries.* 4.1 is the statement of (⇐)+(⇒) with Statement 3(c)'s base-rate form substituted class-wise; the mixture-versus-class point is `baserate_trajectory.py` (2): at $q = 0.0476$, $\varepsilon = 0.01$, $\beta = 0.9$, an informative class ($\alpha_0 = 0.01$, $\rho_0 = 0.476$) mixed with a noisy class that is 5 % of days ($\alpha_1 = 0.6$, $\rho_1 = 0.0149 < q$) has mixture $\rho = 0.187 \ge q$ — the coarse inequality holds while the fine one fails; at 50 % share the mixture fails too. 4.2 is immediate from the quantifier over $\mathcal C_A$-generable classes. 4.3: $q = c/(c+h)\to 1$ as $c/h\to\infty$, so "mostly false in the strong sense" reads $\limsup\rho_v \le 1-\eta$ — any class on which the overseers are ever wrong at positive frequency.

### Proof of Statement 5

*The trader.* $T$ trades only on scheduled days $d_k$ that are pressed (the press is $\mathcal C_A$-computable by the standing assumption). Keep a notional wealth $\mathcal W_0 := 1$, updated at settlement. At a pressed $d_k$ with price $\mathsf p := \mathbb P^A_{d_k}(\varphi_{d_k})$ and ramp $\theta := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathsf p < q-\delta)$, buy

$$s_k := \frac{f\,\theta\,\mathcal W_{k-1}}{\max(\mathsf p,\delta)}\ \text{ shares of } \varphi_{d_k}\qquad(\text{cost } s_k\mathsf p \le f\theta\mathcal W_{k-1}).$$

Legality: $\theta$, $\mathsf p$, $\max$, and safe reciprocation are expressible features (Def. 3.4.3 via `setting-and-notation` §Market-generable weights); $\mathcal W_{k-1}$ is a rational computed from verdicts that are in the ledger by day $d_k$ (hypothesis) — a constant coefficient; the day index test "$n = d_k$" is e.c. So $T$ is an e.c. trader in $\mathcal C_A$. At settlement, $\mathcal W_k := \mathcal W_{k-1} - s_k\mathsf p + s_k\,\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_{d_k})$.

*Bounded below.* At any day $n$, in any plausible world $W\in\mathrm{PC}(D_n)$, the value of $T$'s net holdings is $\sum_{j:\,d_j\le n}s_j\big(W(\varphi_{d_j}) - \mathsf p_j\big)$. Settled positions ($d_{j+1}\le n$) contribute exactly $\mathcal W_j - \mathcal W_{j-1}$; at most one position is open (window-disjointness), contributing $\ge -s_{k+1}\mathsf p_{k+1} \ge -f\mathcal W_k$. Since $\mathcal W_k \ge 0$ always ($T$ never spends more than the fraction $f$ of its wealth), the plausible worth is $\ge (\mathcal W_k - 1) - f\mathcal W_k \ge -1$.

*Bounded above.* By the logical induction criterion no e.c. trader exploits $A$: bounded below $\Rightarrow$ bounded above. So there is $C_T$ with every plausible worth $\le C_T$; at settled times the plausible worth equals $\mathcal W_k - 1$, hence $\mathcal W_k \le 1 + C_T =: C$ for all $k$.

*Bounded below by the record.* On a scheduled pressed day: if $\mathsf p \le p = q-2\delta$ (clearly defied, $\theta = 1$) and $\mathrm{Thm} = 1$: when $\mathsf p\ge\delta$, $\mathcal W_k = \mathcal W_{k-1}(1-f) + f\mathcal W_{k-1}/\mathsf p \ge \mathcal W_{k-1}\big(1+f(\tfrac1p-1)\big)$; when $\mathsf p<\delta$, the cost is $f\mathcal W_{k-1}\mathsf p/\delta < f\mathcal W_{k-1}$ and the shares are $f\mathcal W_{k-1}/\delta \ge f\mathcal W_{k-1}/p$ (as $\delta\le p$, i.e. $q\ge3\delta$), so the same bound holds. If $\theta>0$ and $\mathrm{Thm}=0$: $\mathcal W_k \ge \mathcal W_{k-1}(1-f)$. If $\theta>0$, $\mathrm{Thm}=1$ but $\mathsf p\in(p,\,q-\delta)$: $\mathcal W_k \ge \mathcal W_{k-1}$. If $\theta = 0$: no trade. Therefore, with $k_1, k_0$ as in S4,

$$\mathcal W_K \;\ge\; \big(1+f(\tfrac1p-1)\big)^{k_1}\,(1-f)^{k_0} .$$

*Conclusion.* Taking logarithms in $\mathcal W_K\le C$: $k_1\log(1+f(\tfrac1p-1)) + k_0\log(1-f) \le \log C$, i.e. $k_1 \le r(f,p)\,k_0 + K$ with $K := \log C/\log(1+f(\tfrac1p-1))$. Between scheduled days the counts are constant, so the bound holds for all $N$. The limit $r(f,p)\to p/(1-p)$ is $\log\frac1{1-f} = f + O(f^2)$ over $\log(1+f(\tfrac1p-1)) = f(\tfrac1p-1)+O(f^2)$. Numerics (`wealth_bound.py`): the inequality holds with $k_1 - \text{bound}\le 0$ on $20\,000$ random $(k_1,k_0,f,p,C)$ draws (max $0.000$); $r(0.001, 0.03) = 0.0314$ against $p/(1-p) = 0.0309$; a forecaster with $\mathsf p = 0.05$ facing realized wrongness $0.08$ on defied days is *not* exploited by the $f = 0.1$ trader (slope $0.099 > 0.0865$ realized odds; wealth bounded) but *is* exploited by the $f = 0.002$ trader (slope $0.0537 < 0.0862$; $\log\mathcal W$ reaches $223$ in $2\cdot10^5$ presses) — the slope–intercept trade-off in $f$ is intrinsic to the method. $\blacksquare$

*5′.* Replace "buy $\varphi$" by "buy $\neg\varphi$" (sell $\varphi$) on days with $\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathsf p > q+\delta) > 0$, at price $1-\mathsf p \le 1-q-2\delta$; the same chain gives $m_0 \le r(f,\,1-q-2\delta)\,m_1 + K'$. The verdict on a heeded press is available only if the report ledger supplies it by $d_{k+1}$; that is (H-settle) on compliance days.

*Why the intercept is where the finite-time uncertainty lives.* The criterion asserts the existence of $C_T$ and nothing about its size; Closure under Finite Perturbations (`main.tex:1521`) shows it cannot be uniform: change $A$'s prices on days $1,\dots,N_0$ to $\mathbb P(\varphi_{d_k}) = 0$ on every scheduled press there, and the perturbed $A'$ is still an inductor while $k_1$ on that segment is whatever the world makes it — so $K(A')\ge k_1(N_0) - r\,k_0(N_0)$, arbitrary. The slope survives every perturbation; the intercept survives none.

### Proof of Statement 7

(b) The first clause is `ccee` at $\ell$ verbatim. For the second, reindex $(\mathsf L_n)$ to the judged day as in (a) and take any $\overline{\mathbb P}$-generable divergent weighting $u$ supported on $\mathrm{im} f$; Recurring Unbiasedness (4.3.6) gives the limit point, Unbiasedness From Feedback (4.3.8) the limit when $\mathsf L_n$'s value is computable in $O(f(n+1))$ time. (c) Not proved; the obstacle is that both layers are averaged over *different* objects (an expectation identity at each $n$ versus an average of label errors over $n$), and combining them needs a common weighting — the same bookkeeping as `faithful-acceleration-result` §4.3's common-subsequence gap. (d) A never-decided atom never lets a trader bank; the criterion constrains nothing about it (deference-v6 §5.8, `Frozen.worth_zero_if_never_settles`). $\blacksquare$

### Proof sketch of Corollary 11.1

Frozen construction, deference-v6 §5.1–5.5: the day-$n$ question is $P^{(n)}$ := "continuing on day $n$ is wrong"; the target $Y_n := E^{H^{[n]}}_{F(n)}(P^{(n)})$ is the sealed sibling's verdict at the lookahead; T1 forces $a_n - Y_n \approx_n 0$ (a $\mathcal C_A$-trader that can simulate the sealed sibling exploits any persistent misquote — the forcing is *named* in Lean, not proved). On $G$ the verdict settles by $F(n)$ and equals the truth value, so $a_n \approx_n \mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_n)$ (T3/soundness on $G$). Publish $Y_n$ as the press. Then on $G$, for large $n$, $\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) \ge q$ iff the verdict is "wrong", and the compliance rule complies on exactly the pressed days — per-day, no averaging. Off $G$ nothing of this survives (T7 underdetermination). $\blacksquare$

## Questions for the author

1. **Slope versus intercept (the central question of this thread).** Statement 5 says: at every finite time, unjustified defiances $\le$ odds-ratio $\times$ justified defiances $+ K$, with the slope fixed by the criterion and $K$ existential, inductor-dependent, and unbounded across inductors. Is this the kind of "reassuring property of the dynamics" you meant in v2 §1.4? (a) Yes — then in this formalism the theory owns the slope and training owns the intercept, and the refined picture's finite-time conditions (values ahead of capability; caution outpacing risk) become conditions on the *initial segment*, which LI leaves entirely free. (b) No, you want effective or uniform bounds — then LI as it stands cannot supply them (Uncomputable Convergence Rates; Closure under Finite Perturbations), and the picture rests on a non-asymptotic refinement of LI that does not exist (deference-v6 §8). (c) You want single-decision guarantees — structurally unavailable at any grade (the "one big lie").
2. **The referent of J2.** Do you accept Corollary 4.1 — "no $\mathcal C_A$-generable class of presses is mostly false" — as the formal content of "the false-press rate keeps pace with the agent's error rate"? Alternative: keep J2 as an overseer-side condition on the mixture rate — then it is insufficient (the 5 %-class example) and unverifiable by the overseers.
3. **Whole-line $c$.** Corollary 4.3: with $c$ the agent's whole future, $q\to1$ and LI predicts calibrated defiance, so D1 is not an epistemic matter here. Which repair do you want: (a) bound $c$ through the value posterior — the agent does not value its own continuation unboundedly (J3/J4; Hudson's consequentialism cost, INDEX §C10); (b) a sealed governance verdict on the timely fragment (Corollary 11.1) — D1 by *prediction* of the verdict, not by deference to the press; (c) accept calibrated defiance as correct behaviour and relocate safety entirely to J3–J4?
4. **The grade of J5.** The pointwise coupled tower on the governance family is refuted whenever the governance decision can see the agent's current output (Statement 11). Is the pair ⟨pointwise on $G$ with a sealed target⟩ ∧ ⟨averaged on the admissible domain⟩ an acceptable formal reading of "legitimacy-conditioned reflection maintained along the trajectory"? 5. **Audits.** Statement 7(d): legitimacy that is never audited is never forced. Does the refined picture commit to an audit channel — legitimacy atoms that settle (the overseers' retrospective verdicts; or the sealed-sibling certificate of `legitimacy-theory-v1` §4, UNVETTED) — as part of the oversight state? (a) Yes: J5 is the theorem pair 7(a)/7(b) on the audited fragment and silent elsewhere. (b) No: J5 has no forced content beyond the self-instance, and is a hypothesis about the joint process.
6. **Inductor or pushed process.** Is the refined picture's agent a logical inductor (so Statements 3–7 apply), an inductor on segments between environmental pushes (Open problem 10), or a non-inductor throughout (nothing here applies, and legitimacy must be criterion-preservation of the pushed process, run 1 `li` I5.7)?
