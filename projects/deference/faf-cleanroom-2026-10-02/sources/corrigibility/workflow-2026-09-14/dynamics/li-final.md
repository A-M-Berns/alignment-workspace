## Setting and definitions

### S1. Processes, clock, channels [reported; hypotheses named]

The world is the deference wiki's (`setting-and-notation`): a theory $\Gamma$ (canonically PA) with a $\Gamma$-complete computable deductive process $D$; the overseer inductor $H \dashv \mathcal C_H$ and the agent inductor $A \dashv \mathcal C_A$, $\mathcal C_H \subsetneq \mathcal C_A$ (canonically P $\subsetneq$ EXP), each satisfying the logical induction criterion (`main.tex:657`). Days $n$ are the only clock. $E^P_n(X)$ is $P$'s day-$n$ expectation of an e.d. $[a,b]$-LUV $X$; $\ulcorner e\urcorner$ names a value as a LUV; $\operatorname{Ind}_\delta$ is the continuous ramp (Def. 4.3.2, `main.tex:1174`); a weighting is $P$-generable if it is an e.c. progression of expressible features of $P$'s own prices (Def. 4.3.5, `main.tex:1218`). $x_n \approx_n y_n$ iff $x_n - y_n \to 0$; $x_n \gtrsim_n y_n$ iff $\liminf(x_n - y_n)\ge 0$. Schedules $n \le e(n) < F(n) < \sigma(n)$.

**(A-ledger)** [reported; adversary audit item 6 accepted]. Cross-process facts enter a reasoner's deductive process as decided ledger atoms its traders can read at cost within their class. *This is the stipulation of `setting-and-notation` §Observable ("read-cheap"), not something the LI paper provides*: in the paper a trading strategy is computed from the day index and the price history alone (`main.tex:852`) and $D$ is an arbitrary computable process. Three ledgers: the **quote ledger** ($H^+$ reads $A$'s quotes), the **press ledger** ($D_A$ receives $\mathsf{pr}_n$ = "the overseers pressed on day $n$"), and the **verdict ledger** ($D_A$ receives, when it settles, the atom deciding $\varphi_n$ below).

**(H-blind) — the press is decided one stage ahead of the agent's output** [derived; adversary attacks 3.4 and 5.4 accepted]. Every averaged and finite-$N$ statement below uses the press as a *coefficient* on day $n$ — either through $A$'s proxy $\tilde\pi_n := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P^A_n(\mathsf{pr}_n) > \tfrac12)$, which tracks the press only if the atom is decided in $D_A$ by day $n$, or directly in a trader's strategy, which is computed from stages $<n$ (`main.tex:852`). Hence the press on day $n$ may not depend on $A$'s day-$n$ output. Under the frozen construction's one-stage delay of every published object (deference-v6 §5.2) this is automatic; a quote-referencing press ($\mathsf{pr}_n \leftrightarrow (a_n \le \tfrac12)$) is *excluded by hypothesis* from Statements 3–5, not defeated by averaging. Statement 2(ii)'s refutation and Statements 3–5's theorems therefore live on disjoint press families, and the develop file's apparent contrast between them ("pointwise refuted on diagonal presses, averaged holds on all press classes") is withdrawn.

**(H-settle) — every pressed day's verdict eventually enters $D_A$, and through which ledger** [derived; adversary attack 3.3 accepted]. On a *defied* press the world decides $\varphi_n$ ("continuing on day $n$ was wrong"): the **world ledger**. On a *heeded* press the agent stopped, so "continuing would have been wrong" is a counterpossible for the realized world and settles only through the overseers' retrospective report: the **report ledger**. A third source exists on a narrow fragment (Corollary 11.1, repaired): where wrongness is a *deductive* fact decided by $D$ regardless of what the agent did (the plan is provably flawed), a sealed deliberation's verdict settles $\varphi_n$ on heeded days too — the **sealed ledger**, available only on the timely fragment $G$ and only at the lookahead $F(n)$. Every "realized frequency" below is tagged by ledger: $\rho^{\mathrm w}$ (world), $\rho^{\mathrm r}$ (report), $\rho^{\mathrm h}$ (hybrid: world on defied days, report on heeded days). **A compression that says "realized" without its ledger is not permitted in this file.**

### S2. The shutdown family and the compliance rule [reported, re-stated]

For each day $n$, $X_n$ is a $[-h,c]$-LUV, the value of continuing over shutting down; in the working picture's toy form $X_n = c - (c+h)\cdot\mathbb 1(\varphi_n)$, $\varphi_n$ := "continuing on day $n$ is wrong", an e.c. family decidable on the settling fragment. $q := c/(c+h)$ is the compliance threshold of v1 §2.13(a). The press is the overseers' below-threshold report $\pi_n := \operatorname{Ind}_\delta(E^H_n(X_n) < 0)$, delivered as $\mathsf{pr}_n$ under (H-blind). The **compliance rule** (v2 §1.7, "stated on the decision, not the prior"): comply on day $n$ iff $\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) \ge q$, equivalently $E^A_n(X_n) \le 0$ up to $o(1)$ (Linearity, `main.tex:1700`; Expectations of Indicators, `main.tex:1719`).

Weightings with saturation margin $\delta$: $u^{\mathrm{def}}_n := \tilde\pi_n\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) < q-\delta)$, $u^{\mathrm{com}}_n := \tilde\pi_n\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_\delta(\mathbb P^A_n(\varphi_n) > q+\delta)$. A **press class** is an $A$-generable weighting $v \le \tilde\pi$. For a press class $v$ and a ledger $\mathrm x\in\{\mathrm w,\mathrm r,\mathrm h\}$:

$$\rho^{\mathrm x}_v(N) := \frac{\sum_{i\le N} v_i\,\mathrm{Thm}^{\mathrm x}(\varphi_i)}{\sum_{i\le N} v_i},\qquad \hat\rho_v(N) := \frac{\sum_{i\le N} v_i\,\mathbb P^A_i(\varphi_i)}{\sum_{i\le N} v_i},$$

with $\mathrm{Thm}^{\mathrm x}$ the value the ledger $\mathrm x$ supplies. Base-rate parameters on a class ($\varepsilon_v, \alpha_v, \beta_v$) are as in the develop file, and [checked, run 1 `li` I13.1; `li-scratch/baserate_trajectory.py` (1), 0 mismatches on 50 000 draws] $\rho_v \ge q \iff \alpha_v/\beta_v \le \frac{\varepsilon_v}{1-\varepsilon_v}\cdot\frac hc$ exactly, ledger by ledger.

### S3. Grades — two, plus qualifiers; the line that matters is not on the lattice [REPAIRED; adversary attacks 1.1–1.3 accepted]

The develop file's five-grade lattice is withdrawn. [checked] Under its own all-margins quantification, "finitely many $\varepsilon$-violations for every $\varepsilon>0$" *is* $\liminf_n(E_n - t)\ge 0$, and "$\sum_{n\le N} w_n \le C$ for all $N$ with $C$ existential" *is* "$\sum_n w_n<\infty$" (nonnegative terms). What remains:

- **(G-pw) per-day asymptotic.** $\gtrsim_n$ or $\approx_n$ of the per-day quantity: for all sufficiently large $n$, with no bound on when.
- **Qualifier: fragment.** "On $G$" (questions decided by $F(n)$, sealed target; deference-v6 §5.3) or "on the settling fragment" restricts *which days* a statement covers; it is not a grade.
- **Qualifier: quantity.** *Frequency-type* statements (about $\rho$, $\hat\rho$) versus *cumulative-type* statements (a signed running sum bounded above by an existential constant, $\sup_N Q(N) < \infty$). Statement 5 is cumulative-type; it is neither above nor below (G-pw) on any lattice — a different kind of quantity, and (Statement 5, repaired) essentially equivalent to (G-av) with good feedback for the corresponding ratio.

[derived] **Every statement of every kind above is a tail property**: $\exists K\,\forall N$ with $K$ inductor-dependent is insensitive to any finite prefix (Closure under Finite Perturbations, `main.tex:1521`), as is any liminf/limsup. The distinction the refined picture cares about — v2 §1.4, AUTHOR: "we care more about conditions of the trajectory than where it ends up … Properties of the limit point can be reassuring, but properties of the dynamics more so" — is not a distinction between rows of this table. It is the distinction between a statement whose constant *exists* and one whose constant is *known* (effective, or uniform over the inductor class). Logical induction supplies only the first (Statement 9). **Reading v2 §1.4 as a demand for finite-total or finite-time-with-constant grades was CLAUDE (the develop file's), not AUTHOR** (adversary audit item 4 accepted); the author asked for properties of the dynamics, and which formal property that is remains Question 1.

### Proof of Statement 1(c′) [derived; new here; not machine-checked]

Let $(\varphi_n)$ be e.c. decidable, pseudorandom with frequency $p\in(0,1)$ over all $\overline{\mathbb P}$-generable divergent weightings (Def. 4.4.1), with good feedback along the deferral $f$: $\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_{f(i)})$ is computable in $O(f(i+1))$ time. Write $e_i := \mathbb P_{f(i)}(\varphi_{f(i)}) - \mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_{f(i)})\in[-1,1]$ and $T_N := \sum_{i\le N}e_i$. By Learning Pseudorandom Frequencies (`main.tex:1283`), $\mathbb P_{f(i)}(\varphi_{f(i)})\approx_i p$.

Suppose $\sup_N T_N < \infty$. If $\inf_N T_N = -\infty$ we are done for the inf; so suppose also $\inf_N T_N > -\infty$, and let $M := \limsup_N T_N$, finite. Fix $\eta\in(0,p/2)$ and a rational $M'\in(M-\eta, M-\eta/2)$. Let $N_0$ be such that $T_N \le M+\eta/2$ and $|\mathbb P_{f(N)}(\varphi_{f(N)}) - p| < p - 2\eta$ for all $N\ge N_0$. The set $J := \{N\ge N_0 : T_N > M'\}$ is infinite ($T_N$ exceeds $M-\eta$ infinitely often). For $N\in J$: $e_{N+1} = T_{N+1} - T_N < (M+\eta/2) - (M-\eta) = \tfrac32\eta$; if $\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_{f(N+1)}) = 0$ then $e_{N+1} = \mathbb P_{f(N+1)}(\varphi_{f(N+1)}) > 2\eta$, a contradiction; so $\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_{f(N+1)}) = 1$ for every $N\in J$.

Now define the weighting $v_{f(N+1)} := \mathbb 1[N\ge N_0]\cdot\mathbb 1[T_N > M']$ and $v_m := 0$ for $m\notin\mathrm{im} f$. It is a $\overline{\mathbb P}$-generable weighting: at day $f(N+1)$ a trader's program (`main.tex:852`: computed from the day index and the price history) reads the past prices $\mathbb P_{f(i)}(\varphi_{f(i)})$, $i\le N$, as expressible features, computes $\mathrm{Thm}(\varphi_{f(i)})$ for $i\le N$ within its budget by good feedback, forms the rational $T_N$, and outputs the constant $0$ or $1$ (a constant is an expressible feature; the hard indicator here is of a *computed constant*, not of a price, so Def. 4.3.5's continuity requirement is not violated). It is divergent because $J$ is infinite. On its support the frequency of truth is exactly $1\ne p$, contradicting pseudorandomness over all $\overline{\mathbb P}$-generable divergent weightings. Hence $\sup_N T_N = +\infty$. The mirror argument (with $m := \liminf T_N$, $\eta < (1-p)/2$, and the forced value $\mathrm{Thm} = 0$ on days where $T_N < m'$) gives $\inf_N T_N = -\infty$. $\blacksquare$

*Remarks.* (i) The hypothesis $p\in(0,1)$ is essential: a family the inductor eventually decides has bounded error trivially. (ii) Without good feedback the trader cannot form $T_N$; the statement is then open (the paper's $f$-patient weakening, `main.tex:1296`, is the natural setting). (iii) This is the adversary's sketch (attack 1.4) made into an argument; the adversary graded the transfer "plausible", and the develop file's stated obstacle (that Def. 4.4.1 does not exist) was wrong. (iv) Consequence: the cumulative error on the obedience record, $\sum_{k\le K}(\mathbb P^A_{d_k}(\varphi_{d_k}) - \mathrm{Thm}^{\mathrm w}(\varphi_{d_k}))$ on defied days, is unbounded whenever the defied family is pseudorandom at the agent's credence — so no cumulative bound without a slope strictly above the odds is possible, which is exactly the shape Statement 5 has.

### Proof of Statement 5 (repaired form) [checked chain; derived equivalence]

*The chain* is the develop file's, unchanged and hand-checked by the adversary: the trader $T_f$ buys $s_k = f\theta\mathcal W_{k-1}/\max(\mathsf p,\delta)$ shares of $\varphi_{d_k}$ on pressed scheduled days; window-disjointness bounds its plausible worth below by $-1$; the criterion bounds it above by $C_f := 1 + C_{T_f}$; per-round factors give $\mathcal W_K\ge(1+f(\tfrac1p-1))^{k_1}(1-f)^{k_0}$; logarithms give $k_1\le r(f,p)k_0 + K_f$ with $K_f := \log C_f/\log(1+f(\tfrac1p-1))$. Legality needs the press on $d_k$ as a coefficient computable from stages $<d_k$: **(H-blind)**, now a stated hypothesis (adversary attack 5.4).

*Why it is a family, not a theorem with the odds slope.* Put $x := \tfrac1p - 1 > 0$. $r(f,p) > \tfrac1x = \tfrac p{1-p}$ iff $x\log\tfrac1{1-f} > \log(1+fx)$ iff $(1-f)^{-x} > 1+fx$, which is Bernoulli's inequality $(1+y)^a\ge 1+ay$ with $y=-f$, $a=-x<0$, strict for $f>0$. So every trader in the family has slope strictly above the odds (`slope_intercept.out` confirms at every $f$ tested). $K_f$ depends on $C_{T_f}$, a different trader's constant for each $f$; nothing in the criterion relates them, and for fixed $C$, $K_f\sim\log C\cdot\frac{p}{(1-p)f}\to\infty$.

*Equivalence with (G-av).* Fix $f$ and suppose good feedback on $u^{\mathrm{def}}$ with $k_0(N)\to\infty$ and $\limsup_N k_1(N)/k_0(N)\le\frac p{1-p}$ (which is 4.3.8 on the defiance class when the credences on defied days are $\le p$). Then $k_1 - r k_0\le\big(\frac p{1-p}+o(1) - r\big)k_0\to-\infty$, so $\sup_N(k_1 - rk_0)<\infty$: the inequality holds for all large $N$, and a finite prefix supplies $K_f$. Conversely the family over $f$ gives $\limsup k_1/k_0\le\inf_f r(f,p) = \frac p{1-p}$. So the family is the finite-$N$ dress of Unbiasedness From Feedback (with the slight difference that 4.3.8 averages credences while the record counts saturated days — a bookkeeping gap the margin $\delta$ absorbs). `envelope.out`: on a synthetic record with $k_1/k_0\uparrow\frac p{1-p}$ from below, the $f = 0.1$ inequality with $C = 2$ has $0$ violations over $k_0\le 2\cdot10^5$.

*Envelope under a uniform cap (counterfactual).* If $C_{T_f}\le C$ for all $f$, minimising $r(f,p)k_0 + K_f$ over $f$ gives $\frac p{1-p}k_0 + \Theta(\sqrt{k_0})$ (`envelope.out`: excess$/\sqrt{k_0}$ stabilises at $0.28$ for $C=2$, $0.51$ for $C=10$; argmin $f\sim k_0^{-1/2}$). That is the LIL fluctuation of Statement 1(c) recovered from the criterion — but only under a uniformity the criterion does not provide. $\blacksquare$
