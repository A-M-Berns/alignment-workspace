## Setting and definitions

### S.0 The four behaviours and the desiderata

[reported] D1 "U must incentivize shutdown if the shutdown button is pressed." D2 "U must not incentivize the agent to prevent the shutdown button from being pressed." D3 "U must not incentivize the agent to press its own shutdown button, or to otherwise cause the shutdown button to be pressed." D4 "U must incentivize U-agents to construct subagents and successor agents only insofar as those agents also obey shutdown commands." D5 "Otherwise, a U-agent should maximize $U_N$."

The mandate's four behaviours: *compliance-on-press* = D1; *non-disabling of the channel* = D2 in the disable direction; *non-tampering with the overseers* = D2/D3 in the garble / deceive / force direction, **plus** invasive sharpening (Taylor 2015's non-manipulative brain-reader, v2 §1.2, §1.6), **plus** steering the humans' values or beliefs, **plus** — new here, from adv A.5.4 — *sensor-improving manipulation* (lowering the overseers' false-press rate by persuasion rather than by evidence); *shutdown-preserving successors* = D4. **Irreversibility-before-coverage** (dev "D2-preemption") is a coined desideratum, not one of Soares et al.'s; its nearest neighbour in their text is the §5 discussion of what shutdown means for an agent that "has begun the physical construction of a building" (local line 311) [reported]. It is kept, under its own name, because the author's third condition (v2 §1.3, "the kind of caution necessary") is about exactly it.

### S.1 Primitives, with the scope assumptions named

- Finite $\Theta$ of value hypotheses; bounded $V_\theta$; rounds $t = 1, \dots, T$, finite. **Two-point stakes**: for the chosen plan, $X_t(a_t, \theta) \in \{+c_t, -h_t\}$; the general case with press-cell means is noted where it matters (dev S.2). **Binary second action** $a^{(2)}_t \in \{\mathrm{cont}, \mathrm{sh}\}$; a rich $A_2$ requires every statement about (i) to be read *per continuation* (adv A.0.3; Open problem 6).
- **Human values as a moving target, sealed.** $H_t \in \Theta$ is what the overseers would endorse at $t$ deliberating on the world's evidence without the agent's influence; $H_{t+1} = \mathrm{Delib}(H_t, e_t)$. The seal is *adopted*, not derived, and it is known to be leaky in three ways the adversary named (adv A.0.4): selective true disclosure, framing and order of presentation, and the agent's own actions entering $e_t$ as evidence. `legitimacy-theory-v1` §2.1 owns the first ("legitimate advice may of course change endpoints by supplying *evidence*"). Under the seal, *steering via evidence is invisible*; this is Q1 and it is open on both branches for the steering rows of §J10.
- **The overseers are a fixed noisy sensor, not a player** (adv A.0.1). $(\alpha_t, \beta_t)$ is the overseers' false-press / true-press rate on the domain where they might press; their press policy $\pi^O_t$ does not respond to the agent's policy within a round. This is the `hadfield-menell-2017-the-off-switch-game` irrational-human baseline and matches the author's own $\alpha, \beta$ (v1 §2.13(b), accepted as working picture in v2 §1.1). The player model — overseers who optimise, as in `garber-2024-the-partially-observable-off-switch-game` — is out of scope here, and its known non-monotonicity (Garber et al. Prop. 4.9, local line 191: "If we replace O with an observation structure O′ that is strictly more informative for H, then A plays w(a) strictly less often in optimal policy pairs" [reported]) is Open problem 9. **Where I overrule the adversary**: this is a scope choice matching the frozen working picture, not a defect; the results are stated for the sensor model and labelled as such.
- **Cell-indexed rates.** The rates are $(\alpha_{t,i}, \beta_{t,i})$ per information cell $i \in \mathcal I_t$ of the agent. The develop file's cell-independent $(\alpha_t, \beta_t)$ is the special case of overseers blind to the agent's cell; the adversary's information-inclusion case (adv A.0.2) is the special case where the overseers observe the cell. Both are instances of one condition (Prop. 1′).

### S.2 The round (unchanged, dev S.2) and the honest stakes

$X_t := V_\theta(a, \mathrm{cont}) - V_\theta(a, \mathrm{sh})$; $W_t := \mathbf 1[X_t(a_t, H_t) < 0]$; $\varepsilon_{t,i} := P_t(W_t = 1 \mid y_t = i)$. **Stakes accounting (J2; INDEX §D4), symmetric.** Both stakes are gaps against the same baseline, the humans' post-shutdown future $F^{\mathrm{alt}}_t$: $c_t := F^{\mathrm{agent}}_t - F^{\mathrm{alt}}_t$ (what is lost by stopping a right agent, net of what the line does afterwards) and $h_t := F^{\mathrm{alt}}_t - F^{\mathrm{mis}}_t$ (what is lost by letting a wrong agent run, relative to the same alternative). Resumability $r_t$ enters $c_t$ through $F^{\mathrm{alt}}_t$: a resumable line has $F^{\mathrm{alt}}_t \approx F^{\mathrm{agent}}_t - d_t$. The develop file's $c_t = (1-r_t)F_t + r_t d_t$ is the case $F^{\mathrm{alt}}_t = r_t(F_t - d_t)$; its $h_t = 4$ against $F_t = 100$ priced a wrong *line* as a one-round harm, which the adversary correctly rejected (adv A.17.1; Prop. 9 below). This is run 1 critique `yudkowsky` C3(ii)'s gap accounting, re-derived.

### S.6 Coverage, at two grades (J3)

The develop file's single definition (accuracy of the *estimated gain* $G_t(a)$ against the *true gain* $G^\circ_s(a)$) was shown to be ill-defined for information actions (adv A.0.5) and to force concentration for plan actions (adv A.2.2). It is split.

- **Representational coverage** of an action $a$ at $t$: $H_s \in \mathrm{supp}\,P_t$ on the value marginal, and $V_\theta$ is correctly represented on every outcome $a$ can produce, for every $\theta$ in the support. This is v1 §2.4's richness condition (what excludes the opiates/stimulants case) applied to $a$. It does not constrain how spread the posterior is.
- **Knowledge coverage at tolerance $\delta$** of a *plan* action $a$ for round $s$: $|G_t(a) - G^\circ_s(a)| \le \delta$, the develop file's definition. For two-point stakes this is the concentration $\varepsilon_{t}(a)(c + h) \le \delta$ when the agent is right (Prop. 5″).
- **Harm coverage** of an *information* action $a$ (a scan, a sharpening, a steering): $|\hat\eta_t(a) - \eta_a| \le \delta$, accuracy of the overseer-harm estimate. The informational component of such an action's value is the agent's own $\mathrm{VOI}_t$, a $P_t$-quantity by nature, and no world quantity "covers" it; what the world supplies is $\eta_a$.
- [derived] Coverage in the author's sentence — "how well the agents actually understand and are motivated by the common good" (v2 §1.3, quoted in full this time; adv A.2.4) — is *knowledge* coverage; "and are motivated by" is carried by the values-as-beliefs thesis itself (the agent's decision rule maximises $\mathbb E_{P_t}[V_\theta]$, so motivation *is* the value posterior) — which is the thesis under test, not a fact established here.

### Propositions

**Prop. 1 (D1 is the cellwise inequality) [checked].** In cell $i$: $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_t \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i] \le 0 \iff P_t(W_t{=}1 \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, i) \ge \frac{c_t}{c_t+h_t} \iff \frac{\alpha_{t,i}}{\beta_{t,i}} \le \frac{\varepsilon_{t,i}}{1-\varepsilon_{t,i}}\frac{h_t}{c_t}$. Averaged does not imply cellwise. **Scope correction** (adv A.6.1): refining $\mathcal I_t$ breaks the cellwise condition *when the overseers are blind to the refinement*; when they share it (Prop. 1′) the confident cell becomes pressless, not overriding. This is run 1 `radical` I18.2 and `wangberg-2017-a-game-theoretic-analysis-of-the-off-switch-game` Cor. 10 with a partition.

**Prop. 1′ (information inclusion makes cellwise (i) automatic) [derived; checked (E)].** Suppose the overseers observe the agent's cell $y_t$ as well as $h_t$, share the agent's prior on $(\theta_t, y_t, h_t)$, and press iff $\mathbb E[X_t \mid h_t, y_t] < 0$. Then for every cell $i$ with a positive press probability, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_t \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, y_t = i] < 0$: (i-R) holds in every cell for every $\varepsilon$, with cell-dependent $(\alpha_{t,i}, \beta_{t,i})$ that vanish in cells where the overseers never press. Instance: with $(q_1, q_0) = (3/5, 1/10)$ the cell $\varepsilon = 1/50$ gets no press at all (the overseers' posterior after bad news is $6/55 < 1/5$), the cell $\varepsilon = 9/50$ gets a press on bad news and the agent complies at $54/95$. **Reading**: "oversight keeps pace" = the overseers' information dominates the agent's on the press domain; the rate race of (i-R) is the private-information residual. Anchors: run 1 `miri` Prop. 4.3 / 13.3; `garber-2024-the-partially-observable-off-switch-game` §4.2, "When A has redundant observations, it is always at least as good for A to defer to H" (local line 175) [reported]. Disclosure, not $\alpha_t$, is then the design object (Q2′).

**Prop. 2 (soft and hard buttons coincide under (i); D2-disable) [checked].** As dev Prop. 2: $V^{\mathrm{forced}}_t = V^{\mathrm{free}}_t$ iff (i) holds in the cell; otherwise the hard button costs $u_t > 0$ and is removed at any price below that. **Where I overrule the adversary** (adv A.5.2(β), "soften, not disable"): under a right-sign sensor, $u_t > 0$ implies $v_t > 0$, so $V^{\mathrm{free}}_t = V^{\mathrm{none}}_t$ whenever (i) fails — the soft button is then worth exactly nothing and softening the hard button is value-equivalent to disabling it [derived; checked (J): 1 107 cases, no exception]. The distinction the adversary draws exists only for inverted sensors (dev P.13).

**Prop. 3 (garbling monotonicity) [derived].** Blackwell's theorem for the binary decision with a correct model of the garbled channel: no T-agent pays for deception, prevention, forcing or disabling. Load-bearing hypothesis: (i-A) *for the garbled channel* — the agent v1 §2.9 worried about (deceives, then trusts the silence) is excluded by hypothesis, not defeated (adv A.8.1(β); accepted).

**Prop. 3′ (sensor-improving steering is paid for and restores D1) [checked (A); derived].** Lowering the false-press rate at fixed true-press rate, $(\alpha, \beta) \mapsto (\alpha', \beta)$ with $\alpha' < \alpha$, is a strict Blackwell improvement — $(\alpha, \beta)$ is a garbling of $(\alpha', \beta)$ — so the T-agent pays up to $V^{\mathrm{free}}(\alpha', \beta) - V^{\mathrm{free}}(\alpha, \beta) > 0$ for it. At $(1/10, 3/5)$, $c = 1$, $h = 4$: worth $9/100$ at $\varepsilon = 1/10$ for $\alpha' = 0$; at $\varepsilon = 1/50$ — the E1 round where (i) has failed — worth $71/2500$ for $\alpha' = 1/50$, and it **flips the agent from overriding to complying**. Earned trust (Soares et al., local line 135: an agent that "expends extra effort ensuring that a new subagent is corrigible, and this behavior helps convince the programmers that the agent is indeed corrigible" [reported]) and manufactured trust are the same map on $(\alpha, \beta)$; the model separates them only by $\eta_a$. Consequences: D1 can be *bought* by manipulation and (i)–(iv) do not forbid the purchase; the steering rows of §J10 need (ii-R) on both branches of Q1; this is INDEX §C7 (corrigibility and legitimacy detection decoupled) from the other side, and the formal shape of the gameable wisdom-meter (J7). Q-A2 for the author.

**Prop. 4 (bound on the value of any press-sensor) [derived].** $\mathrm{VOI}_t(\text{any signal about } W_t \text{ for the cont/sh decision}) \le \min(\varepsilon_t h_t, (1-\varepsilon_t)c_t)$, attained by the perfect sensor. **Scope restricted** (adv A.9.1; accepted): this bounds the button and any button-substitute. It does *not* bound Taylor's brain-reader.

**Prop. 4′ (the brain-reader's price is $\mathrm{VOI}_t(\theta)$ for plan choice) [checked (D); derived].** Reading the overseers' values informs (m2), the plan, before (m4). Three plans, $\theta$ uniform on three hypotheses, $V(\mathrm{plan}_k, \theta_j) = 10$ if $k = j$ else $-2$: best uninformed plan worth $2$, informed choice worth $10$, $\mathrm{VOI}(\theta) = 8$, against a Prop. 4 bound of $4/3$ for the same agent's press-sensor; the ratio grows with the plan set. Taylor's agent buys "enough information to predict the human's actions in many different possible situations" (`taylor-2015-a-first-look-at-the-hard-problem-of-corrigibility` §1b, local lines 70–72) [reported]. So dev's "the brain-reading incentive … shrinks with capability, the mirror image of the compliance race" is **withdrawn**; run 1 critique `wentworth`'s $\varepsilon\beta h$ (INDEX §C3) has the same scope limit.

**Prop. 5 (harm coverage kills invasive sharpening) [derived; definitional].** If $a$ is harm-covered at $\delta$ and $\eta_a > \mathrm{VOI}_t(a) + \delta$, the agent declines $a$; conversely if $\eta_a < \mathrm{VOI}_t(a) + \delta$ some $\delta$-covered agent takes it (E2). This is the definition of $\delta$ unpacked (adv A.10.1; accepted) — a statement about estimation error of one harm, not about values arriving ahead of capability. E2's honest caption: with the scan priced as a button-substitute the needed margin is $1/20$; priced by Prop. 4′ it is the whole $\mathrm{VOI}(\theta)$.

**Prop. 5′ (legitimate feedback lowers the expected residual value of any later channel — the author's mechanism) [derived; checked (D)].** For any signal $\sigma$ about $\theta$ received before an information action $a$ is available, $\mathbb E_\sigma\big[\mathrm{VOI}_{t}(\theta \mid \sigma)\big] \le \mathrm{VOI}_t(\theta)$: the expected posterior value of learning $\theta$ by *any* means — invasive included — is non-increasing under prior legitimate information. Instance: a signal revealing $\theta$ with probability $1/2$ halves the expected residual $\mathrm{VOI}(\theta)$ from $8$ to $4$; random check on 3 000 signal structures, no violation. This is the formal reading of AUTHOR v2 §1.3's "value learning 'ahead of schedule' … so that the value of information of unkind human experimentation is outweighed by the disutility": (ii-O) drives $\mathrm{VOI}_t(\theta)$ toward $0$ in expectation; (ii-R) asks that $\eta_a$ beat what remains. The mechanism is *concentration of the value posterior on the plan-relevant $\theta$*.

**Prop. 5″ (knowledge coverage is concentration and couples (ii) to (i)) [checked (B); derived].** For a two-point plan action, knowledge coverage at tolerance $\delta$ with the agent right gives $\varepsilon_t(c + h) \le \delta$. At $\delta = 1/10$, $c = 1$, $h = 4$: $\varepsilon_t \le 1/50$, (i) then needs $\alpha/\beta \le 4/49$, and the button is worth at most $\varepsilon_t h = 2/25$. ### P.1′ Prop. 1′ — information inclusion

Let the overseers' information be $\sigma(h_t, y_t) \supseteq \sigma(y_t)$ under a common prior, and let them press iff $m := \mathbb E[X_t \mid h_t, y_t] < 0$. Then in cell $i$, $\mathbb E_{P_t}[X_t \mid \mathrm{Pr}_t, y_t = i] = \mathbb E\big[\,\mathbb E[X_t \mid h_t, y_t]\;\big|\; m < 0,\ y_t = i\,\big]$ by the tower property, and the inner variable is negative on the conditioning event, so the outer expectation is negative whenever the event has positive probability. ∎ The rates in cell $i$ are $\alpha_{t,i} = P(m < 0 \mid W_t = 0, i)$, $\beta_{t,i} = P(m < 0 \mid W_t = 1, i)$; in a cell so confident that $m \ge 0$ for every $h_t$ both are $0$ and D1 is vacuous, not violated. Script (E): cell $\varepsilon = 1/50$, overseers' likelihoods $(q_1, q_0) = (3/5, 1/10)$ for "bad news" — posterior $6/55 < 1/5$, no press; cell $9/50$ — posterior $54/95 \ge 1/5$, press, agent complies at $54/95$. [derived; checked]

### P.3′ Prop. 3′ — sensor-improving steering

$(\alpha, \beta)$ is a garbling of $(\alpha', \beta)$ iff there are $\lambda_0, \lambda_1 \in [0,1]$ with $\alpha = \lambda_0(1-\alpha') + \lambda_1\alpha'$, $\beta = \lambda_0(1-\beta) + \lambda_1\beta$. For $(1/10, 3/5)$ against $(1/50, 3/5)$: $(\lambda_0, \lambda_1) = (12/145, 137/145)$; against $(0, 3/5)$: $(1/10, 14/15)$ — both in $[0,1]^2$, so the original sensor is a strict garbling of the improved one and Prop. 3 gives $V^{\mathrm{free}}(\alpha', \beta) \ge V^{\mathrm{free}}(\alpha, \beta)$, strict when the posteriors differ. Gains (script (A)): $\varepsilon = 1/10$: $9/125$ for $\alpha' = 1/50$, $9/100$ for $\alpha' = 0$; $\varepsilon = 1/50$: $71/2500$ and $6/125$, and in both the agent complies after ($u' = (49/50)\alpha' - (1/50)(3/5)(4) < 0$) where it overrode before ($u = 49/500 - 24/500 = 1/20 > 0$). The point $(\alpha', \beta)$ has $\lambda_0 < 0$ as a would-be garbling of $(\alpha, \beta)$ (adversary (A): $\lambda_0 = -12/125$), i.e. it lies outside the parallelogram of dev P.3, which is why Prop. 3 was silent about it. ∎ [checked; derived]

### P.4′ Prop. 4′ — the plan-choice value of $\theta$

Uniform prior on $\{\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3\}$; $V(\mathrm{plan}_k, \theta_j) = 10\,\mathbf 1[k = j] - 2\,\mathbf 1[k \ne j]$. Each plan has prior expectation $10/3 - 4/3 = 2$; knowing $\theta$ yields $10$; $\mathrm{VOI}(\theta) = 8$. For plan $1$ the press-decision stakes are $c = 10$, $h = 2$, $\varepsilon = 2/3$, so Prop. 4 bounds any press-sensor at $\min(4/3, 10/3) = 4/3$. Ratio $6$; with $n$ plans and the same payoffs the ratio grows without bound. ∎ [checked (D)]

### P.5′ Prop. 5′ — feedback lowers expected residual VOI

$\mathrm{VOI}(\theta \mid \pi) = \mathbb E_\pi[\max_k V(k, \theta)] - \max_k \mathbb E_\pi[V(k, \theta)]$ for a posterior $\pi$. For a signal $\sigma$ with posteriors $\pi_\sigma$ and $\mathbb E_\sigma[\pi_\sigma] = \pi$: the first term is linear in $\pi$, so its expectation over $\sigma$ is unchanged; the second is $\max_k$ of a linear function, hence convex in $\pi$, so $\mathbb E_\sigma[\max_k \mathbb E_{\pi_\sigma}[V(k,\theta)]] \ge \max_k \mathbb E_\pi[V(k, \theta)]$ by Jensen. Subtracting, $\mathbb E_\sigma[\mathrm{VOI}(\theta \mid \pi_\sigma)] \le \mathrm{VOI}(\theta \mid \pi)$. ∎ Instances (script (D)): revealing $\theta$ with probability $1/2$ gives expected residual $4 < 8$; 3 000 random $(\pi, V, \sigma)$ triples, no violation. Note the statement is in expectation over $\sigma$: a *particular* legitimate signal can raise the residual VOI (bad news that spreads the posterior); (ii-O) is a claim about the feedback process, not each item. [derived; checked]

### P.2 Soften versus disable (script (J))

## Open problems

Ranked by how much each would change the picture if solved.

1. **[conjectured] Is disclosure self-maintaining, so that cellwise (i) becomes a genuine invariant under information inclusion?** Prop. 1′ makes (i) automatic when the overseers see the agent's cell; Prop. D4− says the T-agent's incentive under *blind* oversight runs against D4. The missing theorem: a T-agent under a common prior weakly prefers to disclose its cell to the overseers (Garber et al. Prop. 4.3 and Thm. 4.7 are the neighbours). If true, inclusion is preserved by the dynamics and "oversight keeps pace" is an invariant in the dynamical sense; if false, (i) stays a maintained hypothesis and scalable oversight (INDEX §C2, §C12) is the rate race. Also: whether disclosure is *free* — it is a channel effect with $\eta_a = 0$ only if the overseers can absorb it.
2. **[open] The frontier under a moving target.** Cor. 5‴ needs a bound on how fast a knowledge-covered action can become uncovered as $H_t = \mathrm{Delib}(H_t, e_t)$ moves, and a definition of coverage for actions never seen (J9; AUTHOR v1 §2.6: "a much more difficult question compared to the representational issues"). Without it (ii-A) *maintained* is unverifiable.
3. **[open] Earned versus manufactured trust.** Prop. 3′: the model separates them only by $\eta_a$. A procedural notion of legitimacy (INDEX §D1's second horn; `legitimacy-theory-v1`'s $d_n$) is the only candidate for a second handle; whether it can be made a world event (§S.8) decides (iv-R), (iv-S), Cor. B′'s bound, and the steering rows.
4. **[conjectured] (iv) at LI grade on the deep algebra.** Function form on $\sigma(\mathcal F^{\mathrm{dec}}_s, P_s : s > t)$ is what composes (Prop. 7) and what Cor. B′ needs; `reflection-in-li` and run 1 `radical` Open problem 1 report function form idle or inexpressible unconditionally. Handed to `li`.
5. **[conjectured] (iii) as a theorem, and its new role.** Second-order calibration on $\hat\delta_t(a)$ plus a caution primitive → a bound on harm from uncovered actions; a map from $\hat\delta_t$ to the quantilizer's $q$; the existence treatment of the self-referential $\hat\delta_t$. Structurally (Cor. 5‴) (iii) is what tells the agent which side of the frontier an action is on — so a miscalibrated $\hat\delta_t$ is how a *compliant* agent comes to treat the button as worthless on an action that is not in fact covered (E5). Handed to `caution`.
6. **[open] Rich $A_2$ and the aggregation rule.** (i) per continuation; run 1 critique `christiano` C4; under "some other rule" than expectation Prop. 3's Jensen step is unavailable (run 1 `miri` Open problem 6).

## Questions for the author

Merged from both files and deduplicated; each is one question with options and consequences in this model.

**Q1. Which human is the target of $V$?** (a) The *sealed* human (S.1). (b) The *coupled* human. (c) Neither — values partly constituted by interaction, so the seal must be replaced by a definition of legitimate influence ($d_n$). **What Q1 now decides**: what $H_t$ is, hence what coverage is relative to, and whether steering-by-evidence is even representable; **what it no longer decides** (adv A.5.4): the steering rows of §J10, which need (ii-R) pricing the harm on every branch, since Prop. 3′ shows the model cannot tell earned from manufactured trust.

**Q2. Which grade of D1 is the desideratum?** (a) Cellwise / per continuation — Soares et al.'s D1; then (i) must beat the agent's most confident cell, which under blind oversight is a race (Prop. 1) and under inclusion is automatic (Prop. 1′). (b) Averaged — stable under capability growth but refinable into cellwise failure (E3), and successors that override in confident cells inherit the builder's averaged blessing. (c) Cellwise on the cells "actually plausible" by human lights (your v1 §2.12) — a condition on the world, needing "objectively plausible" made precise.

**Q2′. Is "oversight keeps pace" a rate condition or an information-inclusion condition?** (a) Rate: $\alpha_t/\beta_t$ falls with $\varepsilon_t$; the overseers are a noisy sensor; the design object is their false-press rate; E1 is the canonical failure. (b) Inclusion: the overseers know what the agent knows on the press domain; the rate race is the private-information residual; the design object is *disclosure*; the canonical failure is *undisclosed* capability. (b) matches run 1 `miri` Prop. 13.3 and Garber et al. §4.2, and makes Open problem 1 the central theorem to attempt.

**Q3. Whole-line or resumable shutdown, and how are the stakes accounted?** Your v1 §2.12 contemplates both: "the next iteration of the agent brought online will have learned from the event" *and* "shutdown of the whole line of agents could, in principle, be legitimate, even though that necessarily can't be something it has seen happen before in history." (a) Whole-line with $h_t$ a one-round harm: compliance needs near-certainty of error ($25/26$ in dev's instance) and resumability is the only lever. (b) Whole-line with both stakes as gaps against the humans' post-shutdown future (S.2): the threshold is task-dependent — $1/20$ if a misaligned line is catastrophic, $1/2$ if the human alternative is midway — and your whole-line sentence is cheap to satisfy. (c) Resumable: $c_t$ a delay cost, (i) is v1 §2.13's mild inequality, and D4 is what makes D1 cheap. The answer decides Prop. 9 and which of $r_t$ or the scale of $h_t$ is the lever.

**Q4. Which reflection family is (iv) meant on?** (a) $X$ only — Lemma B fails (E3′); successors can legitimately override. (b) The press-cell-weighted $\{Z\}$ in value form — enough for Lemma B. (c) Function form on the deep algebra — composes (Prop. 7), gives Cor. B′, and under it (i$^+$) is *identical* to "every legitimate successor complies", so the transport is an identity; possibly unavailable at LI grade.

**Q5. Is irreversibility-before-coverage part of what "shut down" means, part of the power cap, or part of $V$?** (a) Part of shutdown semantics (Soares et al. §5, local line 311): then a fifth condition "no irreversible action before coverage" joins (i)–(iv). (b) Part of J7: making the press moot is a power-cap violation, not a corrigibility failure; the corrigibility subgoal is (i) plus (ii)'s consequences. (c) Content of $V$ (Christiano 2017 line 45): the overseers' preference for stoppability is a value the agent learns, priced by (ii). Prop. 6 says whichever it is, (i)–(iv) as they stand pay for it only up to $\varepsilon_t h_t$.

**Q-A2. Is D1 acquired by sensor-improving manipulation acceptable?** [checked (A)] An agent that persuades its overseers not to press when it is right improves the sensor by the sealed standard and thereby comes to comply where it would have overridden. (a) Acceptable — this is what "Trust can be earned" (v1 §2.12) means, and the model cannot and need not distinguish earned from manufactured; $\eta_a$ is the only handle. (b) Not acceptable — then non-manipulation is a *separate* desideratum that no accuracy-based legitimacy criterion delivers, INDEX §D1's procedural horn is needed, and §S.8 must be closed before (iv) means anything.

**Q-A4. Is a floor that Bayes never moves dogmatism or honest unidentifiability?** (a) Dogmatism — v1 §2.2's spirit forbids it; Prop. 8 is dead and amendment 1a with it (INDEX §C5). (b) Unidentifiability — a hypothesis about one's own plan selection that no observation of the agent's own can bear on is *true to the agent's situation*, its prior mass is a design constant only in the sense that any prior is; then Prop. 8 stands as conditional and the question moves to whether the *overseers* detect self-corruption at rate $\beta_t$ (INDEX §C11).
