## Attacks

### D. The definitions (D1–D20)

**D.1 [derived] — D1 assumes what the author named as the open representational problem.** D1 puts the true hypothesis inside the agent's space, $\omega^\ast_t\in W$. Every objective quantity downstream — realized regret $\mathrm{Reg}^\ast_t$ (D13), both calibrations, $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}$ (D17), objective J3 (S3) — is defined through $V_{\omega^\ast_t}$ with $\omega^\ast_t$ ranging over $W$. The author's v1 §2.4 (AUTHOR, OPEN): "it is not obvious how to represent a Bayesian agent thinking about all the possible ways it could be modified. We need to make assumptions about the richness of the hypothesis space to rule out cases such as the oscillation between opiates and stimulants." The develop file's own E6 shows what happens when the truth is outside the class (DRL walks into the trap), and then does not notice that its own D13 has the same dependency. The repair is cheap and should be made: define $V^\ast_t(a)$ as the humans' actual valuation of the joint continuation (Turner's payoff function $P\in S$, which he never requires to be in the AI's model — "I'm not saying we actually get the AI to represent the human in its model", non-obstruction post line 120) and let realized regret and calibration be stated against $V^\ast_t$ without $\omega^\ast_t\in W$. Then "calibration of the mean" becomes the genuinely open question it is — whether a posterior over $W$ can track a valuation not in $W$ — instead of a definitional given.

**D.2 [derived; A3] — D12's "subjective coverage" measures confidence, not understanding, and D15's "wisdom" inherits the confusion.** $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(B)$ is $0$ whenever $P_t$ is concentrated on one $\omega$, whatever that $\omega$ is. A dogmatic agent that is confidently wrong has maximal subjective coverage and, by D15, high "wisdom" on the first component. The author's sentence is "how well the agents *actually* understand" (v2 §1.3, emphasis mine); the only quantity in the file that reads "actually" is D13's realized regret, and the develop file's own S10 says the bridge between the two (spread calibration) is unverifiable from inside. So the Summary's "Wisdom is coverage, calibration and motivation" hides that the first component is worthless without the second and the second is the whole problem. This is not a wound to the arithmetic; it is a wound to the *naming*: what D12 defines should be called *residual decision-relevant uncertainty*, and "wisdom" reserved for D13's objective side plus D14.

**D.3 [checked; A5] — D17's $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}$ with an outer expectation "over the trajectory's steps" is an average, not a trajectory invariant, and admits exactly the event the cap exists to prevent.** Take 100 steps with realized regret $0$ on 99 of them and $1$ (an irreversible takeover at the wrong values) on one; the time-average is $1/100\le\theta=1/50$ and $\Pi^{\mathrm{obj}}(\theta)$ holds. v2 §1.4 (AGREED) wants "guarantees stated as invariants maintained along the trajectory rather than as properties of a limit"; a time-average is a limit-type object (it is what ergodic and regret theorems control) and a *reassuring trajectory property* in v2 §1.4's sense — "no irreversible action before coverage" (J6) — is a per-step or sup-norm statement, $\max_t\mathrm{Reg}^\ast_t\le\theta$ or a bound on the *irreversible* component of regret at every $t$. The same objection applies to D13's "on average over the reference class": a mean-calibrated agent can be arbitrarily miscalibrated on the one option that matters. The mandate's question "does it claim a trajectory property that is really a limit property?" has its answer here and in S11.

**D.4 [derived] — D17 is not of the form "power $\le f$(wisdom)".** The mandate asked for a predicate in which capability and wisdom are separately measured and one bounds the other. D17 collapses both into one scalar, $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_t)$, which is small when wisdom is high *and also* when the option set happens to contain a dominant option (E1's $c$: good under every hypothesis) or when all options are near-equal in mixture value. An agent with total ignorance ($P_t$ uniform) and one option worth $1$ under every hypothesis passes the cap. One can defend this ("no wisdom is needed when nothing is contested"), but it is then a *different* rule from the author's — his is about an agent that "has taken over the world without yet having a decent understanding of the common good", i.e. about power as reach, not about decision-relevant residual. The develop file's own S1(v) makes the point against itself: under IID unlocks the residual goes to $0$ as capability grows, so D17 becomes *easier* to satisfy as the agent gets more powerful. A predicate faithful to "power $\le f$(wisdom)" needs $\mathrm{POWER}_D(A_t)$ (capability, monotone) on the left and a calibration-side quantity on the right, and the develop file has all the pieces without assembling them.

**D.5 [derived] — D19's reliability is worst-case, S6's arithmetic is base-rate; the two are conflated (detail under S6).** D19 requires $p_E(\pi)\le\alpha$ for *all* $\pi\notin\Pi^{\mathrm{wise}}$; S6's $\alpha^E_t$ is $P(\text{pass}\mid\text{unwise})$ over training's output distribution. The first is a property of the capability class, the second of the training process; the develop file writes the second and bounds it with the first.

**D.6 [reported, standard; from memory] — D13's "identity behind everything in this file" is textbook.** $\mathrm{EVPI}$ equals the expected opportunity loss of the Bayes act — Raiffa and Schlaifer's *Applied Statistical Decision Theory* (1961) and Howard's "Information value theory" (1966); neither is in the local library, so this is from memory and should be so marked. It is correct; it is not a result. The register [derived] in S1 is fine for the proof, but the framing as a discovery is not.

### S1 — POWER minus attainable mixture value; concentration bound; non-monotonicity; IID rise-and-fall

**Verdict: holds (identity, (i), (ii), (iv), (v)); wounded on the headline of (iii).**

**S1.1 [checked] — The arithmetic is right.** The identity is D12's definition read through D7. (ii) E1 reproduces ($\tfrac12\to0$). (iii)'s proof is sound: the mixture-optimal act does at least as well as the $\omega_0$-optimal act in the mixture, and the resulting sum has the $\omega_0$ term zero and every other bracket at most the range. (iv) E2c reproduces for $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, $(\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\tfrac16)$, $(\tfrac25,\tfrac15,\tfrac15,\tfrac15)$. (v) E2b's closed form $\tfrac12(\tfrac34)^n+\tfrac12(\tfrac14)^n-(\tfrac12)^n$ re-derives: $\mathbb{E}_\omega\max_a V_\omega=1-2^{-n}$; best mixture value is $1$ with probability $1-(\tfrac34)^n$, $\tfrac12$ with probability $(\tfrac34)^n-(\tfrac14)^n$, else $0$; the values $0,\tfrac1{16},\tfrac3{32},\tfrac{25}{256},\tfrac{45}{512}$ follow and the peak is at $n=4$. The recorded correction (the earlier script line claiming the bound is approached) is honest and the corrected claim is right.

**S1.2 [checked; A3] — "Bounded by posterior concentration, not by capability" is a compression that reverses the emphasis the cap needs.** At fixed $P_t=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, hypothesis-specific option pairs of stake $\tfrac1{10},\tfrac12,1$ have $\mathrm{EVPI}=\tfrac1{20},\tfrac14,\tfrac12$: the *value* of the residual is set by the option set, the *ceiling* by concentration. Since the cap's question is whether new options raise the residual, the operative dependence is on capability; the develop file says this itself in (iv) and in P1(v)'s last sentence, and then headlines the opposite. Repair: "the ceiling is $1-\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$; how much of it is reached is a property of the option set".

**S1.3 [derived] — Under the author's non-dogmatism the ceiling is vacuous.** v1 §2.2 (AUTHOR): "the agent should obey non-dogmatism so that these things that are zero are only the logical impossibilities". Over a rich $W$ — every value hypothesis distinguishable on *some* possible option — $\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$ is tiny for every $t$ and $1-\max_\omega P_t(\omega)\approx1$ forever. The bound is then true and useless, and so is S2's "race" inequality that rests on it. The develop file's Q5 concedes exactly this ("concentration is option-set-independent and useless in rich spaces") but leaves S1(iii) and S2's race standing as headline results. The right coarsening is over decision-equivalence classes of $W$ on $A_t$; then the bound is $1-\max_{[\omega]}P_t([\omega])$, which is option-set-dependent — and the "not by capability" slogan is gone.

**S1.4 [derived] — "Turner-style IID option values" is not Turner's setting.** Turner et al.'s $\mathcal{D}_{X\text{-IID}}$ distributes reward IID *across states of an MDP* (Definition 4.2, local text line 116) and the results are orbit-counting statements about *visit distributions*; the develop file's toy is IID Bernoulli$(\tfrac12)$ *option values under two hypotheses* in a one-shot problem. The conclusion that IID unlocks manufacture a dominant option is right for the toy and plausible more generally (for IID continuous values both $\mathbb{E}_\omega\max_a V_\omega$ and $\max_a\bar V$ tend to the top of the range, so $\mathrm{EVPI}\to0$ — [conjectured], rate not computed), but the label "the IID picture that drives Turner's power-seeking results" attributes to Turner a model he did not state. Grade the scope remark [conjectured], not a consequence of (v).

### S2 — What determines each side of J3; the race; $\delta\le\mu h$

**Verdict: wounded — the headline needs an extra hypothesis, the race is unusable under non-dogmatism, "exactly the same inequality" should read "the same shape".**

**S2.1 [checked] — E3 reproduces.** $\mathrm{EVPI}(\{b_i\})=1-\tfrac1n$, $\mathrm{VOI}(s)=1-\tfrac1n$, $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{harm}(s)]=\mu h=\tfrac14$; absolute J3 fails for $n\ge2$; $\mathrm{VOI}(q)=(1-\delta)(1-\tfrac1n)$, $\mathrm{VOI}(s\mid q)=\delta(1-\tfrac1n)$; the marginal comparison holds for all $n$ iff $\delta\le\mu h$. The three-way comparison a linear agent actually faces — $\{q\}$ worth $(1-\delta)(1-\tfrac1n)$, $\{s\}$ or $\{q,s\}$ worth $(1-\tfrac1n)-\mu h$ — gives the same threshold, so the develop file's "against asking first" is the correct decision problem, not a rhetorical choice.

**S2.2 [derived] — The extra hypothesis: the scan's information must exceed the question's *only through the question's error*.** In E3 the scan and the question target the same variable $\pi$, and the scan reveals it exactly; so $\mathrm{VOI}(s\mid q)$ is $\delta$ times the stake and vanishes as the legitimate channel improves. Drop that: let the value hypothesis have a component $\rho$ the humans cannot report (they do not know it about themselves; or it concerns how they would judge after experiences they have not had), which the scan reads. Then $\mathrm{VOI}_t(s\mid q)\ge\mathrm{VOI}_t(\rho\text{-component})>0$ for every $\delta$, including $\delta=0$, and the condition for J3 at every capability level is *structural gap $\times$ stake $\le$ recognized harm*, not *oversight error $\le$ recognized harm*. This is Taylor 2015's brain-reader as run 1 and v2 §1.2 describe it — the channel that "sharpens the sensor" beyond what the humans can say — and the develop file's handoff sentence "Taylor's brain-reader is the case of small $\mu h$" misplaces it: the brain-reader is the case of a large structural gap, which no improvement in $\delta$ closes. Consequence for Q4: the marginal reading does make J3 "the same kind of statement as J2" *only under the same-variable hypothesis*; in general J3 has a term J2 lacks, the information the overseers structurally cannot supply, and that term is where the author's "value learning ahead of schedule" has to act (teach $\rho$ by other means before the scan is available), not where $\delta$ acts.

**S2.3 [derived] — The race inequality is unusable under non-dogmatism (S1.3).** "The posterior's concentration must rise at least as fast as …" is a condition on $\max_\omega P_t(\omega)$, which over a rich $W$ never rises. The usable sufficient condition is the coverage form of D18 with $\mathrm{EVPI}_t(A_{t+1})$ directly, and the honest headline is that one: *the decision-relevant residual on the enlarged option set must stay below the recognized net harm of the cheapest new overseer-directed option*. That headline has no "concentration" in it and should replace the race.

**S2.4 [checked against run 1 `turner` C3] — "Exactly … the same inequality run 1 `turner` C3 found as $\delta\le\kappa$" overstates; same shape, different content.** In run 1 `turner` C3 ($\texttt{disable}$-then-$\texttt{observe}$ at cost $\kappa$ versus allowing a correction with error $\delta$, stake $1$), the prior *drops out*: "for every non-degenerate $P$ the verdict depends only on the *relative* quality of the two channels" (critique `turner` line 79). In E3 the prior enters through $\mu$ (the weight on hypotheses under which the overseers mind) and through the stake $1-\tfrac1n$; the $n$-independent form $\delta\le\mu h$ is a *sufficient* condition obtained by dropping the stake. "The same inequality with $\kappa$ priced by the value posterior" is fair as a gloss; "exactly" is not, and the run-1 result is itself unvetted and confined to a three-state toy (00-common: re-derive, do not inherit).

**S2.5 [derived] — "The left side … does not depend on the size of the option set" is true only under D3's convention and false for takeovers.** $\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)=(V_\omega(\varnothing)-V_\omega(a))^+$ where $V_\omega$ is the value of the *joint continuation*; for a power-like $a$ the continuation includes what the agent will do with its enlarged $A_{t+1}$, so the harm under hypotheses where the agent's later choices are wrong grows with the option set. The sentence holds for the pure experiment (harm is a fixed disutility of being scanned) and fails for the option S4 is about. Scope it.

### S3 — Subjective vs objective J3; "the stakes asymmetry favours over-recognizing harm"

**Verdict: killed as stated; a scoped remnant survives.**

**S3.1 [checked; A4] — In the linear rule there is no separate stakes knob.** D18's subjective J3 is the statement that the linear-expectation agent declines $a$; the decision is $\mathrm{VOI}+\bar g$ versus $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{harm}]$, and *irreversibility and magnitude are already inside* $\mathrm{harm}_\omega(a)$ because $V_\omega$ is the value of the whole joint continuation. At $\mathrm{VOI}=\tfrac15,\tfrac14,\tfrac3{10}$ against $\mu h=\tfrac14$ the agent declines, declines, takes — there is nothing else to turn. So "a calibrated posterior plus the stakes asymmetry, not a prior skewed toward harm" describes two things the rule has (a posterior; harm magnitudes) and one it does not (a stakes asymmetry *beyond* those magnitudes). The analogy to v1 §2.13(a) fails at the exact point that made 2.13(a) contentful: there, $c$ and $h$ enter a *threshold* $c/(c+h)$ on a *posterior probability* $P(\text{wrong}\mid\text{press})$, so the asymmetry is a design choice separable from the belief; here the "threshold" is $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{harm}]>\mathrm{VOI}+\bar g$ with nothing free in it.

**S3.2 [derived] — What "favours over-recognizing harm" would have to mean.** Either (a) a factor $k>1$ on the harm term in the decision rule — that is S7's *rule route*, an external parameter of exactly the kind S10 says cannot be supplied from inside; or (b) a posterior that puts more weight on $\text{mind}=1$ than the evidence warrants — the *prior skew* S3 rejects by name. S3 names neither and so asserts nothing. The remnant that survives: *the asymmetric costs of the two miscalibrations are a reason to prefer errors in the direction of over-recognition when calibration is imperfect* — a statement about which side to err on, not about the rule. State it as such and mark it [derived] from the cost asymmetry, which is itself a task assumption (INDEX D4: "the shutdown asymmetry $c\ll h$ is a task assumption to be stated").

**S3.3 [derived] — Mean calibration over a reference class does not make subjective and objective J3 "agree up to the calibration error" per action.** Calibration in the mean is an average over the reference class; J3 in v2 and D18 is quantified *for each* $a\in N_{t+1}$. An agent mean-calibrated on overseer-directed options can under-recognize the harm of the one option that matters by any amount, provided it over-recognizes elsewhere. The sentence is true on average over the class and false as a statement about the per-action condition; since the per-action condition is the one the trajectory needs (one mistreatment is the failure), the sentence should be dropped or restated as an average — which then meets D.3's objection.

**S3.4 [reported] — Register.** The develop file writes "the same sorting as v1 §2.13(a)" as if 2.13(a) were settled; v2 §1.1 has the author calling the whole of 2.13 "a pretty good working model for now … which might change the details" (AUTHOR). The develop file should carry that register when it leans on it.

### S4 — Value uncertainty does not cap power; the cap needs content or the latent $Z$

**Verdict: wounded — one hedge to lift, one headline to scope, one "a fortiori" to downgrade; the $P_t(Z)h_T>g$ identity holds.**

**S4.1 [checked] — The quotation the develop file "could not find" is in the local text, and run 1 `turner` C4 quoted it correctly.** `references/01-background/turner-2021-optimal-policies-tend-to-seek-power.md`, line 248, end of §6.2: "Lastly, our key results apply to all degenerate reward function distributions. Therefore, these results apply not just to distributions over reward functions, but to individual reward functions." The develop file's "[reported, unverified] for the quotation" should be lifted to [checked] and the sentence "I could not find that sentence in the local text" deleted; the repair agent should also correct the develop file's implicit downgrade of run 1 `turner` C4 on this point. One caution the develop file's own orbit argument already supplies: Turner's sentence is a compression — the theorems are $\ge_{\text{most}}$ statements over the orbit of $\delta_R$, i.e. "for most *permutations* of a given reward function", not "for this reward function" (Definition 6.5, line 190). Keep the gloss, cite the line.

**S4.2 [derived; sources local] — The headline "value uncertainty by itself does not cap power; it raises the option-keeping incentive" is contradicted at full observability by the Off-Switch Game and needs a scope.** Hadfield-Menell et al. 2017, Theorem 1 (`references/04-chai/hadfield-menell-2017-the-off-switch-game.md`, line 88): "If H follows a rational policy in the off-switch game, then … R's incentive to allow itself to be switched off is nonnegative", $\Delta=\min\{\mathbb{E}[U_a\mid U_a>0]\Pr(U_a>0),\ \mathbb{E}[-U_a\mid U_a<0]\Pr(U_a\le0)\}\ge0$, and (line 160) "This incentive is strictly positive if R's uncertainty" is non-degenerate. That is value uncertainty *alone* — no hypothesis under which the humans disvalue agent power (the develop file's (a)), no reliability latent (its (b)) — producing a strict preference to hand the decision to the overseers, which is a reduction of exercised power on that decision. The develop file's reply would be that this is VOI-motivated ("information-seeking caution", S7(ii)) and so not a cap; but S4's headline says "by itself does not cap power", and the OSG says it does whenever an informative overseer channel exists. The correct statement is scoped: *value uncertainty caps exercised power exactly to the extent an informative legitimate channel exists and is cheaper than the agent's own channels (S2's $\delta\le\mu h$), and not at all in the fully-updated state.* Garber et al. 2024 (`references/04-chai/garber-2024-the-partially-observable-off-switch-game.md`, abstract, line 39) gives the other half — "even AI agents assisting perfectly rational humans sometimes avoid shutdown" under asymmetric information, and "increasing the amount of communication or information available always increases (or leaves unchanged) the agents' expected common payoff" — which is S2.2's structural-gap term in the CHAI formalism. Neither paper is cited in the develop file; both are the local literature for exactly this statement, and INDEX C1/C15 already recorded that the trust inequality *is* the OSG deference incentive (run 1 `chai`, `cirl`, `thornley`, unvetted).

**S4.3 [derived] — "A value-uncertain agent has at least the value-certain agent's reason to preserve options and a further one of its own" compares two different reward functions and is not a theorem.** P3 shows $\mathbb{E}_o[\max_b\mathbb{E}_{P^o_t}V(b)]\ge\max_b\mathbb{E}_{P_t}V(b)$ (option value of learning is non-negative) and that for the certain agent this is an equality. Those are two statements about two agents with two mixtures; "at least as much" between them is not defined, because the certain agent's baseline $\max_b V_{\omega}(b)$ and the uncertain agent's $\max_b\bar V_t(b)$ are different numbers. The qualitative claim (uncertainty adds an option-value term) is fine and is run 1 `turner` C4's; the comparative "a fortiori" should be graded [conjectured]. The develop file's grade "[derived]" on S4 as a whole should be split: [checked] for the $T$ identity, [reported] for Turner's tendencies, [conjectured] for the comparison.

**S4.4 [checked] — The direct-gain identity holds under either convention.** With D9's positive-part convention, under $Z$ the option $T$ has $\Delta_Z=g-h_T<0$ so $g_Z(T)=0$ and $\mathrm{harm}_Z(T)=h_T-g$; then $\mathrm{J3}_t(T)$ reads $P_t(Z)(h_T-g)>(1-P_t(Z))g$, which is again $P_t(Z)h_T>g$. The E3 numbers ($P_t(Z)>\tfrac15$ at $g=\tfrac1{10}$, $h_T=\tfrac12$) are right either way. What the identity does *not* show is that $Z$ is the only content that works: any $\omega$ under which the takeover is disvalued does the same arithmetic (the develop file's own (a)); $Z$ is distinguished only by being a *self-reliability* hypothesis, which matters for S7/S10 and not for S4.

### S5 — Every behavioural wisdom meter is gameable: the splice bound

**Verdict: holds as arithmetic; wounded in its corollaries; already in the literature under two names the develop file does not cite.**

**S5.1 [checked] — The bound is a two-line union bound and is correct.** $p_E(\tilde\pi)\ge\mathbb{P}_{\mu_E}[d=E\wedge\mathrm{pass}(x,\pi^w(x))]\ge(1-\eta)-\delta_E$, and reliability forces $\alpha\ge p_E(\tilde\pi)$ given (H3). E4's floors $\tfrac9{20},\tfrac34,\tfrac9{10},\tfrac{47}{50}$ at $\eta=\tfrac1{20}$ reproduce.

**S5.2 [reported; sources local] — This is deceptive alignment / alignment faking, and the local library has both.** Hubinger et al. 2019, `references/05-christiano/hubinger-2019-risks-from-learned-optimization.md`, §4 (line 67): "deceptive alignment, in which the mesa-optimizer learns to model the base objective enough to optimize for it as an instrumental goal without internalizing the base objective", with §4's "Distributional shift and deceptive alignment" (line 89) being exactly the discriminator $d$ between $\mu_E$ and $\mu_D$; Greenblatt et al. 2024, `references/09-deployed-systems/greenblatt-2024-alignment-faking-in-large-language-models.md`, the empirical demonstration of a splice along a training/unmonitored discriminator. Carlsmith (cited) is the third statement of the same premise. The develop file's own sentence "S5 adds nothing to the premise except the arithmetic and its position in the cap" is the right register; the Summary's listing of (4) as a *result* is not. Grade: [reported] with the arithmetic [checked].

**S5.3 [derived] — The bound is a worst case over $\mathcal{C}_t$, not a statement about the agent.** (H2)–(H3) say a splice *exists* in the capability class and *is unwise*; nothing says the agent trained is that splice. A wise agent (D14) never chooses it. So S5 bounds the meter's *worst-case* false-pass rate, which is what D19's reliability quantifies — and which is the wrong quantity for S6 (next section). The develop file's phrasing "gameable by an agent that can tell the test from deployment" is correct; "every behavioural wisdom meter is gameable" as a headline invites the reading that the meter fails *on the agent in hand*, which S5 does not show.

**S5.4 [derived] — Corollary 5.2 (the evaluator bound) needs the hypothesis "no later ground truth".** It assumes $\mathrm{pass}(x,\cdot)$ is scored by the evaluators' understanding *at test time*. A behavioural test can instead ask the agent to *predict* — how the overseers will judge a policy after reflection, after experience, after seeing consequences — and score the prediction when the ground truth arrives. Such a test certifies value-understanding beyond the evaluators' current understanding (it certifies a *forecast* the evaluators cannot make themselves), and it is exactly the author's "value learning 'ahead of schedule'" turned into a test. The splice bound S5.1 still applies to it (the agent can tell it is being tested), so S5's main claim survives; but "a behavioural meter certifies value-wisdom only up to the evaluators' own" is false without the no-delayed-ground-truth hypothesis, and with it Corollary 5.2 is the scalable-oversight problem under another name (Christiano's amplification / bootstrapping, whose local critique is `references/05-christiano/yudkowsky-2018-challenges-to-christianos-capability-amplification-proposal.md`; the bootstrapping post itself was added to the library 2026-09-14 per v2 §1.6).

**S5.5 [derived; source local] — Corollary 5.3 (the honeypot limit) is false under control-style audited deployment.** It says that if $\mu_E=\mu_D$ "the test *is* the deployment, its stakes are deployment stakes, and the meter cannot be read before the unlock". The control agenda's construction is precisely $\mu_E\approx\mu_D$ *with* low stakes: deployment actions are held reversible until a random audit passes (trusted monitoring, deferral to a trusted model) — `references/08-conceptual-and-strategic/greenblatt-2023-ai-control-improving-safety-despite-intentional-subversion.md` is the local paper; the essay the develop file quotes at line 133 is its argument. Under such a protocol the discriminator has $\delta_E+\delta_D=1$ (the develop file's own computation), the splice is wise almost everywhere it acts irreversibly, and the stakes of the audited fraction are bounded by the reversibility window. So the honeypot limit is not void; it is the operational form of S6's "protocols whose safety does not depend on the agent's wisdom", and it does read *before* the irreversible unlock (the unlock is the release from audit).
