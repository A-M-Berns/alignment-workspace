# Audit: the revised Theorem 1 of *Unbounded Embedded Agency* (live version, 2026-08-22)

**Provenance.** Written by a Keeper session, 2026-08-22, from [lesswrong-post--live-2026-08-22](01-primary/lesswrong-post--live-2026-08-22.md) (the current post) and Appendix C of Wyeth–Hutter–Leike–Taylor, *Limit-Computable Grains of Truth* (`06-related-work/papers/wyeth-2025-limit-computable-grains-of-truth.pdf`, pp. 31–34, the `(query, eval)` fixed-point construction). Everything below is **corpus-proved-but-unvetted-by-the author**; the "verdict" register on each item is mine. Nothing here has been checked with Cole.

**Headline (register: my assessment, unvetted).** The revised statement is better-formed than the 2025 one and its *conclusion* follows from its *key lemma* by a one-line argument I give in §3. The key lemma — the "trust bound" holds at every history at the constructed fixed point — is where all the content lives, and the proof sketch for it has two real gaps (§4): the modified point-to-set map does not have a closed graph at boundary points (same phenomenon as argmax ties), and the reset predicate is a global condition on infinitely many coordinates, which the product-topology Kakutani argument cannot see. I think both are repairable, and §5 gives the repair I'd propose: move the "reset" from the oracle into the agent. That reformulation also makes the author's conjecture in [summer-research-plan-2026--research-ideas-may-2026](01-primary/summer-research-plan-2026--research-ideas-may-2026.md) §Updatelessness ("belief that you're UDT1.1 does the work that Cole's constructed oracle does") precise — see §6. The "for all time w.h.p." clause needs a different martingale than the one cited (§3.3), and $\delta$ is unquantified in the statement (§2).

## 1. Objects (fixing notation the post leaves implicit)

- $O$: a reflective oracle (Grains-of-Truth Def. 32, step form). $\xi$: rOSI, the universal mixture over **joint** action/percept histories, $\xi(h)=\sum_T w(T)\,\bar\lambda^O_T(h)$ over all $O$-machines $T$.
- Three value functions, which the post uses with one symbol each but which are different objects:
  - **EDT values** $Q_\xi(h_{<t},a_t):=\mathbb E_\xi\big[\sum_{i\ge t}\gamma_i r_i \,\big|\, h_{<t}a_t\big]$, future *actions drawn from $\xi$'s own action-conditionals* $\zeta(a\mid h):=\xi(a\mid h)$. $V_\xi(h):=\sum_a \xi(a\mid h)Q_\xi(h,a)$.
  - **Policy values in the $\xi$-environment** $Q^{\pi}_\xi(h,a)$, $V^{\pi}_\xi(h)$: expected return when percepts come from $\xi(e\mid h a)$ and actions from a fixed policy $\pi$. So $\xi=\xi^{\zeta}$ and $Q_\xi=Q^{\zeta}_\xi$.
  - **Optimal values** $V^*_\xi(h):=\sup_\pi V^\pi_\xi(h)$, $\pi^*_\xi$ an optimal policy (exists and is rO-computable, Grains-of-Truth Thm 18).
- $\pi_S(h)$: randomize among $\arg\max_a Q_\xi(h,a)$ using the oracle to break ties (Thm 18 procedure: one oracle call).
- **The self-hypothesis** $\xi^{\pi_S}$: the $O$-machine that samples percepts from $\xi(e\mid ha)$ and actions from $\pi_S$. It is in the mixture with prior $w(\xi^{\pi_S})$ and posterior $w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)=w(\xi^{\pi_S})\,\xi^{\pi_S}(h)/\xi(h)$. The post writes $w(\pi_S\mid h)$ for the same thing. Two facts about it that matter:
  - (F1) Its percept conditionals are $\xi$'s own, so **percepts never move its posterior**. Unwinding the self-reference, $\xi(e\mid ha)=\big[\sum_{T\ne\xi^{\pi_S}} w(T\mid ha)\,T(e\mid ha)\big]/(1-w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid ha))$: the self-hypothesis is a parasite on the rest of the mixture's percept predictions. This is why the 2025 "let $\psi$ be deterministic" assumption has disappeared — it was needed to stop percepts from contradicting $\psi^{\pi_S}$.
  - (F2) Its posterior moves only through actions: $\mathrm{odds}(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid ha)\ \ge\ \mathrm{odds}(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)\cdot \pi_S(a\mid h)$, because $\xi(a\mid h)\le w\,\pi_S(a\mid h)+(1-w)$. So the posterior is **non-decreasing** when $\pi_S$ is deterministic at $h$, and drops by at most a factor $\pi_S(a\mid h)\ge 1/|A|$ in odds at a tie. This is the post's "$|A|$ bits in the worst case".

## 2. The statement, and what it now claims

> **Theorem 1 (live).** There is a reflective oracle relative to which, for every $\varepsilon>0$, $w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h_{<t})>1-\delta$ implies $V^{\pi_S}_\xi(h_{<t})>V^*_\xi(h_{<t})-\varepsilon$. In particular $\pi_S$ remains $\varepsilon$-(Bayes)optimal for all time with high probability.

Three remarks on the statement itself (register: verified reading of the text).

1. **$\delta$ is unquantified.** It must be $\exists\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)$. From §3, $\delta\approx\varepsilon/(1+|A|)$ suffices for the pointwise claim and $\delta\approx\varepsilon^2$ for the all-time claim.
2. **"Optimal" is now subjective.** The conclusion compares $\pi_S$ to the optimal policy for *$\xi$'s own percept model*, not for a true environment $\psi$. The 2025 version claimed optimality for $\psi$ under $\xi\ge(1-\delta)\psi^{\pi_S}$. The live version is exactly "self-reflective AIXI behaves like AIXI (Bayes-optimally w.r.t. its own beliefs) when it trusts itself" — which is the result the post always said it wanted ("when self-reflective AIXI converges to AIXI"). This matters for [summer-research-plan-2026--research-ideas-may-2026](01-primary/summer-research-plan-2026--research-ideas-may-2026.md) §Interpretational Issues, which reads the theorem as an optimality result in the 2025 sense; the updated reading is weaker but cleaner, and the "self-knowledge assumption sneakily rules out environments" objection becomes "sneakily rules out *belief states*", since no true environment appears.
3. The old theorem is kept in an appendix as "Original Broken Argument"; the $\psi$-deterministic assumption and Lemmas 1–2 are gone from the main line.

## 3. The conclusion follows from the trust bound in one line (register: verified by me)

Call the **trust bound at $h$** the inequality

$$\max_{a}\ \mathbb E_\xi\Big[\textstyle\sum_{i\ge t}\gamma_i r_i\ \Big|\ h_{<t}a\Big]\ \ \ge\ \ w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h_{<t})\cdot V^*_\xi(h_{<t}).\qquad(\mathrm{TB}_h)$$

This is what the modified fixed point is built to guarantee. Given $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$, here is the whole proof of the pointwise conclusion.

**Lemma A (mixture decomposition).** For every $h,a$: $Q_\xi(h,a)=\sum_T w(T\mid ha)\,Q^{T}(h,a)$ where $Q^T(h,a)$ is the expected return under hypothesis $T$'s joint distribution after $ha$. For $T=\xi^{\pi_S}$, $Q^{T}(h,a)=Q^{\pi_S}_\xi(h,a)$ — the value of *actually following $\pi_S$ forever* in the $\xi$-environment. With returns in $[0,1]$:

$$Q_\xi(h,a)\ \le\ w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid ha)\,Q^{\pi_S}_\xi(h,a)\ +\ \big(1-w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid ha)\big).$$

**Proof of the pointwise conclusion.** Let $a$ be any action in the support of $\pi_S(h)$, so $Q_\xi(h,a)=\max_{a'}Q_\xi(h,a')\ge w\,V^*_\xi(h)$ by $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$, with $w:=w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)\ge 1-\delta$. Put $w':=w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid ha)$; by (F2), $1-w'\le \delta':=|A|\delta/(1-\delta)$ roughly. Lemma A gives

$$Q^{\pi_S}_\xi(h,a)\ \ge\ \frac{Q_\xi(h,a)-(1-w')}{w'}\ \ge\ Q_\xi(h,a)-\frac{1-w'}{w'}\ \ge\ V^*_\xi(h)-\delta-\frac{\delta'}{1-\delta'}.$$

Averaging over $a\sim\pi_S(h)$: $V^{\pi_S}_\xi(h)\ \ge\ V^*_\xi(h)-\delta-\delta'/(1-\delta')\ \approx\ V^*_\xi(h)-(1+|A|)\delta$. $\square$

Three things to notice.

- **No horizon, no $T_\varepsilon$, no expansion of maxima.** $Q^{\pi_S}_\xi(h,a)$ already prices in the entire future under $\pi_S$, including any future loss of self-trust. The 2025 proof's Lemma 1 tried to expand $V^{\pi_S}$ into nested maxima — the step the author identified as the hole — and that step is simply unnecessary once the hypothesis is "I am $\pi_S$" rather than "the continuation is optimal". This is the structural reason the revised theorem is healthier: the self-hypothesis's continuation *is* the actual agent, so off-policy values never enter.
- **The constant is explicit**: $\varepsilon=(1+|A|)\delta+O(\delta^2)$, versus the $T_\varepsilon\delta$ bookkeeping of 2025.
- **All the content is in $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$.** Without it, the bad fixed point (a private-chat remark: [scrubbed]) is a perfectly good reflective oracle at which $\max_a Q_\xi(h,a)$ is far below $wV^*$.

### 3.3 The "for all time with high probability" clause (register: verified, with a correction)

The sketch says "$n$ bits of evidence against $\pi_S$ has probability less than $2^{-n}$" (Ville's inequality on the likelihood ratio $M_t=\xi(h_t)/\xi^{\pi_S}(h_t)$ under $\xi^{\pi_S}$). True, but it does not give the needed bound: falling from posterior $1-\delta$ to $1-2\delta$ is one bit, and one bit can happen with probability up to $1/2$. The right martingale is $1-w_t$ (the mass on the *other* hypotheses), which is a non-negative $\xi$-martingale with mean $1-w_0\le\delta$; Ville gives $P_\xi\big(\inf_t w_t\le 1-\delta''\big)\le \delta/\delta''$, and since $\xi\ge w_0\,\xi^{\pi_S}$, the same event has $\xi^{\pi_S}$-probability $\le \delta/(\delta'' w_0)$. So: with $\delta=\varepsilon^2$ and $\delta''=\varepsilon$, the trust bound applies at every future node except on an event of probability $\le\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon^2)$, and on that event the loss is at most $1$; total loss $O(\varepsilon)$. Fine — but it is the $1-w_t$ martingale, not the $2^{-n}$ argument, and it is "w.h.p. according to $\xi$ (or $\xi^{\pi_S}$)", not according to any true environment. Note also from (F2) that when $\pi_S$ never ties, $w_t$ is non-decreasing and no probabilistic clause is needed at all — the 2025 theorem's "only one action is Bayes-optimal" condition survives in that form.

## 4. The key lemma: does the modified fixed point exist, and is it a reflective oracle? (register: GAP)

The construction (post, "Proof sketch") modifies the Grains-of-Truth point-to-set map $f:K\to 2^K$ on $K=[0,1]^{\mathcal T\times(\mathbb Q\cap[0,1])\times A}\times\Delta(A)^{\mathcal T}$: for every *minimal* prefix $h$ at which, **as assessed by the current `eval`**, $\max_a Q_\xi(h,a)<w(\pi_S\mid h)V^*_\xi(h)$ ("$h$ violates the trust bound"), `eval'` of $\pi_S$ on the whole subtree below $h$ is set to $\pi^*_\xi$'s behaviour ("resetting the subtree"). Elsewhere `eval'`/`query'` follow the usual rules. Then: Kakutani–Fan gives a fixed point; at a fixed point no subtree is reset (contradiction argument); hence $O$ is reflective and $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ holds everywhere.

### 4.1 The contradiction argument is sound *if a fixed point exists* (verified)

Suppose $(\mathrm{query},\mathrm{eval})$ is a fixed point and $h$ is a minimal violating prefix. Then `eval` says $\pi_S=\pi^*_\xi$ below $h$. For every machine *other than* $\pi_S$ the inductive accuracy argument (Grains-of-Truth p. 34, "by induction on the number of computation steps") goes through unchanged — it uses only that `query` is consistent with `eval` and that the machine's own `eval` follows the normal rules; a machine computing $Q_\xi$ consults $O$ *about* $\pi_S$ rather than running $\pi_S$. So at the fixed point the assessed $Q_\xi(h,\cdot)$ equals the true $Q_\xi(h,\cdot)$ in the world where $\pi_S$ behaves as `eval` says, i.e. as $\pi^*_\xi$ below $h$. Pick $a^*\in\operatorname{supp}\pi^*_\xi(h)$ with $\xi(a^*\mid h)\le\pi_S(a^*\mid h)$ (exists since both sum to 1), so $w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid ha^*)\ge w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)$. By Lemma A's lower half, $Q_\xi(h,a^*)\ge w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid ha^*)\,Q^{\pi^*}_\xi(h,a^*)= w\,V^*_\xi(h)$: the trust bound holds at $h$, so $h$ is not violating — contradiction. Hence at any fixed point nothing is reset, `eval` follows the normal rules everywhere, $O$ is an honest reflective oracle, and $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ holds at every $h$. $\checkmark$

### 4.2 Gap 1 — closed graph fails at the boundary (the argmax-tie phenomenon)

Kakutani–Fan (Grains-of-Truth Thm 33) needs $f$ upper semicontinuous, i.e. closed graph: if $(q_n,e_n)\to(q,e)$, $(q'_n,e'_n)\in f(q_n,e_n)$, $(q'_n,e'_n)\to(q',e')$, then $(q',e')\in f(q,e)$. Take a sequence of points at which $h$ *strictly* violates the bound (so $e'_n(\pi_S,h)=$ "$\pi^*$-behaviour"), converging to a point at which the bound holds with **equality**. Under the post's rule ("we only reset the subtree when a strict inequality is satisfied") $f(q,e)$ at the limit prescribes the *normal* behaviour at $h$ — randomization among $\arg\max_a Q_\xi(h,\cdot)$ — while the limit $e'(\pi_S,h)$ is $\pi^*_\xi$'s behaviour. These differ whenever $\pi^*_\xi(h)\notin\arg\max_a Q_\xi(h,\cdot)$, which is the generic situation at such a boundary point (the whole point of resetting is that the EDT-argmax and the optimal action disagree there). So $(q',e')\notin f(q,e)$: **the graph is not closed.** The remark "$f$ remains upper semicontinuous because we only reset when a strict inequality is satisfied" has the direction backwards — strictness makes the reset set *open*, which is what breaks closedness. Using $\le$ instead just moves the failure to sequences approaching equality from the non-reset side.

This is precisely the discontinuity the original existence proof handles for `query` by leaving $\mathrm{query}(T,\mathrm{eval}(T))$ *unconstrained* at $p=\mathrm{eval}(T)$ (Grains-of-Truth p. 33). The analogous fix here is to let $f$ prescribe, at equality, **any convex combination** of the normal behaviour and the $\pi^*$ behaviour. Then $f$ is convex-valued with closed graph (modulo Gap 2) — but the fixed point may now be a **partial reset**: `eval`$(\pi_S)$ on the subtree is $\lambda\cdot\pi^*+(1-\lambda)\cdot$normal with $\lambda\in(0,1)$. The §4.1 contradiction argument does not exclude this: with a partial reset, $Q^{\text{eval-}\pi_S}(h,a^*)$ is $\lambda V^*+(1-\lambda)(\text{something smaller})$, and equality $\max_aQ_\xi=wV^*$ can be consistent. At such a fixed point `eval`$(\pi_S)\ne$ the actual computation of $\pi_S^O$ (which always does the argmax), so **$O$ is not a reflective oracle** about $\pi_S$ and the theorem's "there is a reflective oracle relative to which …" is not established. Whether partial-reset fixed points actually occur, or can be shown harmless, is open; §5 suggests a way around.

### 4.3 Gap 2 — the reset predicate is not a pointwise condition on $K$

Every condition in the original $f$ involves finitely many coordinates (one `query` coordinate vs one `eval` coordinate; one `eval` coordinate as a combination of two others). "$\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)<w(\pi_S\mid h)V^*_\xi(h)$ as assessed by `eval`" compares two *real numbers* that are encoded in infinitely many coordinates (the outputs of the estimating machines at every precision, or the step-function thresholds of the comparison queries at every rational $p$). On arbitrary (non-fixed) points of $K$ — which $f$ must be defined on — these "assessed reals" are not continuous functions of the point in the product topology (a sup/threshold over infinitely many $[0,1]$-valued coordinates is not continuous under pointwise convergence). So even granting §4.2's convex-hull repair, closed graph is not automatic: the predicate deciding which branch $f$ is in must itself be continuous in the required sense. This is fixable: route the comparison through a **single oracle-comparison machine** $C_h$ (compare two $O$-estimable reals with one query, as in Thm 18's tie-breaking), and define the reset branch pointwise by $\mathrm{eval}(C_h)$ — then the reset rule becomes "`eval'`$(\pi_S,h')=\mathrm{eval}(C_h)(1)\cdot[\pi^*\text{-behaviour}]+\mathrm{eval}(C_h)(0)\cdot[\text{normal}]$", which is a finitely-many-coordinate convex condition like all the others. But that *forces* the §4.2 convex-hull version, i.e. partial resets at equality are then part of the construction, not an artefact.

### 4.4 Minor: "V and Q are computed normally since their computation is not performed by machine $\pi_S$"

Correct, and this is the load-bearing observation of §4.1. The footnote "…and technically other eval at other points, because computation is encoded recursively" is the right caveat: $\pi_S$'s own `eval` at a history is a combination of `eval`s of its successor configurations, and the reset must be applied to *all* configurations of $\pi_S$ reachable on the subtree, not just the root configuration of each history. The sketch is silent on this; it is bookkeeping, not a gap.

## 5. Proposed repair: put the reset in the agent, not the oracle (register: CONJECTURE with a verified core)

Define $\pi_S^{\dagger}$ as the $O$-machine: on history $h$, for each prefix $h'\preceq h$ run the one-query comparison $C_{h'}$: "$\max_aQ_\xi(h',a)<w(\xi^{\pi_S^\dagger}\mid h')\,V^*_\xi(h')$?" (the posterior is that of $\pi_S^\dagger$'s *own* self-hypothesis). If some prefix answers yes, play $\pi^*_\xi(h)$; otherwise play $\arg\max_aQ_\xi(h,\cdot)$ with oracle tie-breaking. All quantities are $O$-estimable, so $\pi_S^\dagger$ is an ordinary $O$-machine and **every** reflective oracle is accurate about it. No modification of $f$ is needed.

**Claim (verified, same argument as §4.1).** At *any* reflective oracle $O$, no prefix $h'$ has $C_{h'}=$ "yes" with probability 1. (If it did, $\pi_S^\dagger=\pi^*_\xi$ on the whole subtree of $h'$, so by Lemma A $Q_\xi(h',a^*)\ge w\,V^*_\xi(h')$, so $C_{h'}$ answers no w.p. 1 — contradiction.) Hence at every history where the comparison is strict, the trust bound holds and $\pi_S^\dagger$ does exactly what $\pi_S$ does, and §3's one-line bound applies with any reflective oracle.

**What remains (open, but now a bookkeeping question rather than an existence question).** Equality nodes, where $C_{h'}$ randomizes with some $\lambda\in(0,1)$. There the agent genuinely mixes $\pi^*_\xi(h)$ and the EDT-argmax; the argmax branch is covered by §3 (its EDT value is exactly $wV^*$); the $\pi^*$ branch needs the recursion $D(h):=V^*_\xi(h)-V^{\pi_S^\dagger}_\xi(h)\le \lambda\,\gamma\,\mathbb E\,D(\text{child})+(1-\lambda)(\delta+\delta')$, which closes to $\sup D\le \delta+\delta'$ *if* the posterior at the child is still $\ge1-\delta$; in the $\pi^*$ branch the posterior can drop by the factor $\lambda$ in odds (F2), but that branch is taken only with probability $\lambda$, so the expected damage is $O(\delta)$ per level. I have not written this out carefully; it looks like it gives $\varepsilon=O(|A|\delta)$ or at worst $O(|A|\delta/(1-\gamma))$.

**Why this reformulation is interesting beyond the technicality.** $\pi_S^\dagger$ is $\pi_S$ plus a clause that, at any reflective oracle, provably never fires (strictly). Its *presence* is what removes the bad fixed points — exactly the logic of the chicken rule in modal UDT ("if you prove you won't take $a$, take $a$"), whose presence changes the agent's self-model so that spurious proofs cannot be fixed points. Cole's oracle-side construction is the same device, relocated into the oracle's equilibrium selection. This connects to the author's *lateral.md* W3 ("the chicken rule is a temperature floor") in `research/deference-trust-lab/findings/`, and it sharpens the summer-plan sentence "Cole's proposal avoids spurious proofs by virtue of not knowing its own source code … This seems like a weak defense": on this reading the defense is not the ignorance, it is the floor. (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED: that Cole would describe it this way.)

## 6. Implications for the next steps in the summer plan (register: my analysis, unvetted)

The summer plan proposes: *if a UDT1.0 agent $(1-\delta)$-believes that its policy is UDT1.1, then it is $\varepsilon$-optimal*, with the remark that "belief that you're UDT1.1 does the work that Cole's constructed oracle does".

1. **The remark is right in a precise sense.** Cole's constructed oracle does one thing: it enforces $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$, a no-self-sabotage condition. $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ is exactly what "my continuation is the optimal policy" gives for free: if the agent's mixture puts weight $\ge1-\delta$ on a hypothesis whose continuation is $\pi^*$, then Lemma A's lower half gives $Q(h,\pi^*(h))\ge(1-\delta)V^*$ immediately. So the Stag-Hunt self-miscoordination that UDT1.0 can fall into is the UDT analogue of Cole's bad fixed point, and the UDT1.1-belief is the analogue of the trust bound.
2. **But the one-line bound of §3 does *not* transfer**, and this is the crux for the proposed theorem. Lemma A's upper half relates the EDT value to the value of *the hypothesis's own continuation*. For Cole the hypothesis is "I am $\pi_S$", whose continuation is the actual agent — so the bound is about the agent's true value, horizon-free. For "I am UDT1.1", the continuation is $\pi^*$, not the actual UDT1.0 agent, so Lemma A only says UDT1.0's chosen action is $O(\delta)$-optimal *for the optimal continuation*. Turning "every action is $2\delta$-optimal for $\pi^*$-continuation" into "the actual policy is $\varepsilon$-optimal" is the performance-difference/expand-the-maxima argument — valid here (no off-policy underestimation, since the continuation is the max), but it brings back horizon constants ($1/(1-\gamma)$ or $T_\varepsilon$) *and* a gap condition: UDT1.0's argmax of $(1-\delta)Q^{\pi^*}+\delta Q^{\text{rest}}$ can differ from $\pi^*$'s action at near-ties, and any deviation from a deterministic $\pi^*$ sends the posterior on "I am UDT1.1" to **zero** — the belief is brittle in exactly the way "I am me" is not (F2). So the theorem as stated is plausible only with a margin assumption ("$\pi^*$'s action beats the runner-up by more than $\approx\delta$") or a single-shot/finite-horizon setting, and the "remains optimal" clause is the hard part.
3. **A robust version probably *is* Cole's revised theorem, with $V^*$ read updatelessly.** The natural fix for the brittleness is to let the self-hypothesis be "I am me (UDT1.0)" and to impose the trust bound $\max_aQ(h,a)\ge w\,V^{\text{UDT1.1}}$ as the equilibrium-selection condition — but that is $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ with $V^*_\xi$ replaced by the prior-optimal (UDT1.1) value. So the proposed theorem may be best stated as: *in the updateless valuation, the trust bound (equivalently, the self-trust floor of §5) characterizes when UDT1.0's equilibrium is UDT1.1-optimal*; "believing you are UDT1.1" is then the name of the selected equilibrium, not a separate hypothesis in the mixture. This also answers the plan's "I'm not sure which angle is most promising" between correlated oracles and a different optimality notion: the revised theorem shows the work is in equilibrium selection, which is orthogonal to both.
4. **Unchanged by the revision:** the plan's objections about oracle-call independence (Omega cannot be modelled as correlated with the agent) and about interleaved-conditionals optimality stand; the live theorem is still updateful and still uses $V^*_\xi(h)$ conditioned on $h$. The self-knowledge/self-modification objection now reads as a constraint on belief states rather than environments (§2.2), which is arguably *more* damning for tiling applications: the premise $w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)>1-\delta$ says the agent believes it will not change, full stop.

## 7. Checklist for Cole (if this is sent)

1. Quantify $\delta$ ($\exists\delta(\varepsilon)$; $\delta\approx\varepsilon/(1+|A|)$ pointwise, $\varepsilon^2$ for all-time).
2. State explicitly that optimality is relative to $\xi(e\mid ha)$, and that the deterministic-$\psi$ assumption is gone because of (F1).
3. Replace the nested-maxima reasoning with Lemma A (§3) — the proof of the conclusion from $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ is three lines.
4. Fix the u.s.c. claim (§4.2): the reset set must be handled by convexification at equality, and then either show partial-reset fixed points cannot occur or move the reset into the agent (§5).
5. Make the reset predicate pointwise via a comparison machine (§4.3).
6. Replace the "$n$ bits $\Rightarrow 2^{-n}$" clause by the $1-w_t$ martingale (§3.3), or add the no-ties condition under which $w_t$ is monotone.
