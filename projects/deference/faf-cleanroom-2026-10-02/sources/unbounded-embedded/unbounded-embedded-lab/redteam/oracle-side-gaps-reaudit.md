## Two finite instances used throughout (register: PROVED (prose), exact rational arithmetic)

Both live in the finite shadow of [00-founding-sketches](../notes/00-founding-sketches.md) §1: a deterministic environment tree, a hypothesis class over *joint* action/percept histories in which hypotheses differ only in action conditionals, a self-hypothesis "I am $\tilde\pi$" of prior weight $w_0$, and a residual of weight $1-w_0$. Percepts are deterministic, so $V^*_\xi$ is the deterministic optimum and does not depend on $\tilde\pi$. A **fixed point** for an agent $\pi$ is a $\tilde\pi$ that equals $\pi$'s behaviour when $\pi$'s computation is carried out against the mixture $\xi[\tilde\pi]$ built from $\tilde\pi$ — the finite analogue of the oracle being accurate about $\pi$. Rewards are terminal, in $[0,1]$, undiscounted within the episode. (For every history to have positive $\xi$-probability one may add a uniform-action hypothesis of weight $u$; all strict inequalities below have slack $\ge 0.02$ and survive $u<10^{-2}$, so I present the $u=0$ numbers.)

### Instance A — a partial-reset fixed point (two steps)

Root actions $a_1,a_2$. After $a_1$ the agent chooses reward $x=0.93$ or $0$; after $a_2$ it chooses $1$ (swim) or $0$ (sink). $V^*(\text{root})=1$ via $a_2$; $\pi^*(\text{root})=a_2$. Self prior $w_0=0.9$ ($\delta=0.1$), so the trust threshold at the root is $w_0V^*=0.9$. Residual (weight $0.1$): takes $a_1$ or $a_2$ with probability $1/2$ each; after $a_1$ picks $x$ with probability $1/2$; after $a_2$ always sinks. At the leaves' parents the last-step $Q$ is the immediate reward, so every fixed point has the self-policy pick $x$ after $a_1$ and swim after $a_2$; the trust bound there is $x\ge w\,x$ and $1\ge w\cdot 1$, never violated. Write $t:=\tilde\pi(a_2\mid\text{root})$. Then

$Q_\xi(\text{root},a_1)=0.93\cdot\dfrac{0.9(1-t)+0.025}{0.9(1-t)+0.05},\qquad Q_\xi(\text{root},a_2)=\dfrac{0.9\,t}{0.9\,t+0.05}.$

At $t^*=7/36$: $Q_\xi(\text{root},a_1)=0.93\cdot\tfrac{30}{31}=\tfrac{9}{10}=w_0V^*$ exactly, and $Q_\xi(\text{root},a_2)=\tfrac{7}{9}<\tfrac{9}{10}$. So at $t^*$ the trust bound holds **with equality**, the EDT best response is $\mathrm{BR}(\text{root})=a_1$, and $\pi^*(\text{root})=a_2\neq\mathrm{BR}$.

Honest fixed points of $\pi_S$ (argmax, no reset): $t=0$ ($Q(a_1)=3441/3800\approx0.9055>0.9$, $Q(a_2)=0$; BR $=a_1$ ✓; trust bound strict ✓) and $t=1$ ($Q(a_2)=18/19\approx0.947>0.9$, $Q(a_1)=0.465$; BR $=a_2$ ✓). Convexified-reset fixed point: $t=t^*$ with reset weight $\lambda=t^*=7/36$: $\tilde\pi(\text{root})=\lambda\,\delta_{a_2}+(1-\lambda)\,\delta_{a_1}$ has $\tilde\pi(a_2)=7/36=t^*$ ✓. At this point $\pi_S$'s actual computation outputs $a_1$ with probability $1$ (strict argmax), while the oracle's picture of $\pi_S$ puts $7/36$ on $a_2$: **the oracle is not reflective about $\pi_S$.** Values: $V^{\tilde\pi}=\tfrac{7}{36}\cdot1+\tfrac{29}{36}\cdot0.93\approx0.944$; the two honest points have $0.93$ and $1$.

### Instance B — no honest trust-bound fixed point; strict-reset map has no fixed point (three steps)

Root: `stay` ($c_0=0.7$) or `go`. After `go` (node $n_1$): `stay` ($c_1=0.85$) or `jump`. After `jump` (water): `swim` ($1$) or `sink` ($0$). $V^*(\text{root})=V^*(n_1)=1$, $\pi^*=$ (go, jump, swim). Self prior $w_0=0.8$ ($\delta=0.2$). Residual (weight $0.2$): go, jump, sink — deterministic. State $(j,k):=(\tilde\pi(\text{go}\mid\text{root}),\ \tilde\pi(\text{jump}\mid n_1))$; the water node is a last step, so swim always. Posterior after `go`: $w_1(j)=\dfrac{0.8j}{0.8j+0.2}\le0.8$.

$Q_\xi(n_1,\text{jump})=\dfrac{0.8\,jk}{0.8\,jk+0.2}\le\tfrac45<c_1=0.85\quad\text{for all }(j,k),$ so $\mathrm{BR}(n_1)=$ stay always. Trust bound at $n_1$: $\max(c_1,Q(n_1,\text{jump}))=0.85\ge w_1\cdot1$ — **always satisfied** (never fires).

$Q_\xi(\text{root},\text{go})=w_1(j)\,\big(k+0.85(1-k)\big)\le0.8$, with equality iff $(j,k)=(1,1)$; $Q_\xi(\text{root},\text{stay})=0.7$. Trust bound at the root, $\max(0.7,Q(\text{go}))\ge0.8$: **strictly violated at every $(j,k)\neq(1,1)$, equality at $(1,1)$.**

- Honest fixed points of $\pi_S$: $k=0$ forced; then $Q(\text{go})=0.85\,w_1(j)\le0.68<0.7$ so BR(root) $=$ stay, $j=0$. Unique honest fixed point $(0,0)$, value $0.7$, and the trust bound **fails** there ($0.7<0.8$). So **no reflective-oracle analogue satisfies the trust bound at every node in this instance.** (The theorem's *conclusion* is not refuted: loss $0.3=1.5\delta$, within the audit's $(1+|A|)\delta=0.6$.)
- Strict-reset map $F$: every $(j,k)\neq(1,1)$ is reset to $\pi^*=(1,1)$; at $(1,1)$ there is no strict violation, so $F(1,1)=\{\mathrm{BR}\}=\{(1,0)\}$. **$F$ has no fixed point.** Kakutani's conclusion fails because closed graph fails along $(1,1-\tfrac1n)\to(1,1)$: $F(1,1-\tfrac1n)=\{(1,1)\}\to(1,1)\notin F(1,1)$.
- Convexified map ($F(1,1)=\{(1,\lambda):\lambda\in[0,1]\}$): unique fixed point $(1,1)$ with $\lambda=1$ — a **full reset at an equality node**. There the oracle's picture of $\pi_S$ is (go, jump) but $\pi_S$'s computation gives (go, stay): dishonest.
- Floored agents: current-node $\pi^\dagger$ has the unique fixed point $(1,0)$ — the floor fires *strictly* at the root ($\max(0.7,0.68)<0.8$) and plays `go`; $n_1$ plays BR $=$ stay; value $0.85$, loss $0.15\le1.5\delta$. Prefix-checking $\pi_S^\dagger$ has the unique fixed point $(1,1)$ with the root comparison answering "violated" with probability $1$ at equality; value $1$. Both are honest reflective-oracle analogues for their own agent.

## Q1 — Gap 1: closed graph at the equality boundary (verdict: CONFIRMED, strengthened)

**Setting in $K$ (verified reading of App. C).** $K=[0,1]^{\mathcal T\times(\mathbb Q\cap[0,1])\times A}\times\Delta(A)^{\mathcal T}$ with the product topology; $\mathcal T$ is the countable set of machine-configurations, so every coordinate of a point $(q,e)$ is free — $K$ imposes no consistency between coordinates, and $f$ must have closed graph on *all* of $K$, not just near fixed points. In the unmodified $f$ every constraint on $(q',e')$ reads finitely many coordinates of $(q,e)$: $q'(T,\alpha,p)$ is pinned by the single coordinate $e(T)(\alpha)$ (with $p=e(T)(\alpha)$ left free — this is exactly how App. C keeps the graph closed at *its* boundary), and $e'(T)$ is an affine function of the evals of $T$'s successor configurations weighted by one $q$-coordinate.

**The modified $f$.** Write $\mathrm N_{h}(q,e)$ for the value the unmodified rules give to $e'(\pi_S,h)$ (the eval of the first successor configuration of $\pi_S$ on input $h$; a continuous function of finitely many coordinates), and write $X_h(q,e)$, $Y_h(q,e)$ for the "assessed" values of $\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)$ and $w(\pi_S\mid h)V^*_\xi(h)$ under whatever encoding is chosen (Q2). The post's rule: if $X_h<Y_h$ and no proper prefix of $h$ has this, then $e'(\pi_S,h')=e(\pi^*,h')$ for every $h'\succeq h$ (where $\pi^*$ is the rO-machine computing $\pi^*_\xi$, cited by the audit as Grains-of-Truth Thm 18 — numbering not re-verified here); otherwise $e'(\pi_S,h)=\mathrm N_h(q,e)$.

**Explicit failing sequence, "$<$" version (register: PROVED (prose)).** Pick any $(q,e)\in K$ with $X_h(q,e)=Y_h(q,e)$, no prefix of $h$ violating, $e(\pi^*,h)=\delta_{a_2}$, and the successor-configuration eval of $\pi_S$ at $h$ equal to $\delta_{a_1}$, so $\mathrm N_h(q,e)=\delta_{a_1}\neq e(\pi^*,h)$. This is legal because $K$ is a product (Instance A at $t^*$ realizes it with *consistent* coordinates: $X=Y=9/10$, $\mathrm N=\delta_{a_1}$, $\pi^*=a_2$). Let $(q_n,e_n)$ agree with $(q,e)$ except on the finitely many coordinates that lower $X_h$ to $Y_h-\tfrac1n$ (threshold encoding: shift the step of $q(M_X,1,\cdot)$; estimator encoding: shift the estimator evals; continuous encoding $G$ of Q2: shift one dyadic coordinate). Then $(q_n,e_n)\to(q,e)$ pointwise, $h$ strictly violates at every $n$, so $f(q_n,e_n)$ forces $e'_n(\pi_S,h)=e_n(\pi^*,h)=\delta_{a_2}$, and $(q'_n,e'_n)\to(q',e')$ with $e'(\pi_S,h)=\delta_{a_2}$. But at the limit there is no strict violation, so $f(q,e)$ forces $e'(\pi_S,h)=\mathrm N_h(q,e)=\delta_{a_1}$. Hence $(q',e')\notin f(q,e)$: **the graph is not closed.** The post's remark "$f$ remains upper semicontinuous because we only reset the subtree when a strict inequality is satisfied" has it backwards — strictness makes the reset region *open*, and u.s.c. of a correspondence that jumps between two different single-valued branches needs the *switching set* to be closed on both sides, which is impossible unless the two branches agree on the boundary (they do not: $\delta_{a_1}\neq\delta_{a_2}$).

**"$\le$" version.** Reset iff $X_h\le Y_h$: take $X_h(q_n,e_n)=Y_h+\tfrac1n$. Now no reset at each $n$, $e'_n(\pi_S,h)=\mathrm N_h(q_n,e_n)\to\mathrm N_h(q,e)=\delta_{a_1}$, while the limit point is reset and $f(q,e)$ forces $\delta_{a_2}$. Same failure from the other side. Comparison-coordinate reading (Q2, reading C: reset iff $c:=q(D_h,1,\tfrac12)=1$): $c_n=1-\tfrac1n\to1$ gives the "$\le$"-type failure; "reset iff $c>0$" with $c_n=\tfrac1n$ gives the "$<$"-type failure.

**Attempted refutations, and why they fail.**
- *"At equality the argmax already equals $\pi^*$, so the two branches agree on the boundary."* False in general: Instance A at $t^*$ has equality with $\mathrm{BR}=a_1\neq a_2=\pi^*$; Instance B at $(1,1)$ has equality with $\mathrm{BR}(n_1)=$ stay $\neq$ jump. (It happens to be true in plain sink-or-swim, which is why the phenomenon is invisible there.)
- *"Closed graph fails but Kakutani's conclusion might still hold."* Refuted by Instance B: the strict-reset map has **no fixed point at all** ($F$ maps everything to $(1,1)$ or $(1,0)$, and $F(1,1)=\{(1,0)\}$, $F(1,0)=\{(1,1)\}$).
- *"Take the closure of the graph."* That is the convexification below; its fixed points are not fixed points of the strict map (Instance B: the closure's fixed point $(1,1)$ has $\mathrm{BR}=(1,0)$).

**Convexification (register: PROVED (prose) that it restores closed graph; PROVED by instance that it introduces partial resets).** Let $f$ prescribe, at equality, any point of $\operatorname{conv}\{\mathrm N_{h'}(q,e),\,e(\pi^*,h')\}$; equivalently (reading C) $e'(\pi_S,h')=\Lambda_{h'}\,e(\pi^*,h')+(1-\Lambda_{h'})\,\mathrm N_{h'}(q,e)$ with $\Lambda_{h'}$ a continuous function of the comparison coordinates of the prefixes of $h'$ (§Q3 fixes $\Lambda_{h'}=1-\prod_{h''\preceq h'}(1-c_{h''})$). Reading C: $e'$ is a polynomial in finitely many coordinates, so its graph is closed; values are singletons, hence convex and nonempty. Reading T with continuous $X,Y$: on $\{X_h>Y_h\}$ and $\{X_h<Y_h\}$ (open) the branch is locally constant; on $\{X_h=Y_h\}$ any limit of points of the two branches or their hull lies in the hull by Carathéodory (two generators, compact simplex). So Kakutani–Fan (Thm 33) applies and a fixed point exists.

**What the fixed point is.** At a fixed point with $\Lambda_{h'}=\lambda\in(0,1]$ on a subtree, $e(\pi_S,h')=\lambda\,e(\pi^*,h')+(1-\lambda)\,\mathrm N_{h'}$, and $\mathrm N_{h'}$ equals the true output distribution of $\pi_S^O(h')$ (accuracy holds for all *non-root* configurations of $\pi_S$, whose rules are unchanged, and $\pi_S$ halts w.p. $1$). So $O$'s answers about $\pi_S$ describe $\lambda\,\pi^*+(1-\lambda)\,\pi_S^O$, not $\pi_S^O$: **$O$ is not reflective about $\pi_S$ whenever $\lambda>0$ and $\pi^*(h')\neq\pi_S^O(h')$ somewhere on the subtree.** Instance A shows such fixed points occur ($\lambda=7/36$, alongside two honest ones); Instance B shows they can be the *only* fixed points ($\lambda=1$ at an equality node, and no honest trust-bound point exists). They cannot be ruled out.

**Are they harmless?** For $\pi_S$: no — the theorem's existential "there is a reflective oracle relative to which …" is not witnessed by them, and in Instance B it is not witnessed by anything. For the *floored* agent whose computation *is* "$\Lambda$-mix $\pi^*$ with the argmax": yes — the same point is an honest reflective oracle for that agent, with values $0.944$ (A) and $1$ or $0.85$ (B, depending on the floor variant) against $V^*=1$. This is the precise sense in which the convexified oracle-side construction proves Theorem 1 for $\pi_S$-with-a-floor and not for $\pi_S$ (§Recommended repair).

## Q2 — Gap 2: is the reset predicate a finite-coordinate, continuous condition on $K$? (verdict: CONFIRMED as stated, REFUTED as an independent obstruction)

**How "as assessed by eval" can be read.** The post gives no encoding. The quantities $\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)$, $w(\pi_S\mid h)$, $V^*_\xi(h)$ are reals produced by rO-machines, and a point of $K$ carries them only through (T1) the evals of estimator machines that emit rational approximations — a distribution over output strings at each precision, i.e. infinitely many $\Delta(A)$-coordinates; (T2) the query coordinates $q(M_x,1,p)$, $p\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]$, of a sampler machine $M_x$ with $\Pr[M_x^O=1]=x$ — at a fixed point the step function $\mathbf 1_{p<x}$ (free at $p=x$); or (C) a single comparison machine $D_h$ that flips a fair coin, runs $M_X$ on heads and outputs its bit, runs $M_Y$ on tails and outputs the complement, so $\Pr[D_h^O=1]=\tfrac12(1+Y-X)$, and one query $q(D_h,1,\tfrac12)$ answers "$X<Y$?" (this is the one-query comparison the audit attributes to the paper's tie-breaking).

**The literal claim is true (register: verified).** Under (T1)/(T2) the predicate reads infinitely many coordinates, and the natural readings are discontinuous in the product topology: with $\hat x(q):=\sup\{p:q(M_x,1,p)=1\}$, take $q_n(M_x,1,p)=\mathbf 1_{p<x_0}+\mathbf 1_{p=r_n}$ with distinct rationals $r_n\downarrow x_0+\tfrac12$; then $q_n\to\mathbf 1_{p<x_0}$ pointwise but $\hat x(q_n)\to x_0+\tfrac12\neq x_0=\hat x(\lim q_n)$. A $\liminf$/$\limsup$ of estimator outputs behaves the same way. Under (C) the predicate reads one coordinate, $c_h=q(D_h,1,\tfrac12)$, but the *branch condition* "$c_h=1$" (or "$c_h>0$") is a step in $c_h$ — discontinuous, and that is exactly the Q1 failure again.

**But the obstruction dissolves (register: PROVED (prose)).** Enumerate the dyadic rationals $(p_k)_{k\ge1}$ and set $G_x(q):=\sum_k2^{-k}\,q(M_x,1,p_k)$. This is a uniformly convergent series of coordinate projections, hence **continuous on all of $K$**. At a fixed point $q(M_x,1,p)=\mathbf 1_{p<x}$ for $p\neq x$ and is free at $p=x$, so if $x<y$ then $G_x(q)\le\sum_{p_k\le x}2^{-k}<\sum_{p_k<y}2^{-k}\le G_y(q)$ (a dyadic lies strictly between $x$ and $y$), and symmetrically; so **$G_X<G_Y\iff X<Y$ at every fixed point**, while $G$ is continuous everywhere. Defining the reset branch by "$G_X<G_Y$" makes the predicate a strict inequality between continuous functions on $K$ — the reset region is open, its complement closed — and the convexified $f$ of §Q1 then has closed graph by the case analysis given there. The audit's worry that the predicate "must itself be continuous in the required sense" is therefore met by a choice of encoding; Gap 2 is not a separate hole.

**Where every route lands.** Whatever the encoding, the reset weight at the equality set must be a free coordinate for the graph to be closed (Q1). Route (C) makes this vivid: the only closed-graph rule in the single coordinate $c_h$ is the linear one, $e'(\pi_S,h')=c_h\,e(\pi^*,h')+(1-c_h)\,\mathrm N_{h'}$, so **routing the comparison through $C_h$ forces the convexified construction** — the audit's §4.3 conclusion is confirmed. Route $G$ lands in the same place with more words. And $c_h$ at a fixed point is $O$'s answer probability at the exact tie $\Pr[D_h^O=1]=\tfrac12$, which reflective oracles leave free: the "partial reset" is nothing but the oracle's tie-break on the comparison, i.e. a legitimate output of the *floored* agent's computation and an illegitimate description of $\pi_S$'s.

**Two things the post's text does not settle.** (i) Which machines' evals are read — if the assessed $Q_\xi$ is computed by a machine that *simulates* $\pi_S$'s code rather than *querying* $O$ about $\pi_S$, then at a reset point it sees $\pi_S^O$ (the argmax), not the reset picture, and the contradiction argument's premise "$Q$ is assessed in the world where $\pi_S=\pi^*$ below $h$" fails (see Q6). (ii) The reset applies to the *root configuration* of $\pi_S$ on each input $h'$; the intermediate configurations must keep their normal rules, otherwise "$\mathrm N_{h'}$" is not the machine's actual behaviour (audit §4.4 bookkeeping — confirmed necessary).

## Q3 — Subtree consistency of oracle-decided resets (verdict: CONFIRMED structural, harmless for the value bound)

**The oracle is per-query.** $O$ answers $(T,\alpha,p)$ with a fixed probability; distinct invocations of $\pi_S$ on distinct inputs $h,h'$ are distinct computations with independent randomness, and the $(\mathrm{query},\mathrm{eval})$ space represents $\pi_S$ by one $\Delta(A)$-coordinate per input. A joint event "the subtree of $h$ is reset" — $\pi_S$ plays $\pi^*$ at $h$ *and* at every descendant, with a common coin — has no representation: the coordinates $e(\pi_S,h)$, $e(\pi_S,ha)$, … are marginals. So $f$ cannot enforce agreement; it can only assign each descendant a reset *weight*. In the convexified construction the coherent choice (closed graph, monotone in the prefix decisions, equal to the strict rule at $c\in\{0,1\}$) is $\Lambda_{h'}=1-\prod_{h''\preceq h'}(1-c_{h''})$: the probability that at least one prefix comparison, redrawn independently at $h'$, answers "violated". This is exactly the behaviour of the audit's prefix-checking $\pi_S^\dagger$ with independent oracle calls per prefix. **The founding-sketch worry stands:** at an equality node with $c_h=\lambda\in(0,1)$ the realized play is a per-node mixture, not a mixture of the two policies $\pi^*$ and $\mathrm{BR}$; "the reset subtree" is not a subtree.

**What coherence is actually needed for.** Only the contradiction argument (Q6) uses "reset at $h$ $\Rightarrow$ $\pi^*$ on the whole subtree", and it uses it at $c_h=1$, where $\Lambda_{h'}=1$ for every descendant — coherent. So the argument "at a fixed point no *strict* violation occurs" survives per-node independence (audit §5 claim, with the correction below). The value recursion for the floored agent (founding sketch §2; T1b) is per-node: the $\pi^*$-branch at $h'$ costs nothing at $h'$ and defers to children; the $\mathrm{BR}$-branch at $h'$ is taken only when the comparison at $h'$ itself answers "not violated", so the trust bound holds (weakly) at $h'$ and Lemma A applies. Neither step needs the branches at $h'$ and its children to be correlated. **Verdict: per-node independence is harmless for the bound.**

**Correction to audit §5 (register: verified).** "At any reflective oracle no prefix has $C_{h'}=$ yes with probability $1$" is too strong: $c_{h'}=1$ is a legal oracle answer whenever $\max_aQ_\xi(h',a)\le w\,V^*_\xi(h')$, and the argument only yields $\ge$, so $c_{h'}=1$ **at an equality node** is possible — Instance B realizes it ($\pi_S^\dagger$'s unique fixed point is $(1,1)$ with $c_{\text{root}}=1$ and $Q(\text{go})=0.8=w_0V^*$). It is harmless: the agent then plays $\pi^*$ on the subtree. The correct statement is "no prefix is *strictly* violated at a fixed point".

**Does $\pi_S^\dagger$ inherit a *problem*?** Not for the bound, but for the coincidence property: below an equality node with $c_h=\lambda$, every descendant is reset with probability $\ge\lambda$ *forever* (the root prefix is re-checked at each node), so $\pi_S^\dagger$ drifts toward $\pi^*$-play deep in the tree even at nodes where its own trust bound holds strictly. The current-node floor $\pi^\dagger$ has no such drift (Q4).

## Q4 — The current-node floor $\pi^\dagger$ (verdict: CONFIRMED well-defined; every reflective oracle is accurate about it; it fires at genuine fixed points, harmlessly)

**Definition.** $\pi^\dagger$ on input $h$: build the comparison machine $D_h$ for $X=\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)$ and $Y=w(\xi^{\pi^\dagger}\mid h)\,V^*_\xi(h)$, where $\xi$ is the rOSI mixture and $\xi^{\pi^\dagger}$ the self-hypothesis machine (percepts from $\xi$, actions by querying $O$ about $\pi^\dagger$); query $O(D_h,1,\tfrac12)$ once; on "1" output a sample of $\pi^*_\xi(h)$, on "0" output the argmax of $Q_\xi(h,\cdot)$ with the standard one-query tie-break.

**Well-definedness (register: PROVED (prose) modulo the paper's estimability facts).** Needed: (a) $Q_\xi(h,\cdot)$, $V^*_\xi(h)$ are $O$-estimable — the post asserts both value functions are rO-computable, and the audit cites the paper's optimal-policy theorem for $\pi^*_\xi$; (b) $w(\xi^{\pi^\dagger}\mid h)=w(\xi^{\pi^\dagger})\,\xi^{\pi^\dagger}(h)/\xi(h)$ is a ratio of $O$-estimable reals with $\xi(h)>0$, hence $O$-estimable; (c) an $O$-estimable $x\in[0,1]$ yields a machine $M_x$ with $\Pr[M_x^O=1]=x$ exactly (draw a uniform real bit by bit, refine the estimate of $x$ until the comparison is decided; halts w.p. $1$); (d) $\max$ and products of estimable reals are estimable; (e) $\pi^\dagger$ refers to its own index inside the self-hypothesis — Kleene's recursion theorem, already needed for rOSI itself. So $\pi^\dagger$ is an ordinary probabilistic oracle machine that halts with probability $1$, and **every** reflective oracle satisfies the accuracy sandwich for it — no change to $f$, no fixed-point selection. Caveat (unresolved bookkeeping, not a gap): whether the paper's notion of "rO-computable value function" delivers estimates that halt w.p. $1$ at *every* reflective oracle, or only at oracles where certain ties do not occur, was not re-verified from the paper's text here; the post relies on the same fact.

**No contradiction argument, and none needed (register: PROVED by instance).** For $\pi_S$ the post derives "reset $\Rightarrow$ continuation is $\pi^*$ $\Rightarrow$ $Q_\xi(h,a^*)\ge w\,V^*$". For $\pi^\dagger$ the continuation after firing is $\pi^\dagger$, not $\pi^*$, so $Q_\xi(h,a^*)\ge w'\,Q^{\pi^\dagger}_\xi(h,a^*)$ with $Q^{\pi^\dagger}_\xi(h,a^*)$ possibly $<V^*$; no contradiction follows, and indeed the floor can fire strictly at a fixed point. Instance B: the unique $\pi^\dagger$-fixed point is $(j,k)=(1,0)$; at the root $\max(0.7,\,0.8\cdot0.85)=0.7<0.8$ is a strict violation, so the floor fires with probability $1$ and plays `go`; at $n_1$ the bound holds ($0.85\ge0.8$) and BR $=$ stay. Value $0.85$ against $V^*=1$: loss $0.15=0.75\,\delta$, inside the audit's $(1+|A|)\delta$. At equality nodes the floor fires with the oracle's tie-break probability (Instance A: $\lambda=7/36$, value $0.944$).

**Coincidence with $\pi_S$.** At every node where $\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)>w\,V^*_\xi(h)$ strictly, $\pi^\dagger(h)$ is literally $\pi_S$'s computation (same argmax, same tie-break) — regardless of what happened at ancestors. Note the fine print: "$\pi_S$'s computation" is evaluated against a $\xi$ whose self-hypothesis is "I am $\pi^\dagger$"; the floor's *presence* changes which fixed points exist (Instance B: $\pi_S$'s only fixed point is $(0,0)$, $\pi^\dagger$'s is $(1,0)$), which is the whole point.

**What is not yet proved for $\pi^\dagger$.** The horizon-free bound $V^*_\xi(h)-V^{\pi^\dagger}_\xi(h)\le C\,\delta_h/(1-\delta_h)$ of the founding sketch §2 is SKETCHED, not proved: the delicate step is the posterior at the child after a rarely-taken $\pi^*$-action (odds can drop by the factor $\pi^\dagger(a^*\mid h)/\xi(a^*\mid h)$, which is small when the floor fires with small probability), handled there by optional stopping on the martingale $1-w_t$ plus a change of measure. Instance B is consistent with the bound; it is not a proof. This is T1b's job, not T1c's.

## Q5 — The finite-horizon finite-hypothesis Kakutani step (verdict: CONFIRMED clean with the hull at equality, not without)

**Setting.** Finite tree of histories $\mathcal H$, finite action set $A$, behaviour $\tilde\pi\in P:=\prod_{h\in\mathcal H}\Delta(A)$ (compact convex), a finite hypothesis class with a self-hypothesis of prior $w_0$ whose action conditionals are $\tilde\pi$, and every history of positive $\xi$-probability. Then $Q_{\tilde\pi}(h,a)$, $w_{\tilde\pi}(h)$, $V^*_{\tilde\pi}(h)$ are **continuous** functions of $\tilde\pi$ (rational functions with positive denominators; $V^*$ is a max over finitely many deterministic policies of continuous functions). Define $r_h(\tilde\pi):=\max_aQ_{\tilde\pi}(h,a)-w_{\tilde\pi}(h)V^*_{\tilde\pi}(h)$ (continuous) and the correspondences $\mathrm{BR}_h(\tilde\pi):=\Delta(\arg\max_aQ_{\tilde\pi}(h,a))$, $\Pi^*_h(\tilde\pi):=\Delta(\arg\max_aQ^*_{\tilde\pi}(h,a))$ — both nonempty, convex, closed-graph (the argmax correspondence of a continuous function on a finite set).

**Strict reset.** $F_h(\tilde\pi)=\Pi^*_h$ if some $h'\preceq h$ has $r_{h'}<0$, else $\mathrm{BR}_h$. Closed graph fails exactly as in Q1 — Instance B is this map, and it has no fixed point. **Kakutani does not apply and its conclusion fails.**

**Convexified.** $F_h(\tilde\pi)=\Pi^*_h$ if some prefix has $r_{h'}<0$; $=\mathrm{BR}_h$ if all prefixes have $r_{h'}>0$; $=\operatorname{conv}(\mathrm{BR}_h\cup\Pi^*_h)$ otherwise. Closed graph: if $\tilde\pi_n\to\tilde\pi$ and the limit is in the open region $\{r_{h'}>0\ \forall h'\preceq h\}$ or $\{\exists h'\preceq h:\ r_{h'}<0\}$, the $\tilde\pi_n$ are eventually there too and the branch's own closed graph finishes; on the boundary, $\sigma_n\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathrm{BR}_h(\tilde\pi_n)\cup\Pi^*_h(\tilde\pi_n))$ can be written $\sigma_n=s_n\beta_n+(1-s_n)\rho_n$ with $\beta_n\in\mathrm{BR}_h(\tilde\pi_n)$, $\rho_n\in\Pi^*_h(\tilde\pi_n)$ (two convex generators — Carathéodory is not even needed); extract convergent subsequences and use the two closed graphs. Values nonempty, convex, in the compact convex $P$. **Kakutani applies cleanly; a fixed point exists.** The per-node weighting $\Lambda_h$ of Q3 is one selection inside the hull; any continuous selection works.

**Where equality-node mixing enters, and whether "eval equals behaviour".** The hull is used only on $\{r_{h'}=0\}$: the fixed point may put weight $\lambda\in(0,1]$ on $\Pi^*_h$ there. For $\pi_S$ (whose computation is $\mathrm{BR}_h$, full stop) this is the finite partial-reset problem: the fixed point $\tilde\pi$ is not $\pi_S$'s behaviour against $\xi[\tilde\pi]$ unless $\lambda=0$ or $\Pi^*_h=\mathrm{BR}_h$. Instance A (a fixed point with $\lambda=7/36$ alongside honest ones) and Instance B (the *only* fixed point has $\lambda=1$ at an equality node) show it does not dissolve. For the floored agent it dissolves completely: the comparison and its tie-break are part of the strategy, so $\tilde\pi$ *is* $\pi^\dagger$'s behaviour against $\xi[\tilde\pi]$ — the finite analogue of "every reflective oracle is accurate about $\pi^\dagger$". In the finite model the hull can be replaced by the smaller set $\{\lambda\,\rho+(1-\lambda)\beta:\lambda\in[0,1]\}$ for fixed selections $\beta,\rho$; this changes nothing above.

**A remark on what the finite model shows about existence for $\pi_S$ (register: PROVED by instance; scope: finite shadow).** Kakutani on the *unmodified* $\mathrm{BR}$ map always gives honest fixed points of $\pi_S$, but Instance B shows all of them can violate the trust bound. Hence "there is an honest fixed point satisfying $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ at every $h$" — the lemma the revised proof needs — is **false in the finite shadow** for $\delta=0.2$. Whether an rOSI history realizes such a posterior configuration is not shown; I put CONJECTURED (~0.8) on "the lemma also fails in the rOSI setting", since the residual in Instance B is one deterministic rO-computable hypothesis and nothing about it is exotic.

## Q6 — The contradiction argument, re-derived (verdict: CONFIRMED sound *given* a fixed point of the strict $f$)

**Claim.** If $(q,e)\in f(q,e)$ for the strict-reset $f$, then no history is reset, so $e$ obeys the unmodified rules everywhere and $O:=q$ is a reflective oracle at which $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ holds for every $h$.

**Step 1 — accuracy for every machine other than $\pi_S$ (survives the modification).** App. C's induction "by the number of computation steps" proves $\Pr[T^O=\alpha]\le e(T)(\alpha)\le1-\Pr[T^O\neq\alpha]$ for a configuration $T$ using only (i) the rule at $T$ (deterministic step / coin / oracle call weighted by $q$), and (ii) for oracle calls, that $q$ is the oracle. Neither (i) nor (ii) is touched for configurations of machines other than $\pi_S$, nor for the *non-root* configurations of $\pi_S$; the $q'$-constraints (which pin $q(\pi_S,\alpha,p)$ to $e(\pi_S)(\alpha)$) are also untouched. So every estimator machine — for $Q_\xi(h,\cdot)$, $V^*_\xi(h)$, $w(\pi_S\mid h)$, and $\pi^*_\xi(h)$ — is accurate at the fixed point, **where its true $O$-behaviour is computed with $O$'s answers about $\pi_S$, i.e. with the reset picture $\tilde\pi:=e(\pi_S,\cdot)$.** Hence "assessed by eval" $=$ true value in the world $\xi[\tilde\pi]$. This step needs the reading (Q2(i)) that these machines reach $\pi_S$ by *querying* $O$, never by simulating $\pi_S$'s code: a simulating hypothesis sees $\pi_S^O$ (the argmax) and at a reset point would disagree with the querying self-hypothesis. The post's parenthesis "though it may make queries about $\pi_S$" is exactly this reading; it should be stated as a requirement.

**Step 2 — at a reset minimal $h$, $\tilde\pi=\pi^*_\xi$ on the whole subtree.** By the reset rule applied to every root configuration of $\pi_S$ on inputs $h'\succeq h$ (audit §4.4: the rule must be applied to root configurations of all inputs on the subtree, not to the tree of configurations of a single input; bookkeeping, confirmed).

**Step 3 — Lemma A's lower half.** $Q_\xi(h,a)=\sum_Tw(T\mid ha)\,Q^T(h,a)\ge w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid ha)\,Q^{\tilde\pi}_\xi(h,a)$ since returns are $\ge0$. Pick $a^*\in\operatorname{supp}\pi^*_\xi(h)$ with $\pi^*_\xi(a^*\mid h)\ge\xi(a^*\mid h)$ (exists: both sum to $1$ over the support of $\pi^*_\xi(h)$, the second summing to at most $1$). Then $w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid ha^*)=w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)\,\tilde\pi(a^*\mid h)/\xi(a^*\mid h)\ge w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)$ and $Q^{\tilde\pi}_\xi(h,a^*)=Q^{\pi^*}_\xi(h,a^*)=V^*_\xi(h)$, so $\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)\ge w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)\,V^*_\xi(h)$.

**Step 4.** Step 3 says $h$ is not *strictly* violating, but $h$ was reset only because it was strictly violating: contradiction. So nothing is reset, $e$ obeys the unmodified rules, App. C's argument gives a reflective oracle, and $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ holds everywhere (else $h$ would have been reset). $\square$

**What the argument does not do.** It presupposes a fixed point of the *strict* $f$, which Kakutani–Fan does not deliver (Q1) and which can fail to exist (Instance B). Under the convexified $f$ Step 4 breaks in two ways: a weight $\lambda\in(0,1)$ makes $Q^{\tilde\pi}_\xi(h,a^*)<V^*_\xi(h)$ possible so Step 3 gives nothing (Instance A), and $\lambda=1$ is consistent with the equality that Step 3 does yield (Instance B). The eval-accuracy facts for other machines survive in both cases; what fails is the inference from "not strictly violated" to "not reset".

## What this means for Cole's theorem as stated (register: my analysis; scopes marked)

**The existence of the special reflective oracle is not established.** The proof sketch has one load-bearing step — a fixed point of the modified $f$ that is honest about $\pi_S$ — and the step fails twice over: the strict map is not u.s.c. and can have no fixed point (Instance B, finite shadow); the u.s.c. repair yields fixed points that are honest about a floored agent and dishonest about $\pi_S$ (Instances A and B), and Instance B has *no* honest fixed point satisfying the trust bound at every node. So the lemma "there is a reflective oracle at which $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ holds for every history" is false in the finite shadow and CONJECTURED (~0.8) false in the rOSI setting. This does not by itself refute the theorem's *conclusion*: in Instance B the honest fixed point loses $1.5\delta$, inside the audit's explicit constant. Whether Theorem 1 for $\pi_S$ is true remains **OPEN**; what is dead is the route "select an oracle where the trust bound never fails".

**What is established (minimal honest statement).**
1. PROVED (prose), audit §3: at any reflective oracle and any $h$, $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ implies $V^{\pi_S}_\xi(h)\ge V^*_\xi(h)-(1+|A|)\delta_h-O(\delta_h^2)$ with $\delta_h:=1-w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)$.
2. PROVED (prose), this note: the convexified oracle-side construction has a fixed point (Kakutani–Fan with a continuous encoding of the comparison), and that fixed point is a reflective oracle for the prefix-checking floored agent $\pi_S^\dagger$ — equivalently, $\pi_S^\dagger$ is an ordinary rO-machine and App. C's unmodified existence theorem already covers it.
3. PROVED (prose): at every reflective oracle, $\pi^\dagger$ and $\pi_S^\dagger$ coincide with $\pi_S$'s computation at every node where the trust bound holds strictly (for $\pi_S^\dagger$: and at all its prefixes), and no node is strictly violated in the sense that matters for the bound (at each node the $\mathrm{BR}$-branch is taken only when the bound holds weakly there).
4. SKETCHED (T1b, founding sketch §2): for every reflective oracle, $w(\xi^{\pi^\dagger}\mid h)>1-\delta$ implies $V^{\pi^\dagger}_\xi(h)>V^*_\xi(h)-C\delta$ with $C=O(|A|)$; the missing step is the posterior drop after a low-probability floor action.

**On the statement itself.** Beyond the audit's remarks ($\delta$ unquantified; optimality is w.r.t. $\xi$'s own model), the theorem's "there is a reflective oracle relative to which …" should be read as an *equilibrium selection* claim, and equilibrium selection through the oracle is exactly what the (query, eval) space cannot express beyond per-query marginals (Q3). The natural repair moves the selection into the agent, where it becomes a *for every reflective oracle* statement about a slightly different agent.

State the result for the floored agent, preferably **(iii)**, as a *for every reflective oracle* theorem: "$\pi^\dagger$ is $\pi_S$ with a one-query floor; at every reflective oracle, $w(\xi^{\pi^\dagger}\mid h)>1-\delta$ implies $V^{\pi^\dagger}_\xi(h)>V^*_\xi(h)-C\delta$; and $\pi^\dagger(h)=\pi_S(h)$ wherever $\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)>w(\xi^{\pi^\dagger}\mid h)\,V^*_\xi(h)$." Reasons for (iii) over (ii): no drift below equality nodes; a per-node bound with no prefix bookkeeping; one query. Reason to keep (ii) in mind: in Instance B it reaches $V^*$ exactly where (iii) loses $0.75\delta$ — the prefix memory sometimes helps, but the bound does not need it. If Cole wants Theorem 1 for $\pi_S$ *itself*, the honest next step is Instance B's question: is there an rOSI history at which every reflective oracle violates $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ — and if so, does the conclusion survive anyway (it does in B, by $1.5\delta$)? That is a new theorem, not a repair.
