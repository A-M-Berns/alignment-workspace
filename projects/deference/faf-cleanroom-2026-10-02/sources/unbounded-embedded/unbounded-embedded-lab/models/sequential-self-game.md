## 0. Headlines

- **Negative half (REFUTES the unqualified reading; machine-verified).** In sink-or-swim, for a fixed non-dogmatic prior with self-weight $1-\delta$, the trapped policy (never jump) is a fixed point of the self-game whenever the off-policy residue is mostly sinkers ($s\ge1-c/b$), *for every $\delta$*. It satisfies the premise of the revised Theorem 1 ($w(\text{self}\mid\epsilon)=1-\delta$) and violates its conclusion by $b-c$, independent of $\delta$. So the theorem's "there is a reflective oracle" existential is essential (§2).
- **Positive half, plain agent (PROVED prose, machine-verified).** At any fixed point and any history $h$ where the trust bound holds, $V^*_\xi(h)-V^{\pi}_\xi(h)\le\dfrac{1-w_h}{w_h}$ with $w_h:=w(\text{self}\mid h)$ — the sibling's $\delta/(1-\delta)$ with $w_h$ in place of $1-\delta$, **no $|A|$ factor even for mixed argmax** (the averaging trick of [updateless-self-game](updateless-self-game.md) Theorem C′ transfers verbatim, §3). The constant is asymptotically tight: the sink-or-swim trap with $c=(1-\delta)b$ attains $\text{gap}/O_h=1-\delta$.
- **Positive half, floored agent $\pi^\dagger$ (PROVED prose, with a REFUTED sub-claim).** Every fixed point of the current-node floor is near-optimal at every history: $V^*_\xi(h)-V^{\pi^\dagger}_\xi(h)\le O_h\big(3+\ln^+(1/O_h)\big)$ where $O_h:=(1-w_h)/w_h$, horizon-free, $|A|$-free (§4). The sketch's stronger claim "$\le C\,O_h$ with $C=O(|A|)$, horizon-free" is **REFUTED, machine-verified**: an explicit chain of equality nodes has loss $\tfrac{O_h}{2}\sum_{k<K}\frac1{1+O_h2^k}\approx\tfrac{O_h}{2}\log_2(1/O_h)$ (§4.3). The correct rate is $\Theta(\delta\log(1/\delta))$. Fixed points of $\pi^\dagger$ exist by Kakutani under the continuous-extension convention (§4.5). Strict resets can fire with probability $1$ at a fixed point; they are harmless at that node and leave the self-posterior unchanged or higher (§4.4).
- **Structural lemma found on the way (PROVED prose, machine-checked).** Conditioning on an action selects the hypotheses that play it, so the non-self mixture after $(h,a)$ is itself a policy in the $\xi$-environment: $\bar Q(h,a)=Q^{\bar\pi}_\xi(h,a)\le Q^*_\xi(h,a)$ (Lemma A′, §1). Consequences: a refined bound $(1-w_h)V^*_\xi(h)[1+\bar p_hO_h]$, and pure fixed points always exist at $T=2$ (§4.5) — unlike the updateless sibling. Existence of pure fixed points for $T\ge3$ is open (CONJECTURE, search evidence only).
- **Consequence for Cole's theorem (CONJECTURE for the lift, PROVED in the finite model).** Replacing $\pi_S$ by $\pi_S^\dagger$ turns "$\exists$ oracle" into "$\forall$ fixed point" at the price of $\varepsilon=O(\delta\log(1/\delta))$ instead of $O(\delta)$.

## 1. Definitions (the finite model)

**Tree.** Histories $h=a_1e_1\cdots a_te_t$ with $a_i\in A$, $e_i\in E$ finite, depth $t\le T$. A history is *terminal* if $t=T$ or the tree assigns it no actions (used for "episode ends", e.g. after `stay`). Every non-empty history carries a reward $r(h)\ge0$ received on arrival, discounted by $\gamma^{t-1}$ ($\gamma\le1$; $\gamma=1$ allowed). **Normalisation:** every path return $\sum_{i\le T}\gamma^{i-1}r(h_{1:i})$ lies in $[0,1]$, so every conditional return from any $h$ lies in $[0,1]$ (this is Cole's "rewards shifted and rescaled to $[0,1]$" and it is what Lemma A needs; the Python rescales random instances by the maximum path return).

**Hypotheses.** A finite class $\{S\}\cup\{\nu_1,\dots,\nu_m\}$ of *universes* over joint action/percept histories; each non-self $\nu$ is a pair of fixed kernels $\nu(a\mid h)$, $\nu(e\mid ha)$, with joint $\nu(h)=\prod_i\nu(a_i\mid h_{<i})\nu(e_i\mid h_{<i}a_i)$. Prior: $w(S)=1-\delta$, $\sum_iw(\nu_i)=\delta$. Write $\xi_{-S}(h):=\sum_iw(\nu_i)\nu_i(h)$ for the (unnormalised) non-self mixture and $\bar\pi(a\mid h):=\xi_{-S}(ha)/\xi_{-S}(h)$ for its action conditional.

**The self-hypothesis** $S$ has action kernel $\pi$ (the agent's policy, a fixed point to be determined) and percept kernel $\xi(e\mid ha)$ — the mixture's own percept conditional (audit fact F1). Unwinding: $\xi(e\mid ha)=\xi_{-S}(e\mid ha)$ whenever $\xi_{-S}(ha)>0$, i.e. **the self-hypothesis's percept model is the non-self mixture's percept conditional, and it does not depend on $\pi$.** Where $\xi_{-S}(ha)=0$ the equation $\xi(e\mid ha)=w\,\xi(e\mid ha)+(1-w)\xi_{-S}(e\mid ha)$ is vacuous ($w=1$); convention: use the prior-weighted non-self percept mixture $\sum_iw(\nu_i)\nu_i(e\mid ha)/\delta$ there. (This fallback is needed only at $(h,a)$ that no non-self hypothesis reaches; it never affects sink-or-swim or the chain family.) So $\xi_S(h)=\prod_i\pi(a_i\mid h_{<i})\,\xi(e_i\mid h_{<i}a_i)$, $\xi(h)=(1-\delta)\xi_S(h)+\xi_{-S}(h)$, and the posterior $w_h:=w(S\mid h)=(1-\delta)\xi_S(h)/\xi(h)$.

**Facts F1, F2 (machine-checked at every node of every instance).** F1: $w_{hae}=w_{ha}$ — percepts never move the self-posterior. F2: $\operatorname{odds}(S\mid ha)=\operatorname{odds}(S\mid h)\cdot\pi(a\mid h)/\bar\pi(a\mid h)$, so the posterior rises when the agent plays a deterministic action that the non-self mixture plays with probability $<1$, and falls at a mixing node by the factor $\pi(a\mid h)$ in odds at worst.

**Values.** EDT values $Q_\xi(h,a):=\mathbb E_\xi[\text{return}\mid ha]$ with future actions from $\xi$'s own action conditionals $\xi(a'\mid h')=\xi(h'a')/\xi(h')$; $V_\xi(h)=\sum_a\xi(a\mid h)Q_\xi(h,a)$; $M(h):=\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)$. Policy values $Q^\pi_\xi,V^\pi_\xi$: percepts from $\xi(e\mid ha)$, actions from $\pi$. Optimal values $Q^*_\xi,V^*_\xi$: percepts from $\xi(e\mid ha)$, the agent controlling all future actions (backward induction). Because the percept kernel is $\pi$-independent, **$V^*_\xi$ and $\pi^*_\xi$ do not depend on $\pi$** in this model. $\pi^*_\xi(h)$ is fixed once and for all as the first action attaining $V^*_\xi(h)$ (the floored agent needs one canonical choice; any fixed selection works).

**Zero-probability actions — continuous extension.** If $\xi(h)=0$ set $\xi(a\mid h):=\pi(a\mid h)$ and $w_h:=1$. Then at any $(h,a)$ with $\xi(ha)=0$ one gets $Q_\xi(h,a)=Q^\pi_\xi(h,a)$, which is exactly $\lim_{\pi(a|h)\downarrow0}Q_\xi(h,a)$ (Lemma A below with $w_{ha}=1$; the ratio is constant in $\pi(a\mid h)$ on $(0,1]$). Under this convention $\pi\mapsto Q_\xi(h,\cdot)$ and $\pi\mapsto w_h$ are continuous on the product of simplices (§4.5). Where the convention matters: the sink-or-swim good point ($\xi(\text{stay})=0$) and every $\xi$-null subtree; the sibling's "unavailable" convention would make those nodes vacuous instead.

**Lemma A (mixture decomposition; machine-checked).** For every $(h,a)$ with $\xi(ha)>0$:
$$\xi(a\mid h)\,Q_\xi(h,a)\;=\;w_h\,\pi(a\mid h)\,Q^\pi_\xi(h,a)\;+\;(1-w_h)\,\bar\pi(a\mid h)\,\bar Q(h,a),\qquad \bar Q(h,a):=\frac{\sum_iw(\nu_i)\nu_i(ha)Q^{\nu_i}(h,a)}{\xi_{-S}(ha)}\in[0,1],$$
where $Q^{\nu}(h,a)$ is the return expected under $\nu$'s own joint after $ha$. Equivalently $Q_\xi(h,a)=w_{ha}Q^\pi_\xi(h,a)+(1-w_{ha})\bar Q(h,a)$ — the audit's form.

**Lemma A′ (the non-self mixture is a policy in the $\xi$-environment; PROVED prose, machine-checked at every node).** $\bar Q(h,a)=Q^{\bar\pi}_\xi(h,a)\le Q^*_\xi(h,a)\le V^*_\xi(h)$. *Proof.* $\bar Q(h,a)=\mathbb E_{\xi_{-S}}[\text{return}\mid ha]$, and the joint measure $\xi_{-S}(\cdot\mid ha)$ factorizes into its action conditionals $\bar\pi$ and its percept conditionals, which coincide with $\xi$'s by F1 wherever $\xi_{-S}>0$ (nodes where they might differ have $\bar\pi$-probability $0$). So it is the value of the policy $\bar\pi$ against the percept kernel $\xi(e\mid\cdot)$, which $V^*_\xi$ dominates. $\square$ This is stronger than the audit's "$\bar Q\le1$" and it has a consequence for existence (§4.5): in this model *no hypothesis can be more optimistic than the agent's own optimum*, because conditioning on an action selects exactly the hypotheses that play it. (It lifts to rOSI unchanged: F1 holds there and $V^*_\xi$ is a sup over all policies.)

**Fixed point (plain agent).** $\pi$ with $\operatorname{supp}\pi(\cdot\mid h)\subseteq\arg\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)$ at every non-terminal $h$ (including $\xi$-null ones — the finite analogue of an oracle consistent about $\pi_S$ on every input). *Pure* = deterministic.

**Trust bound at $h$.** $(\mathrm{TB}_h):\ M(h)\ge w_h\,V^*_\xi(h)$. Write $O_h:=(1-w_h)/w_h$ for the odds against the self-hypothesis; $w_h>1-\delta$ iff $O_h<\delta/(1-\delta)$.

**Floored agent $\pi^\dagger$ (current-node floor, [00-founding-sketches](../notes/00-founding-sketches.md) §2).** At $h$: if $M(h)<w_hV^*_\xi(h)$ play $\pi^*_\xi(h)$; if $>$ play any mix over $\arg\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)$; at equality play $\lambda_h\,\delta_{\pi^*_\xi(h)}+(1-\lambda_h)\sigma_h$ with $\sigma_h$ any mix over the argmax set and $\lambda_h\in[0,1]$. A *fixed point of $\pi^\dagger$* is a $\pi$ satisfying this at every non-terminal $h$ for the $\xi$ built from $\pi$ (the self-hypothesis is "I am $\pi^\dagger$"). Loss: $\varepsilon(h):=V^*_\xi(h)-V^\pi_\xi(h)\in[0,1]$.

## 2. Sink-or-swim: the two-fixed-point self-game (T1a; the negative result)

**Instance** ([00-founding-sketches](../notes/00-founding-sketches.md) §1). Island: `stay` (reward $c$, terminal) or `jump`; water: `swim` (reward $b$) or `sink` ($0$); $0<c<b\le1$; deterministic percepts; $\gamma=1$. Non-self hypotheses: $H_{\text{sink}}$ (jump, then sink), weight $\delta s$; $H_{\text{swim}}$ (jump, then swim), weight $\delta(1-s)$. Write $j:=\pi(\text{jump}\mid\text{island})$.

**Theorem A (sink-or-swim characterization; PROVED prose, machine-verified on a $270$-point $(b,c,\delta,s)$ grid).** At every fixed point the agent swims in the water (the last step's $Q_\xi$ is the immediate reward, $b>0$). At the island $Q_\xi(\text{stay})=c$ and
$$Q_\xi(\text{island},\text{jump})\;=\;b\cdot\frac{(1-\delta)j+\delta(1-s)}{(1-\delta)j+\delta}.$$
Hence:
- *trapped* ($j=0$) is a fixed point iff $b(1-s)\le c$, i.e. $s\ge1-c/b$ — **independent of $\delta$**;
- *good* ($j=1$) is a fixed point iff $b(1-\delta s)\ge c$, i.e. $\delta s\le1-c/b$;
- a *mixed* fixed point $0<j<1$ exists iff both pure conditions hold strictly, and then it is unique: $j^*=\dfrac{\delta\,(c-b(1-s))}{(1-\delta)(b-c)}$, at which $Q_\xi(\text{jump})=c$ exactly. (The sketch's "when both pure fixed points exist" needs *strictly*: at a boundary the solution $j^*$ is $0$ or $1$.)

*Proof.* Posterior after `jump` is proportional to $((1-\delta)j,\ \delta s,\ \delta(1-s))$; only the swimmer and the (swimming) self return $b$. The three cases are the sign of $Q_\xi(\text{jump})-c$, which is affine in $j$ after clearing the positive denominator; solving $=0$ gives $j^*$. At $j=1$, $\xi(\text{stay})=0$ and the continuous extension gives $Q_\xi(\text{stay})=Q^\pi(\text{stay})=c$, the same value. $\square$

**Trust bound.** $V^*_\xi(\text{island})=b$, $w_{\text{island}}=1-\delta$. At a trapped fixed point $M=c$, so $(\mathrm{TB})$ holds iff $\delta\ge1-c/b$. At the good point $M=b(1-\delta s)\ge(1-\delta)b$ always. So $(\mathrm{TB})$ rejects the trap exactly when $\delta<1-c/b$, and in the regime $\delta<1-c/b\le s$ **both pure fixed points exist for the same prior** and $(\mathrm{TB})$ selects the good one. (Machine-verified on the grid; the sketch's statements are all exact.)

**The negative result (register: PROVED in the finite model; the transfer to rOSI is INTERPRETATION).** Take $\delta<1-c/b$ and $s\ge1-c/b$. The trapped fixed point has $w(\text{self}\mid\epsilon)=1-\delta>1-\delta'$ for every $\delta'>\delta$ — the premise of the revised Theorem 1 — while $V^{\pi}_\xi(\epsilon)=c$ and $V^*_\xi(\epsilon)=b$: the conclusion fails by $b-c$, which does not shrink as $\delta\to0$ (for fixed $b,c$ the trap exists for every $\delta$). Thus in the finite shadow, "consistent self-knowledge" alone does not give near-optimality; the existential over oracles in Cole's statement does real work, and what it buys is exactly $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$. This echoes a point made in private discussion [scrubbed] and `comparison-mupi-vs-uea` §3's oracle-sourced trap, exhibited with exact numbers.

**Where the mixed point sits.** With $s<1$, the mixed point satisfies $(\mathrm{TB})$ iff $c\ge(1-\delta)b$; e.g. $b=1,c=\tfrac12,\delta=\tfrac12,s=\tfrac34$ gives $j^*=\tfrac12$, gap $\tfrac14\le O_h=1$. The Python checks the bound of §3 at every trust-bound fixed point (pure or mixed) on the grid.

## 3. The one-line bound at any fixed point (the positive result for the plain agent)

**Theorem B (PROVED prose; machine-verified at every trust-bound node of every pure fixed point of the random instances of §5, and at the sink-or-swim mixed points).** Let $\pi$ be any fixed point (pure or mixed) and $h$ any non-terminal history with $w_h>0$ at which $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ holds. Let $\bar p_h:=\sum_{a\in\operatorname{supp}\pi(\cdot|h)}\bar\pi(a\mid h)\le1$ be the non-self mixture's mass on the agent's supported actions. Then
$$V^*_\xi(h)-V^\pi_\xi(h)\;\le\;(1-w_h)\Big[(1-\bar p_h)\,V^*_\xi(h)+\frac{\bar p_h}{w_h}\Big]\;\le\;\frac{1-w_h}{w_h}\;=\;O_h .$$
In particular $w_h>1-\delta$ implies $V^\pi_\xi(h)>V^*_\xi(h)-\dfrac{\delta}{1-\delta}$. **No factor $|A|$, and no "no-ties" clause is needed.**

*Proof.* If $\xi(h)=0$ then $w_h=1$, $Q_\xi(h,\cdot)=Q^\pi_\xi(h,\cdot)$, the fixed-point condition says the support maximizes $Q^\pi_\xi(h,\cdot)$, and $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ says this maximum is $\ge V^*_\xi(h)$; so the gap is $0=O_h$. Otherwise, for every $a\in\operatorname{supp}\pi(\cdot\mid h)$ we have $\xi(ha)>0$, $Q_\xi(h,a)=M(h)\ge w_hV^*_\xi(h)$ (fixed point + trust bound), and Lemma A with $\bar Q\le1$:
$$w_h\,\pi(a\mid h)\,Q^\pi_\xi(h,a)\;\ge\;\xi(a\mid h)\,w_hV^*_\xi(h)-(1-w_h)\bar\pi(a\mid h).$$
Sum over the support, using $\sum_a\pi(a\mid h)Q^\pi_\xi(h,a)=V^\pi_\xi(h)$, $\sum_a\xi(a\mid h)=w_h+(1-w_h)\bar p_h$ (F2 in unnormalised form: $\xi(a\mid h)=w_h\pi(a\mid h)+(1-w_h)\bar\pi(a\mid h)$), and divide by $w_h$:
$$V^\pi_\xi(h)\;\ge\;V^*_\xi(h)\big(w_h+(1-w_h)\bar p_h\big)-\frac{(1-w_h)\bar p_h}{w_h},$$
which rearranges to the first inequality; the second uses $V^*_\xi\le1\le1/w_h$ and $\bar p_h\le1$. $\square$

**Remarks.**
- *Why the audit's $(1+|A|)\delta$ was loose.* The audit ([audit-revised-theorem-1](../../audit-revised-theorem-1.md) §3) bounds each supported action separately via the posterior *after* the action, paying F2's worst-case drop $\pi(a\mid h)\ge1/|A|$ at ties, and then averages. Summing Lemma A in unnormalised form *before* dividing weights the per-action slack by $\bar\pi(a\mid h)$, and $\sum_a\bar\pi(a\mid h)\le1$ absorbs the multiplicity — the same mechanism as [updateless-self-game](updateless-self-game.md) Theorem C′. The audit's extra $\delta$ term came from $Q_\xi/w'\ge Q_\xi$ where $Q_\xi/w'\ge V^*$ was available. Sketch §4's "no-ties version $\delta(1+1/(1-\delta))$" is therefore superseded: the tie-free and tied cases have the same bound $O_h$.
- *Tightness (machine-verified).* Sink-or-swim with $b=1$, $c=1-\delta$, $s=1$: the trap is a fixed point, satisfies $(\mathrm{TB})$ with equality, and has $\text{gap}/O_h=(b-c)/O_h=1-\delta$. So $\sup\text{gap}/O_h\to1$ as $\delta\to0$; the constant $1$ in $O_h$ cannot be improved. On random instances (§5) the worst ratio observed was below this.
- *Transfer of the sibling's findings (b), (c).* Both transfer exactly: (b) the $|A|$ factor is avoidable for mixed argmax by averaging; (c) the bound is $\delta/(1-\delta)$ — here $O_h$, with the *posterior* $w_h$ replacing the prior $1-\delta$, which is what Cole's pointwise statement asks for.
- *Sink-or-swim consistency check.* At a trapped fixed point with $(\mathrm{TB})$ ($\delta\ge1-c/b$): gap $b-c\le\delta b\le\delta\le O_h$. ✓
- *Refinement via Lemma A′ (machine-verified at every trust-bound node).* Using $\bar Q\le V^*_\xi(h)$ instead of $\bar Q\le1$ in the proof gives $V^*_\xi(h)-V^\pi_\xi(h)\le(1-w_h)\,V^*_\xi(h)\big[1+\bar p_h\,O_h\big]\le O_h\,V^*_\xi(h)$. At $\bar p_h=0$ (the non-self mixture never plays what the agent plays — the sink-or-swim trap) the gap is at most $(1-w_h)V^*_\xi(h)$, attained with equality by the tight trap above.

## 4. The floored agent $\pi^\dagger$ (T1b)

### 4.1 Theorem C (every fixed point of $\pi^\dagger$ is near-optimal; PROVED prose, horizon form machine-verified)

**Theorem C.** Let $\pi$ be any fixed point of $\pi^\dagger$ (§1). For every non-terminal $h$ with $w_h>0$, with $O_h=(1-w_h)/w_h$ and $\ln^+x:=\max(\ln x,0)$:
$$\varepsilon(h)\;=\;V^*_\xi(h)-V^{\pi}_\xi(h)\;\le\;O_h\big(3+\ln^+(1/O_h)\big)\qquad\text{and}\qquad \varepsilon(h)\;\le\;O_h\,(1+T).$$
No trust-bound hypothesis, no $|A|$, no tie condition. In particular $w_h>1-\delta$ gives $\varepsilon(h)\le\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\big(3+\ln\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}\big)=O(\delta\log(1/\delta))$.

*Proof.* **Step 0 ($w_h=1$).** If $\xi_{-S}(h)=0$ then the whole subtree of $h$ has $w\equiv1$ and $Q_\xi=Q^\pi_\xi$ (no non-self mass; continuous extension). There $M(h')=\max_aQ^\pi_\xi(h',a)\le V^*_\xi(h')$ always, so every node is R or E; by backward induction $V^\pi_\xi=V^*_\xi$ on the subtree (at a node whose children are optimal, $Q^\pi_\xi=Q^*_\xi$, the node is E, and both $\pi^*_\xi(h')$ and any argmax of $Q^\pi_\xi$ are optimal). So $\varepsilon(h)=0=O_h$. Assume $0<w_h<1$ from now on; every node below $h$ in the argument has $w>0$ (odds only change by finite factors along played actions).

**Step 1 (node classification).** Write $a^*_h:=\pi^*_\xi(h)$, $\mathcal A_h:=\arg\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)$. Call $h$ of type **R** if $M(h)<w_hV^*_\xi(h)$ (then $\pi(h)=\delta_{a^*_h}$; put $\lambda_h:=1$); type **G** if $M(h)\ge w_hV^*_\xi(h)$ and $\operatorname{supp}\pi(h)\subseteq\mathcal A_h$ (strict argmax, or equality with $\lambda_h=0$, or equality with $a^*_h\in\mathcal A_h$; put $\lambda_h:=0$); type **E** otherwise: $M(h)=w_hV^*_\xi(h)$, $a^*_h\notin\mathcal A_h$, $\pi(h)=\lambda_h\delta_{a^*_h}+(1-\lambda_h)\sigma_h$ with $\lambda_h\in(0,1]$ and $\operatorname{supp}\sigma_h\subseteq\mathcal A_h$. (An E node with $\lambda_h=1$ is a reset-with-probability-one at an equality node.)

**Step 2 (node identity).** Since $\pi^*_\xi$ and $\pi$ see the same percept kernel and the same immediate rewards, $V^*_\xi(h)-Q^\pi_\xi(h,a^*_h)=\sum_e\xi(e\mid ha^*_h)\,\varepsilon(ha^*_he)=:\bar\varepsilon_h$ (absolute discounting: no extra factor). Hence, exactly,
$$\varepsilon(h)\;=\;\lambda_h\,\bar\varepsilon_h\;+\;\ell_h,\qquad \ell_h:=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_h}\pi(a\mid h)\big(V^*_\xi(h)-Q^\pi_\xi(h,a)\big)\ \ (\ell_h=0\text{ at R nodes}).$$
This is the recursion of [00-founding-sketches](../notes/00-founding-sketches.md) §2 — with one correction: its "non-reset branch has no dependence on children" is right, but the *posterior at the children* is what the reset branch passes on, and it is not $w_h$ (Step 4).

**Step 3 (node loss).** At G and E nodes $(\mathrm{TB}_h)$ holds and every $a\in\mathcal A_h\cap\operatorname{supp}\pi(h)$ has $Q_\xi(h,a)=M(h)\ge w_hV^*_\xi(h)$, so the per-action inequality in the proof of Theorem B gives
$$\ell_h\;\le\;(1-w_h)\Big[(1-\lambda_h)V^*_\xi(h)+\bar p_h\Big(\tfrac1{w_h}-V^*_\xi(h)\Big)\Big],\qquad \bar p_h:=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_h\cap\operatorname{supp}\pi(h)}\bar\pi(a\mid h).$$
At an E node $a^*_h\notin\mathcal A_h$, so $\bar p_h\le1-\bar\pi(a^*_h\mid h)$. Define $\alpha_h:=(1-w_h)(1-\lambda_h)V^*_\xi(h)\le(1-\lambda_h)\min\{1,O_h\}$ and $\beta_h:=(1-w_h)\bar p_h(1/w_h-V^*_\xi)\le O_h\bar p_h$; so $\ell_h\le\alpha_h+\beta_h$, with $\beta_h\le O_h(1-\bar\pi(a^*_h\mid h))$ at E nodes, $\beta_h\le O_h$ and $\alpha_h=0$ at G nodes (Theorem B's computation), $\alpha_h=\beta_h=0$ at R nodes.

**Step 4 (odds along the $\pi^*$-path).** At R and E nodes the agent plays $a^*_h$ with probability $\lambda_h$; by F2 and F1, $O_{ha^*_he}=O_h\,\bar\pi(a^*_h\mid h)/\lambda_h$ for every $e$. So a reset with $\lambda_h=1$ never lowers the posterior (it raises it unless the non-self mixture also plays $a^*_h$ surely), while an equality node with $\lambda_h<1$ *raises the odds against the self by $1/\lambda_h$*. Consequently, along any sequence $h_0=h,\ h_{k+1}=h_ka^*_{k}e_{k+1}$ of R/E nodes, with $\Lambda_k:=\prod_{i<k}\lambda_{h_i}$ and $P_k:=\prod_{i<k}\bar\pi(a^*_{h_i}\mid h_i)$:
$$\Lambda_k\,O_{h_k}\;=\;O_{h_0}\,P_k\qquad(\text{exact; both sides are }0\text{ once }P_k=0).$$

**Step 5 (unrolling; the measure question).** Iterating Step 2 from $h$ until the path hits a G node or a terminal node ($\varepsilon=0$):
$$\varepsilon(h)\;=\;\mathbb E\Big[\sum_{k\ge0}\Lambda_k\,\ell_{h_k}\Big],$$
the expectation being over the percepts $e_{k+1}\sim\xi(\cdot\mid h_ka^*_k)$ along the $\pi^*$-driven path. *This is an exact identity, not an optional-stopping estimate.* $\Lambda_k$ is the probability that the agent itself plays $a^*$ at $h_0,\dots,h_{k-1}$, so the weight $\Lambda_k\cdot\prod\xi(e_{i}\mid h_{i-1}a^*_{i-1})$ is the self-hypothesis's own path measure $\xi_S(\cdot\mid h)$ restricted to the event "the agent chose the $\pi^*$-action at every step so far". No change of measure from $\xi$ is needed, and the sketch's "optional stopping under $\xi$, then a $1/w_h$ factor" is not what happens: the path measure is neither $\xi$ nor dominated-by-$\xi$-up-to-$1/w_h$ in the useful direction, because $O_{h_k}$ grows like $O_h/\Lambda_k$ along the chain (Step 4).

**Step 6 (the $\beta$ sum telescopes — horizon-free).** Along any fixed percept path, by Steps 3–4, with $K$ the index of the stopping G node (or the terminal index, in which case the last term is absent):
$$\sum_k\Lambda_k\beta_{h_k}\;\le\;O_h\Big[\sum_{k<K}(1-\bar\pi(a^*_{h_k}\mid h_k))P_k+P_K\Big]\;=\;O_h\big[(1-P_K)+P_K\big]\;=\;O_h .$$

**Step 7 (the $\alpha$ sum — where the logarithm lives).** $\sum_k\Lambda_k\alpha_{h_k}\le\sum_k(1-\lambda_{h_k})\min\{\Lambda_k,\ \Lambda_kO_{h_k}\}=\sum_k(1-\lambda_{h_k})\min\{\Lambda_k,\ O_hP_k\}\le\sum_k(1-\lambda_{h_k})\min\{\Lambda_k,O_h\}$. (i) Crudely, each term is $\le O_h$ and there are at most $T$ terms: $\le O_hT$, giving the horizon form. (ii) If $O_h\ge1$, $\sum_k(1-\lambda_k)\Lambda_k=\Lambda_0-\Lambda_{K+1}\le1\le O_h$. If $O_h<1$, let $k_0$ be the first index with $\Lambda_{k_0}<O_h$ (or $K+1$). For $k\ge k_0$ the tail is $\le\Lambda_{k_0}<O_h$ (telescoping). For $k<k_0$: $\Lambda_{k_0-1}\ge O_h$ forces $\sum_{i<k_0-1}(1-\lambda_i)\le\sum_{i<k_0-1}\ln(1/\lambda_i)=\ln(1/\Lambda_{k_0-1})\le\ln(1/O_h)$ (using $1-x\le\ln(1/x)$), and the term $k=k_0-1$ contributes at most $O_h$. So $\sum_k\Lambda_k\alpha_{h_k}\le O_h\big(2+\ln^+(1/O_h)\big)$.

Adding Steps 6 and 7 and averaging over percept paths: $\varepsilon(h)\le O_h(3+\ln^+(1/O_h))$, and (i) gives $\varepsilon(h)\le O_h(1+T)$. $\square$

### 4.2 What the sketch got right and wrong (register: PROVED/REFUTED as marked)

- Reset branch loses nothing at the node and passes the loss to the $\pi^*$-children: **right** (Step 2).
- Non-reset branch loses $O(|A|\delta_h)$: **right but loose** — it loses at most $O_h$, and at an E node the argmax part loses $\alpha+\beta$ with the $\beta$ part telescoping (Steps 3, 6).
- "Optional stopping gives $\mathbb E[\delta_{h_\tau}]=\delta_h$; changing measure costs $1/w_h$; hence $C\delta_h/(1-\delta_h)$ horizon-free": **REFUTED.** The posterior along a chain of equality nodes is $O_{h_k}=O_hP_k/\Lambda_k$, which can be $2^k O_h$; the loss is $\Theta(O_h\log(1/O_h))$ in the worst case (§4.3). The correct horizon-free statement is Theorem C.

### 4.3 The lower bound: a chain of equality nodes (REFUTES "$C\,O_h$ horizon-free"; machine-verified)

**Instance** (`chain_instance`). Nodes $h_0,\dots,h_K$ along `go`; at $h_k$ ($k<K$) the alternative `out` is a leaf with reward $r_k$; at $h_K$, `go` is a leaf with reward $1$ and `out` a leaf with reward $0$; deterministic percepts, $\gamma=1$. One non-self hypothesis of weight $\delta$: plays `go` at $h_0..h_{K-1}$ and `out` at $h_K$ (so its own continuation value is $0$ everywhere on the chain). $V^*_\xi\equiv1$ on the chain, $\pi^*_\xi=$ `go`. Set $O_0:=\delta/(1-\delta)$ and $r_k:=1/(1+O_02^k)$.

**Claim (machine-verified for $\delta=2^{-m}$, $K=m$, $m\in\{2,3,4,5,6,8,10\}$).** The policy "`go` with probability $\tfrac12$ at $h_0,\dots,h_{K-1}$; `go` at $h_K$" is a fixed point of $\pi^\dagger$ (and not of the plain agent) at which every $h_k$, $k<K$, is an equality node with $w_{h_k}=r_k$, $h_K$ is a strict-argmax node, and
$$\varepsilon(h_0)\;=\;\frac{O_0}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{1}{1+O_0 2^k}\;\approx\;\frac{O_0}{2}\log_2\frac1{O_0}.$$
*Why.* At $h_k$: `out` is never played by the non-self hypothesis, so $Q_\xi(h_k,\text{out})=r_k$ (continuous extension irrelevant: $\pi(\text{out})>0$); `go` has $Q_\xi(h_k,\text{go})=w_{h_k\text{go}}\,(1-\varepsilon(h_{k+1}))+0<w_{h_k}$ because the mixing halves the self-odds ($w_{h_k\text{go}}<w_{h_k}$). So $M(h_k)=r_k=w_{h_k}\cdot V^*$ exactly when $w_{h_k}=r_k$, which the halving produces inductively ($O_{h_{k+1}}=2O_{h_k}$, Step 4 with $\bar\pi(\text{go})=1$, $\lambda=\tfrac12$). Equality nodes allow any $\lambda$, so $\lambda=\tfrac12$ is consistent; at $h_K$ the non-self plays `out`, so `go` has $Q_\xi=1>w_{h_K}$ (strict, no loss). The node loss at $h_k$ is $\tfrac12(1-r_k)$ with chain weight $2^{-k}$. Exact values: $\text{gap}/O_0=0.675,\ 1.145,\ 1.631,\ 2.124,\ 2.621,\ 3.619,\ 4.618$ for $m=2,3,4,5,6,8,10$ — growing by $\approx\tfrac12$ per doubling of $1/\delta$, as predicted; the horizon form $O_0(1+T)$ holds throughout.

So Theorem C's $\ln(1/O_h)$ is necessary up to the constant: the true worst case is $\Theta(O_h\log(1/O_h))$, i.e. $\Theta(\delta\log(1/\delta))$, and it is *not* $O(|A|\delta)$. Note that the loss at $h_0$ is still $o(1)$ as $\delta\to0$, so the qualitative repair ("$\forall$ fixed point, $\varepsilon\to0$ with $\delta$") survives.

### 4.4 Resets that fire with probability one (machine-verified)

The subtree version's contradiction argument ([audit-revised-theorem-1](../../audit-revised-theorem-1.md) §5) does not apply to the current-node floor, and indeed a *strict* reset can fire w.p. $1$ at a fixed point: `strict_reset_check` takes the chain with $r_0=0$ and the agent playing `go` surely at $h_0$, with the $\lambda=\tfrac12$ chain from $h_1$. Then $Q_\xi(h_0,\text{go})=w_{h_0}(1-\varepsilon(h_1))<w_{h_0}$ and $Q_\xi(h_0,\text{out})=0$, so $M(h_0)<w_{h_0}V^*_\xi(h_0)$ strictly ($\delta=\tfrac1{16}$, $K=4$: $M=0.856<0.9375$). The reset is harmless at $h_0$ ($\varepsilon(h_0)=\varepsilon(h_1)=0.087$) and the self-posterior at $h_1$ is unchanged ($w_{h_1}=w_{h_0}=\tfrac{15}{16}$) because both the agent and the non-self mixture play `go`; in general a sure reset multiplies the self-odds by $1/\bar\pi(a^*\mid h)\ge1$ (Step 4) — it can only *raise* self-trust. In the $2{,}000$ random instances of §5 no pure floored fixed point had a strict reset anywhere; the phenomenon needs a lossy $\pi^*$-subtree below the node, which the chain supplies.

### 4.5 Existence of fixed points (PROVED prose, Kakutani; the convention matters)

Under the continuous extension, $\pi\mapsto Q_\xi(h,a)$ is continuous on the product of simplices $\prod_h\Delta(A)$: by Lemma A in unnormalised form, $Q_\xi(h,a)=\frac{(1-\delta)\xi_S(ha)Q^\pi_\xi(h,a)+\xi_{-S}(ha)\bar Q(h,a)}{(1-\delta)\xi_S(ha)+\xi_{-S}(ha)}$ with $\xi_S(ha)$ and $Q^\pi_\xi$ polynomial in $\pi$; if $\xi_{-S}(ha)>0$ the denominator is bounded away from $0$; if $\xi_{-S}(ha)=0$ the expression is identically $Q^\pi_\xi(h,a)$ (extended by that value at $\xi_S(ha)=0$). Likewise $w_h$ is continuous (identically $1$ when $\xi_{-S}(h)=0$), and $V^*_\xi$ is constant. Define the correspondence $B(\pi):=\prod_hB_h(\pi)$ with $B_h(\pi)=\{\delta_{a^*_h}\}$, $\Delta(\mathcal A_h(\pi))$, or $\operatorname{conv}(\{\delta_{a^*_h}\}\cup\Delta(\mathcal A_h(\pi)))$ according to the sign of $M(h)-w_hV^*_\xi(h)$. Values are nonempty, convex, compact. Closed graph: if $\pi^n\to\pi$ and $\sigma^n\in B(\pi^n)$, $\sigma^n\to\sigma$, then at a node where the sign at $\pi$ is strict the same sign holds eventually and $\sigma(h)$ lies in the closed set $\{\delta_{a^*}\}$ or (by upper hemicontinuity of the argmax, $\mathcal A_h(\pi^n)\subseteq\mathcal A_h(\pi)$ eventually) in $\Delta(\mathcal A_h(\pi))$; at an equality node all three possible branches of $\pi^n$ give $\sigma^n(h)\in\operatorname{conv}(\{\delta_{a^*}\}\cup\Delta(\mathcal A_h(\pi)))$ eventually, which is closed. Kakutani gives a fixed point. The same argument with $B_h=\Delta(\mathcal A_h(\pi))$ gives **mixed fixed points of the plain agent** for every instance. (Without the continuous extension — the sibling's "unavailable" convention — the graph is not closed and the argument fails, exactly as [updateless-self-game](updateless-self-game.md) §6 found.)

**Pure fixed points: exist at $T=2$ (PROVED prose); at $T\ge3$ open (CONJECTURE that they always exist; search evidence only).** At $T=2$ the depth-1 decisions are $\pi$-independent and optimal, so under the pure policy "$x$ at the root" one has $Q_\xi(\epsilon,x)=w^+_xQ^*_\xi(\epsilon,x)+(1-w^+_x)Q^{\bar\pi}_\xi(\epsilon,x)\ge Q^{\bar\pi}_\xi(\epsilon,x)$ (Lemma A′) and $Q_\xi(\epsilon,y)=Q^{\bar\pi}_\xi(\epsilon,y)$ for $y\ne x$; hence the root action maximizing the non-self continuation $Q^{\bar\pi}_\xi(\epsilon,\cdot)$ is a pure fixed point (of the plain agent; for the floored agent, either that action or, if it violates $(\mathrm{TB})$, the reset action $\pi^*_\xi(\epsilon)$, which then satisfies the floor's condition by the same computation). A first attempt at a $T=2$ counterexample ("each hypothesis knows its own world") failed for exactly this reason — action-conditioning makes $\xi$'s percept model agree with whichever hypotheses play the action, so there is no Jensen gap for a hypothesis to exploit. For $T=3$ the depth-1 policy depends on the root action through the posterior, and I have neither a proof nor a counterexample: the targeted search `pure_existence_search` (near-deterministic hypotheses, $|A|=2$, $|E|\in\{1,2\}$) ($800$ instances) and the $2{,}000$ random instances of §5 always found a pure fixed point of both agents. This differs from the updateless sibling, where pure existence fails ([updateless-self-game](updateless-self-game.md) §6); the sequential model's F1 coupling is the plausible reason.

## 7. Where the finite model differs from Cole's rOSI setting (and whether it matters for lifting)

1. **Finite horizon, finite hypothesis class, exact fixed points instead of a reflective oracle.** A fixed point here is a policy consistent with its own argmax; in rOSI the oracle is consistent about *every* machine, and $\pi_S$ is one machine. Theorems B and C are pointwise-at-$h$ and their proofs use only Lemma A, F1, F2 and backward induction over the $\pi^*$-path, all of which have rOSI counterparts (audit §1, §3). The infinite-horizon version of Step 5 needs the $\pi^*$-path sum to converge — fine with discounting ($\varepsilon\le1$ and the tail is $\gamma^k$-small). *Likely lifts.*
2. **The other hypotheses are fixed kernels.** In rOSI other machines may query the oracle about $\pi_S$ (simulate the agent), so $\bar Q(h,a)$ and $\bar\pi$ depend on the fixed point. Nothing in B or C uses their independence from $\pi$ — only Lemma A′ ($\bar Q\le V^*_\xi$) and the existence proof do, and A′ survives (its proof uses only F1). *Existence in rOSI is Cole's problem, not ours: $\pi_S^\dagger$ is an ordinary $O$-machine, so every reflective oracle is already a fixed point of it.*
3. **$V^*_\xi$ is $\pi$-independent here** because the percept kernel is. In rOSI it is also determined by the non-self posterior's percept predictions (F1), but machines that simulate $\pi_S$ make it $\pi_S$-dependent. The floor compares against $V^*_\xi$ *at the fixed point*, so this is a matter of what the floor computes, not of the bound. *Does not matter.*
4. **Continuous extension at $\xi$-null nodes.** In rOSI every action has positive $\xi$-probability (the universal mixture contains a uniform randomizer), so the convention is never invoked; here it is needed for existence and for the sink-or-swim good point. *A finite-model artifact; rOSI is the easy case.*
5. **Returns normalised to $[0,1]$, absolute discounting.** Cole's normalisation; the only place returns enter is Lemma A's $\bar Q\le1$ (now $\le V^*_\xi$). *Same.*
6. **One canonical $\pi^*_\xi$.** Cole's $\pi^*_\xi$ is any optimal policy (rO-computable). The floored agent's fixed point needs a fixed selection; with a $\pi$-dependent selection the closed-graph argument would need the selection to be continuous, which it need not be. *Choose one selection in the rOSI definition too.*
7. **The trust bound is stated with the posterior $w_h$, not the prior.** This is what the pointwise theorem needs; the "for all time w.h.p." clause would come from the $1-w_t$ martingale (audit §3.3) and is not re-done here.
8. **What does not lift as stated: the negative result's interpretation.** Sink-or-swim exhibits a fixed point violating the conclusion *for the plain agent*; Cole's theorem quantifies over oracles and claims only that a good one exists, so the negative result contradicts nothing he wrote — it shows the existential is load-bearing and that the finite shadow of his oracle-side construction is $(\mathrm{TB})$-selection. Whether his modified $f$ actually produces a reflective oracle is the audit's Gap 1/2 and is untouched here.
