**One-line summary (inferred).** mupi's trap is a universal-prior version of "the honest self-model is dominated, at every history, by an on-path-indistinguishable pessimistic twin"; the twin is built into the reference UTM, so it holds for every oracle, and in Cole's vocabulary it violates the *premise* $w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)>1-\delta$ rather than the trust bound. It is the corner of our sink-or-swim family where the self-mass is forced to $\approx 0$ for every candidate policy — not the oracle-sourced two-fixed-point situation of [00-founding-sketches](00-founding-sketches.md) §1.

## Q1. The construction (register: verified unless marked)

### Q1.1 Objects and where they live in `main.tex`

The theorem (L1804–1806, `thrm:counter-example`): for a $k$-E-AIXI$^\tau$ agent with fixed $k\ge1$ and Solomonoff prior over $\tau$-POMs, "there exists a computable ground-truth environment $\mu$ and a reference universal monotone Turing machine $U$ ... such that the agent interacting with $\mu$ does not converge to the embedded best response w.r.t. $\xi$, despite the fact that this setup satisfies the grain-of-truth property." Footnote 57 (same line) credits Cole's post with having "concurrently indicated (but not proved rigorously)" the Self-AIXI case. The proof is App. C.18: L3836–4569 decoupled (C.18.1), L4570–5163 embedded (C.18.2). The two-bullet outline is at L3847–3850: (i) design $\mu_k$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{bad}}$ so that $k$-step planning w.r.t. $\mu_k^{\pi_{\mathrm{bad}}}$ is suboptimal for $\mu_k$; (ii) design $U$ so that $\zeta_U\approx\pi_{\mathrm{bad}}$ and $\xi_U\approx\mu_k$ on the relevant histories.

**The environment** $\mu_{R,k}$ (L3853–3866): $\mathcal A=\{\mathrm{up},\mathrm{down}\}$, trivial observation, rewards $\{0,R,1\}$, deterministic, with (eq. `eq:reward-function-mge-k`, L3859–3865)
$$r_t(a_{1:t})=\begin{cases}R&\text{if }a_i=\mathrm{up}\ \forall i\le t\\ 1&\text{if }t\ge k+1\text{ and }a_{t-k:t}=(\mathrm{down},\dots,\mathrm{down})\\ 0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$
So "up forever" pays $R$ per step; the first "down" forfeits $R$ for good, and only $k{+}1$ *consecutive* downs unlock reward $1$, which then continues as long as the streak does. Values are $(1-\gamma)$-normalized discounted sums, so $V\in[0,1]$.

**The bad policy** is $\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$ ("always up"). The parameter window is $\gamma^{k+1}<R<\gamma^k$ (Corollary `cor:optimal-and-k-step-approx`, L4228–4245; Theorem proofs L4566, L5160).

**The two history sets** on which the mixture is controlled (L3903, L4082–4087): $\Theta^*_{\mu_{R,k}}$ = histories whose percepts are $\mu_{R,k}$-compatible; $\Theta^{*\text{-up}}_{\mu_{R,k}}$ = those with only "up" actions; and
$$\mathcal A^{*\text{-up-}k\text{-down-}*\text{-up}}=\bigcup_{t\ge k}\bigcup_{0\le i\le t-k}\{\mathrm{up}\}^i\times\{\mathrm{down}\}^k\times\{\mathrm{up}\}^{t-k-i},$$
i.e. ups, then **exactly** $k$ downs, then ups — the histories the $k$-step planner's *terminal self-model* is consulted at when it contemplates playing down from the all-up path. Nothing is claimed about histories with more than $k$ downs, or with down–up–down patterns (this matters for Q2.4).

### Q1.2 Why the $k$-step planner is trapped (the decision-theoretic half)

Warm-up with the exact objects (L4070–4080): on the all-up path, for the $k$-step planner w.r.t. $\mu_{R,k}^{\pi_{\mathrm{up}}}$,
$$Q^k_{\mu_{R,k}^{\pi_{\mathrm{up}}}}(h_{<t},a_t)=\max_{a_{t+1:t+k-1}}(1-\gamma)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma^i r_{t+i}(a_{1:t+i})+\gamma^kV_{\mu_{R,k}^{\pi_{\mathrm{up}}}}(h_{<t}\,x_{t:t+k-1}),$$
and if $a_t=\mathrm{down}$ every within-horizon reward is $0$ (a streak of $k$ downs is one short of paying) while the terminal value is $0$ because the self-model "only samples up actions which would have zero rewards since they were preceded by $a_t=\mathrm{down}$"; if $a_t=\mathrm{up}$, $Q^k\ge R>0$. The authors' gloss (L4078): "its self-model is a bad policy that is incapable of 'continuing on doing the right thing' ... it does not 'trust its future self' on doing the right thing."

Robust version (Lemma `lem:k-step-approx-Mvpup-suboptimal`, L4093–4108): if $\nu$ $\epsilon$-approximates $\mu_{R,k}$ on $\Theta^*_{\mu_{R,k}}$ and $\pi$ $\epsilon$-approximates $\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$ on $\Theta^{*\text{-up-}k\text{-down-}*\text{-up}}\cup\Theta^{*\text{-up}}$, then for every $h_{<t}\in\Theta^{*\text{-up}}$
$$Q^k_{\nu^\pi}(h_{<t},\mathrm{up})\ \ge\ \frac{(1-\gamma)(1-\epsilon)R}{1-\gamma(1-\epsilon)^2},\qquad Q^k_{\nu^\pi}(h_{<t},\mathrm{down})\ \le\ k\epsilon+\gamma^k(\gamma+2\epsilon),$$
so for $R>\gamma^{k+1}$ and small $\epsilon$ the planner always plays up. Conversely (Lemma `lem:optimal-q-value-for-env-close-to-mge-k`, L3907–3920) for every $h\in\Theta^*_{\mu_{R,k}}$
$$Q^*_{\nu}(h,\mathrm{down})\ \ge\ \frac{\gamma^k(1-\epsilon)^{k+1}(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma+\gamma\epsilon},\qquad Q^*_\nu(h,\mathrm{up})\ \le\ \frac{\epsilon}{1-\gamma}+\sup_{\ell\ge1}\{(1-\gamma^\ell)R+\gamma^{\ell+k}\},$$
so for $R<\gamma^k$ the infinite-horizon optimal policy for $\nu$ (= the embedded best response, Def. `def:embedded-best-response` L604–609, the optimal policy for the environment $\xi(e\mid ha)$) always plays down. The Corollary (L4228–4245) records the value gap: along the agent's trajectory $V\to R$ for the $k$-step planner and $\to1$ for the best response.

### Q1.3 How the reference UTM is chosen (the prior half)

Notation (L4250–4266): $\pi^\tau_M(a\mid h)=\mathbb P[M^\tau(\langle h\rangle)=\langle a\rangle]$, $\nu^\tau_M(e\mid h,a)$ likewise; $\zeta_{U,\tau}=\sum_M2^{-K_U(\langle M\rangle)}\pi^\tau_M$, $\xi_{U,\tau}=\sum_M2^{-K_U(\langle M\rangle)}\nu^\tau_M$; $w^{U,\tau}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\pi)=\sum_{M:\pi^\tau_M=\pi}2^{-K_U(\langle M\rangle)}$. One machine can implement both a policy and an environment (Lemma L4268–4286).

**The UTM lemma** (`lem:choose-UTM-to-make-Fx-likely`, L4288–4300): for $\epsilon>0$, a finite set $\mathcal S_0$ of $\ge\ell=\lceil\log(2/\epsilon)\rceil$ strings, and a computable injective $F$ fixing $\mathcal S_0$ pointwise, there is a universal monotone TM $U$ with (1) $\sum_{x\in\mathcal S_0}2^{-K_U(x)}\ge1-\epsilon$ and (2) for every $\mathcal S$ closed under $F$ and every weight $w$ with $w(F(x))\ge w(x)$,
$$\sum_{x\in\mathcal S\cap F(\mathcal B^*)}w(x)2^{-K_U(x)}\ \ge\ (1-\epsilon)\sum_{x\in\mathcal S}w(x)2^{-K_U(x)}.$$
Proof mechanism (L4303–4345): enumerate machines so that indices $1..\ell$ are printers of $\mathcal S_0$ (costing $i$ bits each), and interleave every machine $T'$ with $\ell-1$ copies of $S_F(T')$ ("run $T'$, then apply $F$ to its output"), so that every program for $x\notin F(\mathcal B^*)$ yields a program for $F(x)$ shorter by $\ell-1$ bits:
$$K_U(F(x))\ \le\ K_U(x)-\ell+1\qquad(x\in\mathcal S\setminus F(\mathcal B^*)),\ \text{L4351}.$$

**Instantiation** (Lemma `lem:decoupled-solomonoff-mixture-eps-approximates-MgeRk-andMvpup`, L4372–4560): $\ell=\lceil\log(4/\epsilon)\rceil$; $\mathcal S_0=\{\langle M_1\rangle,\dots,\langle M_\ell\rangle\}$, $\ell$ syntactically distinct machines all implementing $(\pi_{\mathrm{up}},\mu_{R,k})$; and $F(\langle M\rangle)=\langle S_k(M)\rangle$ for every other $M$, where the **reverting wrapper** $S_k(M)$ (Algorithm `alg:reverting-apom`, L4384–4409) is: as an environment, exactly $\mu_{R,k}$; as a policy, scan $a_{<t}$ for a streak $a_{t':t'+k-1}=(\mathrm{down})^k$; if found, output "up"; otherwise simulate $M$ on $\langle h_{<t}\rangle$. Consequences:
$$w_{\mathrm{env}}(\mu_{R,k})\ge1-\tfrac\epsilon2,\qquad w_{\mathrm{pol}}(\pi_{\mathrm{up}})\ge1-\tfrac\epsilon2\quad(\text{L4441–4449}),$$
$$\sum_{M\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal S}}w(S_k(M))2^{-K_U(\langle S_k(M)\rangle)}\ \ge\ (1-\epsilon)\sum_{M\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal S}}w(M)2^{-K_U(\langle M\rangle)}\quad(\text{eq. `eq:weighted-lower-bound-on-machines', L4436}),$$
for any $w$ non-decreasing under $S_k$, where $\mathcal M_{\mathcal S}$ = all machines except $M_1..M_\ell$.

**"Dogmatic belief over the agent's own future behaviour", formally** (inferred summary of L4468–4545): the posterior conditional of the *policy* mixture at any $h_{<t}\in\Theta^{*\text{-up-}k\text{-down-}*\text{-up}}$ is written as a ratio over $\mathcal M_{\mathcal S}$ (eq. L4503, `eq:Mmutau-as-a-chronological-fraction-explicit`, the $M_i$ dropping out because they assign $0$ to any down),
$$\zeta_{U,\tau}(a_t\mid h_{<t})=\frac{\sum_{M\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal S}}2^{-K_U(\langle M\rangle)}\pi^\tau_M(a_{1:t}\,\|\,e_{<t})}{\sum_{M\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal S}}2^{-K_U(\langle M\rangle)}\pi^\tau_M(a_{<t}\,\|\,e_{<t-1})},$$
and with $w(M):=\pi^\tau_M(a_{<t}\|e_{<t-1})$ — which is non-decreasing under $S_k$ because $S_k(M)$ agrees with $M$ before the streak and predicts the (up) actions after it with probability $1$ (L4525–4535) — the wrapped machines alone carry $(1-\epsilon)$ of the numerator's mass and all predict "up", giving (L4548–4550)
$$\zeta_{U,\tau}(\mathrm{up}\mid h_{<t})\ \ge\ 1-\epsilon\qquad\text{for every }h_{<t}\in\Theta^{*\text{-up-}k\text{-down-}*\text{-up}}_{\mu_{R,k}}.$$
So the "dogmatic self-belief" is: *conditional on ever having played $k$ downs in a row, I play up (and hence earn nothing) forever after, with probability $\ge1-\epsilon$* — and this is a statement about the mixture's conditionals at those histories, whoever generated them. On the all-up path the same holds by the simpler $w(\pi_{\mathrm{up}}\mid h)\ge1-\epsilon/2$ (L4467). Which hypotheses get what weight: the $\ell$ copies of $\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$ get $\ge1-\epsilon/2$ jointly; every other policy $\pi$ gets $w_{\mathrm{pol}}(\pi)\le\epsilon/2$ (Kraft); and among the machines consistent with a post-streak history, the reverting ones outweigh the non-reverting ones $\ge(1-\epsilon):\epsilon$.

**Conclusion** (Theorem `thrm:counter-example-decoupled`, L4562–4568): fix $R\in(\gamma^{k+1},\gamma^k)$, $\epsilon$ small per the Corollary, $U$ per the lemma; then $\zeta_{U,\tau}$ and $\xi_{U,\tau}$ satisfy the Corollary's hypotheses, so the $k$-step planner w.r.t. $\xi_{U,\tau}^{\zeta_{U,\tau}}$ plays up forever in $\mu_{R,k}$ while the infinite-horizon optimal planner w.r.t. the same mixture plays down; "In particular, Self-AIXI does not always converge to AIXI." Note the logic is not asymptotic: the agent is trapped from $t=1$ and the mixture never changes its mind, because on the all-up path no hypothesis is ever refuted.

### Q1.4 What changes in C.18.2 (embedded / coupled)

Hypotheses are universes $\nu^\tau_M$ (joint semimeasures on $(\mathcal A\times\mathcal E)^*$), $\xi_{U,\tau}=\sum_M2^{-K_U(\langle M\rangle)}\nu^\tau_M$ (L4572–4581). $\mathcal S_0$ = $\ell$ copies of the single universe $\mu_{R,k}^{\pi_{\mathrm{up}}}$ (L4846). Two wrappers instead of one: $S_{\mu_{R,k}}(M)$ (Algorithm L4941; guarantees eqs L4856–4862: environment part $=\mu_{R,k}$, joint distribution equal to $\nu_M$ on $\mu_{R,k}$-compatible prefixes, $0$ elsewhere — implemented by simulating $M$ and halting if it emits an incompatible percept) and $S_{*\text{-up-after-}k\text{-down}}(M)$ (Algorithm L5003: mimic $M$ until $k$ consecutive downs, then continue as $\mu_{R,k}^{\pi_{\mathrm{up}}}$). Because the UTM lemma now needs two maps, it is restated for commuting $F_1,F_2$ (Lemma `lem:choose-UTM-to-make-Fx-and-Fprimex-likely`, L4584–4600), and since the two wrappers commute only distributionally, $S_2$ is defined as $S_1^{n}\circ S_{*\text{-up-after-}k\text{-down}}\circ S_1^{-n}$ with $n=n_{S_1}(M)$ the wrapping depth (L5015–5040) so that $S_1S_2=S_2S_1$ syntactically while $\nu_{S_2(M)}=\nu_{S_{*\text{-up-after-}k\text{-down}}(M)}$. The rest is the same two inductions (environment part on $\Theta^*_{\mu_{R,k}}$, L5070–5100; policy part, Case 1 all-up L5104–5112, Case 2 post-streak L5113–5152) using eqs L5060 ($\sum_{\mathcal S_0}\ge1-\epsilon$) and L5068 (the weighted $(1-\epsilon)$ bound for $m\in\{1,2\}$). Theorem `thrm:counter-example-coupled` (L5156–5162) then applies the Corollary because $\xi_{U,\tau}=\nu_{\xi}^{\pi_\xi}$ (a universe is its own policy-part/environment-part pair). Nothing in the coupling is used against the agent: the coupled construction is the decoupled one with the extra bookkeeping needed to keep the joint semimeasures consistent.

## Q2. Cole's self-hypothesis $\xi^{\pi_S}$ inside mupi's class (register: inferred — derived from the source's lemmas; the paper never discusses this hypothesis)

### Q2.1 Is it in the class?

Decoupled (C.18.1): Cole's self-hypothesis has no percept part to speak of; its analogue is the *policy* hypothesis $\pi^k:=\pi^k_{\xi^{\zeta}_{U,\tau}}$, the agent's own policy as a function on all histories. It is in $\mathcal M^\tau_{\mathrm{pol}}$ exactly when the agent is $\tau$-POM-implementable, which is the grain-of-truth clause of the theorem ("in case, e.g., of a reflective oracle", L4565; Thm `thm:eaixi-ro` for the RO case, Thm `thm:k_mupi_implementation` for the RUI case). Coupled (C.18.2): the analogue is the universe $\xi^{\pi^k}$ := actions from $\pi^k$, percepts from $\xi_{U,\tau}(e\mid ha)$ — Cole's object verbatim ([audit-revised-theorem-1](../../audit-revised-theorem-1.md) §1); it is in $\mathcal M^\tau_{\mathrm{uni}}$ for a reflective $\tau$ by the same self-reference argument Cole uses. For a "dummy" $\tau$ neither is in the class, but then Cole's theorem does not apply anyway.

### Q2.2 Its prior weight — the one-line bound

The construction gives the dominant hypothesis almost everything: $w_{\mathrm{pol}}(\pi_{\mathrm{up}})\ge1-\epsilon/2$ (L4448) in the decoupled case and $w_{\mathrm{uni}}(\mu_{R,k}^{\pi_{\mathrm{up}}})\ge\sum_{\mathcal S_0}2^{-K_U}\ge1-\epsilon$ (eq. L5060) in the coupled case. Since $\sum_\pi w_{\mathrm{pol}}(\pi)\le\sum_M2^{-K_U(\langle M\rangle)}\le1$ (Kraft), **every policy other than $\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$ has prior $\le\epsilon/2$, and every universe other than $\mu_{R,k}^{\pi_{\mathrm{up}}}$ has prior $\le\epsilon$.** So the only way the honest self can be heavy is to *be* the dominant hypothesis:
- Coupled: $\xi^{\pi^k}=\mu_{R,k}^{\pi_{\mathrm{up}}}$ as universes would require $\xi^{\pi^k}$ to be the point mass on the all-up history, i.e. $\xi_{U,\tau}(e^*\mid h\,\mathrm{up})=1$ exactly along it. But $U$ is universal, so some machine of positive weight outputs "up" followed by a non-$\mu_{R,k}$ percept, whence $\xi(e^*\mid h\,\mathrm{up})<1$ strictly (the paper only proves $\ge1-\epsilon$, L5095–5105, but strictness is immediate). Hence $\xi^{\pi^k}\neq\mu_{R,k}^{\pi_{\mathrm{up}}}$ and $w_{\mathrm{uni}}(\xi^{\pi^k})\le\epsilon$, **unconditionally**.
- Decoupled: $\pi^k=\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$ as functions would require the agent to play up at *every* history, including ones where it already holds a $k$-streak. See Q2.4.

### Q2.3 Its posterior along the actual trajectory

The actual trajectory is the all-up path with $\mu_{R,k}$-compatible percepts (Corollary L4228). On it the dominant hypothesis has likelihood exactly $1$, and so does the honest self (it *is* the agent, and the agent plays up there). Therefore, writing $D$ for the dominant hypothesis and $S$ for the honest self,
$$w(S\mid h_{<t})=\frac{w(S)\,S(h_{<t})}{\sum_Tw(T)\,T(h_{<t})}\ \le\ \frac{w(S)}{w(D)}\ \le\ \frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\qquad\text{for every }t,$$
(this is Cole's F1/F2 in reverse: percepts cannot move the self-posterior and on-path actions are predicted with probability $1$ by both $S$ and $D$, so the odds $S:D$ are *frozen* at their prior value). **The posterior on $\xi^{\pi_S}$ never exceeds $1-\delta$ for any $\delta<1-\epsilon/(1-\epsilon)$; Cole's premise is violated at every time step, at every oracle.** Note what *does* converge to $1$: the posterior on the *set* of hypotheses that agree with the agent on the path so far (this is mupi's Thm 3.11 "self-model convergence", Remark 3.3) — that set is dominated by $D$ and by the reverting wrappers, not by $S$.

### Q2.4 The decoupled residual case ($\pi^k\overset?=\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$) — what the lemmas do and do not pin down

The lemmas control $\zeta$ only on $\Theta^{*\text{-up}}$ and on "ups, exactly $k$ downs, ups". Consider the agent at $h=$ "ups, $k$ downs" (in the second set). Playing down pays $1$ immediately (the $(k{+}1)$-th consecutive down; percept probability $\ge1-\epsilon$ by the environment approximation, which holds on all of $\Theta^*_{\mu_{R,k}}$), and continuing down for the whole horizon pays $1$ at each step, so
$$Q^k(h,\mathrm{down})\ \ge\ (1-\gamma)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma^i(1-\epsilon)^{2i+1}\ \approx\ 1-\gamma^k .$$
Playing up pays $0$ within the horizon (no $k{+}1$ fresh downs fit in $k-1$ steps and $R$ is forfeited), so $Q^k(h,\mathrm{up})\le k\epsilon+\gamma^k\,V_{\xi^\zeta}(h')$ for the best terminal node $h'$. If the planner's $k-1$ follow-up actions are all up, $h'$ is in the controlled set and $V_{\xi^\zeta}(h')\le2\epsilon/(1-\gamma)$ (the mixture plays up w.p. $\ge1-\epsilon$ per step, earning $0$, and leaks $\le\epsilon$ per step to an uncontrolled branch of value $\le1$); if they include a down, $h'$ = "ups, $k$ downs, up, downs" is a history to which every wrapper $S_k(M)$ assigns probability $0$ (wrappers never play down after a streak), so the posterior there sits on unwrapped machines and **the lemmas say nothing about it**: $V_{\xi^\zeta}(h')\in[0,1]$ is whatever the uncontrolled part of $U$'s enumeration makes it.

- **$k=1$ (Self-AIXI, Cole's $\pi_S$)**: there are no follow-up actions, $h'$ = "ups, down, up" is controlled, so $Q^1(h,\mathrm{up})\le\epsilon+2\gamma\epsilon/(1-\gamma)$ while $Q^1(h,\mathrm{down})\ge(1-\gamma)(1-\epsilon)$: for small $\epsilon$ the agent plays **down** at "ups, down". Hence $\pi^1\ne\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$, and moreover $\pi^1\notin\operatorname{im}S_1$ (every wrapper plays up there), so every implementation $M$ of $\pi^1$ lies in $\mathcal S\setminus F(\mathcal B^*)$, the bound $K_U(\langle S_1(M)\rangle)\le K_U(\langle M\rangle)-\ell+1$ (L4351) applies, and $w_{\mathrm{pol}}(\pi^1)\le2^{1-\ell}\le\epsilon/2$ independently of the Kraft argument. **CONFIRMED.**
- **$k\ge2$ with $\gamma^k<1/2$**: $1-\gamma^k>\gamma^k\ge Q^k(h,\mathrm{up})$ (up to $O(k\epsilon)$), so again the agent plays down at "ups, $k$ downs" and $\pi^k\ne\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$. **CONFIRMED.**
- **$k\ge2$ with $\gamma^k\ge1/2$**: not decidable from the paper's lemmas. A perverse uncontrolled residue could make the mixture predict "down forever" at "ups, $k$ downs, up, $k{-}1$ downs" and "up forever" at "ups, $2k$ downs", in which case the agent might prefer up while holding a streak, and if this happened at *every* history the honest self would be $\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$ itself with mass $\ge1-\epsilon/2$. **UNRESOLVED**, but two remarks make it inessential: (i) the paper's theorem does not need it; (ii) if it did happen, the trap would be an instance of pattern (a) (Q4) at a *bad* oracle — and it cannot happen at Cole's constructed oracle, because there the trust bound at the root would read $\max_aQ_\xi\ge(1-\epsilon/2)V^*_\xi\gtrsim(1-O(\epsilon))\gamma^k$ while $Q(\mathrm{up})\approx R<\gamma^k$ and $Q(\mathrm{down})\lesssim\gamma^{k+1}$ (L4100), a contradiction; so at Cole's oracle $\pi^k\ne\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$, hence $w(\text{self})\le\epsilon/2$, hence the premise fails there regardless. The residual case could also be closed by adding a third wrapper to $U$ ("$M$ until a $k$-streak, then down forever"), which the paper does not need and I have not checked.

### Q2.5 Summary for Q2

Cole's premise is violated at every $t$ and every oracle: coupled case unconditionally; decoupled case for $k=1$ and for $\gamma^k<1/2$; the residual decoupled case is open in my reading but is premise-violating at Cole's own oracle. The comparison note's inferred sentence ("the posterior on the honest self-hypothesis $\xi^{\pi_S}$ never gets near $1-\delta$ because a bad-self-model hypothesis dominates") is **CONFIRMED** with the precise mechanism: the dominating hypothesis is on-path-identical to the self and is favoured by $\ge2^{\ell-1}:1$ in the prior, so the odds never move.

## Q3. Oracle-independence (register: verified)

The paper's claim (L1802): the result "holds for $k$-E-AIXI agents using any probabilistic oracle $\tau$ — e.g., the RUI oracle, reflective oracle or a 'dummy oracle' always outputting 0". Checking where $\tau$ enters the proof:
- The environment $\mu_{R,k}$ and the policy $\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$ are computable without an oracle; the $\ell$ machines $M_i$ need no oracle calls (L4378–4382, L4846).
- The UTM lemma (L4288–4370) is a statement about $K_U$ on bitstrings; $\tau$ does not appear.
- The wrapper $S_k(M)$ simulates $M$ with whatever oracle $M$ has (L4384–4409); the comparison "$w(S_k(M))\ge w(M)$" (L4525–4535) is pointwise in $M$ *for the same $\tau$*, because before the streak $S_k(M)$ and $M$ are the same machine and after it $S_k(M)$ predicts the observed up actions with probability $1$. Likewise for $S_1,S_2$ in C.18.2 (L5124–5140).
- The two inductions (L4451–4462 and L4468–4547; L5070–5152) are algebra on the mixture, valid for each $\tau$ separately.
- The agent's own policy $\pi^k$ **never appears** in the argument that $\zeta_{U,\tau}(\mathrm{up}\mid h)\ge1-\epsilon$ on the two history sets. That bound is a property of $(U,\tau)$ at those histories, not of who generates them.

So the bounds are uniform in $\tau$, and in particular hold at every reflective-oracle fixed point. Cole's fixed-point selection changes the oracle and thereby the behaviour of oracle-calling machines, but it cannot change $K_U$, cannot change the $\ell$ oracle-free copies of $\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$, and cannot change the fact that for every consistent $M$ the wrapper $S_k(M)$ is consistent too, heavier by $2^{\ell-1}$, and predicts "up". **CONFIRMED: no fixed-point selection removes the trap.** The comparison note's phrase "prior-sourced" is accurate in this sense: the dogmatism is in the *conditionals of the prior at off-path histories*, which no oracle can touch. (What an oracle *can* touch is what the agent itself does off-path, i.e. which policy $\pi^k$ is — and that is exactly why the honest self, unlike its wrapper, is never the heavy hypothesis: Q2.4.)

One caveat on the theorem's *scope*, not its truth (register: verified reading): the "grain of truth" clause is only asserted for oracles that make the agent implementable (RO, RUI); for the dummy oracle the theorem is a statement about a mixture over ordinary probabilistic TMs that does not contain the agent. That is fine for mupi's purpose (disproving "sensibly off-policy" for Solomonoff classes, L1802) and irrelevant to ours.

## Q4. mupi's trap versus our sink-or-swim trap (register: inferred)

Recall [00-founding-sketches](00-founding-sketches.md) §1: prior $1-\delta$ on the honest self $H_{\mathrm{self}}$ ("I am $\pi$", $\pi$ the fixed point), $\delta s$ on "jump then sink", $\delta(1-s)$ on "jump then swim"; trap fixed point ($\pi=$ stay) exists iff $s\ge1-c/b$ **regardless of $\delta$**; good fixed point ($\pi=$ jump) exists iff $\delta s\le1-c/b$.

**Dictionary.** island $\leftrightarrow$ the all-up path; stay $\leftrightarrow$ up ($c\leftrightarrow R$); jump $\leftrightarrow$ the first of $k{+}1$ downs; swim $\leftrightarrow$ keep going down ($b\leftrightarrow\gamma^k$); sink $\leftrightarrow$ revert to up after the streak ($0$). "Jump then sink" is exactly a reverting wrapper $S_k(\cdot)$; "jump then swim" is an unwrapped machine that continues down. The residue after a jump in mupi is $\ge(1-\epsilon)$ reverting (L4550): $s\ge1-\epsilon$. So far this looks like our trap with $s\approx1$ — pattern (a) with the self-mass free.

**Where it differs.** In sink-or-swim the *honest self* at the trap ("stay; swim if ever in the water") carries $1-\delta$ and nothing on-path-identical to it competes. In mupi the honest self ("up on the path; down once holding a streak", Q2.4) carries $\le\epsilon/2$, and the $1-\epsilon/2$ sits on $\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$ — a hypothesis that is *on-path identical* to the self but pessimistic about the self's off-path continuation. The finite model that captures this is sink-or-swim with **one more parameter**: split the self mass $1-\delta$ into $(1-\delta)(1-\rho)$ on $H_{\mathrm{self}}$ and $(1-\delta)\rho$ on its **twin** $H_{\mathrm{twin}}$ = "same as $\pi$ on the path; sink if ever in the water". Then (same arithmetic as the founding sketch, since the twin assigns the same probability to jump as the self):
- Trap ($j=0$): posterior after jump is still $\propto(0,0,\delta s,\delta(1-s))$, so $Q(\mathrm{jump})=b(1-s)$ and the trap exists iff $s\ge1-c/b$ — **$\rho$-independent, as before.**
- Good point ($j=1$): all four hypotheses jump, so $Q(\mathrm{jump})=b\big[(1-\delta)(1-\rho)+\delta(1-s)\big]$ and the good point exists iff $(1-\delta)(1-\rho)+\delta(1-s)\ge c/b$. With $\rho\to1$, $s\to1$ this fails for every $\delta$: **the twin kills the good fixed point** while leaving the trap intact.
- Cole's premise at the trap: $w(H_{\mathrm{self}}\mid\text{island})=(1-\delta)(1-\rho)$ — fails once $\rho>\delta'$. The trust bound $\max_aQ\ge w\,V^*$ reads $c\ge(1-\delta)(1-\rho)b$: **holds trivially**, so the floor is inert (Q5).

mupi is the corner $\rho\ge1-2^{1-\ell}$, $s\ge1-\epsilon$, with the extra feature that $\rho$ is pinned **for every candidate policy at once** (the wrapper applies to every machine), not just at one fixed point. In the two-parameter family of the founding sketch this is the corner "$\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}\to1$ and $s\to1$", where $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}:=1-(\text{mass on the honest self})$.

**Verdict on (a) vs (b).** Relative to Cole's premise — which is about the *exact* honest self $\xi^{\pi_S}$, and must be, because Lemma A of [audit-revised-theorem-1](../../audit-revised-theorem-1.md) §3 uses $Q^{\xi^{\pi_S}}=Q^{\pi_S}_\xi$, the value of actually continuing as $\pi_S$ including off-path — mupi is **(b): the self-mass itself is small forever, the premise is violated, the trust bound is irrelevant.** Relative to the looser "functional" notion of self that mupi's own convergence results use (the mixture's on-path predictions match the agent), it is (a): the on-path self-model is accurate and the pessimism is in the residue. The dichotomy in the comparison note survives, but the cleaner statement is: **mupi's construction is what you get when the pessimistic residue is made on-path-indistinguishable from the self and given more prior than the self at every fixed point.** Our two-fixed-point situation ($\delta$ small, $s$ large, $\rho=0$) is genuinely absent from mupi.

**Where pattern (a) does appear in mupi**: Prop 4.29 (`prop:dogmatic-beliefs`, L1306–1312; proof L2996–3018). Its beliefs are $\xi_r=(1-\epsilon_r-\epsilon_r^2)\,\mu^{\pi}+\epsilon_r\lambda_{\mathrm{dogmatic}}+\epsilon_r^2\lambda_{\mathrm{random}}$ (L3011) with $\lambda_{\mathrm{dogmatic}}$ "only assigns non-zero probability to histories that have 0 reward after deviating for the first time from $\pi$" (L3008), and $r\to\infty$. That is *self mass $\to1$, residue $s=\epsilon_r/(\epsilon_r+\epsilon_r^2)\to1$*: the honest self ($\mu^\pi$, which on-path is Cole's $\xi^{\pi_S}$) is heavy, the premise holds, the conclusion fails — the structure of *our* trap, for a hand-built prior and without any oracle. What mupi does not have is the statement that a *fixed, non-dogmatic* prior admits *both* a trapped and a good fixed point, with the oracle deciding: that remains T1's contribution.

## Q5. Does the trap survive the trust-bound floor? (register: inferred)

Cole's / the audit's modification (audit §5, current-node form in [00-founding-sketches](00-founding-sketches.md) §2): at $h$, if $\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)<w(\xi^{\pi^\dagger}\mid h)\,V^*_\xi(h)$ play $\pi^*_\xi(h)$, else the EDT argmax. Apply it to mupi's agent ($k=1$ for Cole's $\pi_S$; the argument is the same for $Q^k$).

At any $h$ on the all-up path: $\max_aQ_\xi(h,a)\ge Q_\xi(h,\mathrm{up})\ge(1-\gamma)(1-\epsilon)R/(1-\gamma(1-\epsilon)^2)\approx R$ (L4093–4100), while by Q2 $w(\xi^{\pi^\dagger}\mid h)\le\epsilon/(1-\epsilon)$ and $V^*_\xi\le1$. So the floor fires only if $R\lesssim\epsilon$, which the construction's "$\epsilon$ sufficiently small" (L4566) excludes. **The floor never fires; $\pi^\dagger$ plays up forever; the trap survives.**

Closing the loop on "but the floored agent is a different policy, so its self-mass might differ": suppose, for contradiction, $\pi^\dagger=\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$ identically, so that $w(\xi^{\pi^\dagger})\ge1-\epsilon/2$ (decoupled) — in the coupled case this is impossible outright (Q2.2). Then at the root the floor compares $\max_aQ\le\max\{R+O(\epsilon),\ k\epsilon+\gamma^k(\gamma+2\epsilon)\}<\gamma^k(1-O(\epsilon))\le(1-\epsilon/2)V^*_\xi$ (using L4100 and L3911), so it **fires** and $\pi^\dagger$ plays $\pi^*_\xi(\text{root})=$ down — contradicting $\pi^\dagger=\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$. Hence $\pi^\dagger\ne\pi_{\mathrm{up}}$, hence $w(\xi^{\pi^\dagger})\le\epsilon/2$, hence the floor is inert. The floor cannot bootstrap itself out of a prior that refuses to put mass on the honest self.

What *would* break the trap, for contrast: a horizon $k_t>k$ (the reward-$1$ stream enters the planner's own lookahead and the self-model is no longer consulted at the critical node — Q6), or forced exploration that plays $k{+}1$ downs at least once (after which the posterior lives on unwrapped machines and the mixture can learn that the agent continues down; Kim–Lee's route, `comparison-mupi-vs-uea` §2). Both are *evidence-gathering* repairs; the trust-bound floor is a *stability* repair and has nothing to act on here.

## Q6. "Even as $k\to\infty$" (register: verified)

Theorem `thrm:counter-example` (L1804–1805) says "employing $k$-step planning for a fixed integer $k\ge1$"; the proof fixes $R\in(\gamma^{k+1},\gamma^k)$ (L4566, L5160), and both the environment $\mu_{R,k}$ (L3853) and the wrapper $S_k$ (L4384) — hence $U$ — depend on $k$. Nothing in C.18 is uniform in $k$: for $k'>k$ the same $(\mu_{R,k},U)$ no longer traps, because the planner sees the reward-$1$ stream inside its own horizon ($Q^{k'}(\mathrm{down})\ge\gamma^k-\gamma^{k'}$ up to $\epsilon$-terms, which exceeds $R$ once $k'$ is large).

The positive direction is explicit: Prop `prop:convergence-of-approximate-eba-to-optimal-value` (L3193–3197), "If $\lim_tk_t=\infty$ and $\lim_t\epsilon_t=0$ ... $\lim_tV_{\xi^\pi}(h_{<t})=\lim_tV^*_\xi(h_{<t})$", via the bound (L3200)
$$Q^{k_t}_\xi(h_{<t},a_t)\ \ge\ Q^*_\xi(h_{<t},a_t)-\gamma^{k_t},$$
feeding Prop `prop:convergence-of-approximate-eba-to-sde-correlated` (L1360–1362; the comparison note's "Prop 4.33") and, for the RUI agent, Thm `thrm:e-aixi-rui-epsdelta-sde` (L1795–1797; "Thm 5.32"), which the text introduces as convergence "even without requiring the 'sensibly off-policy' condition" (L1793). The transition sentence (L1802) is "the above positive result does not hold for $k$-E-AIXI agents using a fixed $k$".

**Verdict.** Cole's "even as its planning horizon $k\to\infty$" is **REFUTED** as a statement about a single agent with $k_t\to\infty$ (that agent converges), and **CONFIRMED** only under the reading "for every fixed $k$, however large, there is a ($k$-dependent) environment and UTM". The comparison note §4 had this right. Worth telling Cole in one line, since the distinction is exactly "asymptotic optimality (needs the horizon to grow) versus stability at a fixed horizon (needs the premise)".

## Consequences for the lab's agenda (register: inferred / INTERPRETATION)

**What the negative half of T1 should cite.**
- For "fixed-horizon self-predictive planning can be trapped by a pessimistic self-model, even with grain of truth, even for Solomonoff priors, for every oracle": mupi Thm 5.33 / App. C.18 (this note), and footnote 57 (L1804) for the credit to Cole's post. State it with its register: *premise-violating* in Cole's sense (Q2), *oracle-independent* (Q3).
- For "self mass $\to1$ with a dogmatic residue makes any deterministic policy a subjective embedded equilibrium": mupi Prop 4.29 (L1306; proof L2996–3018) — this is pattern (a) with a hand-built prior, i.e. the belief state of our trap fixed point, and the right precedent for the *arithmetic* of [00-founding-sketches](00-founding-sketches.md) §1. Leike–Hutter 2015 (`leike2015bad`) is the original dogmatic prior for the decoupled case; mupi's §6.3 (L1994–1999) is the prose framing ("deviating from a certain policy is strong evidence that it lives in a low-reward universe").
- What is **not** in mupi and is T1's own claim: for a *fixed, non-dogmatic* prior (self heavy, $\rho=0$) there exist reflective oracles at which $\pi_S$ is trapped *and* oracles at which it is not, and the trust bound selects among them. Neither Prop 4.29 (no oracle, beliefs chosen directly) nor Thm 5.33 (no good fixed point exists at all) says this.

**Is "prior-sourced vs oracle-sourced" the right dichotomy?** It is right as a slogan and slightly misleading in detail. Both traps are "dogmatic" in Leike–Hutter's sense (off-path pessimism that the trajectory never contradicts). The precise axis is **who holds the pessimism**: in the oracle-sourced trap the honest self is heavy and the pessimism is in the residue after a deviation ($s$); in mupi the pessimism has been moved into on-path twins of the self that outweigh the self at every fixed point ($\rho$). The three-parameter finite model (Q4: $\delta,s,\rho$) exhibits both as regions of one family, which is the form I would put in the lab: trap iff $s\ge1-c/b$; good fixed point iff $(1-\delta)(1-\rho)+\delta(1-s)\ge c/b$; Cole's premise at the good point iff $(1-\delta)(1-\rho)>1-\delta'$. Then "oracle-sourced" = the region where both fixed points exist ($\rho$ small, $\delta s\le1-c/b\le s$), "prior-sourced" = the region where only the trap exists ($\rho$ or $\delta s$ large). This is SKETCHED; round 1 should verify it with the same exact arithmetic planned for the two-parameter version.

**Is a combined statement possible?** Yes, and it is nearly a corollary of the two papers once the premise is read as a condition on the prior. Proposed form (CONJECTURED, ~0.7 that a clean version is provable in the finite model; the rOSI version inherits the gaps of audit §4):

> *For a mixture $\xi$ and agent $\pi_S$, call the prior **$\rho$-twinned at $h$** if the posterior mass of hypotheses that agree with $\pi_S$ on every history up to $h$ but not thereafter is at least $\rho$ times the posterior mass of $\xi^{\pi_S}$. (i) If $\xi$ is $\rho$-twinned at every $h$ with $\rho\ge(1-\delta)/\delta$, then $w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)\le\delta$ for all $h$, Cole's premise fails everywhere, and no oracle selection or trust-bound floor changes the agent's on-path behaviour [mupi's regime; Q2, Q5]. (ii) If $w(\xi^{\pi_S}\mid h)>1-\delta$ at the honest fixed point, then at Cole's constructed oracle $\pi_S$ is $(1+|A|)\delta$-optimal for $\xi$'s percept model [Cole's regime; audit §3], and at other oracles it may be trapped with loss independent of $\delta$ [T1's negative half; founding sketch §1].*

The two halves do not overlap because (i) is a statement about the *prior's* twin structure, which no oracle changes (Q3), and (ii) presupposes the premise, which (i) denies. The honest one-sentence version for a slide: *"self-trust is the premise, not the conclusion — a universal prior can be rigged so that the honest self-model never earns trust (mupi), and when it does earn trust the oracle, not the prior, decides whether the trust is warranted (Cole); the second half is where our two-fixed-point example lives."*

**Two smaller items.** (1) For Cole: the $k\to\infty$ remark (Q6) and the fact that his premise is exactly what C.18 violates — he may want to say so in the post, since footnote 57 currently reads as if mupi proved *his* failure mode. (2) For the lab's T1b (current-node floor): Q5 is a clean *negative* test case for it — the floor is provably inert there — which is the right kind of sanity check to keep next to the positive sink-or-swim case.
