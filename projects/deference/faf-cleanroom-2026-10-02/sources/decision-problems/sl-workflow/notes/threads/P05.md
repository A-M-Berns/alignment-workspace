### 1.2 Thesis and framing

- Opening sentence: "I provide conditions under which CDT=EDT in Bayes-net causal models."
- The fairness framing (inherited from P04): "a lot of informal reasoning about the differences between CDT and EDT doesn't actually give the same problem representation to both decision theories. One can easily imagine handing a causal model to CDT and a joint probability distribution to EDT, without checking that the probability distribution could possibly be consistent with the causal model. Representing problems in Bayes nets seems like a good choice for comparing the behavior of CDT and EDT. CDT takes the network to encode causal information, while EDT ignores that and just uses the probability distribution encoded by the network."
- The informal observed-parents lemma: "It's easy to see that CDT=EDT if all the causal parents of an agent's decision are observed. CDT makes decisions by first cutting the links to parents, and then conditioning on alternative actions. EDT conditions on the alternatives without cutting links. So, EDT differs from CDT insofar as actions provide evidence about causal parents. If all parents are known, then it's not possible for CDT and EDT to differ." Hence "any argument for CDT over EDT or vice versa must rely on the possibility of unobserved parents."
- The hidden parent named: "to recover the usual results in the classical thought experiments, it's common to add a node representing 'the agent's abstract algorithm' which is a parent to the agent and any simulations of the agent. This abstract algorithm node captures the correlation which allows EDT to cooperate in the prisoner's dilemma and one-box in Newcomb, for example."
- The programme sentence: "Here, I argue that sufficient introspection still implies that CDT=EDT. Essentially, the agent may not have direct access to all its causal parents, but if it has enough self-knowledge (unlike the setup in Smoking Lesion Steelman), the same screening-off phenomenon occurs. This is somewhat like saying that the output of the abstract algorithm node is known. Under this condition, EDT and CDT both two-box in Newcomb and defect in the prisoner's dilemma."

### 1.3 Case 1: mixed-strategy ratifiability — definitions and Theorem 1

The objects, in the post's notation (bold-italic in the original): a Bayes net; an action node $A$ with values $a$; a decision node $D$ whose value is the mixed strategy, i.e. a distribution on $A$; $D$ "gives the probability of $A$". The constraints, verbatim:

1. "(_EDT maximization constraint._) The EDT agent must choose a mixed strategy in which $P(a) > \epsilon$ only if the action is among those which maximize expected utility."
2. "(_CDT maximization constraint._) The CDT agent is under the same restriction, but with respect to the causal expectation."
3. "(_Exploration constraint._) I further restrict all action probabilities to be at _least_ epsilon, to ensure that the conditional expectations are well-defined."
4. "(_Ratifiability constraint._) I'll also assume ratifiability of mixed strategies: the belief state from which CDT and EDT make their decision is one in which they know which mixed strategy they select. Put another way, the decision is required to be stable under knowledge of the decision." Three sources of such self-knowledge are offered: source code, experience ("how these things go"), or a decision rule that "explicitly looks for a choice consistent with mixed-strategy ratifiability". Representation: "a node representing the selection of mixed strategy, which I'll call $D$ (the 'decision' node) which is the direct parent of $A$ (our action node)."
5. "(_Mixed-strategy implementability._) I also assume that $A$ has no other direct parents, representing the assumption that the choice of mixed strategy is the _only_ thing determining the action. This is like the assumption that the environment doesn't contain anything which correlates itself with our random number generator to mess with our experimentation … It's allowable for things to be correlated with our randomness, but if so, they must be _downstream_ of it. Hence, it's also a form of my 'law of logical causality' from earlier [= P03]."

Note that constraints 1–2 are written with $P(a) > \epsilon$ where constraint 3 demands $P(a) \geq \epsilon$: read together, the mixed strategy puts *exactly* $\epsilon$ on every non-maximizer and the remaining mass on maximizers — an $\epsilon$-floor, not a uniform tremble. (Definition 10's $C^\varepsilon = (1-\varepsilon)C + \varepsilon\,\mathrm{Unif}$ puts $\varepsilon/|A_d|$ on each action; the two coincide up to reparametrization only for $|A_d| = 2$ — noted for the translation.)

- **Theorem 1 (verbatim).** "Under the above assumptions, the consistent choices of mixed strategy are the same for CDT and EDT."
- **Proof (verbatim, complete).** "The CDT and EDT expected utility calculations become the same under the mixed-strategy ratifiability condition, since $D$ screens $A$ off from any un-observed parents of $D$. Besides that, all the rest of the constraints are already the same for CDT and EDT. So, the consistent choices of mixed strategies will be the same. ◻"
- Gloss: "It's natural to think of these possible choices as equilibria in the game-theoretic sense. My constraints on the decision procedures for EDT and CDT don't force any particular choice of mixed strategy in cases where several options have maximal utility; but, the condition that that choice must be self-consistent forces it into a few possibilities."
- The dialectical claim against CDT: "the ratifiability constraint is something which CDTers have argued for independent reasons, to fix problems which otherwise arise for CDT. So denial of this assumption seems to be an unavailable response for CDTers."
- The action-versus-distribution paragraph: "The way I've defined CDT and EDT may seem a bit unnatural, since I've constrained them based on max-expectation choice of _actions_, but stated that they are choosing _mixed strategies_. Shouldn't I be selecting from the possible probability distributions on actions, based on the expected utility of those? This would invalidate my conclusion, since the CDT expectation of different choices of $D$ can differ from the EDT expectation. But, it is impossible to enforce ratifiability while also ensuring that conditioning on different choices of $D$ is well-defined. So, I think this way of doing it is the natural way when a ratifiability constraint is in play.[1]" Footnote 1: "Really, I think the reasoning in this paragraph is much too fast, but there's a lot to be said on the subject and it would overcomplicate this post."

### 1.4 Case 2: approximate ratifiability

- The objection the case answers: the argument "takes under-specified decision procedures … and concludes a thing about what happens in the under-specified cases (effectively, any necessary tie-breaking between actions with equal expected utility must choose action probabilities consistent with the agent's beliefs about the probabilities of its actions). Wouldn't the argument just be invalid if we started with fully-specified versions of CDT and EDT, which already use some particular tie-breaking procedure? Shouldn't we, then, take this as an argument against ratifiability as opposed to an argument for CDT=EDT?" Conceded: "Certainly the conclusion doesn't follow without the assumption of ratifiability."
- "(_Continuous EDT_) The EDT agent chooses mixed strategies according to some fixed way which is a continuous function of the belief-state (regarded as a function from worlds to probabilities). This function (the 'selection function') is required to agree with maximum-expected-utility choices when the expectations are well-defined and the differences in utilities between options are greater than some $\epsilon > 0$." "(_Continuous CDT_) The same, but taking CDT-style expectations."
- "(_Approximate Ratifiability_) Let the true mixed strategy which will be chosen by the agent's decision rule be $d^*$. For any other $d \in D$ such that $|\ln(d(a)) - \ln(d^*(a))| > \epsilon$ for any $a \in A$, we have $P(D = d) = 0$." Implementability still assumed; the exploration constraint is dropped ("This will also get rid of the (perhaps annoying) exploration constraint").
- Argument (verbatim): "Approximate ratifiability doesn't perfectly block evidence from flowing backward from the action to the parents of the decision, like perfect ratifiability did. It does bound the amount of evidence, though: since the alternate $d$ must be very close to $d^*$, the likelihood ratio cannot be large. Now, as we make epsilon arbitrarily small, there is some delta which bounds the differences in action utilities assigned by CDT and EDT which gets arbitrarily small as well. Hence, the EDT and CDT selection functions must agree on more and more. By Brouwer's fixed-point theorem, there will be equilibria for the CDT and EDT selection functions. Although there's no guarantee these equilibria are close to each other the way I've spelled things out, we could construct selection functions for both CDT and EDT which get within epsilon of any of the equilibria from theorem 1."

### 1.5 Case 3: abandoning mixed strategies

- The objection: implementability "smuggles CDT-like reasoning into EDT." The informal reply: without implementability "CDT will learn the wrong causal network … the extra parents of $A$ are mistakenly treated as children"; "mixed-strategy implementability might be a feature of causal networks that agents can form by their own experimentation in the world." Then the retraction of the reply: "However, this reply is not very strong. The truth is that 'mixed strategies' are an ontological artifact: you actually take one action or another. Only some agent-architectures will first choose a mixed strategy and then randomize according to the chosen probabilities. Even if so, mixed-strategy implementability implies an absurd degree of trust in that randomization."
- The replacement: "we replace 'mixed strategies' with _the agent's own estimate of its action probabilities_." Rock-paper-scissors between Alice and Bob who are "'reasonably' good at predicting each other": "If Alice can predict her own choice at any point, she is concerned that Bob will pick up on the same pattern … This process will iterate until it is too late to change decisions." Moral: "'mixed strategies' can emerge naturally from following incentives against predictability … the agent pushes its own beliefs away from certainty, since if it were certain that it would take some specific action, then a different action would look more appealing."
- Formalization: the $D \to A$ link is made *deterministic* ("the decision procedure of the agent _completely_ determines the action from the beliefs of the agent"); "$D$ should now be thought of as _the agent's beliefs about which action it will take_, and nothing else"; the link is *rational* (max-expectation by the CDT or the EDT notion); self-knowledge approximate ("the agent's probabilities about $D$ are zero outside some epsilon of its true distribution on $A$ at the time of making its decision. (If we required perfection, Alice might get stuck in an infinite loop …)"); conclusion: "our approximate knowledge of $D$ screens off any correlations between $A$ and parents of $D$, as epsilon shrinks to zero. Notably, this requires that the relationship between $D$ and its parents is continuous". The division-by-zero problem is acknowledged and deferred ("we can do other things, which I'll leave to reader imagination for now").

### 1.6 "Consequences for Counterfactuals" — the radical moral and the self-assessment

- "The arguments above are fairly rudimentary. The point I'm trying to drive at is more radical: there is basically one notion of counterfactual available. It is the one which both CDT and EDT arrive at, if they have very much introspection. It isn't particularly good for the kinds of decision-theory problems we'd like to solve: it tends to two-box in realistic Newcomb's problems (where the predictor is imperfect), defect in prisoner's dilemma, et cetera. My conclusion is that these are not problems to try and solve by counterfactual reasoning. They are problems to solve with updateless reasoning, bargaining, cooperative oracles, predictable exploration, and so on."
- Self-location in the literature: "I don't think any of this is very new … Philosophers seem to have a fairly good understanding of how CDT equals EDT when introspection is possible; see SEP on objections to CDT. The proofs above are just versions of the tickle defense for EDT. However, I think the AI alignment community may not be so aware of the extent to which EDT and CDT coincide. Philosophers continue to distinguish between EDT and CDT, while knowing that they wouldn't differ for ideal introspective agents, on the grounds that decision theories should provide notions of rationality even under failure of introspection." On AI: "It's worth asking whether advanced AIs may still have some fundamental introspection barriers … From where we stand now, looking at positive introspection results over the years, from probabilistic truth to reflective oracles to logical induction, I think the answer is no." Closing hedge: "The meagre reasons in the post above hardly seem to suffice to support this broad view, however. Perhaps my Smoking Lesion Steelman series gives some intuition for it."

### 2.3 What "CDT=EDT" can mean in v2

Three readings, in increasing strength; the audit in §3 says which survive.
1. **Trivial (subjective):** adopt Definition 20's evidential criterion — $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d}(a) = (P_{s_d}(\cdot\mid a), V_{s_d}(\cdot\wedge a))$ wherever defined — so the causal act value $V^a_{s_d}(a)$ equals the news value $V_{s_d}(a)$ on $A_d^+$ by fiat. Nothing about the tree is used; the disagreement is legislated away (Remark 3.11's warning).
2. **Referent-coincidence (objective):** at a decision-point, the evidential evaluator at the calibrated state agrees with a *tree-intrinsic* causal evaluator (R2-real, R1-state, R2-SIA, R1-prior) — the question P2:faithful FA-20′ answers: at Definition-7-recorded points with realized observations, R1-state = R2-real = R3 = conditioning on positive acts. This is the reading under which P05's Theorem 1 becomes a v2 theorem (§3.1).
3. **Set-coincidence (P05's literal statement):** the *ratifiable sets* — the procedures that are calibrated-and-$T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$-approved and those calibrated-and-$T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$-approved for the chosen referent — coincide. Reading 2 at every calibrated point implies reading 3 for that referent; the selection-forcing tree (§3.4) separates the sets when the recording hypothesis fails.

### 3.1 Theorem 1 as a v2 theorem (P05-1 … P05-3)

**P05-1 [derived] — Statement.** Fix a concrete problem $B$ (Definition 5, Definition 6 independent redraws), a procedure $C$, and a queried point $d$ with $\nu_{B,C}(O_d) > 0$, such that

- (I) **implementability + single consultation + veridicality** $\;=\;$ $B$ *records at $d$ for $C$* (Definition 7: every $O_d$-run passes exactly one $d$-node, that node is subtree-veridical, the instance is action-veridical, and the leaf-world satisfies an action in $A_d$ only if it is the one drawn there);
- (R) **ratifiability** $\;=\;$ $s_d$ is strictly observation-calibrated (Definition 8), which by Remark 3.6 / Proposition 7 *forces* $P_{s_d}(a) = C(d)(a)$ — the agent's state reports its own mixture;
- (E) **exploration** $\;=\;$ either $C(d)$ has full support, or the act values are read as Definition 10 tremble limits (R3 of P2:faithful Definition F2) so that they are defined on all of $A_d$;

### 3.2 The four assumptions, one by one

**P05-4 [derived] — #1 compatibility: REFINED (split three ways).** P05's "same problem representation" is *automatic* in v2 (Remark 1.1: both evaluators consume one state $s_d$; the tree is one object). **P05-5 [derived; DY-2 and CA-14′ read] — #2 exploration: REFINED, with a defect P05 did not see.** Exploration's only job in P05-1 is to make $A_d^+ = A_d$. v2 offers two replacements: read the act values as Definition 10 tremble limits (R3; FA-25′(2′): under limit calibration and recording, R3 is total, success-satisfying and equals the $O_d$-conditioned deviation), or Remark 3.9's advice-stance pinning. ### 3.5 Cases 2 and 3, footnote 1, and the counterfactual moral

## 5. Smoking Lesion, the tickle defence, and mixed-strategy ratifiability — what this post contributes

**Smoking Lesion.** P05's contribution is the sentence "if it has enough self-knowledge (unlike the setup in Smoking Lesion Steelman), the same screening-off phenomenon occurs" — the recognition, one step past P01–P03, that the steelman *needs* the failure of self-knowledge and that self-knowledge of the *mixture* suffices, with no introspection of a tickle. In v2 this is Proposition 11 (the S2 state is no realizable worldview under any self-model; check A reproduces both the strict and the masked verdicts) and Proposition 12 (observed lesion ⇒ agreement at every point, population correlation surviving; check B). The literature P05 points at agrees on the mechanism and disagrees on the scope: Lewis (`lewis-1981-causal-decision-theory.txt:298–306`, read) grants that "the Tickle Defence does establish that a Newcomb problem cannot arise for a fully rational agent" and objects that "decision theory should not be limited to apply only to the fully rational agent" — P05's answer, that AIs will have no "fundamental introspection barriers", is a bet v2 does not make: calibration is a normative predicate on (procedure, problem) pairs (Definition 15), and Lewis's partly rational agent is a miscalibrated state, i.e. an abstract problem inconsistent for that procedure (Remark 3.13) — whether that is the right treatment of Lewis is L3/L5's question. Egan (`egan-2007-counterexamples-to-cdt.txt:922–935`, read) states P05's verdict in the ratificationist idiom — "In The Smoking Lesion, not smoking is unratifiable: once Susan becomes convinced that she will choose not to smoke, her smoking or not ceases to be evidence one way or the other for her having the lesion" — and his parity charge against CDT's ratificationists ("If an appeal to ratifiability succeeds here, then the EDTer's appeal to ratifiability … will succeed as well") is P05's Theorem 1 read from the other side. Levinstein–Soares's footnote (quoted in `references/INDEX.md` §2.2 from `:172–175`; source file not opened by me) — the Smoking Lesion "requires the agent to be uncertain about their own desires" and Death in Damascus "is immune to the so-called 'tickle defense'" — names the class where P05's theorem does *not* deliver agreement with the SIA/deviation evaluators: in v2, the class with the agent's own simulation above its live node (the miniature, P05-10; TN-V2, P05-9) or a shared seed (P05-8).

**The tickle defence.** P05 says of itself "The proofs above are just versions of the tickle defense for EDT", and the audit confirms it with one refinement: the defence, in v2, is a theorem about *recorded* points (P05-1; import I1), and its resource is strict calibration's forced self-transparency (Remark 3.6), which Remark 3.9 rightly says masking deletes — P05 kept the resource and paid for EDT's content with exploration instead, which is v2's own choice (Definition 10 over Definition 9). Oesterheld's premise (`oesterheld-tickle-defense-intro.txt:346–347`, read: "the agent knows all the immediate causes of its choice") is stronger than P05's (knows its mixture), and both are self-transparency in v2; his scope claim (`:571–576`, read: "the tickle defense only implies agreement between CDT and EDT in medical Newcomb-like problems … Eells … seems to simply miss the possibility of Newcomb-like problems from logical dependences that persist when all common causes are known") is, tree-side, the Definition 6 / Definition 6′ split: under independent redraws no dependence between the simulation's draw and the live draw survives knowledge of $C(d)$ (P05-8, P05-9 — evidential $=$ classical CDT even in Newcomb-type trees), and under the shared seed one does (P05-8). So v2 locates the Eells/Oesterheld disagreement at Question 12. Skyrms's self-reference objection, quoted by Oesterheld (`:500–521`, read) and stated in his own paper (`skyrms-1990-ratifiability-and-the-logic-of-decision.txt:372–381`, read: "Self-knowledge of expected utility maximization in a curious way prohibits the existence of values for x and y such that Jeffrey expected value maximization is well defined and conflicts with Savage expected utility maximization"), is Remark 3.6 plus Remark 3.9's collapse in 1990 dress, and v2's three exits are P05's exploration (Definition 10), masking (Definition 9), and tie-freedom at mixed equilibria (Remark 4.3). Herrmann's thesis (`herrmann-2023-naturalizing-decision-theory-thesis.txt`, 217 kB) contains no occurrence of "tickle", "ratifiab", "Smoking" or "screen" [checked by regex count] — the naturalizing programme does not engage this literature, which is a datum for L3/L4.

**Mixed-strategy ratifiability.** P05's specific idea — ratifiability of the mixture, with implementability as the condition that makes mixtures available — has a precise ancestor in Skyrms 1990's footnote 14 (`:508–516`, read): "if we expand the acts to include costless implementation of arbitrary random strategies, and preclude any correlation between the Savage states and these mixed acts, we get a ratifiable act. But perhaps implementation of a random strategy might carry with it a cost … Perhaps the mean demon might be able to predict whether the decision-maker will randomize. Perhaps the mean demon could predict the outcome of the randomization." Skyrms's three defeaters map onto v2 exactly: randomizing costs nothing (the label is free); predicting *whether* one randomizes is harmless (under Definition 6 the predictor samples $C(d)$ — it knows the mixture — and P05-9/P05-10 show agreement survives); predicting the *outcome* is Definition 6′ and fatal (P05-8). The SEP's Weirich/Harper sentence (`sep-causal-decision-theory.md:344`, read: "A self-ratifying option emerges, however, if the man may flip a coin … Assuming that Death cannot predict the coin flip's outcome") is Definition 6 in one clause. Where the author's version differs from the literature's: the literature's mixed ratifiability is an *escape from instability* for causal decision theory (Harper, Weirich, Arntzenius's "mixed decisions", Joyce's deliberational equilibrium — all as indexed in `references/INDEX.md` §2.2, sources not opened by me beyond the SEP and Skyrms); P05's is a *self-knowledge condition imposed on both theories* to force their agreement; v2's (Remark 3.7, Remark 4.3, Remark 5.1) is a *calibration equation on labels* whose solutions exist trivially at recorded points (CA-12′: the act values do not depend on the label, so the ratifiable labels are just the argmax mixtures) and need Kakutani only where recording fails (SE-18′, SE-19, DY-3). The theorem itself — statement, existence, the Dutch-book connection — is C2's; what this thread hands C2 is P05-1's statement, the battery of §3.4 with the two genuine failures (P05-11 selection, P05-13 implementability), and the observation (P05-9) that recording is sufficient but not necessary.

## Toolkit issues

1. **Symbolic `argmax` returned a verdict where the sign varies on the declared domain.** In the first run of `p05_checks.py`, `dp.argmax({'live=a': -2*(q-1), 'live=b': q})` with `q = ex.P.q` (declared on $(0,1)$) returned `['live=a']` without raising, although $2(1-q) - q = 2 - 3q$ changes sign at $q = \tfrac23$; the same call on `{-3*(q-1)/2, 3*q/2}` raised `UndecidableSign`. This is an instance of the known "symbolic sign sampling is process-order-dependent" issue (`00-common.md`; toolkit-audit §5–6): the generic sample points happened to fall on one side. I therefore never read a symbolic argmax off the toolkit; all argmaxes in my notes are at numeric parameters. Re-checked directly: `dp.argmax({'live=a': 2-2*q, 'live=b': q})` → `['live=a']` and `dp.argmax({'a': 2-3*q, 'b': 0})` → `['a']`, with `dp._samples([q])` = `[{q: 2/7}, {q: 1/2}, {q: 5/9}]` — all three sample points lie below $\tfrac23$, so a difference whose sign flips on $(\tfrac23, 1)$ is never sampled there; the $\tfrac32$-scaled pair happens to flip at $\tfrac12$, which is a sample point, hence the raise. Suggested fix: `_samples` should include points near both ends of the declared interval and raise when any two samples disagree — which it does — so the residual risk is coarse sampling; document that `argmax` on symbolic values is at best a hint.
2. **`theorem1_condition` / `theorem2_evaluator` on a trembled procedure raise `UndecidableSign` ("symbol epsilon has no declared domain")** because they call `argmax` on $\varepsilon$-symbolic values and `dp.EPS` carries no domain — the known "raise via argmax" issue, one more instance. Workaround used: compute $\sum_q R_q G_q$ with `run.reach`/`run.G` directly (my `referents`).
3. **No act-conditional tremble-limit helper.** `calibrated_state(…, 'limit')` returns Definition 10's self-certain limit *state* (correctly), so conditioning it on an unplayed act divides by zero; `limit_conditional` gives the limit of $\nu_{C^\varepsilon}(\cdot\mid O_d)$, not of $\nu_{C^\varepsilon}(\cdot \mid a \wedge O_d)$. R3 (Remark 3.9's tremble-induced evidential $\mathrm{cf}$; P2:faithful Definition F2) has to be assembled by hand (`r3()` in my script via `dp.limit0(run_e.nu_cond(X, act & d.obs), EPS)`). A gap, not a bug.
4. **`Run.G` under `'shared'` refuses** (documented; SE-1(d)'s design choice) — my `referents` skips R2-real under the shared seed, consistent with the ledger's "definable but not tree-intrinsic".
