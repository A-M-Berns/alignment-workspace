### 1.2 The four summary theses (the post's own numbering)

1. "I can Dutch-book any agent whose subjective counterfactual expectations don't equal their _conditional_ expectations. I conclude that counterfactual expectations should equal conditional probabilities. IE, evidential decision theory (EDT) gives the correct counterfactuals." (Link: P12, `12-dutch-booking-cdt-revised-argument`.)
2. "_However_, the Troll Bridge problem is real and concerning: EDT agents are doing silly things here." (Link: P11, [11-troll-bridge-5](../../posts/11-troll-bridge-5.md).)
3. "One way out is to maintain that _subjective counterfactual expectations should equal conditional expectations_ while also maintaining a distinction between those two things: counterfactuals are not _computed from_ conditionals. As we shall see, this allows us to ensure that the two are always equal in _real_ situations, while strategically allowing them to differ in some _hypothetical_ situations (such as Troll Bridge). This seems to solve all the problems!"

### 1.3 "What does it mean to pass Troll Bridge?" — definitions

- **Respecting logic.** Troll Bridge "really relies on the agent 'respecting logic' in a particular sense": "updating to probability 1 (/0) when something is proven (/refuted)". In the comments (to Vanessa Kosoy) this is made precise: "'Respect logic' means either (a) assigning probability one to tautologies (at least, to those which can be proved in some bounded proof-length, or something along those lines), or, (b) assigning probability zero to contradictions (again, modulo boundedness). These two properties should be basically equivalent (ie, imply each other) provided the proof system is consistent."
- **RL trolls are unfair.** "Learning agents who don't reason logically, such as RL agents, can be fooled by a troll who punishes exploration. However, that doesn't seem to get at the point of Troll Bridge. It just seems like an unfair problem, then."
- **First test of "real Troll Bridge":** "does the example make crossing totally impossible? Or does it depend on the agent's starting beliefs?" — "It's a refusal to cross _regardless of prior_ which seems so problematic."
- **Second test:** "whether we can strengthen the issue to 100% failure _while still having a sense that the agent has a way out, if only it would take it_." The RL strengthening (punish unjustified crossing too) passes the first test but "there is no longer any sense that the agent is crazy … Here, there is nothing the agent can possibly do."
- **Loose logic** ("Passing Troll Bridge", the linked earlier note): blocks the proof but "seems worryingly unprincipled, because we can 'improve' the epistemics by tightening the relationship to logic, and get a decision-theoretically much worse result." Target: "solutions which 'solve troll bridge' in the stronger sense of getting it right while fully respecting logic."
- **"Just be CDT"**: "By CDT, I don't mean classical causal decision theory -- I mean decision theory which uses _any_ notion of counterfactuals, be it based on physical causality, logical causality, or what-have-you."

### 1.4 The Subjective Theory of Counterfactuals; PCDT

- Attribution to Sam Eisenstat (verbatim): "Sam presented Troll Bridge as an argument in favor of CDT. … I gradually came to realize that Sam didn't see us as needing all that. For him, counterfactuals were simply a more general framework, a generality which happened to be needed to encode what humans see as the correct reasoning."
- The probability analogy (verbatim, key clause): "it was important that at some point someone sat down and worked out the theory of probability under the assumption that those questions were _entirely subjective_, and the only _objective_ things we can say about probabilities are the basic coherence constraints, such as P(~A)=1-P(A), etc." Hence: "starting from the assumption that a very broad range of counterfactuals can be subjectively valid, we should seek the few 'coherence' constraints which distinguish rational counterfactual beliefs from irrational."
- How this passes Troll Bridge: "The Troll Bridge argument is blocked at the step where [the agent proves that crossing implies bad stuff] implies [the agent doesn't cross]. The agent's _counterfactual_ expected value for crossing can still be high, even if it has proven that crossing is bad."
- **Permissive CDT (PCDT)**, verbatim four features: "There is a basic counterfactual conditional, C(A|B)." / "This counterfactual conditional obeys the axiom C(A|B)&B -> A." / "There may be additional axioms, but they are weak enough to allow 2-boxing in Newcomb as subjectively valid." / "There is no chicken rule or forced exploration rule; agents always take the action which looks counterfactually best." Gloss on the axiom: "counterfactuals had better take the actual world to the actual world. This means a counterfactual hypothesis sticks its neck out, and can be disproven if B is true (so, if B is an action, we can _make_ it true in order to test)."
- Exploration excluded from PCDT: "_with_ exploration, we would eventually take disastrous actions … if there was a destroy-the-world button, the agent would eventually press it." Replacement: **VOI-exploration** — "PCDT naturally chooses some actions which are suboptimal in the short term, due to the long-term value of information."
- Verdict: "_**I claim PCDT is wrong.**_ … I think my Dutch Book argument is fairly damning". "Sam advocated the PCDT-like direction for some time, but eventually, he came to agree with my Dutch Book argument."
- **Shovel-ready questions for PCDT** (the list I audit one by one in §6): (Q-A) further axioms — "formalize PCDT in logical induction, by adding a basic counterfactual A↦B to the language"; want A↦B and A↦¬B "mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive" but not "too many 'basic logic' assumptions … since it could make counterlogicals undefinable, leading us back to problems taking counterfactuals when we know our own actions"; (Q-B) which axioms pass Troll Bridge; (Q-C) learning theory for PCDT "somewhat similar to InfraBayes, in the sense of relying on VOI exploration instead of forcing exploration", needing: a no-traps assumption (weak/strong/stretch versions), a good-feedback assumption (RL with discounting; incremental utility feedback; or "abandon the utility as a function of history at all, and instead rely only on subjective expectations"), a **no-Newcomblike assumption** — "In other words, a 'CDT=EDT' assumption. (A Newcomblike problem is precisely one where CDT and EDT differ.)" with the stronger form "the agent _eliminates the eliminable hypotheses_, and distinguishes between the rest based on subjective plausibility; and (_**according to PCDT, but not according to me**_) this is the best we can hope to do", and realizability ("The right assumption has to do with there being a trader capable of tracking the environment sufficiently"; the rock-paper-scissors-with-randomness example: "a trader who predicts that every option will be at best as good as ⅓ ⅓ ⅓ on average"); (Q-D) tiling theory for PCDT — "tiling must require a no-newcomb type restriction. PCDT, faced with the possibility of encountering a Newcomblike problem at some point, _should absolutely_ self-modify in some way"; and "the tiling result necessarily excludes updateless-type problems, such as counterfactual mugging. None of the proposals considered here will deal with this."

### 1.5 The Inferential Theory; RCDT; the PCH/LCH learning example; conditional contracts

- **Inferential theory** ("the core intuition behind EDT"): "_we should reason about the consequences of actions in the same way that we reason about information which we add to our knowledge_"; "_hypothetical reasoning and counterfactual reasoning are one and the same_." Bayesian EDT and proof-based EDT (MUDT) are its two instances. Verdict: "_**I think the inferential theory is probably wrong.**_ Almost any version of the inferential theory will imply getting Troll Bridge wrong … That's because the inference **[action a implies bad stuff] & [action a] ⊢ [bad stuff]** is valid." Jessica Taylor's "counterfactual nonrealism" is said to bundle the subjective theory with the inferential theory.
- **The line between**: "_counterfactual reasoning should match hypothetical reasoning **in the real world**, but shouldn't necessarily match it **hypothetically**._" Proof of concept: a CDT "whose counterfactual expectations for crossing and not crossing _just so happen_ to be the same as its evidential expectations, namely, cross = +10, not cross = 0. This isn't Dutch-bookable". In the Troll Bridge hypothetical "we _prove_ that [cross]->[U=-10]. This will make the _conditional_ expectations poor. But this _doesn't have to change the counterfactuals_. So (within the hypothetical), the agent can cross anyway. And crossing gets +10. So, the Lobian proof doesn't go through." Against a troll keyed to the counterfactual/conditional disparity: "the troll would, then, be punishing anyone who made decisions in a way different from EDT. Since we know EDT doesn't cross, it would be obvious that no one should cross. So we lose the sense of a dilemma".
- **RCDT** (restrictive counterfactual decision theory), two clauses verbatim: "RCDT should have extra constraints on the counterfactual expectations, sufficient to _guarantee that the counterfactuals we eventually learn will be in line with the conditional probabilities we eventually learn_. IE, the two asymptotically approach each other (at least in circumstances where we have good feedback; probably not otherwise)." / "The constraints _should not_ force them to be exactly equal at all times. _In particular_, the constraints _**must not**_ force counterfactuals to 'respect logic' in the sense that would force failure on Troll Bridge. For example, If A→B implies A↦B, then a proof that crossing the bridge is bad could stop us from crossing it. We can't let RCDT do that."
- **The Newcomb learning example.** Small box $\$1$, big box $\$10$ iff a perfect predictor believes the agent 1-boxes. PCH ("physical causality hypothesis"): $C(U\mid\text{1-box}) = 10\,P(\text{1-box})$, $C(U\mid\text{2-box}) = 1 + 10\,P(\text{1-box})$. LCH ("logical causality hypothesis"): $C(U\mid\text{1-box}) = 10$, $C(U\mid\text{2-box}) = 1$. When the action is predictable, "LCH and PCH will agree quite closely on the expected utility of said action … So little can be learned on such a round"; when the action is uncertain the hypotheses differ, "So it looks like PCH traders should lose money. However, that's not the case." Reason: "Because we have a 'basic counterfactual' proposition for what would happen if we 1-box _and_ what would happen if we 2-box, and _both of those propositions stick around_, LCH's bets about what happens in either case _both_ matter. This is unlike conditional bets, where if we 1-box, then bets conditional on 2-boxing disappear, refunded". After observing 1-box and $+10$: "the _other_ counterfactual -- the one on 2-boxing -- moves to _**+11**_, because PCH is still the dominant belief … the belief about what reward we could have gotten can stay around 11 forever. Any money LCH bet on a payoff of 1 from 2-boxing is probably lost for good." Questions: "Can PCDT really favor PCH forever?"; Sam's rationalization (Omega rewards exploration-1-boxing only); "we want to be able to learn 5&10 correctly by simply experimenting, and PCH is essentially stopping us from doing that here."
- **Avoiding the pathology.** Option 1: more axioms ("(A) learn LCH, while still (B) passing Troll Bridge"). Option 2: **conditional contracts** — "look at conditional bets _instead of_ raw conditional probabilities"; standard conditional bets "must be _responsive_ to a proof of B->A; that is, since B->A means ¬(B&¬A), the bet on A&B must now be worth what a bet on just B would be worth … making the conditional probability zero"; the challenge is "'basic' conditional bets … such that they aren't responsive to proofs of B->A"; "Philosophically, this is a radical rejection of the ratio formula for conditional probability" — more radical than the Popper–Rényi/Lewis-axiom rejections, which keep the ratio "_when it meaningfully applies_"; "We most likely still need the chicken rule here, unlike with basic counterfactuals", and the chicken rule need not doom the approach because in the self-examination step "the chicken rule triggered _or_ the agent had higher conditional-contract expectation on crossing". Option 3: "something else?" Further questions: performance on XOR ("It's not updateless, so obviously, it can't get everything"); learning theory of the working options ("the same as for PCDT, with the exception of the no-newcomb clause"); tiling ("I suspect that PCDT has really poor tiling in Newcomblike situations, whereas RCDT does not. I really want that result").
- **Alignment**: Sam's tiling theorem "requires feedback on counterfactuals … if we consider an agent getting feedback from a human, suddenly it becomes plausible"; sparse feedback; "modeling the human as a logical inductor"; "If utility is a LUV, there can be no explicit utility function at all."
- **Why study this**: "Radical Probabalism and InfraBayes are plausibly _two orthogonal dimensions of generalization for rationality_"; "I don't know how to talk about the CDT vs EDT insight in the InfraBayes world."
- **Conclusion / factoring**: "Provided the formal stuff works out, this might be 'all there is to know' about counterfactuals from a purely decision-theoretic perspective." Decision theory factors into Counterfactuals (Newcomb, 5&10, Troll Bridge, Death in Damascus), Logical Updatelessness (XOR Blackmail, Transparent Newcomb, Parfit's Hitchhiker, Counterfactual Mugging), Multiagent Rationality (PD, Chicken); "we'd 'just' have logical updatelessness and multiagent rationality left."
