## 1. The author's version, exactly (P05, with P02–P03)

### 1.1 What the text says (all quotations read in `posts/05-…md`, `posts/02-…md`, `posts/03-…md`)

**The frame.** "Representing problems in Bayes nets seems like a good choice for comparing the behavior of CDT and EDT. CDT takes the network to encode causal information, while EDT ignores that and just uses the probability distribution encoded by the network." "It's easy to see that CDT=EDT if all the causal parents of an agent's decision are observed … So, any argument for CDT over EDT or vice versa must rely on the possibility of unobserved parents." The standard unobserved parent is "the agent's abstract algorithm".

**Case 1 — the four constraints (P05).** Action node $A$ with values $a$; the agents "select mixed strategies somehow", under: (1) *EDT maximization*: "$P(a) > \epsilon$ only if the action is among those which maximize expected utility"; (2) *CDT maximization*: the same "with respect to the causal expectation"; (*Exploration*) "all action probabilities to be at least epsilon, to ensure that the conditional expectations are well-defined"; (*Ratifiability*) "the belief state from which CDT and EDT make their decision is one in which they know which mixed strategy they select. Put another way, the decision is required to be stable under knowledge of the decision"; represented by "a node representing the selection of mixed strategy, which I'll call $D$ (the 'decision' node) which is the direct parent of $A$"; (*Mixed-strategy implementability*) "$A$ has no other direct parents … the choice of mixed strategy is the *only* thing determining the action … It's allowable for things to be correlated with our randomness, but if so, they must be *downstream* of it. Hence, it's also a form of my 'law of logical causality'."

**Theorem 1 (P05, verbatim).** "Under the above assumptions, the consistent choices of mixed strategy are the same for CDT and EDT. *Proof.* The CDT and EDT expected utility calculations become the same under the mixed-strategy ratifiability condition, since $D$ screens $A$ off from any un-observed parents of $D$. Besides that, all the rest of the constraints are already the same for CDT and EDT. So, the consistent choices of mixed strategies will be the same."

**What is claimed to be needed.** "this argument for CDT=EDT doesn't require any introspection beyond knowing which mixed strategy you're going to choose in the situation you're in." Sources of that knowledge: "Perhaps it knows its own source code … Perhaps it knows 'how these things go' from experience. Or perhaps the decision rule which picks out the mixed strategies explicitly looks for a choice consistent with mixed-strategy ratifiability." And the dialectical lever: "the ratifiability constraint is something which CDTers have argued for independent reasons … So denial of this assumption seems to be an unavailable response for CDTers."

**The acknowledged soft spot (P05 and fn 1).** "Shouldn't I be selecting from the possible probability distributions on actions, based on the expected utility of those? This would invalidate my conclusion, since the CDT expectation of different choices of $D$ can differ from the EDT expectation. But, it is impossible to enforce ratifiability while also ensuring that conditioning on different choices of $D$ is well-defined." Fn 1: "Really, I think the reasoning in this paragraph is much too fast".

**Case 2 (approximate ratifiability).** Continuous selection functions agreeing with max-EU when utility gaps exceed $\epsilon$; $P(D{=}d)=0$ whenever $|\ln d(a)-\ln d^*(a)|>\epsilon$ for some $a$; the exploration floor dropped; "By Brouwer's fixed-point theorem, there will be equilibria for the CDT and EDT selection functions" — with no claim that the two families' equilibria are close, only that selection functions can be built "within epsilon of any of the equilibria from theorem 1".

**Case 3 (abandoning mixed strategies).** "'mixed strategies' are an ontological artifact: you actually take one action or another … mixed-strategy implementability implies an absurd degree of trust in that randomization. So to address the problem formally, we replace 'mixed strategies' with *the agent's own estimate of its action probabilities*." Rock–paper–scissors: "the agent pushes its own beliefs away from certainty, since if it were certain that it would take some specific action, then a different action would look more appealing." Formally the $D\to A$ link is deterministic and rational, $D$ is "the agents beliefs about which action it will take", self-knowledge is approximate ("zero outside some epsilon of its true distribution on $A$"), and screening-off holds in the limit "since the screen-off phenomenon might not occur if the uncertainty is shrinking to zero around a discontinuity."

**The moral and the self-placement.** "there is basically one notion of counterfactual available. It is the one which both CDT and EDT arrive at, if they have very much introspection … it tends to two-box in realistic Newcomb's problems (where the predictor is imperfect), defect in prisoner's dilemma"; the remedy is "updateless reasoning, bargaining, cooperative oracles, predictable exploration". Then: "I don't think any of this is very new in terms of the arguments between CDT and EDT in the literature. Philosophers seem to have a fairly good understanding of how CDT equals EDT when introspection is possible; see SEP on objections to CDT. The proofs above are just versions of the tickle defense for EDT … Philosophers continue to distinguish between EDT and CDT, while knowing that they wouldn't differ for ideal introspective agents, on the grounds that decision theories should provide notions of rationality even under failure of introspection." P06 later records reduced optimism (the edit itself is absent from the fetched body — [reconstructed] from P06's sentence, per `sequence-map.md` §0).

**P02 → P03 → P05: how the object of knowledge moved.** P02's CEDT, $a_{chosen}=\arg\max_a E(do(a)\mid a_{chosen})$, is modelled on correlated equilibrium: "(1) you know which action you will take — not just what probability you have on different actions, as in Nash equilibria; and (2) a max-expected-value calculation still decides to take that action, after knowing what it is." P03 corrects "$\arg\max$" to "*among* the maximal choices", notices the constraint "may also be satisfied by *no* actions" (matching pennies against a skilled predictor), identifies CEDT with Jeffrey's ratifiability, cites Skyrms's *Ratifiability and the Logic of Decision* for "a reasonable interpretation of ratifiability (perhaps the only reasonable interpretation) makes Jeffrey's EDT select actions which are CDT-optimal when CDT chooses via a probability distribution which has been updated on knowledge of which action is to be selected", moves to mixed strategies ("the best-response function is a Kakutani function of the probabilities"), and, following Joyce's *Regret and Instability*, relocates the decision: "The 'decision', as such, is the formation of the epistemic state about the action; it is this which is required to be in equilibrium. The *action* occurs after the decision, and may be anything which is consistent with the decision." The tickle turn: an $\epsilon$-exploring EDT whose "prior knows what policy EDT selects — IE, it knows what it will do up to the randomness involved in exploration" two-boxes against an imperfect predictor and defects against a copy using different random bits; where exploration bits are predictable (Death in Damascus, perfect Newcomb) the two still differ; hence the **Law of Logical Causality**: "If conditioning on any event changes the probability an agent assigns to its own action, that event **must** be treated as causally downstream." The lesion of Smoking Lesion Steelman stays upstream "because an agent obeying ratifiability will know its own decision perfectly, screening off the action from such influences", whereas a lesion that works "like a trembling hand after the decision is made" is downstream.

### 1.2 Claims about the text

**L2-1 [checked; `L2-scratch/p05_theorem1_check.py`] — Theorem 1 is the all-parents-observed adjustment identity, and its whole content is the graph assumption.** With $D$ the only parent of $A$ and the belief state conditioned on $D=d^*$, Pearl's adjustment over the parents of $A$ gives, for any outcome variable $Y$ and any $a$ with $P(a\mid d^*)>0$,
$$P(Y\mid do(A{=}a),\,D{=}d^*) \;=\; \sum_{pa}P(Y\mid a,pa)\,P(pa\mid d^*) \;=\; P(Y\mid a,\,d^*),$$
since $pa=\{D\}$ is fixed at $d^*$. So $\mathbb E[U\mid A{=}a,D{=}d^*]=\mathbb E[U\mid do(A{=}a),D{=}d^*]$ for every $a$, and the two maximization constraints select the same faces. Exact check on 200 random nets ($W\to D\to A$, $W\to U\leftarrow A$, three candidate mixed strategies, exploration floor $\tfrac1{10}$): maximal gap $0$ with $D$ known; with $D$ marginalised the classical Smoking-Lesion gap appears (e.g. smoke: evidential $-196/137$ vs causal $-4/5$); with a lesion-dependent tremble added as a second parent of $A$ (implementability violated) the gap at known $D$ reaches $168/209$. This is the same mechanism Oesterheld states for the beliefs–preferences–decision-theory version ("formally it follows directly from Theorem 3.4.1, Rule 2 of Pearl", `oesterheld-tickle-defense-intro.txt:443–450`, read): the author's $D$ plays the role of Oesterheld's three causes, Eells's "beliefs and desires", Briggs's "beliefs and desires … jointly causally sufficient". The exploration floor is what makes $P(a\mid d^*)>0$ and so the evidential side defined. Register: the theorem is correct and elementary; what it *proves* is that under the stated graph the two evaluators are one function; it does not touch the choice among fixed points.

**L2-2 [derived] — what "knows its mixed strategy" is, formally.** Write $v_d(a):=\mathbb E[U\mid A{=}a,D{=}d]$ (which by L2-1 is also the causal value at the $d$-conditioned state). The "consistent choices" of Case 1 are the fixed points $d^*\in \mathrm{BR}_\epsilon(d^*)$ of the correspondence $\mathrm{BR}_\epsilon(d):=\{m\in\Delta(A): m(a)\ge\epsilon\ \forall a;\ m(a)>\epsilon\Rightarrow a\in\arg\max v_d\}$. The state's dependence on $d$ runs only through the posterior over $D$'s unobserved parents, $P(W\mid D{=}d)$, i.e. through the conditional law of $D$ given its parents read as a likelihood in $d$ — in the Smoking-Lesion reading, "how strongly does my choosing *this* smoking propensity indicate the lesion". This is a fixed point between self-knowledge of the mixture and best response to the self-knowledge; in the house vocabulary the label $C(d)$ appears both as the thing believed ($P_{s_d}(a)=C(d)(a)$, Appendix B's self-transparency) and as the thing approved (Definition 18's support condition), which is exactly v2 Remark 5.1's "ratifiability is to labels what calibration is to branches" and the fixed-point problem of §5 — see §3.

**L2-3 [derived; standard game theory reconstructed] — the exploration floor is Selten's $\epsilon$-perfect-equilibrium device, problem-side.** Case 1's two clauses ("at least $\epsilon$" and "$>\epsilon$ only if maximizing") say that non-maximizers receive exactly $\epsilon$ and maximizers share the remainder — the definition of an $\epsilon$-perfect equilibrium (a completely mixed profile in which every non-best reply has weight $\le\epsilon$; Selten 1975 [reconstructed, unverified here]). P05 never takes $\epsilon\to0$; Case 2 removes the floor by smoothing the selection function instead. v2 has both devices under different names: Definition 10's $C^\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)C+\epsilon\,\mathrm{Unif}$ (problem-side trembles, Remark 5.3's Kreps–Wilson consistency) and Remark 5.2's "smoothed evaluators (trembles, softmax) regain existence by Brouwer" (Case 2's move). Phase 2 found that Remark 3.12's fixed-procedure tremble form is empty on the miniature while the test-sequence form exists (P2:calibration CA-14′, P2:dynamic DY-2/DY-3, read) — which is the precise sense in which Case 1 (a fixed $\epsilon$-floor) and Case 2 (an $\epsilon$-indexed family) are different existence statements, and P05 is silent about the relation.

**L2-4 [derived] — the scope of "CDT=EDT" in P05.** The theorem's conclusion is *set equality of ratifiable mixed strategies*, not agreement of recommendations at arbitrary states and not a selection among several fixed points ("My constraints … don't force any particular choice of mixed strategy in cases where several options have maximal utility"). It holds exactly on the implementable class — randomization independent of everything not downstream of it — and P03 says the two theories still differ where exploration bits are predictable (Death in Damascus: "CDT still proposes to flee half the time … EDT doesn't"; perfect Newcomb). So the honest compression is: *under independent randomization and self-knowledge of the mixture, evidential and causal support conditions coincide as fixed-point conditions*; nothing is said about which fixed point, and nothing about correlated randomness.

**L2-5 [derived] — existence is asserted in Case 1 and argued only in Cases 2–3.** Case 1 has no existence claim beyond "the condition … forces it into a few possibilities". The Kakutani route P03 names needs $d\mapsto v_d(\cdot)$ continuous, i.e. the posterior $P(W\mid D{=}d)$ continuous in $d$ — a condition on how the hidden parents generate the mixed strategy, which P05 identifies only in Case 3 ("this requires that the relationship between $D$ and its parents is continuous"). With continuity, $\mathrm{BR}_\epsilon$ has nonempty convex compact values and closed graph on the compact convex $\Delta_\epsilon(A)$, so Kakutani gives a fixed point for each $\epsilon>0$; this is the shape of P2:seeds SE-18′(a) (read), whose hypotheses in v2 are recording and realized observations. What P05 lacks and v2's phase 2 has is the limit clause: SE-18′(b) shows the $\epsilon\to0$ limit is approved only at the test-sequence state, and along Definition 10's ray only under positivity at the limit. C2 owns the theorem; the handoff is precise: *P05 Case 1 as stated is an $\epsilon$-perfect fixed-point claim without an existence proof; Case 2's Brouwer covers smoothed selection functions, not the $\epsilon$-floor form.*

**L2-6 [derived] — the Law of Logical Causality is the local Markov property read as a graph-construction norm.** In any Bayes net where $A$'s only parent is $D$, $A\perp \mathrm{ND}(A)\mid D$ (local Markov), so every variable $X$ with $P(X\mid A,D)\neq P(X\mid D)$ is a descendant of $A$. The LLC's antecedent ("conditioning on any event changes the probability an agent assigns to its own action" — given the agent's self-knowledge $D$) is exactly "$X\not\perp A\mid D$"; its consequent ("must be treated as causally downstream") is what the Markov property *entails* once implementability is drawn into the graph. So the LLC adds nothing to implementability as a mathematical constraint; its content is the *instruction to draw the graph that way* — to make a predictor of one's exploration bits a child of the act. That is the step v2 Remark 3.11 refuses ("under both [tree-intrinsic counterfactuals], a predictor's output covaries with the action, so classical CDT … would be ruled miscalibrated in Newcomb's problem by definitional fiat"), and it is the step P2:faithful FA-19′/FA-23′ (read) re-describe as a choice of self-location weighting. P03's own words already concede that the LLC's justification is instrumental: it is proposed "for agents obeying ratifiability" so as to "make ratifiability imply CDT=EDT", and the author tells Diffractor that Troll Bridge "will end up formally outside the realm where LLC can be justified" by that desire. Register: LLC is a stipulation in the sequence; whether it is a theorem in v2 is §3's question (Q-D of `sequence-map.md` §5.2).

**L2-7 [derived] — Case 3 is the deliberational picture's fixed point without its dynamics.** "$D$ … the agents beliefs about which action it will take", a deterministic rational $D\to A$, and approximate self-knowledge, is the object Skyrms calls a *state of indecision* ("her probabilities now that she will do the act in question at the end of deliberation", `skyrms-1990-dynamic-models-of-deliberation-tark.txt:149–159`, read via INDEX §2.2 and re-read below) and Joyce's $P_e$ ("Strictly speaking $P_e$ only tells her what she believes she'll do at the end of deliberation", Joyce 2012 as reproduced in `levinstein-soares-2020-…txt:396–408`, read via INDEX; Joyce himself [reconstructed]). The author takes from Joyce the belief/act split and from Skyrms the convergence question ("Skyrms raises interesting issues of the Dutch-bookability of such a process", P03) but supplies no dynamics: rock–paper–scissors is narrated as an iteration "until it is too late to change decisions", and the formal statement is the fixed point only. The novelty in Case 3 is therefore not the object (it is Skyrms's) but the *screening-off reading of it*: approximate knowledge of one's state of indecision screens the act off from the state's hidden parents in the limit, which turns Skyrms's deliberational equilibrium into a tickle. Whether the literature already had that reading is the Eells-1984 / Huttegger question of §2.

**L2-8 [derived; attribution from the text itself] — the author's own placement.** P05 disclaims novelty for the conclusion ("The proofs above are just versions of the tickle defense for EDT"; "I don't think any of this is very new") and locates the audience difference in AI alignment, adding the diagnosis that philosophers keep the distinction "on the grounds that decision theories should provide notions of rationality even under failure of introspection" — Lewis's partly rational agent (`lewis-1981-causal-decision-theory.txt:298–306`, read via INDEX §2.2) — and the bet that advanced AIs will have no "fundamental introspection barriers". So the placement question is not whether CDT=EDT-under-introspection is known (the author says it is) but whether the *specific* package — (a) the mixed strategy as the object of knowledge, (b) the exploration floor, (c) equality of the ratifiable sets rather than of verdicts, (d) the "same Bayes net for both" fairness frame, (e) the LLC — is known, refines the known, or says something else. §2 and §4 take these one by one.

**L2-9 [derived] — the P02→P03 shift retraces Skyrms §5 → Skyrms fn 14 / Harper.** P02's "know which action you will take — not just what probability" is the correlated-equilibrium reading of ratifiability that Skyrms proves as the Ratifiability Lemma and connects to Aumann (`skyrms-1990-ratifiability-…txt:277–358`, read: "if each player were told privately at $t_0$ what ratifiable act she would do at $t_1$, she would have had no incentive to deviate. This means that the players are at a subjectively correlated equilibrium"). P03's retreat to mixed strategies because pure ratifiable acts may not exist is the move of Skyrms's fn 14 ("if we expand the acts to include costless implementation of arbitrary random strategies, and preclude any correlation between the Savage states and these mixed acts, we get a ratifiable act", `:508–516`, read) and of Harper 1986 as reported by Weatherson (`weatherson-gtadt-ratificationist.md:124`, read) and SEP (`sep-causal-decision-theory.md:344`, read) — Harper's own text [reconstructed]. Skyrms's fn 14 also lists the three defeaters of the mixed escape (cost of randomizing; the demon predicts *whether* you randomize; the demon predicts the *outcome*), of which P05's implementability assumption simply excludes the second and third and P05 Case 3 concedes the first in spirit ("an absurd degree of trust in that randomization").


## 2. Taxonomy of the defence

Organized by the *object of self-knowledge* each position assumes, since that is the axis along which the author's version has to be placed. §2.1 is the tickle family (screening-off by knowledge of the causes of one's act); §2.2 is the ratifiability/equilibrium family (stability of the choice under knowledge of the choice); §2.3 collects the objections; §2.4 is the table.

### 2.1 The tickle family: screening off the common cause

**Lewis's presentation and reply (Lewis 1981, `lewis-1981-causal-decision-theory.txt:277–327`, read directly).** The defence as Lewis states it (in quotation marks, as an opponent's argument): "If you are fully rational your choices are governed entirely by your beliefs and desires so nothing can influence your choices except by influencing your beliefs and desires. But if you are fully rational, you know your own mind … By the time you decide whether to eat eggs, your credence function already has been modified by the evidence of the tickle. Then $C(E/G)$ does not exceed $C(E/{-}G)$". *Object of self-knowledge:* the introspectible inclination ("that tickle in the tastebuds — or whatever introspectible state it might be — that manifests your desire for eggs") and, behind it, beliefs and desires. *What is assumed:* full rationality including self-knowledge; choice governed entirely by beliefs and desires. *Lewis's reply, verbatim:* "the Tickle Defence does establish that a Newcomb problem cannot arise for a fully rational agent, but … decision theory should not be limited to apply only to the fully rational agent … A partly rational agent may very well be in a moderate Newcomb problem, either because his choices are influenced by something besides his beliefs and desires or because he cannot quite tell the strengths of his beliefs and desires before he acts." Fn 7 credits Eells with weaker assumptions ("even those weaker assumptions seem to me unduly restrictive"); fn 8 adds two more objections — the fully rational agent "can[not] be uncertain what he is going to choose, hence [it is] hard to see how he can be in a position to deliberate" (citing Jeffrey 1977), and certainty about one's own credences forecloses learning that "those data are untrustworthy".

**Skyrms 1980 (not available; quoted by Oesterheld `:500–511`, read directly): the self-reference objection.** "If the utilities, probabilities, and decision rule determine the action, then it is tantamount to taking which action will be performed as a datum in the evaluation of the alternative courses of action. The whole decision process then threatens to become dangerously self-referential." Oesterheld's gloss and reply (`:512–537`, read): knowing beliefs, preferences and rule does not give knowledge of the *act*, because "he still does not know which decision is implied by them (cf. Eells, 1982, p. 150 …). He has logical uncertainty"; and Oesterheld records the remaining worry that an agent who knows a full description of its decision "could choose any action $a$ with the justification, 'If I choose $a$, then this implies that $a$ is the action recommended by my decision rule …'" (Drescher 2006; Lewis fn 8; Jeffrey 1977). Oesterheld also dates the defence: "Skyrms (1980, p. 130-132) – who, to my knowledge, was the first to discuss the tickle defense in published writing" (`:500–501`); the same-family list is Horgan 1981, Jeffrey 1983 p. 25, Eells, Albert–Heiner 2001, Almond 2010, Horwich 1987 §11.2, Yudkowsky–Soares 2017, "arguably even Nozick (1969, footnote 11)" (`:487–492`).

**Eells 1982 (not available; quoted by Oesterheld `:538–566`, read; by Briggs `:1319–1341`, read; by Khan fn 7–8 `:250–256`, read).** *Object:* "his subjective beliefs and desires" plus the decision rule; Eells's hypothesis, as quoted by Oesterheld, "the only way in which a common cause can cause an agent-like person to perform the symptomatic act is by suitably affecting his body of possessed information … And I shall assume that our agent believes this hypothesis about how the common cause causes the symptomatic act" (Eells 1982 pp. 138f., 146). Briggs's rendering: "Being rational … is a matter of having beliefs … and desires … that are jointly causally sufficient to determine one's actions. Being self-aware is a matter of knowing what one's beliefs and desires are. And believing that one is rational is a matter of believing that one's actions are causally determined by one's beliefs and desires (even if one is not certain how the causal determination works, so that one is not certain what one will do)." Khan fn 8: Eells excludes the common cause acting through the *decision rule* ("the causal influence of whatever is causally responsible for his rationality – his training, genetic make-up, and so on – will be overwhelming", Eells 1982 p. 147). Spohn 2012 (`spohn-2012-…txt:1064–1067`, read) attributes to Eells 1982 p. 176 the first statement of the self-knowledge condition (SK) that the agent's own present decision situation gets probability 1. *Scope:* per Oesterheld (`:571–576`, read), Eells "uses the tickle defense to argue for agreement between CDT and EDT in all decision problems" and "seems to simply miss the possibility of Newcomb-like problems from logical dependences that persist when all common causes are known." Everything about Eells's own text beyond these quotations is [reconstructed].

**Eells 1984 — the metatickle and the dynamics of deliberation (not available; via Oesterheld §6.4 `:657–773`, Khan §3 `:236–420`, Rabinowicz fn xxxv `:440`, SEP `:191–193`, all read).** Oesterheld's reconstruction: "deliberative acts" (computing an expected value) produce "mental evidence" $e_1,\dots,e_n$; conditioning on the record of one's own expected-value calculations screens the act off, so that $P(\text{money}\mid \text{one-box},e_1..e_n)\approx P(\text{money}\mid e_1..e_n)\approx P(\text{money}\mid\text{two-box},e_1..e_n)$ and two-boxing wins. Khan: Eells's "continual conditional expected utility maximization" adds "urgency to act", which Huttegger rejects as "a large deviation from traditional evidential decision theory" (`:95–103` of the window). Rabinowicz fn xxxv: the one-box option's "value as news tends to decrease. The option's evidentiary significance as the signal of the favourable state is gradually screened". SEP: Eells 1984a's dynamic version answers Horwich's mechanism objection; Sobel 1994 argues it does not reconcile in all problems and gives right answers "not … for the right reasons". *Object of self-knowledge:* the agent's own tentative evaluations — the metatickle "is a proposition which describes the agent's beliefs and desires" (Khan fn 7) and, in the dynamic version, the trace of one's deliberation. *Where it fails, per Oesterheld:* for logical-dependence Newcomb the predictor "predicts whether we eventually one- or two-box", not our initial calculations, so the screening does not obtain (`:717–740`); "screen offs by inclination" (Ahmed 2014 p. 105) work only when the predictor reads inclinations rather than final decisions. Oesterheld's structural observation (`:764–771`, read): "the structure of Eells argument is the same as the structure of reasoning in so-called ratificationist versions of EDT."

**Jeffrey's ratifiability as a hypothetical metatickle (Jeffrey 1981/1983 not available).** Jeffrey 1981 p. 486 as quoted by Khan (`:277–281`, read): "it is my credences and desirabilities at the end of deliberation that correspond to the preferences in the light of which I act". Khan fn 10 (`:301`, read) reports Skyrms 1984 p. 74 calling Jeffrey's idea "a hypothetical version of the metatickle defense" [Skyrms 1984 reconstructed]. SEP (`:340`, read): "Jeffrey used ratification as a means of making evidential decision theory yield the same recommendations as causal decision theory … However, Jeffrey (2004: 113n) concedes that evidential decision theory's reliance on ratification does not make it agree with causal decision theory in all cases." So Jeffrey's object of self-knowledge is the *final decision*, supposed hypothetically: "the probability matrix the agent thinks he would have if he finally decided to perform that act" (Jeffrey 1983 p. 16 as quoted by Skyrms `:456–458` and SEP `:336`, both read).

**Price 1986 (not available; abstract obtained through a web search snippet, verbatim as returned — verify against the PhilPapers record before quoting publicly).** "I defend BDT, invoking a familiar principle of statistical inference to show that in such cases a free agent cannot take the contemplated action to be probabilistically relevant to its causes (so that BDT gives the right answer). I argue that my defence does better than those of Ellery Eells and Richard Jeffrey". *Object:* none introspected — the defence runs on agency ("a free agent"), not on knowledge of inclinations; Hájek 2016 §5 (per INDEX 1.20, read there) rebuts it as "no evidence / authority trumps" and says introspective evidence such as "a 'tickle' that you can introspect" (`hajek-2016-…txt:425–430`) is *more* evidence, not less. SEP `:189` groups Horgan 1981 and Price 1986 with Eells as making "similar points". [reconstructed beyond the abstract.]

**Ahmed 2014 (summary only; Ahmed's p. 91 as quoted by Drescher, `eaforum-…md:457–461`, read).** "(4.7) Mental causation: the undesirable event produces the dominant option via and only via its operation upon the agent's current predecision desires and beliefs. (4.8) Transparency: the agent knows enough about her own current desires and beliefs to make the dominated option evidentially irrelevant to them. (4.9) Therefore, the agent takes neither option to be evidentially relevant to the undesirable event." Drescher also records Ahmed's threshold calculations (pp. 105, 110) — the defence is stated in degrees, not certainties — and Treutlein–Oesterheld's (5.1)–(5.3) with the verdict that common-cause problems acting only through beliefs/desires are "incoherent" (`:463`). *Object:* current predecision desires and beliefs, known "enough" — graded transparency.

**Oesterheld (2018/2022, read in full at §5, §6.1–6.4).** Premise: "the agent knows all the immediate causes of its choice" (`:346–347`), justified because "the only causes of a Bayesian decision theorist's choice are the agent's beliefs, the agent's preferences, and the agent's decision theory" (`:355–357`); second premise, knowledge of the mechanism (`:391–402`); conclusion: "if the decision problem is modeled by a causal graph and if the agent knows all causes of its actions (or at least the ones that share a common cause with the payoff), then EDT and CDT assign the same expected values to each action … formally it follows directly from Theorem 3.4.1, Rule 2 of Pearl" (`:443–450`). Scope restriction: "does not apply to Newcomb's problem, the Prisoner's Dilemma against a copy and so forth" because "When an agent decides whether to one- or two-box, it cannot already know that in this particular situation its decision theory implies one-boxing (or two-boxing). Otherwise, the agent would not face a choice at all and at least EDT could not make any recommendations since it cannot assign expected utility to actions that have a probability of 0" (`:451–464`). Treutlein 2018 (fn 9) notes the introspection assumption separates decision theory for machines from that for humans.

**Briggs 2010 §7 (read `:1255–1369`).** The tickle defence as an "avoidance strategy": decision situations like Smoking Lesion "are poorly described and should be ruled impossible at the outset", since for a suitable agent $C(K)=C(K\mid A)$ for all $A,K$, whence "every decision situation involves taking a unanimous vote among the agent's actions". *Object:* beliefs and desires (Eells's version). Briggs's own verdict: "Whether the tickle defense rules out all cases of conflict … is somewhat controversial … it seems clear that a great many suitable agents will have information that renders their actions evidentially irrelevant."

**Huttegger 2023 (not available) via Khan 2023 (read §3, §4, §6, §7).** Huttegger's diagnosis: "both Eells and Jeffrey did not adequately specify how the agent comes to fully know her beliefs and desires and achieve this screening off" (Khan `:283–284` of the window); repair: Skyrms's adaptive dynamics on choice probabilities plus a separate *independence dynamics* driving $P(S_2\mid A_2)$ and $P(S_2\mid A_1)$ together to the **Eells–Jeffrey manifold** "where there is state-act independence" (`:467–470`); the caveat that the reconciliation fails "if the agent believes that the predictor in Newcomb's problem knows more about how he makes decisions than he knows about himself" (SEP `:193` agrees). Khan's result: in Newcomb the manifold lies wholly on the two-boxing side of the indifference plane so any independence dynamics reconciles; in (asymmetric) Death in Damascus the plane is perpendicular to the manifold and reconciliation at the mixed act 0.474 requires "(i) the independence dynamics … does not take the shortest path to the manifold, and (ii) the adaptive dynamics and independence dynamics … reach the Eells-Jeffrey manifold at precisely the point of reconciliation" (`:779–785`); verdict "the metatickle approach has so far failed to reconcile evidential and causal decision theory" (`:379–380` of the window). Khan also notes Skyrms's original finding that pure adaptive dynamics leaves the evidentialist in Newcomb "most probably one-boxing, but also attach[ing] some positive probability to two-boxing" (`:92–94`).

**Others in the family.** Yudkowsky–Soares 2017 fn 3 and fn 11 (via INDEX §2.2, checked there): "The tickle defense of Eells (1984) or the ratification procedure of Jeffrey (1983) can be used to give a version of EDT that smokes in this problem (and two-boxes in Newcomb's problem)"; ratification and metatickles "can in fact be seen as methods for constructing $P^E$ that push $P^E(\text{onebox})$ to 0". Levinstein–Soares 2020 fn 1 (`:172–175`, read): XOR Blackmail is chosen because "the smoking lesion problem requires the agent to be uncertain about their own desires" and XOR "is immune to the so-called 'tickle defense' (Eells 1984)". Miletic 2026 (INDEX 1.32; not read here) argues the Smoking Lesion "doesn't really distinguish" the theories — L1's territory.

**L2-10 [derived] — three objects of self-knowledge in the tickle family, and where the author's $D$ sits.** The family assumes knowledge of (i) an introspectible *inclination* (Lewis's "tickle"; Briggs's desire-or-belief; Ahmed's graded "current predecision desires and beliefs"); (ii) the *complete causal basis of the act* — beliefs, desires and decision rule (Eells 1982; Oesterheld's "all the immediate causes of its choice"; Briggs's "jointly causally sufficient"); or (iii) the *agent's own tentative decision or evaluation record* (Eells 1984's metatickle dynamics; Jeffrey's hypothetically supposed final decision; Huttegger's deliberation-driven independence). The author's object — the mixed strategy $D$ actually selected, known exactly, with the act still uncertain by $\epsilon$-exploration — is none of these: it is downstream of (ii) (it is the *output* of beliefs, desires and rule, as (iii) is) but is a *distribution* rather than a tentative act, is known *exactly* rather than approximately or hypothetically, and is known *before* the act is drawn. In the house vocabulary it is Appendix B's self-transparency, $P_{s_d}(a)=C(d)(a)$, imposed as a premise. That specific object is not stated in any tickle-family text I read; it is stated, as an equilibrium (not screening) condition, in the ratifiability family — §2.2.

**L2-11 [derived] — the scope disagreement, and which side the author takes.** Eells claims agreement in all problems (Oesterheld `:571–576`); Oesterheld, Ahmed (as summarized) and Levinstein–Soares restrict the defence to common-cause ("medical") cases and hold that logical-dependence cases (Newcomb with a predictor of the *decision*, PD against a copy, XOR) are immune, because the agent cannot know its own output without ceasing to face a choice. The author in P05 is on Eells's side of the scope line but with a different mechanism: knowing $D$ "is somewhat like saying that the output of the abstract algorithm node is known", and the $\epsilon$-floor keeps the act uncertain, so the "would not face a choice at all" objection is met by relocating the known object from the act to the mixture. The price, which P03 states and Oesterheld's Newcomb model (`:452–464`) makes vivid, is that this works only when the predictor predicts the *mixture* and not the *draw* — implementability. Where the predictor reads the draw (perfect Newcomb, Death in Damascus, pseudo-random coins — Levinstein–Soares `:428–434`, read: "any mental randomization she performs is still predictable from death's perspective"), the author concedes the theories still differ and Oesterheld's restriction stands. So the two positions coincide on the extension and differ on the description: what Oesterheld calls "logical dependence that persists when all common causes are known" the author calls "exploration bits can be predicted".

**L2-12 [derived] — P05 assumes the endpoint that the dynamic tickle literature tried to reach.** Eells 1984's screening is approximate and cumulative ("gradually screened", Rabinowicz fn xxxv); Skyrms's adaptive dynamics reaches a mixed state of indecision that does *not* screen (Khan `:92–94`); Huttegger adds an independence dynamics to reach state–act independence, and Khan shows the arrival point in instability problems depends on the dynamics' route and relative speed. P05 has no dynamics: state–act independence given $D$ is a *premise* ("$D$ screens $A$ off from any un-observed parents of $D$"), i.e. the agent is placed *on* the Eells–Jeffrey manifold by stipulation. In v2's terms P05 stipulates the fixed point of the calibration manifold (Remark 3.7: "its fixed point is strict calibration") and the literature's question — whether deliberation reaches it, and where — is v2's Remark 5.3 "third road", recorded and not adopted. Register: this makes P05's Theorem 1 *weaker* than Huttegger's reconciliation as a claim about agents (it says nothing about how the knowledge is acquired) and *stronger* as a claim about problems (it holds for every implementable problem, Death in Damascus included, once the fixed point is reached — Khan's "forced conditions" are conditions on *reaching* 0.474, not on 0.474 being ratifiable, which Khan grants: "the uniquely ratifiable act (should one have the option to execute it using a chance device …)", `:487–491`).


### 2.2 The ratifiability / equilibrium family: stability under knowledge of the choice

**Jeffrey (1983; not available).** Informal definition (Jeffrey 1983 p. 16, as quoted by Skyrms `:456–458` and SEP `:336`, both read): "A ratifiable decision is a decision to perform an act of maximum estimated desirability relative to the probability matrix the agent thinks he would have if he finally decided to perform that act." *Object of self-knowledge:* the final decision, supposed hypothetically. *What is known:* Skyrms `:43–46` — "ratifiability was not given any precise definition within Jeffrey's system, and the discussion of its sensitivity to causal considerations has been inconclusive"; SEP `:340` — Jeffrey 2004 p. 113n concedes it does not always agree with CDT, and Joyce 2007 argues "the motivation for ratification appeals to causal relations, so that even if it yields correct recommendations using Jeffrey's formula for expected utility, it still does not yield a purely evidential decision theory."

**Skyrms 1990, "Ratifiability and the Logic of Decision" (read in full).** *Definition:* in a Jeffrey space with Savage act/state partitions, $U(A\mid P)=\sum_j pr(K_j\mid P)V(A\&K_j)$, the identity **(E)** $V(A)=U(A\mid A)$, and "There is only one formal definition of ratifiability that makes sense in this framework: Def. (R): $A_i$ is ratifiable iff $U(A_i\mid A_i)\ge U(A_j\mid A_i)$ for all $j$" (`:113–115`); "Jeffrey expected value is, in this sense, the figure of merit for the ratifiable act — but not for its contrast class" (`:120–121`). *What is known — the "yes" theorem (`:173–191`):* if the chosen act stays ratifiable on $[x,1)$ and $pr(K_j\mid A_i)$ changes continuously in deliberation time, it maximizes Savage EU at the moment of truth. *The "no" theorem (`:228–269`):* "There is no coherent model of deliberation under which the probabilities of the states conditional on the acts change continuously with time, such that for any coherent prior Jeffrey and Savage coincide in the limit" — the Nice Demon (payoffs 1/0, 0/2, prior $.9/.1$ on the diagonal), where "Coherence requires that the initial zero probabilities not be raised. Otherwise the decision-maker is open to a trivial dutch book" (`:250–256`), so $pr(S_1\mid A_1)=pr(S_2\mid A_2)=1$ throughout and the Jeffrey values $1,2$ never meet the Savage utilities. Skyrms flags the ill-definedness that drives it: at the moment of truth the competitors' Jeffrey values "are on conditions which at $t_1$ have probability 0 … an attempt to substitute $V$ for $U$ in definition R would produce nonsense" (`:198–203`). *The Ratifiability Lemma (`:297–319`):* an agent who knows she will condition on an experiment and then maximize, and knows her tie-breaks, "knows that she will choose an act that is ratifiable at $t_0$" — ratifiability as a *consequence* of self-knowledge plus rationality; with common knowledge it yields Aumann's correlated equilibrium and, "If, furthermore, they already know what they are going to do, they are at a Nash equilibrium" (`:349–355`). *Self-knowledge probabilizes Newcomb (`:373–381`):* "there is only one ratifiable act here. That is Act 2. So the decision-maker's initial probabilities at $t_0$ must already be $x=0$ and $y=1$. Self-knowledge of expected utility maximization in a curious way prohibits the existence of values for $x$ and $y$ such that Jeffrey expected value maximization is well defined and conflicts with Savage expected utility maximization." *The Mean Demon (`:382–412`):* zero-sum against a perfect predictor, "Mixed strategies are not available"; "these assumptions overconstrain the initial probabilities. There is no ratifiable act, so the assumptions of the ratifiability lemma cannot be met"; and, dropping knowledge of tie-breaks, "Suppose that no matter what information comes in, at $t_1$ the values of $x$ and $y$ are $2/3$ and $1/3$ respectively … Then at $t_1$ both acts maximize expected utility, and knowledge at $t_0$ that one will do something that maximizes expected utility at $t_1$ is just knowledge that one will do something. You can check that these are the only values for $x$ and $y$ at $t_0$ and $t_1$ which are consistent with the weakened assumption." *Fn 14 (`:508–516`):* the mixed-strategy escape and its three defeaters (cost; the demon predicts whether you randomize; the demon predicts the outcome) — "Solution of the existence problem is not something that we are entitled to take for granted." *Fn 15:* weakly dominated equilibria as the only ratifiable options in Rabinowicz 1986's game. *Conclusion (`:427–437`):* "The main significance of ratifiability does not lie in its use in handling causal pathology … The real importance … is that it has opened up a rich array of topics for investigation: the status of equilibrium as a rationality concept, the nature of the deliberational process, the connections between individual decision theory and the theory of games, and the consequences of self-knowledge for rational decision-makers." *Guide (`:459–466`):* Harper 1984/86/88's definition "is equivalent to that given in section 2 … provided that the counterfactual in Harper's definition is given reasonable truth conditions in terms of the Savage states. This notion is called stability by Rabinowicz (1988)".

**Skyrms 1990, TARK (read `:53–243`).** *Object:* the *state of indecision*, "a probability vector assigning probabilities to each of the $n$ acts … These are to be interpreted as her probabilities now that she will do the act in question at the end of deliberation"; "the adoption of a mixed strategy can be thought of as a way to turn the state of indecision for its constituent pure acts to stone. We will call a mixed act corresponding to a state of indecision, its default mixed act." *Dynamics:* rules that "seek the good" ("raises the probability of an act only if that act has utility greater than that of the status quo"), all sharing fixed points where "the utility of the status quo is maximal"; the Nash map; the requirement that updating "falls short of giving acts with non-maximal current expected utility probability zero" (`:107–110` of the window) — a positivity discipline during deliberation. *Existence (Prop. I):* Brouwer, when the adaptive rule $D$ and the informational feedback $I$ are continuous; "The default mixed act corresponding to $x$ maximizes expected utility at $\langle x,y\rangle$." *Prop. II:* with common prior, updating by emulation and common knowledge, deliberational equilibria are exactly Nash equilibria.

**Harper 1986 (not available — [reconstructed] throughout).** Via SEP `:344` (read): "Weirich (1985) and Harper (1986) define ratification in terms of an option's expected utility given its realization rather than given a decision to realize it … Weirich and Harper also assume causal decision theory's formula for expected utility. In the case of Death in Damascus, causal decision theory concludes that the threatened man lacks a self-ratifying option. A self-ratifying option emerges, however, if the man may flip a coin … Assuming that Death cannot predict the coin flip's outcome, the mixed strategy is self-ratifying." Via Weatherson `:124` (read): "insist, as Harper (1986) did … that if Chooser is rational, they can play a mixed strategy"; via Skyrms `:459–464`: Harper's counterfactual definition is (R) under reasonable truth conditions. Harper's own text was not obtained (Springer paywall; PhilArchive/PhilPapers records returned 403; Semantic Scholar has the record with no abstract — Reading ledger). *Object:* the realized mixed act; *mechanism:* CDT-valued ratifiability plus independence of the coin from the predictor.

**Weirich 1985 (not available; via SEP `:344`, Weatherson `:93`).** [reconstructed] same family as Harper; Weirich 2004 ch. 9 offers "a method of selecting among multiple self-ratifying strategies" (SEP `:362`).

**Joyce 2012 (not available; via Levinstein–Soares `:360–440`, Khan `:180–184`, SEP `:354`, all read; the published abstract was returned by a web search only in the tool's paraphrase and is not quoted here).** As reproduced by Levinstein–Soares: initial $P_0(S)=.9$ makes fleeing look better; "The agent regards herself as rational, i.e., as a causal expected utility maximizer. However $P_0$ assigns lower credence to $F$ than to $S$ even though she currently evaluates $F$ as a better option … Surely, this should lead her to raise her credence in $F$"; iterate $P_{n+1}=P_n(\cdot\mid U_n)$ "if she updates in the right way", to an equilibrium $P_e=P_e(\cdot\mid U_e)$ with $P_e(S)=.5005$; "Strictly speaking $P_e$ only tells her what she believes she'll do at the end of deliberation. There remains the further question of what she actually should do. CDTers disagree about this question, and Joyce says that she is permitted either to go to Aleppo or stay in Damascus. Arntzenius thinks that cdt only ultimately tells you about what you should believe and is silent on which actions are rational." Both "rely primarily on the model of rational deliberation developed in Skyrms (1990)". SEP `:354`: Arntzenius holds the anticipated regret "counts against the option's rationality, whereas Joyce denies this"; Ahmed 2012 and Wedgwood 2013 "reject Arntzenius's and Joyce's responses to Egan because they hold that deliberations should settle on an option." *Object:* the agent's end-of-deliberation credences over her own acts (a state of indecision); the act is then "permitted" if in the support.

**Arntzenius 2008 (abstract read, `arntzenius-2008-no-regrets.txt`; Hájek `:1023–1035` read).** "I suggest dealing with this problem by allowing mixed decisions, and suggest a deliberational model (following Brian Skyrms)"; Hájek: "He understands a 'mixed decision'—a decision to perform one's possible actions with specified probabilities—as having those probabilities at the end of rational deliberation … deliberation is characterised in terms of them: it terminates when they reach an equilibrium. Deliberational decision theory can be given both an evidential and a causal formulation." Also *Desire Reflection* (the abstract): Jeffrey's EDT violates it, CDT satisfies it via Belief Reflection.

**Egan 2007 (read `:860–1116`).** The CDT-valued *Maxim of Ratifiability*; ratificationist CDT "will not endorse shooting in The Murder Lesion, or pressing in The Psychopath Button"; two failures — (i) "if successful, it does too much: If an appeal to ratifiability succeeds here, then the EDTer's appeal to ratifiability in the cases that were supposed to motivate the move to CDT will succeed as well … In The Smoking Lesion, not smoking is unratifiable … (But see Joyce (forthcoming) for an argument that the appeal to ratifiability is, in fact, only available to causal decision theorists.)"; (ii) "it doesn't do enough": in Psychopath Button "Neither action … is ratifiable … It's rational for Paul to refrain … So no theory that imposes a ratifiability requirement is the correct theory of rational decision", with fn 14 distinguishing these cases from Death in Damascus by the presence of "clear intuitions about which action it's rational to perform"; and *Newcomb's Firebomb*, where "neither option is ratifiable" yet one-boxing is clearly right and "CDT without a ratifiability requirement almost always tells us … that it is rational to one-box".

**Weatherson 2023 (read).** Ratificationism "used to be a more popular view … It subsequently fell out of popularity, though it has been recently endorsed by Fusco (n.d.)", for two reasons: no pure ratifiable option (Table 6.1) and only a bad one (Tables 6.2–6.3, the latter Skyrms 1984's, immune to a weak-dominance patch); the mixed-strategy repair after Harper; two arguments for ratifiability (the Chooser–Theory dialogue; "there really isn't a distinction between theorists and practitioners … one can't draw more conclusions from outside a game than what an intelligent player could draw from inside the game"); and "This is where I think the argument for ratificationism really needs mixed strategies." Ahmed 2012 [reconstructed] "says that it is an objection to a theory that it allows dilemmas in cases with finitely many options".

**Oesterheld–Conitzer 2021 (via INDEX §2.2 `:317–335`, checked there; not re-read).** In the Adversarial Offer "none of the three options … is ratifiable"; "The ratificationist may respond by claiming that unpredictable randomization should always be possible. If that were true, then the only ratifiable option would be to take each box with probability 50% … But again, we would like to have a decision theory that works in a broad variety of scenarios, including ones where the agent expects to be somewhat predictable"; an anti-randomization variant defeats even that.

**Levinstein–Soares 2020 (read `:276–510`).** The pseudo-random coin ("we assume any mental randomization she performs is still predictable from death's perspective. ### 2.3 Claims about the ratifiability family and the author's placement in it

**L2-13 [checked; `L2-scratch/skyrms_mean_demon_check.py`] — Skyrms 1990 §6 already contains mixed-strategy ratifiability in the author's Case-3 form.** With payoffs $(-50,0;\,0,-100)$ and a perfect predictor (mass $x$ on (Act 1, State 1), $y=1-x$ on (Act 2, State 2)), no pure act is ratifiable under Def. (R) ($U(A_1\mid A_1)=-50<0=U(A_2\mid A_1)$; $U(A_2\mid A_2)=-100<0$), and Savage EU with the induced state probabilities ties exactly at $x=2/3$: $-50x=-100(1-x)$, both $-100/3$. The zero-sum game's mixed Nash equilibrium is the same point (agent $2/3$ on Act 1, demon $2/3$ on State 1). Skyrms's "weakened assumption" — self-knowledge of EU-maximization *without* knowledge of tie-breaks — is satisfied by exactly this state of self-prediction and by no other, and he says so ("these are the only values … consistent with the weakened assumption"). This is the content of v2's Remark 4.3 (the unique jointly calibrated-and-approved mixture is at the tie, "the mixed Nash equilibrium of the agent against its own statistics") and of P05 Case 3 ($D$ as the agent's action probabilities, approved acts in the support), stated in 1990 with the object of self-knowledge being *the agent's probabilities of her own acts* and mixed acts explicitly excluded. Skyrms does not call it ratifiability of a mixed act — his (R) ranges over pure acts and he treats the Mean Demon as showing that self-knowledge "flirts with paradox" — but the fixed point is there, together with its uniqueness. Register: this is the strongest single piece of evidence in the corpus that the Case-3 *fixed point* is a known position; the Case-1 *implemented mixed act* is Harper's/fn 14's.

**L2-14 [derived] — P03's reading of Skyrms is faithful, and P05's exploration floor is Skyrms's missing contrast class.** Def. (R) is CDT-valued (Savage $U$ conditioned on the act), and Skyrms says it is "the only formal definition of ratifiability that makes sense"; so P03's "a reasonable interpretation of ratifiability (perhaps the only reasonable interpretation) makes Jeffrey's EDT select actions which are CDT-optimal when CDT chooses via a probability distribution which has been updated on knowledge of which action is to be selected" is Skyrms `:113–121` almost verbatim. Under (R), evidential and causal value agree *on the chosen act* by the identity (E) and disagree nowhere else because the competitors' evidential values are undefined at the moment of truth (`:198–203`). P05's $\epsilon$-floor is exactly what gives the evidential side a contrast class — the competitors keep probability $\epsilon$, so $V(A_j)$ is defined — and then L2-1 makes the two contrast classes coincide. So P05 = Skyrms's (R), relocated to mixtures and completed on the evidential side by exploration. What Skyrms would say of the completion is in his "no" theorem: if the $\epsilon$ is a *belief* raised from a coherent zero during deliberation, it is Dutch-bookable ("Coherence requires that the initial zero probabilities not be raised"); if it is a *fact* about the agent's randomization (P05's constraint is on the mixed strategy the agent actually plays), no zero was ever there and the book does not exist. That is v2's problem-side/procedure-side tremble distinction (Definition 10 vs Remark 3.9's routes; §7.1(4); P2:dynamic DY-5) seen from the Dutch-book side.

**L2-15 [derived] — Skyrms's "no" theorem bounds the dynamic version, not P05.** P05 is static: it characterizes fixed points and says nothing about reaching them. Skyrms's negative theorem is about *limits of coherent deliberation with continuous $pr(K\mid A)$* and shows that for the Nice Demon no such process ends with Jeffrey and Savage agreeing; his fn 12 leaves open that "such decision-makers should not be allowed to have the kind of prior given in the example". In P05's frame the Nice Demon has two ratifiable pure strategies (each self-confirming: the demon predicts the mixture, state $S_1$ if $d(A_1)>\tfrac12$, say), both evaluators agree at each, and selection is open — the multiplicity residue, not a disagreement. P03's gesture at "some kind of convergent process" and v2's Remark 5.3 (Skyrms's dynamics as "the third road") are exactly where Skyrms's theorem applies: the reconciliation cannot be a general theorem of coherent continuous deliberation. Handoff to C2 (the theorem) and L3 (the process).

**L2-16 [derived] — existence: mixed-strategy ratifiability exists exactly on the implementable class, and the anti-ratificationist cases are built to leave it.** Skyrms fn 14 (three defeaters), Oesterheld–Conitzer ("including ones where the agent expects to be somewhat predictable"; the anti-randomization offer), Levinstein–Soares (pseudo-random coin; Random Coin) and Egan's Firebomb (neither option ratifiable, one-boxing clearly right) all deny or exploit the independence of the randomization device from the predictor. P05 makes that independence a named assumption (implementability), P03 concedes the residue ("If your exploration bits can be predicted, then EDT and CDT still advise different actions"), and Case 3 tries to shed the assumption by replacing the mixed act with a belief — Skyrms's route, at the cost of approximate self-knowledge and a continuity hypothesis. v2's Definition 6 (independent redraws) *types* implementability in (Remark 3.2), Q12 records the shared-seed alternative, and P2:faithful FA-24′(c) (read) says what turns on it: "under Definition 6 the tree's evidential counterfactual is classical CDT's in Newcomb, under shared seed UDT's — 'EDT one-boxes' is, tree-side, a claim that the predictor shares the agent's seed". So the existence question and the CDT/EDT question are the *same* parameter in v2, which the literature treats as two (existence of ratifiable acts; predictability of one's randomization).

**L2-17 [derived] — multiplicity and selection: open in the literature, open in P05, open in v2.** SEP `:352` calls it "an open problem in causal decision theory"; Skyrms fn 15 and Weatherson's Table 6.3 show ratifiable-but-bad options (weakly dominated equilibria; $X$ with payoff $\varepsilon$); Egan's Firebomb shows the empty case with a clear verdict. **L2-18 [derived] — "why be ratifiable": four justifications in the literature, and the author's is the least common one.** (a) *Stability as a norm* (Jeffrey; Egan's Maxim; Weatherson's dialogue — "There is something very odd about saying that such-and-such is a rational thing to do, but whoever does it will regret it the moment they act"); (b) *theorist = practitioner* (Weatherson: "one can't draw more conclusions from outside a game than what an intelligent player could draw from inside"); (c) *consequence of self-knowledge plus rationality* (Skyrms's Ratifiability Lemma: knowing you will maximize ⇒ knowing you will choose a ratifiable act — ratifiability is derived, not postulated; Joyce 2007 per SEP says the motivation is causal); (d) *calibration / fairness of the problem description* (the author, 2026 chat: "it follows trivially from the idea that the probabilities given in decision problems should be calibrated; ie, the agent needs to understand the decision problem it is in"; v2 Remark 3.6–3.7: recording plus strict calibration *forces* $P_{s_d}(C(d))=1$, "Ratifiability then reads: the label lies on the branch-generated manifold; its fixed point is strict calibration"). (c) and (d) are the same shape — ratifiability as forced by self-knowledge — with different carriers: Skyrms's carrier is the agent's knowledge of her own rationality and tie-breaks; v2's is the problem's honesty about the agent's statistics (recording, Definition 7). The author's 2026 complaint that pure-strategy ratifiability "is in fact ruling out ways the world could be" is Skyrms's Newcomb observation (`:377–381`: self-knowledge "prohibits the existence of values for $x$ and $y$" on which the theories conflict) read as a *defect* rather than a curiosity. Register: (d) is the author's and v2's; I found no source that states it in the calibration form — the nearest is (c).

**L2-20 [derived] — Egan's parity point is P03's title made positive, and Joyce's reply is v2's Q6.** Egan `:922–935`: "if the CDTer's defense works, it works for the EDTer as well, and CDT loses its motivation" — stated as a *problem* for ratificationist CDT. ### 3.2 Claims

**L2-21 [derived] — v2's account is the literature's equilibrium picture with the carrier moved from the agent to the interaction.** Every row of the table has the same shape: where the literature says "the agent knows her beliefs/desires/rule/tie-breaks/act-probabilities", v2 says "the problem records at $d$ and the state is calibrated to the run statistics". Recording (Definition 7) is the exact condition under which $\nu(a\mid O_d)=C(d)(a)$ (Proposition 7's argument; P2:calibration CA-12′: on trees that record at $d$ the act values do not depend on $C(d)$, and "no mixed label is forced"), and it is a property of the *tree*, checkable without any theory of introspection. This is the sense in which v2 answers Lewis's partly-rational-agent objection *without* idealizing the agent: the objection was that decision theory should cover agents lacking self-knowledge; v2 replies that whether the self-knowledge is present is settled by whether the interaction records the act, which is a fact about the problem, and that where it does not (Told-You-So's selection root, Remark 3.4) forced self-knowledge "correctly never applied". Register: this is a *reframing*, not a refutation of Lewis; his partly rational agent reappears in v2 as a problem that fails to record.

**L2-22 [derived] — masked calibration is a formalization of Jeffrey's "would have" matrix, and the manifold is its mixed-strategy form.** Jeffrey's informal definition (quoted in §2.2) speaks of "the probability matrix the agent thinks he would have if he finally decided to perform that act"; Skyrms says the notion "was not given any precise definition within Jeffrey's system" and supplies one only by importing Savage states and reading "would have" as conditioning; Harper reads it counterfactually [reconstructed, Skyrms `:459–464`]. Definition 9's self-model realizes it as an objective statistic: the state the agent *would* have on deciding $m$ is $\nu_{B,C[d\mapsto m]}(\cdot\mid O_d)$, the conditional run law of the deviated procedure — "the state must be a realizable worldview at $d$, not necessarily the realized one" (Definition 9). Strict calibration is the case $m=C(d)$, i.e. the decision one *does* make; so "ratifiable" in Jeffrey's sense — the decision is best relative to the matrix one would have on making it — is, at a recorded point, exactly "the label lies on the manifold and is approved at its own fixed point" (Remark 3.7), which is why v2 can say "Ratifiability is to labels what calibration is to branches" (Remark 5.1). What is new relative to Jeffrey/Skyrms/Harper: the "would have" is *objective* (a fact about the tree and the deviated procedure, not an opinion), *procedure-relative*, and *graded* (strict/masked/limit; per-run/per-occurrence). Caveat (P2:calibration CA-1′, read): on the fair class no masked variant in Appendix B's grid gives both existence and optimality; the local-full-support official form frees only the present act.

**L2-23 [derived] — under Definition 6, calibrated states at recorded points already lie on Huttegger's Eells–Jeffrey manifold.** Huttegger's manifold (Khan `:467–470`) is the locus of state–act independence, $P(S\mid A_2)=P(S)=P(S\mid A_1)$, reached (if at all) by an independence dynamics. In v2, Definition 6 draws the act at a decision node "independently of everything so far", so conditional on reaching a node the draw is independent of every pre-node event (Lemma 3's mechanism); with Definition 7 recording, conditioning on $O_d$ is conditioning on reaching the unique $d$-node, so at *every* point of the calibration manifold (every self-model $m$) $P_{s_d}(X\mid a)=P_{s_d}(X)$ for pre-query $X$ — the Savage-state coordinates. Hence: *independent redraws + recording type in the endpoint that Eells 1984 and Huttegger 2023 tried to reach by deliberation, and that P05 assumed.* The two things the literature separates — arriving at state–act independence (the metatickle's job) and knowing one's own mixture (ratifiability's job) — are in v2 one hypothesis (recording) and one type decision (Definition 6). Under shared seed (Q12; P2:seeds SE-1(d) via FA-19′(i)) the live draw equals the simulation's, the manifold leaves E–J, and "EDT one-boxes" (FA-24′(c)). So Khan's negative result — reconciliation in Death in Damascus needs forced conditions on the dynamics — is, in v2's static frame, not a result about the fixed point (which exists and is unique by L2-13's mechanism) but about *reaching* it, which v2 does not model (Remark 5.4).

**L2-24 [derived] — Remark 3.6 is Skyrms's Ratifiability Lemma with recording in place of rationality-plus-tie-break knowledge.** Skyrms derives "she knows that she will choose an act that is ratifiable at $t_0$" from knowledge that she will maximize and knowledge of her tie-breaks; the tie-break clause is what overconstrains the Mean Demon. v2 derives $P_{s_d}(C(d))=1$ from strict calibration plus recording for a *deterministic* $C$, and $\nu(a\mid O_d)=C(d)(a)$ for a stochastic one (Proposition 7) — self-transparency of the *mixture*, never of the draw. That is precisely Skyrms's *weakened* assumption (no tie-break knowledge): the agent knows the distribution of her acts and that any act in its support maximizes (Definition 18's support condition), and the Mean Demon's $(2/3,1/3)$ is then the unique consistent state (L2-13) — Remark 4.3's phenomenon. Lewis's fn 8 objection ("hard to see how he can be in a position to deliberate") is met the same way by both: the deliberation is over the mixture, and the draw remains open.

**L2-25 [derived] — CA-13′'s two readings of the miniature are Jeffrey's $V$ versus Skyrms's Savage $U$.** P2:calibration CA-13′ (read) finds the strictly-calibrated-and-$T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$-approved set of Remark 4.3's miniature to be $\{\delta_a,\delta_b,(\tfrac23,\tfrac13)\}$ under Definition 17's domain $A_d^+$ — the pure points approved *vacuously* because the unplayed act is $P_s$-null — and the singleton $\{\tfrac23\}$ when the act values are read as tremble limits, "defined at every $q$". Skyrms's (R) evaluates competitors by Savage $U(A_j\mid A_i)$, which is total, and so finds no pure ratifiable act in the Mean Demon and the unique mixed self-prediction; Jeffrey's $V$ is undefined at the null competitor, which is the "nonsense" of `:198–203`. v2's causal evaluator is total "because $\mathrm{cf}$ is" (Definition 17), its evidential one is not; CA-13′'s repair — tremble-limit values — is what gives the evidential evaluator Savage's totality, and Remark 3.9's advice-stance route ("trembles induce a canonical *evidential* counterfactual structure, total, success-satisfying, defined at self-knowledge-nulled acts") is the same repair named as a candidate $\mathrm{cf}_s$ referent (Q6). So the literature's move from Jeffrey to Skyrms (import a total, state-partition-based evaluator to make ratifiability well defined) recurs inside v2 as the move from Definition 17's $\mathrm{EDT}$ to the tremble-limit evaluator. Side fact from P2:seeds SE-19 (read): the ratifiable $q=\tfrac23$ is not the $V$-optimum $q=\tfrac12$ ($V=3q(1-q)$) — Skyrms's "yes" theorem likewise delivers Savage-optimality *at the moment of truth*, not global optimality; the gap is Q4.

**L2-26 [derived] — existence: v2's Kakutani statement has the hypotheses the literature leaves implicit.** Skyrms TARK Prop I needs continuity of the adaptive rule and of the informational feedback; Harper's mixed escape needs the coin independent of the predictor [reconstructed]; Joyce's $P_e$ exists "if she updates in the right way" (Levinstein–Soares `:398`). **L2-27 [derived] — Q6 relocates Joyce's "ratification is causal" objection and P03's LLC to one parameter.** Joyce 2007 (per SEP `:340`) holds that EDT cannot borrow ratification because its motivation is causal; P03's LLC instructs the agent to *draw* predictors downstream so that ratifiability implies CDT=EDT. v2 has no graph, so it can neither define CDT nor legislate the graph; instead P2:faithful FA-19′ (read) finds that classical CDT's counterfactual — hold the prediction at its statistics, force the real draw — *is* tree-intrinsic under Definition 6 in the act-recording algebra (the simulation keeps drawing from $C$ while the real instance is forced), and FA-23′/FA-24′ show the whole family of intrinsic referents to be the family of self-location weightings (real-instance forcing = classical CDT; per-occurrence forcing = CDT+SIA, Theorem 1; deviation = EDT+SSA/UDT, Theorem 2). **L2-28 [derived] — Proposition 12 and Eells's relocation of the correlation.** Eells (via Briggs `:1341–1346`) held that the defence "is perfectly compatible with Susan's treating other people's smoking or not smoking as evidence about whether they have the lesion"; Jackson–Pargetter (via Oesterheld `:617–625`) turned this into an objection (EDT mis-assesses *others'* options). ## 4. Placement verdict

The mandate's question: is mixed-strategy ratifiability a known position under a new name, a genuine refinement, or a different claim? The honest answer is component-wise, because P05's package has five separable parts (L2-8) and v2's version changes the carrier (L2-21).

**L2-31 [derived] — the two objects of self-knowledge are known positions.** (a) *The implemented mixed act, known exactly, with randomization independent of the predictor* (P05 Case 1) is Harper 1986's position as every secondary source describes it (SEP `:344`; Weatherson `:124`; Skyrms `:459–464`, all read; Harper's text [reconstructed]), is Skyrms's fn 14 escape, and is what Weatherson defends in 2023; in game theory it is simply the Nash requirement that a player know her own mixed strategy. (b) *The agent's own action-probabilities, approximately known, with a deterministic rational map to the act* (P05 Case 3) is Skyrms 1990 §6's weakened-self-knowledge state — stated with the uniqueness of $(2/3,1/3)$ in the Mean Demon (L2-13, [checked]) — and Skyrms's TARK *state of indecision*, and Joyce 2012's $P_e$ as Levinstein–Soares report it. The author named Joyce and Skyrms himself (P03). Register: known, under other names ("self-ratifying mixed strategy", "state of indecision", "deliberational equilibrium", Rabinowicz's "stability").

**L2-32 [derived] — the use of the known mixture as the screening variable, giving CDT=EDT on the whole implementable class, is a genuine recombination not found stated in the sources read.** Eells's metatickle screens through beliefs and desires (or the evaluation record); Harper's mixed ratifiability secures existence for CDT; Pearl's Rule 2 gives Oesterheld's proof for known causes; Skyrms's Newcomb probabilization shows pure self-knowledge forces the theories to agree where both are defined. P05 combines them: make the known object the *mixture*, so that (i) the act stays uncertain (answering Skyrms 1980's self-reference objection and Lewis's fn 8 without appeal to logical uncertainty), (ii) the evidential contrast class is defined (the $\epsilon$-floor, L2-14), and (iii) screening-off by the mixture is exact and holds in instability problems too — Death in Damascus with an unpredictable coin, where Khan shows the metatickle *dynamics* reaches the reconciling point only under forced conditions but grants that the point is the uniquely ratifiable mixed act. Oesterheld's `:764–771` observation that metatickle reasoning "is the same [in] structure" as ratificationist EDT shows the recombination was in the air; Yudkowsky–Soares fn 11 ("methods for constructing $P^E$ that push $P^E(\text{onebox})$ to 0") is the same thought for pure acts. The theorem itself is elementary (L2-1: Pearl adjustment with all parents observed). Register: a *refinement* of the Eells–Skyrms line — new in the packaging, not in any single ingredient; coverage caveat: Harper 1986 and Eells 1984 unread, and Harper may already state (i)–(iii) for CDT alone.

**L2-33 [derived] — the exploration floor and the "same net" frame are known devices in new roles.** The floor is Selten's $\epsilon$-perfection / Skyrms TARK's positivity / Rabinowicz's Weak Thesis; its new role is to make Skyrms's (R) evidentially two-sided (L2-14), and its coherence status splits exactly as v2 splits problem-side from procedure-side trembles: raised *beliefs* are Dutch-bookable (Skyrms's "no" theorem), realized *trembles* are not (L2-14–15). The "one representation for both theories" frame is Skyrms's Jeffrey–Savage common framework and Briggs's triple; in v2 it is Remark 1.1 plus Definition 14, and in the author's 2026 vocabulary it is "calibration as a fairness condition" — which P2:identity ID-28 (read) grades as correct as a naming decision, wrong as decision-determination, incomplete as location.

**L2-34 [derived] — the Law of Logical Causality is a different claim, and its nearest relative is FDT's dependency hypothesis.** The LLC is a norm for *drawing* the graph (L2-6), not a screening or equilibrium result; it puts predictors of one's randomization downstream of the act. No tickle- or ratifiability-family text read here contains it; Joyce's evidential autonomy (SEP `:314`: the deliberating agent "need not proportion her beliefs about her own acts to the antecedent evidence") runs in the opposite direction (protecting act-credences from predictors, not subordinating predictors to acts). The closest published structure is Levinstein–Soares's FDT graph, in which the prediction is a subjunctive dependent of "MyAlgorithm(input)" (INDEX 1.10 §4; the §4 text not read here — [reconstructed] from the INDEX entry): the LLC makes the *act's own statistics* play the algorithm-node role. That is consistent with P03's admission that "something like updatelessness is required" and with the author's Troll-Bridge concession to Diffractor that the LLC gets "into trouble *because* it treats it as downstream". In v2 the LLC is the choice of the deviation referent for $\mathrm{cf}_s$, declined by default (Remark 3.11) and relocated by phase 2 to a self-location weighting (L2-27). Register: different claim; question-begging against classical CDT in the sense Remark 3.11 gives, and *not* a theorem of anything in v2 (Q-D of `sequence-map.md` §5.2 is answered "stipulation": what is a theorem is FA-20′'s collapse of the referents at recorded points, which needs no LLC).

**L2-35 [derived] — v2's version is a different claim from all of the above: a condition on problem descriptions whose fixed point is ratifiability.** The literature's ratifiability is a rationality condition on agents (Jeffrey, Egan's Maxim, Weatherson) or a consequence of the agent's self-knowledge (Skyrms's lemma); the author's 2026 statement that mixed-strategy ratifiability "follows trivially from the idea that the probabilities given in decision problems should be calibrated; ie, the agent needs to understand the decision problem it is in" (chat, his own words) is neither: it is an *honesty* condition on the pair (problem, procedure) — v2's Definitions 8–13, with Remark 3.6 deriving self-transparency from recording and Remark 3.7 reading ratifiability as the manifold's fixed point. Its precedents are formal (Skyrms's lemma: ratifiability *derived*, L2-18(c)) and rhetorical (Briggs's "poorly described … ruled impossible", Treutlein–Oesterheld's "incoherent"); its content — the manifold, procedure-relative grades, recording as the hypothesis, the optimality theorems — is not in the sources (L2-29). Its costs are equally clear (L2-30): no causal graph, no dynamics, no representation theorem, no diachronic coherence, no degrees of ratifiability. So: *mixed-strategy ratifiability in P05 is a known position (Harper/Skyrms) recombined with the metatickle into a refinement; in v2 it is a different claim — fairness-as-calibration — whose relation to the literature is that the literature's ratifiability is its fixed point.*
