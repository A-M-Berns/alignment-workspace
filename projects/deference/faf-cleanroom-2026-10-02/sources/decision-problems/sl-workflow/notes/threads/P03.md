### 1.2 Definitions, rules and arguments, in the post's notation

**(D1) The CEDT constraint, corrected.** P02's rule, re-set from the html2text line "achosen=argmaxaEP(.|A=achosen)(U|do(A=a))":
$$a_{\text{chosen}} \in \arg\max_{a}\ \mathbb E_{P(\,\cdot\,\mid A = a_{\text{chosen}})}\big[\,U \mid do(A=a)\,\big],$$
glossed "evidentially conditioning on the chosen action, we should still find that if we causally evaluate different actions, we make the same choice." The improvement: "I should have instead said that the chosen action must be *among* the maximal choices, so that tie-breaking behavior doesn't matter" — i.e. "=" becomes "∈". Purpose: "combine the advantages of CDT and EDT, since we make maximal use of the information revealed by our choice of action, but avoid confusing evidence with influence in doing so."

**(D2) Multiplicity and non-existence.** "This constraint may be satisfied by several actions; I referred to this as a challenge of 'choosing an equilibrium' by analogy to game-theoretic equilibria. I neglected to consider that it may also be satisfied by *no* actions."

**(A1) The self-hating smoke-lover; "CDT at a higher level".** P02 had worried that EV-selection among equilibria "might select equilibria where its actions hide some information". The post's witness: "In the suicidal smoking lesion problem, we might additionally stipulate that the smoke-lovers intrinsically prefer to be non-smoke-lovers, placing −1000 utility on smoke-loving. Then, an equilibrium which leaves a significant chance of not being a smoke-lover looks much better in expected value. So, this method of equilibrium selection would choose the equilibrium where the agent doesn't smoke (and CDT doesn't know enough to switch to smoking, since it looks possible that the agent could smoke and not die)." Diagnosis and rule: "we require CDT at a higher level. The error is in choosing equilibria as if the modification of information states actually modified our attributes; but, alas, the self-hating smoke-lover is a smoke-lover whether they smoke or not." Definition of the higher level: "Choosing equilibria via CDT just means selecting an action by CDT, out of those actions which could be equilibria." Regress noted: choosing "using all information (including an evidential update on which equilibrium is chosen)" brings the selection problem back — "But, this adds no information. Apparently there is no good answer to the equilibrium-selection problem, except that it is equivalent to the action-selection problem, which we solve by choosing from within some equilibrium."

**(I1) Identification with ratifiability.** "my 'CEDT' is just the ratifiability condition. Ratifiability was first introduced by Jeffrey in *The Logic of Decision*. Skyrms showed in *Ratifiability and the Logic of Decision* that a reasonable interpretation of ratifiability (perhaps the only reasonable interpretation) makes Jeffrey's EDT select actions which are CDT-optimal when CDT chooses via a probability distribution which has been updated on knowledge of which action is to be selected." Dynamics: "We might imagine, as Skyrms imagined, that the selection of equilibrium proceeds via some kind of convergent process which starts at a knowledge state which is ignorant of the final choice, and ends up in a knowledge state which is in equilibrium. Skyrms raises interesting issues of the Dutch-bookability of such a process."

**(A2) Absence of equilibria; mixed strategies.** "some decision problems … lack ratifiable choices. One example is matching pennies, in the case that we expect the opponent has some skill in predicting us. If we update on taking either action, CDT then wishes to take the opposite one." Scope disclaimer: "I don't mean for my opinion on the EDT vs CDT debate to solve all problems correctly; I still think something like updatelessness is required." Rule: "Rather than choosing pure actions, we choose mixed strategies, and then pick randomly with the equilibrium probabilities. This guarantees the existence of equilibria, as usual in game theory, since the best-response function is a Kakutani function of the probabilities. This gives the expected solution, choosing randomly with 50-50 odds, in matching pennies." Anticipated objection ("aren't we supposed to evidentially condition on the *actual output of our decision process*, ie, the action we take?") answered by Joyce, *Regret and Instability in Causal Decision Theory*: Joyce "argues in favor of mixed equilibria like this, partly on the basis of making a distinction between what CDT requires you to *believe* vs what CDT requires you to *do*. (He favors a view in which CDT was always intended to yield equilibria like this, and anything else just isn't CDT.)"

**(D3) Decision = formation of the epistemic state.** "The 'decision', as such, is the formation of the epistemic state about the action; it is this which is required to be in equilibrium. The *action* occurs after the decision, and may be anything which is consistent with the decision." Motivation: "the stronger version requiring full knowledge of actions would be inconsistent, since ratifiable actions don't always exist."

**(A3) The strong tickle defense.** Put "the same self-knowledge condition on EDT. Suppose we are using a version of EDT which epsilon-explores. However, we impose the requirement that EDT's prior knows what policy EDT selects — IE, it knows what it will do up to the randomness involved in exploration. EDT can still condition on alternate actions, thanks to the epsilon probability remaining for alternate actions. However, knowing its own policy in this way will remove most of the correlations which allow it to make EDT-like decisions. It won't cooperate with a copy of itself in Prisoner's Dilemma if the copy uses different random bits; it knows the policy which the copy will use, and all remaining wiggle room is in randomness, so it no longer sees its action as correlated with the other player's. It will two-box in a version of Newcomb's problem where Omega can't predict the random bits." Mechanism: "we've conditioned on the 'output of my decision algorithm' node in the causal graph, which was the hidden parent making CDT different from EDT." Historical remark: "Jeffrey *originally* proposed ratifiability as a corrective measure for making *EDT* act like *CDT* in the cases where they disagreed."

**(A4) Where the collapse fails.** "If your exploration bits can be predicted, then EDT and CDT still advise different actions. The 'hard mode' of matching pennies is Death in Damascus: Death can predict your every move, so exploration or no, you meet your demise. Under the ratifiable equilibrium, CDT still proposes to flee half the time (as if frantically randomizing in the hope that Death will fail). EDT doesn't do this. In Newcomb with a perfect predictor, CDT two-boxes and EDT one-boxes. … (Note that we still need to make EDT epsilon-explore in these cases, in order to keep the EDT conditional expected utilities well-defined.)"

**(D4) Law of Logical Causality (LLC).** "If conditioning on any event changes the probability an agent assigns to its own action, that event **must** be treated as causally downstream." Qualification: "This is still a bit rough, but constraints on the structure of counterfactuals are hard to come by, so I'll take what I can get." Consequences: "you must treat Death as causally downstream of your decision in Death in Damascus. You must treat Omega as causally downstream of you in the perfect-predictor version of Newcomb." Limit: "It does *not* mean that you must treat *all* things correlated with your decision as downstream. In Smoking Lesion Steelman, the lesion is still upstream of your decision. The Law of Logical Causality does not touch it, because an agent obeying ratifiability will know its own decision perfectly, screening off the action from such influences. But, this depends on my special version of Smoking Lesion. Perhaps in a different version of Smoking Lesion, where the increased tendency to smoke is more like a trembling hand after the decision is made, the influence is downstream rather than upstream."

**(T) The title's thesis.** "Ratifiability, the very tool which CDT advocates used to fend off a series of counterexamples (including cases like Murder Lesion and the suicidal smoking lesion), can be taken by EDT advocates and turned into a very strong version of the tickle defense. This strong version of the tickle defense makes EDT act as CDT advises in all but the most extreme cases, where predictors are perfect. From this, we propose a constraint on the causal graphs which would make CDT do as EDT would advise." (Egan's lesion case is named here only by name; its structure — the act is evidence of a disposition that makes the act's outcome bad — is all the audit needs.)

### 1.3 Stated assumptions

(i) ε-exploration keeps evidential conditionals defined and is retained even in the perfect-predictor cases; (ii) the agent knows its own policy "up to the randomness involved in exploration" (mixed strategy known, draw unknown); (iii) randomization is implementable and, in the "can't predict the random bits" cases, independent of predictors/copies — the post's PD and Newcomb variants are stated with that independence as their defining feature; (iv) equilibrium selection is a convergent Skyrms-style deliberation; (v) a causal-graph representation exists in which "hidden parent", "upstream/downstream" make sense — the LLC is a constraint on drawing that graph; (vi) "something like updatelessness is required" for the remaining problems (XOR blackmail, transparent Newcomb) — the post does not claim ratifiability solves them.

## 5. Smoking Lesion, the tickle defense, and mixed-strategy ratifiability — what this post contributes

**Smoking Lesion.** The post's specific contribution is the *trembling-hand lesion*: the observation that the classic problem's correlation can be made consistent with self-knowledge only by relocating the lesion's influence *after* the decision. The self-hating smoke-lover does not survive calibration (P03-3).

**The tickle defense.** The post's thesis — self-knowledge of one's policy up to randomization is a *strong* tickle defense that makes evidential choice agree with causal choice except against perfect predictors — is confirmed in mechanism (CA-12′'s cancellation; Lemma 3; FA-20′) and corrected in scope: under Definition 6 the agreement is total at act-recording points, perfect predictors of the *label* included, and fails only where the predictor covaries with the *draw* (shared seed) or where recording fails (selection; self-succession). Egan's parity objection (the defense "does too much") is the post's title made positive, and Joyce's reply (ratification is causal) is answered in v2 by FA-20′: nothing causal is borrowed at recorded points. The post's own historical framing (Jeffrey → EDT-as-CDT; Skyrms's "only reasonable interpretation") is faithful to Skyrms 1990 (P03-2, P03-19), which the post links and which the corpus now has.

**Mixed-strategy ratifiability.** The post introduces it into the sequence (P02 had pure CEDT), gives the Kakutani reason and the matching-pennies example, and — via Joyce — the believe/do split. v2's forms: strict calibration at the label plus Definition 18's tie-freedom (Remark 4.3; C2's MSR), Kakutani existence with named hypotheses (SE-18′), the miniature's $\tfrac23$ (SE-19), the test-sequence limit (DY-3), and the correspondence retyping (Q12/SE-18′) as the formal believe/do split.
