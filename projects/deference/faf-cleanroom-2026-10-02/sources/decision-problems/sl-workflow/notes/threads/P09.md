### 1.2 Thesis and construction (the post's own words)

Premise: "Suppose a CDT agent has causal expectations which differ from its evidential expectations, in a specific decision."

Step 1 (the bet, *during* the act): "We can modify the decision by allowing an agent to bet on outcomes in the same act. Because the bet is made simultaneously with the decision, the CDT agent uses causal expected value, and will bet accordingly."

Step 2 (the sell-back, *after* the act): "Then, immediately after (before any new observations come in), we offer a new bet about the outcome. The agent will now bet based on its evidential expectations, since the causal intervention has already been made."

Example (Death in Damascus, linked to the Levinstein–Soares paper): "A CDT agent will take each action with 50% probability, and its causal expectations expect to escape death with 50% probability. We can expand the set of possible actions from (stay, run) to (stay, run, stay and make side bet, run and make side bet). The side bet could cost 1 util and pay out 3 utils if the agent doesn't die. Then, immediately after taking the action but before anything else happens, we offer another deal: the agent can get .5 util in exchange for -3 util conditional on not dying. We offer the new bet regardless of whether the agent agrees to the first bet."

Acceptances: "The CDT agent will happily make the bet, since the expected utility is calculated along with the intervention. Then, it will happily sell the bet back, because after taking its action, it sees no chance of the 3 util payout."

The "regardless" device and the anticipated reversal: "The CDT agent makes the initial bet even though it knows it will later reverse the transaction at a cost to itself, because we offer the second transaction whether the agent agrees to the first or not. So, from the perspective of the initial decision, taking the bet is still +.5 expected utils. If it could stop itself from later taking the reverse bet, that would be even better, but we suppose that it can't."

Conclusion: "I conclude from this that CDT should equal EDT (hence, causality must account for logical correlations, IE include logical causality). By 'CDT' I really mean any approach at all to counterfactual reasoning; counterfactual expectations should equal evidential expectations."

Scope and the scaling claim: "As with most of my CDT=EDT arguments, this only provides an argument that the expectations should be equal for actions taken with nonzero probability. In fact, the amount lost to Dutch Book will be proportional to the probability of the action in question. So, differing counterfactual and evidential expectations are smoothly more and more tenable as actions become less and less probable. Actions with very low probability will imply negligible monetary loss. Still, in terms of classical Dutch-Book-ability, CDT is Dutch-Bookable."

Closing doubt: "Both CDT and EDT have dynamic inconsistencies, but only CDT may be Dutch-booked in this way. I'm not sure how persuasive this should be as an argument -- how special a status should Dutch-book arguments have?"

**Arithmetic of the example as stated** [checked, by hand and `P09-scratch/p09_did.py` §A below]: at the live decision the CDT agent's causal survival probability is $\tfrac12$, so the bundled bet is worth $3\cdot\tfrac12 - 1 = +\tfrac12$; after acting it takes the reverse bet at evidential survival probability $0$, worth $\tfrac12 - 3\cdot 0 = +\tfrac12$; the two bets together pay $-1 + \tfrac12 + 3[\text{survive}] - 3[\text{survive}] = -\tfrac12$ in **every** world — a sure loss of $\tfrac12$ relative to declining both, and the $3$-util legs cancel identically. The number depends on nothing but the two prices; the *acceptances* depend on the two survival probabilities ($\tfrac12$ causal, $0$ evidential), and that is where every assumption of the post lives.

### 1.3 Stated and unstated assumptions

(A1) A decision with a positive-probability act at which causal and evidential expectations differ — for Death in Damascus, that the agent mixes $\tfrac12$–$\tfrac12$ and Death predicts perfectly (the agent's evidential survival probability after acting is $0$; its causal one is $\tfrac12$). Unstated: the perfect predictor predicts the *randomization* — Levinstein & Soares's stipulation, `references/levinstein-soares-2020-cheating-death-in-damascus.txt:430–437` (read): "we assume any mental randomization she performs is still predictable from death's perspective. If it weren't, then death could not be a perfect or nearly perfect predictor, which is the entire motivation for the original case … We'll call such a coin pseudo-random." In v2 this is the shared-seed run semantics (Q12), not Definition 6 — §3 below.
(A2) The bets do not change the decision: enlarging (stay, run) to four actions leaves the $\tfrac12$–$\tfrac12$ mixture in place. Stated only implicitly; Diffractor's comment attacks exactly this (§1.5).
(A3) The reverse offer is made "regardless of whether the agent agrees to the first bet", and the agent "can't" stop itself from taking it — the device that defeats the sophisticated-choice evasion (the agent who foresees the sell-back and therefore declines the first bet). P12 dropped this clause and P12-3 (read, `notes/threads/P12.md`) shows it then binds only a myopic bettor.
(A4) "before any new observations come in": the second point carries no information the first lacked except the act itself.
(A5) The second-stage evaluation is *evidential* — but the agent is a CDT agent throughout; the post's gloss is that "the causal intervention has already been made". In v2 terms (§3): one procedure, $\mathrm{cf}$ consulted on a different event at the second point (P12-4's finding, confirmed here for P09's ordering).

### 1.4 What P09 is *not* (contrast with P12, for the record)

P09's order is **bet during (causally priced), sell after (evidentially priced)**; P12's order is **buy before (evidentially priced), sell during (causally priced)**, with the bet's payout $(\mathrm{cdt}(a) - U)\cdot s$ designed to be causally *fair* at $a$ so that the base ranking is untouched (P12 §1.2–1.3, read). P09's bet has strictly positive causal value at *every* base act ($+\tfrac12$), so it changes the causal ranking unless attached symmetrically to all acts — which is why P09 attaches it to both stay and run. The sequence-map's glossary entry "bet before (conditional valuation), reverse during (causal valuation)" describes P12, not P09 [checked against both texts].

### 2.2 The two-stage structure as a v2 tree (the mandate's first post-specific question)

**Tree $B_{\mathrm{P09}}$** (`p09_did.py:build`). Worlds are atoms of the algebra generated by $\mathrm{act} \in \{\mathrm{S}, \mathrm{Sb}, \mathrm{R}, \mathrm{Rb}\}$, $\mathrm{death} \in \{\mathrm{S}, \mathrm{R}\}$, $\mathrm{sell} \in \{0, 1\}$. Root: Death's prediction — a hypothetical node carrying the live point $d_1$, routing on the *base* component of its draw (Sb and S both send the run to the death = S subtree). Then the live $d_1$-node. Then, on every branch, a post-act point $d_2^{a} = (s, \{\mathrm{act} = a\}, \{\mathrm{sell}{=}1, \mathrm{sell}{=}0\})$ — four distinct points, one per act, because the agent knows what it did and whether it bought. Leaf payoff $r = U\,[\mathrm{survive}] - k\,[\mathrm{bet}] + K\,[\mathrm{bet}][\mathrm{survive}] + k'\,[\mathrm{sell}] - K\,[\mathrm{sell}][\mathrm{survive}]$ with the post's $k = 1$, $K = 3$, $k' = \tfrac12$ ($U = 10$ throughout; $U$ cancels from every book quantity).

**Is it "one point with an enlarged action set followed by a second point"?** Yes, with two qualifications. (i) The first point is *one* point with four actions — v2 has no "bet dimension"; the bundle is the act, and Death is stipulated to read only its base component (that is what makes the four-action point a conservative extension of the two-action one — P09-6 below). (ii) The "second point" is four points, indexed by the act observed; its observation event is the act, so its strictly calibrated state is $\nu(\cdot \mid \mathrm{act} = a)$ — the "evidential expectation after the intervention" is literally clause 1 of Definition 8 at a post-act point.

**States and calibration.** $d_1$ has $O = \top$, so strict calibration makes $P_{s_{d_1}} = \nu$: the state knows the mixture ($P(\mathrm{act} = \mathrm{Sb}) = \tfrac12$) — Proposition 7's self-transparency — but not the draw. $B$ does **not** record at $d_1$ (`records(d1) = False`): the simulation node carrying $d_1$ lies on every run, exactly as for opaque Newcomb and Remark 4.3's miniature (P12's Toolkit-issues note (ii)). $B$ records at every reached $d_2^a$ (`records(d2_Sb) = True`): one node per run, subtree-veridical (all leaves below carry $\mathrm{act} = a$), action-veridical in the sell coordinate. So the post-act point is the *only* recorded point of the book, and strict calibration there forces certainty of the sell decision (Remark 3.6) — harmless for CDT, which is total, and repaired for EDT by the local mask (Definition 9; the toolkit's masked state with self-model $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ at $d_2^a$ gives both acts positive probability and leaves $\nu(\mathrm{survive} \mid \mathrm{act} = a)$ untouched, Lemma 3 within the observation).

**The cf's.** At $d_1$ two counterfactual structures are compared: *classical* (Death's location held at $P_{s_{d_1}}(\mathrm{death})$, sell held at its marginal) and *deviation* (Death tracks the supposed act — the "predictors are downstream" opinion). At $d_2^a$ the only sensible cf holds $(\mathrm{act}, \mathrm{death})$ fixed, and then $\mathrm{cdt} = \mathrm{edt}$ there identically.

### 3.1 The example and its mechanism

**P09-1 [checked; `p09_did.out` §A, both semantics, and §B–C] — the sure loss is a leaf-wise identity, and it is the whole of the "Dutch book" part. CONFIRMED.** On $B_{\mathrm{P09}}$, for the base label $\tfrac12$: $V_B(\text{bet, sell}) - V_B(\text{no bet, keep}) = -(k - k') = -\tfrac12$, and the per-leaf differences over reached leaves are all exactly $-\tfrac12$ — under shared seed ($-\tfrac12$ vs $0$) and under Definition 6 ($\tfrac92$ vs $5$), for the survival bet and for the mirror death bet, for any $U$ and any $\mathrm{cf}$. The $K$-legs cancel world by world. *Register:* this needs no calibration sense, no referent and no run semantics; it is a statement about two payoff functions on one tree (Remark 3.5). Everything contestable is in the *acceptances* (P09-2).

**P09-2 [checked; `p09_did.out` §A] — P09's Death-in-Damascus book needs the shared-seed run semantics; under Definition 6 there is no discrepancy and no book. REFINED (the post's silent premise named).** Shared seed: the strictly calibrated state at $d_1$ has $P(\mathrm{survive} \mid \mathrm{act} = \mathrm{Sb}) = 0$ while the classical cf holds Death at the marginal and gives survival $\tfrac12$ at every act; $\mathrm{CDT}(d_1)$ values $(\mathrm{S}, \mathrm{Sb}, \mathrm{R}, \mathrm{Rb}) = (4, \tfrac92, 4, \tfrac92)$ → buys the bet with a uniform tie over $\{\mathrm{Sb}, \mathrm{Rb}\}$ = the label (a fixed point); at $d_2^{\mathrm{Sb}}$ the calibrated survival probability is $0$ and both evaluators sell ($-\tfrac12$ vs $-1$). So the book procedure is exactly what $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$ approves at both points, and $V_B = -\tfrac12$ against $0$ for decline-both and $+\tfrac12$ for the evidential procedure (declines the bet, takes the reverse bet — under a perfect predictor the "regardless" reverse bet is a gift of $k'$ to anyone who did not buy). Definition 6: Death's draw is an independent sample of the label, $P(\mathrm{survive} \mid \mathrm{Sb}) = \tfrac12$ = the cf; EDT (masked) and CDT both buy ($\tfrac92 > 4$) and both keep at $d_2$ ($\tfrac{11}{2} > \tfrac92$); $V_B = \tfrac{11}{2}$ for both; no book. The mandate asked whether "a Definition-17 CDT procedure whose $\mathrm{cf}_s$ disagrees with conditioning at a positive-probability act loses value relative to the EDT procedure, and by how much": under shared seed, **yes — $k - k' = \tfrac12$ per run surely relative to decline-both, and $k = 1$ per run relative to the EDT procedure**, at every reached leaf; under Definition 6 the classical cf *does not disagree* with conditioning (P2:faithful FA-19′, C2-15(i)), so the hypothesis is not met and the loss is $0$. This is P12-9 recomputed in P09's own ordering and agrees with it. The academic source of the premise is Levinstein–Soares's pseudo-random coin (`…txt:430–437`, read); in v2 that stipulation *is* Q12's shared seed, and Remark 3.2's "differs only for stochastic procedures" is, for Death in Damascus, the whole difference.

**P09-3 [checked at every $d_2$; derived in general] — the "evidential" second stage is $\mathrm{CDT}$ at a post-act point; one procedure, two supposed events; and P09's "regardless" clause is load-bearing. CONFIRMED (P12-4 transposed) and REFINED.** At each $d_2^a$ the toolkit's `cdt_values` (cf holding act and Death fixed) and `edt_values` coincide identically, because the reverse bet is a chance-free settlement independent of everything upstream (Lemma 3's independence at the sell node). So the post's "the agent will now bet based on its evidential expectations, since the causal intervention has already been made" is not a switch of evaluator: the causal evaluator, supposing "sell", moves nothing that bears on survival, and its supposed value *is* the conditional. The book is therefore a coherence condition across two cf-slots of one procedure (the act slot at $d_1$ and the sell slot at $d_2$) — P12 §2.3's diagnosis holds for P09's ordering too. The "regardless" clause does two jobs: (i) it makes the reverse bet a *sunk anticipated event* at $d_1$, so the bet's causal value $Kc_a - k = +\tfrac12$ is undiminished by the foreseen sell-back (the post: "from the perspective of the initial decision, taking the bet is still +.5") — this is what defeats the sophisticated-choice evasion that P12-3 shows defeats the revised argument as written, and that Oesterheld–Conitzer invoke (`…txt:126–131`, read: "a CDT buyer may then anticipate eventually undoing his choice and therefore not buy a box in the first place [Ahmed, 2014, Section 3.2]"); (ii) under a perfect predictor it costs the bookie $k'$ on every non-bet branch, which is why the symmetric construction (bets on both acts) is the one P09 uses — P09-6.

Bundle with $a$ a bet costing $k$ and paying $K$ on $X$, and at a post-act point $d_2^a$ offer regardless the reverse bet ($+k'$, $-K$ on $X$), with $K e_a < k' < k < K c_a$ (sign case $c_a > e_a$; mirror otherwise). Then $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$ approves buying at $d_1$ (the bundled leg adds $Kc_a - k > 0$ under the supposition of $a$; the anticipated reverse leg is a constant) and selling at $d_2^a$ (by every success-obeying cf, P09-A), and the pair is dominated by decline-both by $k - k'$ on every $a$-branch and equal to it elsewhere — a weak Dutch book with expected loss $C(d_1)(a)\,(k - k')$; it is a strict Dutch book iff the bet is attached to every supported act. *Argument for importing:* it is the form in which the book needs **no** rule for $\mathrm{cf}$ on non-act events (P12's Import 3 is not needed: the two supposed events are an act and a chance-free settlement), so it isolates the coherence charge from the bet-locality question. *Cost:* it presupposes a conservative extension (Import P09-B) — the label after bundling must still support $a$ — and, like P12's, its target is run-semantics-relative (P09-5). ## 5. Smoking Lesion, the tickle defence, and mixed-strategy ratifiability

**Smoking Lesion.** P09 does not mention it. What its structure contributes: the post-act point is the temporal mirror of the tickle defence. Eells's defence screens off the common cause through *pre-act* self-knowledge of inclination (Remark 3.9's "resource"); P09's second point screens off through *post-act* knowledge of the act. In v2 both are Lemma 3 at a calibrated point: under (S1)+(S3) $m \perp (\ell, k)$ under every $\nu_{B,C}$, so at a post-act point $\{m = 1\}$ the calibrated state has $P(k) = P(k \mid m{=}1) = \nu(k)$ and no bet on cancer conditional on smoking is mispriced — the lesion smoker's book of P12-8 (Lewis-CDT with the stipulated (S2) state, $\Delta = \tfrac38$; read, not recomputed) exists only *holding (S2)*, which Proposition 11 says is no realizable worldview, and a during/after book at a calibrated post-act point has nothing to bite. So P09's ordering gives the Smoking-Lesion row of C1/L1's grid a second entry beside P12-8's: "during/after book against the calibrated smoker: none, in every instantiation, by Lemma 3 at the post-act point" [derived]. Handoff C1/L1.

**The tickle defence.** P09's Death in Damascus is the case the tickle defence *cannot* handle, and v2 says why: the defence needs the agent's self-knowledge to exhaust what the predictor knows; under shared seed Death knows the *draw* while the calibrated agent knows only the *mixture* (Proposition 7) — P12's thesis (4), "Something can't correlate with your action unless it contains information you don't have about your action", with Death holding exactly that information (the seed). Under Definition 6 Death knows nothing the label does not contain, the tickle defence is total (P12-13/17), and P09's book vanishes (P09-2). So P09 is the tickle defence's failure condition made pragmatic: *where the predictor has the seed, self-knowledge of the mixture does not screen it off, the classical cf disagrees with the calibrated conditional, and a book prices the gap.* Lewis's worry that a deliberating agent cannot be certain what it will do (`references/INDEX.md` §2.2's transcription of `lewis-1981-causal-decision-theory.txt:312–319` and `lewis-1981-why-aincha-rich.txt:157–166`, read there; not re-read here) is answered at P09's first point exactly as P12 §5 says — mixture self-knowledge without draw knowledge — and dissolved at P09's second point, where certainty about the act is post-act and legitimate.

**Mixed-strategy ratifiability.** P09's "50% each" is an MSR fixed point (P09-4), and the post is a *counterexample* to the slogan "mixed-strategy ratifiability implies CDT = EDT" (P05's title) in the absence of P05's implementability assumption: at the MSR label under shared seed the classical cf and the calibrated conditional differ ($c = \tfrac12$, $e = 0$) and CDT is booked; under Definition 6 — the type-level form of implementability (sequence-map A3: "randomization controls for everything else") — the same label has $c = e$ and nothing is booked. So P09 is P05's Theorem 1 read contrapositively: MSR alone gives the tie across the support (Remark 4.3); MSR *plus* independent randomization (Definition 6) gives $\mathrm{cdt} = \mathrm{edt}$ at supported acts (C2's Theorem C2-A at F3′ points, read at `C2.md:171–186`; P12-6 on the miniature); MSR *with* a seed-reading predictor gives a ratifiable mixture at which the two act values differ and the Dutch book is the price of the difference. That is the "Dutch-book connection" C2's mandate names, in P09's own instance: the book is what enforces $\mathrm{cdt} = \mathrm{edt}$ where implementability fails to deliver it for free. The author's clarification to ESRogs ("equal causal expectations for any action taken with nonzero probability") is MSR's indifference signature and should be cited as the sequence's first statement of it in the equilibrium (rather than Bayes-net) idiom. Handoff C2.
