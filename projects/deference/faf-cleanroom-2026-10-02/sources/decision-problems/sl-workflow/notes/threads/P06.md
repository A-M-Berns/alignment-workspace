### 1.2 The post's argument, in its own terms

Programme restated (paragraphs 2–4, verbatim where load-bearing):

(i) Motive: "Counterfactual reasoning is something we don't understand very well, and which has so many free parameters that it seems to explain just about any solution to a decision problem which one might want to get based on intuition. So, it would be nice to eliminate it from our ontology – to reduce the cases in which it truly captures something important to machinery which we understand, and write off the other cases as 'counterfactual-of-the-gaps' in need of some other solution than counterfactuals."

(ii) Mechanism (the P03–P05 result, as the post summarizes it): "in many cases, EDT learns to act like CDT because its knowledge of its own typical behavior screens off the action from the correlations which are generally thought to make EDT cooperate in one-shot prisoner's dilemma with similar agents, one-box in Newcomb's problem, and so on. This is essentially a version of the tickle defense." Plus the symmetric point: "the same kind of self-knowledge constraint is needed to deal with some counterexamples to CDT; so, CDT can't be justified as a way of dealing with cases of failure of self-knowledge in general. Instead, CDT seems to improve the situation in some cases of self-knowledge failure, while EDT does better in other such cases."

(iii) Normative reading and the fairness clause: "a view in which the self-knowledge constraint is a rationality constraint, so the tickle defense is thought of as being true for rational agents, and CDT=EDT under these conditions of rationality. I suggested that problems for which this was not true had to somehow violate the ability of the agent to perform experiments in the world; IE, the decision problem would have to be set up in such a way as to prevent the agent from decorrelating its actions from things in the environment which are not causally downstream of its actions. This seems in some sense unfair, as the environment is preventing the agent from correctly learning the causal relationships through experimentation. I called this condition the law of logical causality when it first occurred to me, and mixed-strategy implementability in the setup where I proved conditions for CDT=EDT."

The retreat (paragraph 5): "In XOR Blackmail with a perfect predictor, however, mixed-strategy implementability is violated in a way which does not intuitively seem unfair. As a result, knowledge of what sort of thing you do in XOR blackmail is not sufficient to decorrelate your actions from things which you have no control over. Constraining to the epsilon-exploration case, so that conditional probabilities are well-defined, it seems like what happens is that the epsilon-exploration bit correlates the action you take with the disaster (thanks to the XOR which determines if the letter is sent). On the other hand, it seems as if CDT should be able to get the right answer."

The representation problem (paragraph 6): "I'm unable to come up with a causal Bayes net which seems to faithfully represent the problem, so that I can properly compare how CDT and EDT reason about it in the same representation. It seems like the letter has to be both a parent and a child of the action. I thought I could represent things properly by having a copy of the action node, representing the simulation of the agent which the predictor uses to predict; but, I don't see how to represent the perfect correlation between the copy and the real action without effectively severing the other parents of the real action."

The Edit (the post's only formal object): "I was confused by the fact that CDT can't reason as if its action makes the letter not get sent. The following causal graph works well enough." Variables $A$ (action; true iff money sent), $A'$ ("a copy of the action, representing the abstract mathematical fact of what the agent does if it sees the letter"), $L$ (letter sent), $D$ (rare disaster), $U$ (utility). Structural equations: $A = A'$ if $L$, else false; $A'$ exogenous; $L = \mathrm{XOR}(A', D)$; $D$ exogenous; $U = U(A, D)$ "as stated in the original XOR post". Assumption: "epsilon-exploration to ensure that the conditional probabilities are well-defined." Verdicts: "Even if EDT knows its own policy, it sees itself as having control over the disaster. CDT, on the other hand, sees no such connection, so it refuses to send the money."

### 1.3 The problem the post assumes (the "original XOR post")

The post does not restate XOR Blackmail. The canonical statement is Soares–Fallenstein's (2015, *Toward Idealized Decision Theory*) and Levinstein–Soares's (*Cheating Death in Damascus*): a rumor of a termite infestation (loss $X$, canonically \$1,000,000, prior $\delta$, canonically 1%); a truthful predictor who knows the truth sends a letter iff exactly one of {(i) no termites and you will pay on receipt; (ii) termites and you will not pay} holds, the payment being $c$ (canonically \$1,000); you receive the letter. In the post's variables: $L = \mathrm{XOR}(A', D)$ with $A' = 1$ iff "you pay on receiving the letter", $D = 1$ iff termites. I did not have the Soares–Fallenstein text in front of me; the statement above is [reconstructed] and cross-checked against the references index entry for Levinstein–Soares (see §11 Reading ledger; L1 owns the formulation literature).

### 3.2 P06-4 — the representation problem and the Edit's graph [derived]

**P06-4 [derived] — CONFIRMED as a diagnosis, and the Edit's graph is a Definition 6′ tree drawn as a DAG.** "The letter has to be both a parent and a child of the action" is, in v2, the statement that the fiber of $d_L$ is nested: the letter is downstream of the *hypothetical* instance and upstream of the *real* instance of one point. A DAG has one node per variable; the tree has one *point* at two *nodes* (Remark 2.2: a procedure is a function of points, so the two instances answer from one label). The Edit's repair — $A'$ exogenous, $A = A'$ on letter-runs — is a structural-equation model with **no noise term on $A$ given $(A', L)$**; with "epsilon-exploration" placed at $A'$ (the only place the graph leaves for it), $A$ copies the explored answer: this is Definition 6′ (one draw per point per run, reused at the real node), and P06-1's shared-seed column is the graph's verdict table. The graph *cannot* express Definition 6 without a second noise node feeding $A$ — and with one, $A$'s parents given the policy are noise only, implementability holds, and evidential conditioning at $A$ refuses (the tickle defence works). So the sentence "I don't see how to represent the perfect correlation between the copy and the real action without effectively severing the other parents of the real action" is exactly right and is the choice point: representing the perfect correlation *is* severing (the real action's only parent becomes the copy — Definition 6′), and the alternative (fresh noise — Definition 6) is the one in which the post's thesis fails. The post's $do(A)$ — sever $A$ from $A'$ and $L$ — is single-instance forcing at the real node ($G_q$, §8), which under Definition 6 is tree-intrinsic in the act-recording algebra (P2:faithful FA-19′; the XOR tree is F3′ veridically act-recording at $d_L$: the real node is node-action-veridical and subtree-veridical, the hypothetical is neither) and under 6′ is "definable but not tree-intrinsic" (P2:seeds SE-1(d)) — the formal content of "CDT can't reason as if its action makes the letter not get sent".

Levinstein–Soares's own CDT graph for XOR Blackmail (READ, `levinstein-soares-2020-…txt:262–272`, Figure 1: "Predictor is either send or do not send depending on whether there's an infestation and on the agent's predisposition") and the FDT paper's (READ, `yudkowsky-soares-2017-fdt.txt:1440–1460`: Infestation → Predictor ← [predisposition]; Predictor → Obs → Act) have the same shape as the Edit's: a predisposition node parenting both the prediction and the act. **The tickle defence.** P06 is the post where the sequence discovers the defence's scope boundary, and v2 relocates that boundary from the *problem* to the *semantics*: under Definition 6 the defence is a theorem on XOR Blackmail too (Lemma 3; the calibrated act-conditionals agree, P06-1) — P05-A1's "recording theorem" needs only F3′ act-recording here, not Definition 7 — while under 6′ it fails exactly because there is no residual variation in the act beyond the shared seed to be screened off (the post's "knowledge of what sort of thing you do … is not sufficient to decorrelate your actions"). For C2's theorem (C2-A/C2-B, read at `C2.md:27–31`) this means the Definition 6 hypothesis is not removable: under 6′ the strictly calibrated-and-$T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$-approved deterministic set is $\{\mathrm{pay}, \mathrm{refuse}\}$ and the masked/limit/tremble-consistent set is $\{\mathrm{pay}\}$, while every deviation-based evaluator gives $\{\mathrm{refuse}\}$ and forcing is not intrinsic — C2's failure clause "Without Definition 6 … the sets differ" is confirmed on this witness with the sets displayed.
