## 1. Faithful reconstruction

Source: `posts/02-smoking-lesion-steelman-ii.md` (80 lines; LessWrong post of 2017-10-02, karma 2, one comment), read in full. Quotations are verbatim from the html2text fetch; the one formula is mangled there and is re-set below with my reading stated.

### 1.1 Framing and thesis

Opening: "After Johannes Treutlein's comment on Smoking Lesion Steelman, and a number of other considerations, I had almost entirely given up on CDT. However, there were still nagging questions about whether the kind of self-ignorance needed in Smoking Lesion Steelman could arise naturally, how it should be dealt with if so, and what role counterfactuals ought to play in decision theory if CDT-like behavior is incorrect." The post "arrived at a place much closer to CDT than I expected."

**Thesis sentence (T).** "CDT differs from EDT only in those cases where a parent of the decision node is not directly observed. If the causal parents of a decision are all observations, then there is no probabilistic relationship for causal reasoning to remove." Two questions are derived: "1. (Existence of cases where CDT ≠ EDT) Are there necessarily parents of the decision which cannot be observed? 2. (Correctness of CDT) If so, is CDT the correct way to account for this?"

### 1.2 Existence of cases (question 1)

- The steelman of P01 "was very contrived; I had to specifically rob the agents of all introspection and set up an important piece of knowledge about their own decision process which they lacked."
- **Cosmic ray.** "it is always possible for a cosmic ray to hit a computer and change the output of decision calculation. However, CDT doesn't seem to help with this. The right way to guard against this is vie fault-tolerant implementations …; but to the extent that a decision procedure might guard against it by having checks in the world-model saying 'if things look too goofy I've probably been hit in the head', CDT would potentially sever those probabilistic links, keeping the agent from inferring that it is in an error state."
- **Consistency of the agent's logic** (attributed by the post to Sam Eisenstat, "in an in-person discussion"): in "the counterexample to the trolljecture, an agent reasons as if if had control over the consistency of its logic, leading to an intuitively wrong decision. Perhaps consistency should be thought of as a parent of the decision, so that CDT-like reasoning would avoid the mistake." A probabilistic version exists but "is not consistent with logical induction, so I'm *still* not sure whether this situation can arise when an agent is build with best practices; but, I can't entirely dismiss it right now, either." The intuition endorsed: "my action cannot possibly *influence* whether my logic is consistent, even if it provides *evidence* as to whether my logic is consistent". (This is the seed of P11; it is outside v2's type — v2 has no provability — and is handed to P11/L3.)

### 1.3 Correctness of CDT (question 2)

- Johannes Treutlein's Suicidal Smoking Lesion "helped convince me that CDT isn't doing exactly the right thing to account for inputs to the decision process which can't be observed." Tom Everitt (a discussion, as reported by the post) "pointed out that CDT fails to make use of all available information when planning."
- **The football-mug fan.** "suppose that an absent-minded football fan always forgets which team is their favorite. They know that *if* they buy sports memorabilia, they almost always buy their favorite team's gear on impulse. They also know that if they purchased the wrong team's gear, they would hate it; and on the other hand, they love having their favorite team's gear. They find themselves choosing between mugs for several different teams. Should they make a purchase?" Verdicts: "EDT says that conditioned on making a purchase, it was very likely to be the right team. Therefore, it is a good idea to make the purchase. CDT, on the other hand, treats the favorite team as an un-observed input to the decision process. Since it is a causal parent, the probabilistic connection gets cut; so, the CDT agent doesn't make the inference that whatever mug they pick is likely to be the right one. Instead, since they're choosing between several mugs, each mug is individually more likely to be the wrong one than the right one. So, CDT doesn't buy a mug."
- **The ε-utility device** (stated assumption, in the post's parenthetical): the worry that the problem "is ill-specified for the same reason the standard Smoking Lesion is ill-specified: if the agent is running CDT or EDT, how is it possible that it has a bias toward choosing the right team?" is met by "a less extreme version of the trick I used on Smoking Lesion: the decision procedure runs CDT or EDT utility calculations, but then adds epsilon utility to the expected value of any purchase involving the favorite team, which breaks ties in the expected value calculation. This is enough to make the EDT agent reliably purchase the right gear." Note for §2: this is an ε perturbation of *stakes* (a tie-break), not ε-*exploration*; the post contains no exploration device.
- **Diagnosis (D).** "EDT goes wrong by confusing evidence for influence; CDT goes wrong by discarding the evidence along with the influence. To the extent that which action we take tells us important information about ourselves which we didn't already know, we want to take this into account when making a decision." The puzzle: "We can't *both* change our expectation to account for the information *and* hold the probability of causal parents still when making a decision, can we?"

### 1.4 The proposal: CEDT

- Provenance: "I won't be very surprised if the following proposal is already present in the literature. This is a revision of what I proposed in response to Johannes' comment [the Gain rule of P01's thread], which he pointed out did not work (which was similar to *Gandalf's Solution to the Newcomb Problem* by Ralph Wedgewood, which Johannes pointed me to). It also bears some resemblance to a post of Scott's." (Wedgwood's paper is not in `references/`; the "post of Scott's" is agentfoundations item 1279, not fetched — both [reconstructed] if used.)
- Motivation: "when we condition on the *actual* action we will take, we get *good* information, which we *want* to incorporate into our beliefs …. However, when we condition on taking some *other* action, we get *bad* information which we *don't* want to use".
- The correlated-equilibrium analogy: "some outside information tells each agent what action to take, with a joint distribution on actions known to all agents. If all agents would accept this advice, then the joint distribution is a stable equilibrium. So, (1) you know which action you will take — not just what probability you have on different actions, as in Nash equilibria; and (2) a max-expected-value calculation still decides to take that action, after knowing what it is."
- **Rule (R).** html2text: "achosen=argmaxaE(do(a)|achosen)". My reading: $a_{\mathrm{chosen}} = \arg\max_{a} \mathbb{E}\big[\,U \mid do(a),\ A = a_{\mathrm{chosen}}\big]$ — gloss in the post: "the chosen action must be the best causal intervention, after taking into account (as *evidence*) the fact that we chose it." P03 re-sets it as $\arg\max_a \mathbb{E}_{P(\cdot \mid A = a_{\mathrm{chosen}})}[U \mid do(A = a)]$ and corrects "$=$" to "$\in$" (P03: "I should have instead said that the chosen action must be *among* the maximal choices, so that tie-breaking behavior doesn't matter").
- Name: first "CEDT (correlated equilibrium DT)", struck through; "Scott pointed out that this is a pretty bad name, since many concepts of rationality, including Nash equilibria and logical induction, can be seen as having this property of approving of their output under the condition that they know their output. I therefore re-name it to **causal-evidential decision theory** (CEDT)."
- **Multiplicity (M).** "Note that there may be several consistent fixed-points of the above equation. If so, we have a problem of selecting fixed-points, just like we do with Nash equilibria. Bad choices may be self-fulfilling prophecies."

### 1.5 CEDT on the post's examples (the post's own verdicts)

1. Football mug: "every mug becomes an equilibrium solution; taking the mug is evidence that it is the right one, so switching to other mugs looks like a bad idea. Not buying a mug is also an equilibrium. To keep my assumption that the decision is almost always right if the merchandise is purchased, I must suppose that the right equilibrium gets selected (by whatever process does that): the right mug is purchased."
2. Cosmic ray: "if some particular action is strong evidence of cosmic-ray-induced malfunction, such that the AI should enter a 'safely shut down' mode, then this action cannot be an equilibrium; the AI would switch to shutdown if it observed it. Whether the fact that it isn't an equilibrium actually helps it to happen less given cosmic ray exposure is another question."
3. Smoking Lesion Steelman (P01's robots): "non-smoke-loving CEDT robots would decline to smoke no matter what action they thought they'd take, since it only costs them. So, the only equilibrium is for them not to smoke. The smoke-loving CEDT robots would smoke if they were told they were going to smoke, or if they were told they wouldn't; so the only equilibrium for them has them doing so."
4. Suicidal Smoking Lesion (Treutlein's variant): "the non-smoke-lovers again don't smoke. On the other hand, if I've thought this through correctly, smoke-lovers may either smoke or not smoke: if they're told they don't smoke, then they aren't sure whether they'll be killed or not; so, switching to smoking looks too dangerous. On the other hand, if they're told that they *do* smoke, then they know they *will* die, so they're happy to smoke."

Summary claim (S): "in the examples I've looked at, it seems like CEDT always *includes* the desired behavior as a *possible* equilibrium. I'm not sure how to measure the 'best' equilibrium and select it." Selection candidates: expected value — rejected: "it might select equilibria where its actions hide some information, so as to increase EV"; the *gain* "*after* updating on what action is taken, of taking that action rather than alternatives" — "This isn't obviously correct either."

**Conclusion (C).** "This isn't a final solution, by any means, but it is enough to make me willing to say that CDT isn't so far off the mark. As long as a CDT agent knows its own action before it has decided, *and* is given the right causal representation of the problem, then it at least *can* get examples like this right, supposing it happens to be in the right fix-point."

### 1.6 Stated assumptions (collected)

(i) A Bayes-net representation in which the decision node has causal parents, some possibly unobserved, and `do()` is available; (ii) agents that use a piece of self-information (favourite team; utility function) in the *procedure* while lacking it in the *epistemic state* — P01's split, softened by the ε-utility tie-break; (iii) the CEDT fixed point is evaluated at the agent's own equilibrium behaviour ("if they're told they're going to smoke"); (iv) equilibrium selection is external ("by whatever process does that"); (v) "the right causal representation of the problem" is given, not derived.

### 3.1 The thesis sentence (T): "CDT differs from EDT only in those cases where a parent of the decision node is not directly observed"

**P02-1 [derived] — REFINED; not equivalent to Definition 20's criterion; the v2 theorem in its neighbourhood is Lemma 3 + P2:faithful FA-20′.**

*Bayes-net reading.* In a causal graph with `do()`, if the deciding agent conditions on every parent $\mathrm{pa}$ of $A$, then $P(Y\mid do(a),\mathrm{pa}) = P(Y\mid a,\mathrm{pa})$ (the parents are an adjustment set), so `do`-values and conditional values coincide — the post's "only" direction is a theorem of that formalism [reconstructed: Pearl's rule 2 / back-door adjustment, not re-derived here]. The converse fails: an unobserved parent that is irrelevant to the payoff, or whose correlation with the act is nil, produces no difference. So (T) is a *necessary* condition for divergence in the graph formalism, not a characterization.

*v2 reading.* v2 has no graph, so "parent of the decision node" must be re-expressed. The evaluators differ, by definition, exactly where $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d}(a)$ disagrees with $(P_{s_d}(\cdot\mid a), V_{s_d}(\cdot\wedge a))$ on positive-probability acts (Definition 20's evidential criterion fails) or where $A_d^+ \ne A_d$. That statement names the *locus* (the $\mathrm{cf}$ slot) and is silent about *causes*. The causes, under Definition 6, are three, and only the first is a hidden chance parent:

1. *Pooled observations (coverage failure).* Distinct points $d_t$ share $O_d$ but carry different labels; the observation-calibrated state is $\nu(\cdot\mid O_d)$ pooled over types, so $\nu(t\mid a)$ can differ from $\nu(t)$ even though, *at each node*, the draw is independent of $t$ (Lemma 3). This is the post's "unobserved parent" in the letter: the type is upstream, unobserved, and correlated with the act at the pooled state. But the correlation exists only because the *procedure* reads $t$ through a channel the state does not carry (P02-7). The steelman (P01) and the football mug (§3.2) are both of this kind; they differ in sign (the steelman is the case where the pooled evidential value misleads, the mug the case where it informs).
2. *Simulation of the same point* (Newcomb). The predictor's node carries $d$ and draws from $C(d)$; the correlation between prediction and act runs through the *label* (Remark 2.2), which is not an event and cannot be observed. In the sequence's later vocabulary this is P05's "abstract algorithm" parent. Here the two tree-intrinsic referents diverge (R1-state moves the simulation; R2-real does not) and FA-19′'s verdict applies: classical CDT's referent is tree-intrinsic and "condemned by the instance weighting, not by the type".
3. *Shared seed* (Q12): under Remark 3.2's alternative semantics the live draw and a simulation's draw are one sample; conditioning on the draw then carries information the label does not. P06's "the epsilon-exploration bit correlates the action you take with the disaster" is this case (P05-thread and C2-21 already place it there).

*The v2 theorem.* At Definition-7-recorded points with realized observations, FA-20′ (READ, `repair/faithful.md:101`) gives R1-state $=$ R2-real $=$ R3 $=$ Jeffrey conditioning on every positive act — so evidential and causal choice (for any tree-intrinsic referent) agree there, and the residual disagreements live at (1)–(3). This is the honest v2 form of (T): **"under Definition 6, evidential and causal act values can differ only at points that are not recorded — pooled observations, simulations — or under a shared seed; at recorded points they coincide on positive acts."** (T) and this statement are *not* equivalent: (T) is about a graph v2 does not have, is only a necessary condition even there, and presupposes that $\mathrm{cf}$ is graph surgery; v2's version is a theorem about the tree type with the referent left open (Q6). Verdict: REFINED — the post's condition is one of three mechanisms, correctly identified as the one that a "tickle" (self-knowledge) removes, and its graph vocabulary is superseded, which is an improvement in precision (three mechanisms, not one) at a cost (no `do()`, hence no compact statement of "hold the parents fixed").

**P02-2 [derived] — the cosmic-ray worry ("CDT would potentially sever those probabilistic links, keeping the agent from inferring that it is in an error state"): OUTDATED in v2's typing, with a residue.** In v2 the inference "things look goofy, I have probably malfunctioned" is carried by the *state*, not by the evaluator: an observation-calibrated $P_{s_d}$ at the goofy observation already conditions on it (Definition 8 clause 1), and a *limit*-calibrated off-path state (Definition 10) is pinned to the near-tremble statistics — Lemma 2's separation example is literally "the state at an unexpected observation must know it got there by a tremble". Definition 10's tremble $C^\varepsilon$ *is* a cosmic-ray model (with probability $\varepsilon$ the act is uniform, independent of the state). The causal evaluator then supposes acts *from* that already-updated state; nothing is severed. The residue is exactly the case where the evidence of malfunction *is the act itself*, unobservable before acting — which is the CEDT situation (P02-12). Sam Eisenstat's consistency example (P02-3) is outside v2's type (no provability; "counterpossible supposition is excluded by construction", Definition 2) — flagged to P11/L3, no verdict here.

### 3.2 The football mug

Encoding (`p02_mug.py`; toolkit `p02_toolkit_xcheck.py` §2, `validate = []`): chance $t\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1..N\}$; decision node carrying $d_t = (s_t,\top,A)$, $A = \{\mathrm{mug}_1,\dots,\mathrm{mug}_N,\mathrm{none}\}$; leaf world $(t,\mathrm{choice})$; payoff $r = g\,[\mathrm{choice}=\mathrm{mug}_t] - h\,[\mathrm{choice}=\mathrm{mug}_i,\ i\ne t]$. The $N$ points share $O = \top$ and, under any observation-calibration sense, equal $(P,V)$ (Remark 4.4), so they can differ only in $\mathrm{cf}_{s_t}$ or in an ε stakes-perturbation — the "impulse" channel (P02-7). A procedure is a profile $(C(d_1),\dots,C(d_N))$; the post's impulse is the own-mug profile. Structural facts [checked, toolkit]: $\mathrm{covers}(d_1)$ False; `records_for_all(d1).ok` False for every deterministic profile ("t2/… passes 0 d1-nodes") — coverage failure, Remark 3.10's *second* half ($\{\lambda\models\top\}\supsetneq\mathrm{occ}(d_t)$), the same structure P01 found for the steelman; $\max_C V_B = g$ (own-mug).

**P02-4 [checked, `p02_mug.py` (a),(b); toolkit `strict_oc.ok = True`, `T_EDT(own) holds`] — "EDT says … it is a good idea to make the purchase": CONFIRMED at the strictly calibrated pooled state, REFINED under masking.** Under own-mug, $\nu(t{=}i\mid\mathrm{mug}_i) = 1$, so $V_{s_t}(\mathrm{mug}_i) = g$ for *every* $i$ and $\mathrm{none}$ is $P_s$-null (undefined; $A_d^+$ = the mugs). $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ approves any mug — all tie — and the type-indexed tie-break selects $\mathrm{mug}_t$: Definition 18's tie-freedom is doing exactly the work the post's "adds epsilon utility" does. Under masked-local calibration (Definition 9, uniform self-model at $d_1$): values $\mathrm{mug}_1{:}\,10$, $\mathrm{mug}_{2,3}{:}\,4$, $\mathrm{none}{:}\,0$ ($N{=}3,g{=}10,h{=}20$) — the right mug is the *unique* maximizer, because the other types' true continuations are held fixed and only the deliberating point's self-model is freed. So evidential choice buys the right mug at both the strict and the masked grade; the post's EDT verdict stands and is sharpened (no tie under masking).

**P02-5 [checked, (c),(d); toolkit deviation values $(10,0,0,\tfrac{20}{3})$] — "CDT doesn't buy a mug": REFINED — true for one referent, false for every tree-intrinsic one.** The post's causal value, the type held at its pooled marginal, is $g/N - h(N-1)/N$ per mug ($-10$ at $N{=}3,g{=}10,h{=}20$; $\mathrm{none}$ $0$) — buy iff $g > (N-1)h$: with "they would hate it", no purchase. In v2 this is classical CDT with a *rigid* supposition (Remark 1.2(i): the supposed law keeps $P^a(t) = P_{s}(t) = 1/N$). ## 5. Smoking Lesion, the tickle defence, mixed-strategy ratifiability — what this post contributes

**Smoking Lesion.** Two contributions. (a) P02 is the first post in the sequence to conclude that the steelman's lesson is about *self-knowledge* rather than causal structure: "As long as a CDT agent knows its own action before it has decided … it at least *can* get examples like this right". In v2 this is the observation that at recording points the referents collapse (FA-20′) and the lesion's correlation dies (Lemma 3, Proposition 12), so what the steelman needed was not a causal graph but a calibrated state — the diagnosis P01's audit reached from the other side (P01-7, READ). (b) The Suicidal analysis (P02-14) is the sequence's first *multiplicity* result on a lesion problem: two calibrated-and-approved profiles, one optimal; v2 files it under Remark 5.2 and P01 shows the tremble-evidential device lands on the wrong one.

**The tickle defence.** CEDT *is* a tickle defence with the act as the tickle: conditioning on one's own act screens off the type exactly as Eells's introspected inclination does — Lewis's statement of the defence (READ via INDEX `:282`, `lewis-1981-causal-decision-theory.txt:277–297`): "By the time you decide whether to eat eggs, your credence function already has been modified by the evidence of the tickle. Then $C(E/G)$ does not exceed $C(E/{-}G)$". P03 saw this ("a very strong version of the tickle defense"); v2 makes it a theorem about *recording* rather than about lesions (Lemma 3 at a recording point; C2's amendment A5). P02's specific addition is the *exception*: at a pooled observation the act legitimately carries type information (the mug), and there the tickle defence's own machinery — conditioning on the act — is what gets the right answer while the rigid causal evaluator gets it wrong (P02-5). So the post supplies the case the tickle literature's "medical Newcomb" framing lacks: a pooled-observation problem where evidential act-conditioning is *correct* and screening-off would be a *loss*. Lewis's objection that the defence "works only for the fully rational, self-knowing agent" (INDEX `:21`, [reconstructed from the index summary]) is met in v2 by the calibration grade: the mug's agent is self-knowing about its *label* and ignorant of its *type*, and calibration says exactly which.

**Mixed-strategy ratifiability.** P02 is the pure-strategy precursor: it has the fixed-point form ("$a_{\mathrm{chosen}} = \arg\max$"), the multiplicity, and the correlated-equilibrium framing, but not existence (P03 adds "may be satisfied by *no* actions" and Kakutani). The audit's contribution is P02-21: under Definition 6 the passage from "condition on the act" to "condition on the mixture" loses nothing, because the draw is uninformative beyond the label — so P02's pure rule and P03/P05's mixed rule are one device on v2's trees, with the miniature's $\tfrac23$ as their common unique fixed point under the classical referent (P02-9) and the shared seed (Q12) as the one place they would come apart. C2-20's naming ("mixed-strategy ratifiability … strict observation calibration at the label plus Definition 18's tie-freedom") covers P02's rule as its deterministic special case.
