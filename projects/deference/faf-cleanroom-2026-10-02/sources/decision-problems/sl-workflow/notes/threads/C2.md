## 1. The statement in v2 — translating P05's four assumptions

P05's Theorem 1 (READ, `posts/05-…md` §"Case 1"): "Under the above assumptions, the consistent choices of mixed strategy are the same for CDT and EDT", proof: "the CDT and EDT expected utility calculations become the same under the mixed-strategy ratifiability condition, since $D$ screens $A$ off from any un-observed parents of $D$". The four assumptions, with P07's numbering (#1 compatibility, #2 exploration, #3 ratifiability, #4 implementability), and their v2 translations:

**(a) "knows its mixed strategy $D$" (#3).** In v2 the label $C(d) \in \Delta(A_d)$ is not an event of $\mathcal E$ (Remark 5.1: labels are inexpressible internally; Proposition 4: no label can report $q$), so "knowing $D$" cannot be conditioning on an event. It is the *calibration equation for the label*: strict observation calibration (Definition 8) at $d$, which at a point where the leaf-world's act coordinate records the agent's own draw gives $P_{s_d}(a) = \nu_{B,C}(a \mid O_d) = C(d)(a)$ — Appendix B's unnamed *self-transparency*, derived in Remark 3.6 for deterministic $C$ and in Proposition 7's proof for mixed $C$. **C2-1 [derived]** The object of self-knowledge is the *mixture*, not the draw: at a node-action-veridical $d$-node $q$ (Definition F3′ below) the draw at $q$ is the only factor of $\nu(a \wedge O_d)$ that depends on which act was drawn, so $\nu(a \mid O_d) = C(d)(a)$ even when other $d$-nodes (simulations) lie above $q$ and move the reach weights. This is P03's "knows what it will do up to the randomness involved in exploration" and P04-comment the author's "knowledge of the state of your deliberation, not the action itself". The masked (Definition 9) and limit (Definition 10) variants free or refine this: masking frees the label (Remark 3.7) and so *deletes* assumption (a) — under masking the fixed-point structure disappears (P2:calibration CA-10′: on the miniature "the fixed-point structure of Remark 4.3 lives entirely in the identification $m = q$"); limit calibration keeps (a) and adds off-path discipline (Lemma 2). So the v2 rendering of (a) is strict (or limit) observation calibration at $d$, and *not* masked calibration.

**(b) ε-exploration (#2).** P05 uses it "to ensure that the conditional expectations are well-defined". In v2 the evidential act value $V_{s_d}(a)$ is defined only on $A_d^+ = \{a : P_{s_d}(a) > 0\}$ (Definition 17), so under (a) a deterministic $C$ has $A_d^+ = \{C(d)\}$ and $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ approves it vacuously (Remark 3.9's collapse; P2:calibration CA-13′ on the miniature). Four devices supply the missing values (P2:calibration CA-16′–20′'s taxonomy): D1 limit calibration + Definition-17 EDT (content-free at recorded on-path points, CA-17′); D2 Remark 3.12's fixed-procedure tremble-consistency (can be *empty*, CA-14′/P2:dynamic DY-2); D3 procedure-side tremble-conditioning = Theorem 1's evaluator under $C^\varepsilon$; D4 Remark 3.9's advice-stance route — pin the act values at nulled acts by their tremble limits, the *evidential counterfactual structure* $\mathrm{cf}^{\mathrm{tr}}$ = referent R3 of P2:faithful Definition F2. The two honest renderings of (b) are therefore: **(b-int)** the label $C(d)$ has full support (P05's literal "all action probabilities at least $\varepsilon$"), under which Definition 17's domain is all of $A_d$ and no extension is needed; or **(b-R3)** the evidential act values are read as R3 (defined at every act that is tremble-reachable within $O_d$, P2:faithful FA-17′), with the test-sequence form of Remark 3.12 (P2:dynamic DY-3) as the refinement that also handles exact ties. I use (b-R3) as official below and say where (b-int) differs.

**(c) "CDT" (#1, the same Bayes net for both).** v2 has no $do()$; the causal evaluator is Definition 17's $\mathrm{CDT}(d) = \mathrm{Unif}\arg\max_a V^a_{s_d}(a)$ with $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d}$ *counterfactually calibrated* (Remark 3.11's family; Definition 20's contrast) to one of the tree-intrinsic referents of P2:faithful Definition F2 [read in the ledger, `repair/faithful.md`]: **R1-state** = all-instance deviation conditioned on $O_d$, $\mathbb E_{\mu_{C[d\mapsto a]}}[r \mid O_d]$; **R2-real** = single-instance forcing at the node-action-veridical $d$-node(s), reach-weighted, with every other node — the predictor's simulation included — still drawing from $C$ (FA-19′: "classical CDT's counterfactual is tree-intrinsic under Definition 6, in the act-recording algebra"); **R3** = the tremble-limit conditional $\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\big(\nu_{C^\varepsilon}(\cdot \mid a\wedge O_d), \mathbb E_{C^\varepsilon}[r\mid\cdot\wedge a\wedge O_d]\big)$; plus the prior-level **R1-prior** $= V_B(C[d\mapsto a])$ and **R2-SIA** $= \sum_q R_q G_q(C,a)$ (Theorem 1), which are not $O_d$-conditioned and belong to the UDT/anthropic axis (FA-21′'s axis β). P05's "same Bayes net for both" is Remark 1.1's one chassis: both evaluators read one state $s_d$; the EDT side is Definition 20's evidential criterion (the act value $V_{s_d}(a)$ itself). "CDT" without a named referent is not a v2 object, and P05's $do(a)$ on a net whose only parent of $A$ is $D$ is, in v2, **R2-real**: force the act at the real node, hold every other node — including the nodes that read $D$ — at its $C$-statistics (P05: "CDT makes decisions by first cutting the links to parents, and then conditioning on alternative actions").

**(d) implementability (#4, "$A$ has no other direct parents than $D$").** Three v2 ingredients. (i) Definition 6's independent draw: "at a decision node sample $a \sim C(d_q)$, independently of everything so far" — this is the type-level content of "the choice of mixed strategy is the *only* thing determining the action"; Remark 3.2's shared-seed alternative (P2:seeds Definition 6′) is exactly a second parent of $A$ (the seed) shared with the predictor. (ii) The act coordinate of the leaf-world must be *set by the draw at one node*: Definition F3′ (P2:faithful, repaired): every node-action-veridical $d$-node is subtree-veridical and $\mu$-a.s. every $O_d$-run passes exactly one node-action-veridical $d$-node. Its failure modes are P05's "another parent of $A$": Told-You-So's $d_5$ (the leaf $(10,5)$ below the $m{=}10$ edge records $m{=}5$ — the environment overwrites the act coordinate), the selection-forcing tree $S_{\mathrm{sel}}$ (fake leaves carry act coordinates the agent never drew), the routing root. (iii) No fibers of $d$ nested on $O_d$-runs *is not needed* for the theorem (the miniature and Newcomb have a simulation $d$-node above the real one and F3′ still holds); it is needed for the additional identity with the deviation referent R1-state (Definition 7's exactly-one clause, FA-20′(ii)). No Omega copy of the point in the fiber-theoretic sense is required: Definition 6 already makes the copy's draw independent (Remark 3.2), which is P05's point that "things correlated with our randomness … must be downstream of it".

**The theorem, stated.** Fix a finite concrete problem $B$ (Definition 5), Definition 6 run semantics, a queried point $d$ with $\nu_{B,C}(O_d) > 0$ that is veridically act-recording (F3′) for the procedures in play. Write $v_d(a; C)$ for the R3 act value of $a$ at $d$ under $C$.

> **Theorem C2-A (P05's Theorem 1 in v2).** Under (a) strict observation calibration at $d$, (b-R3), (c) $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d}$ counterfactually calibrated to R2-real or to R3, and (d) F3′ + Definition 6: for every $a \in A_d$, $V_{s_d}(a) = V^a_{s_d}(a) = v_d(a; C)$ wherever $V_{s_d}(a)$ is defined, and the R3 extension of $V_{s_d}$ equals $V^a_{s_d}(a)$ everywhere. Consequently the sets of calibrated-and-approved mixed actions at $d$ — the labels $m \in \Delta(A_d)$ with $\mathrm{supp}\, m \subseteq \arg\max_a v_d(a; C[d\mapsto m])$ (Definition 18's tie-freedom, Remark 4.3) — coincide for $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ (R3-extended) and $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$ with either referent. With (b-int) in place of (b-R3) they coincide on full-support labels, and $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$'s set additionally contains every pure label, vacuously (Remark 3.9's collapse).
>
> **Theorem C2-B (the recording strengthening).** If moreover $B$ records at $d$ (Definition 7: exactly one $d$-node on every $O_d$-run), then also $V_{s_d}(a) = $ R1-state$(a)$, the common value $v_d(a)$ does not depend on $C(d)$ at all, and the calibrated-and-approved set is the fixed face $\{m : \mathrm{supp}\, m \subseteq \arg\max_a v_d(a)\}$ — no fixed-point equation remains, no mixed label is ever forced (P2:calibration CA-12′(iii)), and existence is trivial.
>
> **Failure clauses.** Without F3′ (selection, routing, overwritten act coordinates) the evidential values can be undefined for every device (R3 `undef` at Told-You-So's $d_5$, FA-17′) or label-dependent through leaves the agent never drew ($S_{\mathrm{sel}}$: $\tfrac7{11}$), while R2-real stays total and label-free: the sets differ, with the *causal* verdict the good one at Told-You-So. Without Definition 6 (shared seed, Definition 6′) R3′ $=$ R1-state $\ne$ R2-real (Newcomb, XOR, Death in Damascus with Death predicting the seed): the sets differ, and the evidential verdict is the one-boxing / paying / indifferent one. Without Definition 7 (simulation nodes on $O_d$-runs: miniature, Newcomb, TN, Death in Damascus with an independent-sampling predictor) Theorem C2-A holds but R1-state differs, the common value depends on $C(d)$ through the simulations, and the calibrated-and-approved set is a genuine fixed point (Remark 4.3's $\tfrac23$).

Proof of C2-A/C2-B and the battery: §2 (numbers) and §3 (proof). Register of the headline: [derived] for the proofs in §3; every battery cell [checked] with `C2-scratch/battery.py`.

## 3. Proofs

Notation. Fix $B$, $C$, $d$, Definition 6. For a $d$-node $q$ write $R_q(C) := \mu_{B,C}(\text{reach } q)$ and, for $a \in A_d$, $\theta_{q,a}$ for the law of the leaf reached below $q$'s $a$-child when every node below draws from $C$, so $G_q(C,a) = \theta_{q,a}(r)$ (§8's forcing). Call $q$ *node-action-veridical* if for every $a$ every leaf below $q$'s $a$-edge satisfies $a$ (P2:faithful F3′), and let $N_d$ be the set of such nodes.

**Lemma C2-L1 (factorization at veridical nodes) [derived].** Let $q \in N_d$ be subtree-veridical. Then for every $a \in A_d$ and event $X$,
$$\mu_{B,C}\big(\{\text{reach } q\} \cap \{\text{draw } a \text{ at } q\} \cap \{\lambda \models X\}\big) \;=\; R_q(C)\, C(d)(a)\, \theta_{q,a}(X),$$
and every leaf below $q$'s $a$-edge satisfies $a \wedge O_d$.
*Proof.* Definition 6 draws at $q$ independently of the path (the factor $C(d)(a)$) and the continuation below the $a$-child is the sub-run law $\theta_{q,a}$, which involves only nodes below $q$ (other $d$-nodes included, each drawing from $C$). Subtree-veridicality puts $O_d$ on every leaf below $q$; node-action-veridicality puts $a$ on every leaf below the $a$-edge. $\square$

**Lemma C2-L2 (F3′ makes conditioning a reach-weighted forcing) [derived].** Suppose (F3′) every $q \in N_d$ is subtree-veridical and $\mu_{B,C}$-a.s. every $O_d$-run passes exactly one node of $N_d$, and $\nu_{B,C}(O_d) > 0$. Then for every $a$ with $C(d)(a) > 0$:
$$\nu_{B,C}(a \mid O_d) = C(d)(a), \qquad \mathbb E_{\mu_{B,C}}[\,r \mid a \wedge O_d\,] = \frac{\sum_{q \in N_d} R_q(C)\, G_q(C,a)}{\sum_{q \in N_d} R_q(C)} =: v_d(a; C).$$
*Proof.* By the exactly-one clause the $O_d$-leaves are, up to null sets, partitioned by the node of $N_d$ they lie below, and by node-action-veridicality an $O_d$-leaf satisfies $a$ iff it lies below the $a$-edge of that node (an $O_d$-leaf satisfying $a$ but lying below a $b$-edge, $b \ne a$, would satisfy $b$ as well, contradicting disjointness of the action events). Summing Lemma C2-L1 over $q \in N_d$: $\nu(a \wedge O_d) = C(d)(a)\sum_q R_q$ and $\nu(O_d) = \sum_q R_q$, giving the first identity; and $\mathbb E[r \cdot \mathbf 1(a \wedge O_d)] = C(d)(a) \sum_q R_q\, \theta_{q,a}(r)$, giving the second after division. $\square$

Remarks. (i) The identity $\nu(a\mid O_d) = C(d)(a)$ is self-transparency (§1(a)): the state pinned by Definition 8 knows the *mixture*. It holds although $R_q(C)$ may depend on $C(d)$ through simulation nodes above $q$ (Newcomb, TN, the miniature), because $C(d)(a)$ is the only $a$-specific factor. (ii) $v_d(a; C)$ is R2-real of Definition F2 by definition (reach-weighted forcing over the node-action-veridical instances), so Lemma C2-L2 says **EV $=$ R2-real on $A_d^+$** — FA-20′(i)'s content with the hypothesis F3′ (the repaired one; under the notes' original F3 the post-act coin tree refutes it, FA-20(i) killed).

**Lemma C2-L3 (the tremble limit) [derived].** Under F3′ for the trembled procedures $C^\varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon$ small) and $\nu_{B,C}(O_d) > 0$: for every $a \in A_d$, $\mathrm{R3}(a) := \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0^+}\mathbb E_{\mu_{C^\varepsilon}}[r \mid a \wedge O_d]$ exists and equals $v_d(a; C)$.
*Proof.* Apply Lemma C2-L2 to $C^\varepsilon$ (full support, so every act is positive): $\mathbb E_{C^\varepsilon}[r\mid a\wedge O_d] = \sum_q R_q(C^\varepsilon) G_q(C^\varepsilon, a) / \sum_q R_q(C^\varepsilon)$ — the factor $C^\varepsilon(d)(a)$ has cancelled. Each $R_q(C^\varepsilon)$ and $G_q(C^\varepsilon, a)$ is a polynomial in $\varepsilon$ with value $R_q(C)$, $G_q(C,a)$ at $\varepsilon = 0$, and $\sum_q R_q(C) = \nu_C(O_d) > 0$, so the quotient is continuous at $0$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem C2-A.* By Lemma C2-L2 the strictly-calibrated evidential act value equals R2-real on $A_d^+$; by Lemma C2-L3 its R3 extension equals R2-real on all of $A_d$; a $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d}$ counterfactually calibrated to R2-real or to R3 has $V^a_{s_d}(a) = v_d(a; C)$. Definition 18's support conditions for $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ (R3-extended) and $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$ therefore read identically, $\mathrm{supp}\, C(d) \subseteq \arg\max_a v_d(a; C)$, and with (a) the label entering $v_d$ is the true one, so the calibrated-and-approved sets coincide. Under (b-int) the Definition-17 domain of EDT is $A_d^+ = \mathrm{supp}\, C(d)$ (self-transparency), which for a pure label is a singleton and makes $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ vacuous there — the only divergence. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem C2-B.* Definition 7 recording adds: a.s. every $O_d$-run passes exactly one $d$-node (of any kind). Then no $d$-node lies above or below the recorded node on a positive $O_d$-path, so $R_q(C)$ and $\theta_{q,a}$ are free of $C(d)$ — $v_d(a)$ is label-free — and under $C[d\mapsto a]$ the $O_d$-runs are unchanged except for the draw at the recorded node: $\mathbb E_{\mu_{C[d\mapsto a]}}[r \mid O_d] = \sum_q R_q \theta_{q,a}(r)/\sum_q R_q = v_d(a)$, i.e. R1-state $=$ R2-real. With $v_d$ label-free the fixed-point condition $\mathrm{supp}\, m \subseteq \arg\max_a v_d(a)$ is a fixed face, nonempty, containing every mixture of maximizers and no other label: nothing is forced (CA-12′(iii)). $\square$

Definition 6 is necessary for EV $=$ R2-real: `shared.out` (S1–S4: under the shared seed EV′ $=$ R3′ $=$ R1-state′ while the forcing referent keeps the other verdict). $\nu(O_d) > 0$ is necessary for EV to be defined at all (row 5b/5c), and R3 is the only referent that survives its failure (Lemma 2's separation).

## 4. Existence, and which device *is* mixed-strategy ratifiability

Fix $d$, F3′, Definition 6, and write $v(a; m) := v_d(a; C[d\mapsto m])$ for the R3 act value at label $m$ (Lemma C2-L3; a rational function of $m$ whose denominator $\sum_{q\in N_d} R_q(C[d\mapsto m])$ is the probability of realizing $O_d$ through a veridical node). Standing hypothesis for this section: $\nu_{C[d\mapsto m]}(O_d) > 0$ for every $m \in \Delta(A_d)$ — the observation is realized whatever the label (true on every battery row except Told-You-So's $d_{10}$). Candidate devices, in the vocabulary of P2:calibration CA-16′–20′ and P2:dynamic DY-1–3:

- **MSR** (mixed-strategy ratifiability, limit form; device D4): $\mathrm{supp}\, m \subseteq \arg\max_a v(a; m)$ — strict observation calibration at the label $m$, Definition 18's tie-freedom, act values pinned by tremble limits (Remark 3.9's advice route). By Theorem C2-A this is simultaneously $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$(R3) and $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$(R2-real) at the strictly calibrated state.
- **MSR$^{17}$**: the same with Definition 17's domain $A_d^+ = \mathrm{supp}\, m$ (device D1's evaluator at the strict state).
- **FF**: Remark 3.12's fixed-procedure tremble-consistency (device D2): one $m$ normative against *all* its small trembles.
- **TS**: the test-sequence form (P2:dynamic DY-3): $\varepsilon_n \to 0$, $m_n \to m$, $\mathrm{supp}\, m_n \subseteq \arg\max_a v_{\varepsilon_n}(a; m_n)$ with $v_\varepsilon$ the act value at the state strictly calibrated to $C[d\mapsto m_n]^{\varepsilon_n}$.
- **εFP**: Kakutani fixed points on the $\varepsilon$-floored simplex (P2:seeds SE-18′(a)) and their limit points.
- **DE**: Skyrms's deliberational equilibria (READ `skyrms-1990-dynamic-models…txt:100–200`): a state of indecision $x$ (a probability vector over the acts, "her probabilities now that she will do the act in question at the end of deliberation") together with probabilities $y$ of the states of nature, fixed under a dynamics $\phi = \langle D, I\rangle$ whose adaptive rule $D$ "seeks the good" and whose informational feedback $I$ maps $\langle x, y\rangle$ to new state-probabilities; "All dynamical rules which seek the good have the same fixed points. These are the states in which the utility of the status quo is maximal" (`:120–121`, verbatim), the status quo's utility being that of the *default mixed act* $x$ (`:149–159`).

**C2-6 [derived] — DE $=$ MSR, with the calibrate step as the informational feedback.** Take $I(x, \cdot) :=$ the strictly calibrated statistics at label $x$, i.e. $y(x) = \nu_{C[d\mapsto x]}(\cdot \mid O_d)$ on the non-act coordinates, and read the acts' expected utilities at $\langle x, y(x)\rangle$ as $v(\cdot; x)$ (at an F3′ point the state's act-conditionals are $\theta$-laws, Lemma C2-L1, so this is legitimate). The status quo's utility is $\sum_a x(a)\, v(a; x)$; it is maximal among the acts iff every act in $\mathrm{supp}\, x$ attains $\max_a v(a; x)$, i.e. iff $x \in$ MSR. So Skyrms's fixed points are exactly the MSR labels, for every rule that seeks the good — the "third road" of Remark 5.3 with v2's own calibration equation as the feedback. *Hypothesis used:* $I$ continuous, which holds under the standing hypothesis (rational function with positive denominator).

**C2-7 [derived; instances checked] — existence without trembles.** Under the standing hypothesis $v(\cdot; m)$ is continuous on the whole simplex, so the best-response correspondence $m \mapsto \{m' : \mathrm{supp}\, m' \subseteq \arg\max_a v(a; m)\}$ is upper hemicontinuous with nonempty convex compact values, and Kakutani gives MSR $\ne \varnothing$; equivalently Skyrms's Theorem I (`:182–196`: Nash dynamics plus continuous $I$ has a deliberational equilibrium, by Brouwer) gives DE $\ne \varnothing$. No $\varepsilon$-floor is needed, because the R3 extension already defines every act's value at every label — the discontinuity SE-18′(c) locates in Definition 17's domain restriction is exactly what R3 removes. Instances: miniature $\tfrac23$ (SE-19), Death in Damascus $q^\ast(p)$, Psychopath Button $\tfrac7{40}$, Murder Lesion $\tfrac9{28}$, Weatherson's Table 6.1 $\tfrac23$, Skyrms's Mean Demon $\tfrac23$ (§5), all computed in `battery.py` / `lit_checks.py`. Under Definition 7 (Theorem C2-B) the correspondence is constant and MSR is a face.

**C2-8 [derived] — the containments.** $\mathrm{FF} \subseteq \mathrm{TS} \subseteq \mathrm{MSR} = \mathrm{DE} \subseteq \mathrm{MSR}^{17}$, and limit points of εFP lie in TS. *Proof.* FF $\subseteq$ TS: take $m_n = m$. TS $\subseteq$ MSR: at an F3′ point $v_\varepsilon(a; m) = \sum_q R_q(C_m^\varepsilon) G_q(C_m^\varepsilon, a)/\sum_q R_q(C_m^\varepsilon)$ (Lemma C2-L2 applied to the trembled procedure — the factor $m_\varepsilon(a)$ cancels), jointly continuous in $(\varepsilon, m)$ where the denominator is positive, which the standing hypothesis guarantees at $\varepsilon = 0$ and hence nearby; weak inequalities pass to the limit. MSR $\subseteq$ MSR$^{17}$: shrinking the maximization domain to $\mathrm{supp}\, m$ can only weaken the condition. εFP-limits $\subseteq$ TS is SE-18′(b) as repaired (approval at the test-sequence state). $\square$ Strictness: FF can be empty where TS is not (Remark 4.3's miniature, CA-14′/DY-2 — the tie point $q^\ast(\varepsilon) = \frac{4-3\varepsilon}{6(1-\varepsilon)}$ moves with $\varepsilon$; DY-3: the per-$\varepsilon$ fixed point is unique and $\to \tfrac23$); TS $\subsetneq$ MSR at exact continuation ties (CA-4′'s $T_3$, where the tremble's first-order term breaks a tie the limit values leave); MSR $\subsetneq$ MSR$^{17}$ at F3′ points for every deterministic label (CA-13′: $\delta_a, \delta_b$ approved vacuously on the miniature). Without the standing hypothesis TS and MSR can part company along the approach direction (SE-18′(b)'s two-route tree, where the limit procedure has $\nu(O_d) = 0$).

**C2-9 [derived] — what the device is, and what it is not.** *Mixed-strategy ratifiability, in v2, is MSR: strict observation calibration at the label plus Definition 18's tie-freedom, with the act values at self-knowledge-nulled acts pinned by their tremble limits.* It is identical to Skyrms's deliberational equilibrium when deliberation's informational feedback is the calibrate step (C2-6), to the Kakutani fixed point of the best-response-to-own-statistics correspondence (C2-7), and — by Theorem C2-A — to the CDT(R2-real)-ratifiable labels, so P02's CEDT ("the chosen action must be the best causal intervention, after taking into account (as evidence) the fact that we chose it", READ) and P03's evidential reading are one set under Definition 6 and F3′. It is *not*: (i) masked-calibrated-and-approved (Definition 9 frees the label and the fixed point dissolves, CA-10′; DY-1(iii): on the miniature both pure procedures are masked-EDT-consistent at false self-models); (ii) Remark 3.12's fixed-form device (empty on the miniature); (iii) Definition 17's EDT at the strict state (approves every deterministic procedure vacuously). Its trembling-hand *refinement* is TS, which is the form a convergence statement has (DY-3) and is what P05's Case 2 ("approximate ratifiability … By Brouwer's fixed-point theorem, there will be equilibria for the CDT and EDT selection functions") gestures at — with the correction that P05's continuous selection functions give $\varepsilon$-indexed procedures, not one procedure good against all trembles (A5 of P2:calibration).

**C2-10 [derived] — P05's Case 3 and Remark 5.3.** P05's Case 3 makes "$D$ … the agent's beliefs about which action it will take" with a deterministic $D \to A$ link and approximate self-knowledge; this is Skyrms's state of indecision with a pure act at the end ("the adoption of a mixed strategy can be thought of as a way to turn the state of indecision … to stone", `:156–158`) and Remark 5.3's relocation refactor (states stripped from problems; deliberation produces the self-consistent state). C2-6 shows that the fixed points of that picture are the MSR labels whatever the dynamics, so Case 3 adds nothing to Case 1 at the level of *which* labels are consistent; it changes only the story of how one is reached (E-stability, Q5; P2:dynamic DY-19's Galois pair). The rock-paper-scissors passage of Case 3 is the Nash flow toward $(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$.

## 6. The tickle defence as a theorem (Task 4)

P03 (READ): imposing "EDT's prior knows what policy EDT selects" gives "a very strong version of the tickle defense" that "makes EDT act as CDT advises in all but the most extreme cases, where predictors are perfect". Proposition 12 is v2's Eells: with lesion-informative observations, each queried state has $P_s(k\mid m{=}1) = P_s(k\mid m{=}0)$ (Lemma 3 within the observation) and evidential choice smokes. The strongest true version I can state:

> **Theorem C2-C (strong tickle defence).** Let $B$ be any finite concrete problem under Definition 6, $C$ any procedure, $d$ any queried point with $\nu_{B,C}(O_d) > 0$ at which $B$ is veridically act-recording (F3′). Then for every act $a$ the strictly-calibrated evidential act value (R3-extended) equals the classical-CDT value R2-real$(a) = \sum_{q\in N_d} w_q\, G_q(C,a)$, and so does the tremble-limit evidential counterfactual R3. In particular evidential choice at a calibrated point never differs from causal choice by more than Definition 17's domain restriction, at *any* predictor skill $p \le 1$.

*Proof.* Lemmas C2-L2, C2-L3 (§3). $\square$ It contains Proposition 12 (the tickle instantiation is Definition-7 recorded, so C2-B applies: label-free values, $\alpha > 0$ difference, both smoke), Briggs's unanimity, Egan's "not smoking is unratifiable", and Skyrms's probabilization of Newcomb, as instances.

**The extreme cases, each with its calculation [checked].**
1. *Predictors that read the seed* (Definition 6′; the literature's "perfect predictor"): Newcomb S2/S2′ (one-box vs two-box: $(3,2)$ vs $(2q+1,2q+2)$), XOR blackmail S3 ($-1000$ vs $\tfrac{11000(82q-91)}{98q+1}$: pay vs refuse), Death in Damascus S4 ($(0,-1)$ vs $(10-10q, 10q-1)$: stay vs the $\tfrac{11}{20}$ mixture). The broken assumption is (d)'s first ingredient: the seed is a second parent of $A$. Note that $p = 1$ with an *independent* sampler is **not** extreme: row 9 at $p = 1$ gives the tie $q^\ast = \tfrac{11}{20}$ for both evaluators; row 11 two-boxes for both.
2. *Told-You-So's selection* ($d_5$ under $C_0$, row 5a): F3′ fails because the environment overwrites the act coordinate ($(10,5)$ below the $m{=}10$ edge); EV, R3, R1-state are undefined at $m{=}10$ for every $\varepsilon$ (DY-5), R2-real is undefined (no veridical node), only R2-SIA and R1-prior speak ($(5,10)$). So at a selection node the tickle defence is not false but *mute*: there is no evidential value to agree or disagree with. The causal verdict that gets Told-You-So right is the prior-level one (Theorem 1 / $V_B$), not a state-level counterfactual.
3. *Coverage failure* ($S_{\mathrm{sel}}$, row 6): EV depends on the label through leaves the agent never drew; MSR(R3) $= \{\tfrac7{11}\}$ against R2-real's indifference. Broken: (d)'s second ingredient.
4. *Self-succession with a non-veridical first node* (AMD last-draw, row 2a): EV tie $\tfrac23$, R2-real $\delta_a$, R1-state $\delta_b$, Theorem 1 $\tfrac13$ — four answers. Broken: (d).
5. *The deviation referent* (rows 1, 8–12): if "CDT" means the $O_d$-conditioned all-instance deviation, agreement needs Definition 7, and its failure (a simulation node on $O_d$-runs) gives the classical *EDT* verdicts to the deviation referent — which is why P03's Law of Logical Causality ("If conditioning on any event changes the probability an agent assigns to its own action, that event must be treated as causally downstream") is, in v2, the *choice* of the deviation referent over forcing (sequence-map Q-D), and is unnecessary for C2-C.

**C2-13 [derived] — P03's "in all but the most extreme cases" restated.** Evidential choice at a strictly calibrated F3′ point under Definition 6 equals classical-CDT choice *always*; the residue is not a matter of degree in $p$ but of type: the run semantics (6 vs 6′) and the recording status (F3′ vs selection/coverage/self-succession). ## 7. The Dutch book (P09/P12) as a v2 tree (Task 5) — [checked], `C2-scratch/dutchbook.py`, `dutchbook.out`; O&C in `lit_checks.py`

**Encoding.** Root: bet point $d_B$ ($O = \top$, actions buy / decline; buying costs $2\delta$). Below each branch, the original problem — symmetric Death in Damascus, alive $= 1$ — with Death's placement a hypothetical $d'$-node (sample) feeding a chance node of skill $p$, then the real $d'$-node with the *doubled* action set $\{\mathrm{stay}, \mathrm{stay}\_\mathrm{sell}, \mathrm{flee}, \mathrm{flee}\_\mathrm{sell}\}$: "$\_\mathrm{sell}$" returns $B$ for $\delta$ and voids it; kept, $B$ pays $(\mathrm{cdt}(\mathrm{stay}) - U)\cdot s$ on stay-runs (P12's contract, $s = +1$). Label $C(d')(\mathrm{stay}) = q$ (P12: action probabilities preserved across the split). Procedures: $C_{\mathrm{book}}$ = buy, then sell at every act; $C_{\mathrm{dec}}$ = decline; $C_{\mathrm{keep}}$ = buy and keep.

**C2-14 [checked] — the loss is a theorem about $V_B$.** In every run semantics and at every skill, $V_B(C_{\mathrm{book}}) - V_B(C_{\mathrm{dec}}) = -\delta$ (a sure loss on every run). So *if* a procedure buys at $d_B$ and sells at $d'$ it loses $\delta$ per run relative to declining: a value statement under Definition 6 (or 6′), not a stipulation about referents.

**C2-15 [checked] — who buys, who sells.** (i) Definition 6, $p = 1$ or $\tfrac34$: at $d'$, $\mathrm{edt}(\mathrm{stay}) = $ R2-real$(\mathrm{stay}) = $ R3$(\mathrm{stay})$ ($1-q$; $\tfrac34 - \tfrac q2$) — Theorem C2-A — so P12's bet has evidential value $q\cdot(\mathrm{cdt}-\mathrm{edt}) - 2\delta = -2\delta$ at $d_B$: **nobody buys; classical CDT is not Dutch-bookable at an F3′ point under Definition 6.** (ii) Definition 6′, $p = 1$ (P09's Death in Damascus): $\mathrm{edt}'(\mathrm{stay}) = 0$, R2-real$'(\mathrm{stay}) = 1-q$; P12's valuation at $d_B$ is $q(1-q) - 2\delta > 0$ for $\delta < q(1-q)/2$ (proportional to $P(a)$, as P09 says); at $d'$ the forcing evaluator prices $B$ at $\mathrm{cdt} - \mathbb E^{\mathrm{stay}}[U] = 0$ and prefers stay$\_$sell ($1-q-\delta$) to stay ($1-q-2\delta$): it sells; the pair loses $\delta$. The evidential evaluator at $d'$ (R3′) values stay-and-keep at $1-q-2\delta$ against stay$\_$sell at $-\delta$ — it *keeps* and the bet pays: the evidential pair (buy, keep) is coherent. (iii) The $d_B$ evaluation *with self-knowledge*: under strict calibration for $C_{\mathrm{book}}$ the state at $d_B$ knows the continuation sells, and every $O_{d_B}$-conditioned referent (R1-state $=$ R2-real $=$ R3 at the recorded root) values buy at $-\delta$ against decline $0$: **no calibrated $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$- or $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$-approved procedure buys.** P12's $d_B$ valuation ("the agent is betting ahead of time, [so] it will evaluate … according to the conditional expectation $E(U\mid \mathrm{Act}=a)$", with the bet "grafted" and kept) requires a $d_B$ state that mis-models its own continuation — Appendix B's *global* masking ("the state may then mis-model its own downstream behaviour … open to the sophisticated-choice objection") — or an uncalibrated $\mathrm{cf}_{s_{d_B}}$ supposing "buy-and-keep". P12 concedes the sophisticated point ("If that were the case … the agent should not have accepted the bet in the first place") and reads the pattern as a coherence charge; v2 states the charge exactly: *the pair (conditioning at $d_B$; a cf $\ne$ conditioning at a positive act at $d'$) is not jointly realizable as one calibrated state assignment, because at F3′ points under Definition 6 every $O_d$-conditioned tree-intrinsic referent equals conditioning at positive acts (C2-A), so the $d'$-cf is counterfactually miscalibrated and the bet's $d_B$ value $P(a)(\mathrm{cdt}(a) - \mathrm{edt}(a))$ is the measure of that miscalibration.*

**C2-16 [derived] — theorem or fiat (Remark 3.11's question).** The Dutch book is a theorem: a $V_B$ loss against any procedure whose $\mathrm{cf}$ at $d'$ disagrees with conditioning at a positive-probability act *and* whose $d_B$ evaluation prices bets against that disagreement — with the sharpening that under Definition 6 at F3′ points such a $\mathrm{cf}$ is calibrated to *no* $O_d$-conditioned tree-intrinsic referent (FA-20′, C2-A), so what is condemned is a miscalibrated opinion, not classical CDT; and under Definition 6′ the forcing referent is no longer tree-intrinsic (SE-1(d)) and *is* condemned — P09's example is the shared-seed case. The "by fiat" residue Remark 3.11 feared has become a modelling choice about the predictor (Q12), not a choice of referent for $\mathrm{cf}_s$: the book is referent-neutral (it prices the gap to conditioning, whatever produced it) and semantics-sensitive. This is P13's own conclusion — "counterfactual expectations should equal conditional probabilities … at least when the antecedent has positive probability" (P12 thesis (3), Definition 20's evidential criterion) — obtained in v2 as: *at recorded points under Definition 6, counterfactual calibration to the tree implies the evidential criterion at positive acts* (FA-25′(1′)).

**C2-17 [derived] — the probability-zero residue.** P12: for $P(a) = 0$ "CDT strictly prefers that the agent be Dutch Booked rather than just do $a$" ("moderately damning"). In v2: at a strictly calibrated deterministic point the alternative act is nulled; R3 supplies its evidential value; a $\mathrm{cf}$ that disagrees with R3 at the nulled act prefers $a\_$cancel (paying a fee to void a bet it deems unfavourable) to $a$ on $a$-runs, which have probability $0$ — a $V_B$ loss of exactly $0$, and under the trembles $C^\varepsilon$ a loss of order $\varepsilon\cdot$fee: the residue is a limit-calibration statement (Definition 10), $o(1)$ in $\varepsilon$, which is P12's "smaller Dutch Books are less important" made precise. FA-20′(iii): at recorded points R3 agrees with R2-real at nulled acts too, so classical CDT is not even "moderately" condemned there under Definition 6.

**C2-18 [checked, `lit_checks.out`] — Oesterheld & Conitzer 2021.** Adversarial Offer (READ `:55–120`: two boxes at \$1, \$3 in each box predicted not bought, accuracy $0.75$, "No randomization device is available to the buyer (or at least no randomization device that is not predictable to the seller)"). **C2-19 [derived] — P12's dynamic-consistency clause.** "In decision problems where the payoff depends only on actions actually taken, not on your policy … EDT is a dynamically consistent choice" — in v2: trees whose every decision node is subtree-veridical (no simulation queries), where $\mathrm{occ}(d) = \{\lambda\models O_d\}$ (Proposition 3) and Theorem 2's local evaluator is the event-conditioned one; P12's excluded list (mugging, transparent Newcomb, Parfit, XOR) is exactly the trees with hypothetical queries (Remark 3.3).
