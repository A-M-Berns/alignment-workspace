### P01 — Smoking Lesion Steelman (2017-07-02; karma 9; 13 comments) — `01-smoking-lesion-steelman`

**Thesis (two sentences).** The classic Smoking Lesion is ill-posed — "it is assumed that those with a smoking lesion are more likely to smoke, but this is inconsistent with their being EDT agents" — so it cannot justify CDT; a repaired variant (robots that *use* their utility function to decide but are epistemically ignorant of it and cannot introspect, hunted iff they are smoke-lovers) is "solved correctly by CDT, but which is not solved by updateless EDT": ε-exploring evidential smoke-lovers settle into a mixed equilibrium smoking barely above ε, causal smoke-lovers smoke, and "there's not really any more-ignorant position for an updateless agent to back off to". The steelman is offered as a test of the intuition "CDT corrects this error by cutting the relationships which would be screened off if only we could condition on those inputs", with the open question "how this generalizes to other types of lack of self-knowledge".

**Definitions / named rules.** "updateless X" := "the decision theory you get by asking another decision theory to choose a policy as soon as it is born"; "counterfactual" ≡ "causal" for the post; utility-function ignorance = an epistemic module (world-model) that does not know $U$ and a decision module that uses $U$; no introspection ("This blocks things like the tickle defense"). Payoffs — smoke-lover: smokes & killed $-90$, smokes & not killed $+10$, doesn't smoke $-100$ / $0$; non-smoke-lover: $-101$ / $-1$ / $-100$ / $0$.

**Examples.** Smoking Robots; Murder Lesion "ill-posed due to similar problems" (via *Cheating Death in Damascus*); XOR blackmail as "the main effective argument against EDT" but solved by updateless EDT; Newcomb and PD as the SEP's motivations for CDT, judged suspect by "Why Ain'cha Rich?".

**Stated assumptions.** ε-exploration; equal numbers of the two types; the hunter kills all smoke-lovers whatever they do; "a very small amount of noise in agent's utility calculations to explain how this mixed-strategy equilibrium is actually achieved"; "I'll charitably assume that we can find appropriate causal structures"; "I admit this is simply a bad agent design"; "this is just supposed to be a toy example".

**Epistemic status / later edits.** No status header. Retracted in spirit by P02's opening ("After Johannes Treutlein's comment … I had almost entirely given up on CDT") and by P11 ("I've come to think of Troll Bridge as 'the real smoking lesion', since I'm generally not satisfied with how smoking lesion is set up").

### P02 — Smoking Lesion Steelman II (2017-10-02; karma 2; 1 comment) — `02-smoking-lesion-steelman-ii`

**Thesis.** "CDT differs from EDT only in those cases where a parent of the decision node is not directly observed", so two questions decide the matter: whether unobservable parents must exist (Sam Eisenstat's candidate, in person: "the consistency of the logic an agent uses") and whether CDT is the right way to account for them (no: "EDT goes wrong by confusing evidence for influence; CDT goes wrong by discarding the evidence along with the influence" — Treutlein's example, Tom Everitt's point that CDT "fails to make use of all available information when planning", the football-mug fan). Proposal: **CEDT** — $a_{chosen} = \arg\max_a E(do(a)\mid a_{chosen})$, "the chosen action must be the best causal intervention, after taking into account (as evidence) the fact that we chose it" — which "always includes the desired behavior as a possible equilibrium", equilibrium selection unsolved.

**Definitions / rules.** CEDT (first "correlated equilibrium DT", renamed "causal-evidential decision theory" after Scott Garrabrant noted that "many concepts of rationality, including Nash equilibria and logical induction, can be seen as having this property of approving of their output under the condition that they know their output"). Selection candidates: expected value (rejected — "it might select equilibria where its actions hide some information, so as to increase EV"), or the gain "after updating on what action is taken" ("This isn't obviously correct either").

**Examples.** Cosmic ray (a shutdown-indicating action "cannot be an equilibrium"); football mug (every mug and no-mug are equilibria; "I must suppose that the right equilibrium gets selected (by whatever process does that)"); Smoking Lesion Steelman (unique equilibria: non-smoke-lovers don't, smoke-lovers do); Suicidal SL (smoke-lovers "may either smoke or not smoke").

**Assumptions.** The ε-utility tie-break trick to make the fan reliably right without the classic ill-posedness; "As long as a CDT agent knows its own action before it has decided, and is given the right causal representation of the problem, then it at least can get examples like this right, supposing it happens to be in the right fix-point."

**Status.** "This isn't a final solution, by any means"; "I won't be very surprised if the following proposal is already present in the literature" (P03 confirms: it is ratifiability).

**Comments.** tom4everitt (2018-01-08): "Nice writeup. Is one-boxing in Newcomb an equilibria?" — unanswered in the fetched thread.

**v2 pointers (HYPOTHESES).**
1. [derived] CEDT's fixed point is, in v2, "strictly calibrated *and* $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$-approved": strict calibration plus recording forces $P_{s_d}(C(d)) = 1$ (Remark 3.6), and Remark 3.7 reads ratifiability exactly as "the label lies on the branch-generated manifold; its fixed point is strict calibration". CEDT's evaluator is causal, so — unlike $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$, whose domain collapses to $A_d^+ = \{C(d)\}$ at a self-certain state (Remark 3.9) — the maximization stays contentful because $\mathrm{cf}_s$ is total (Definition 17). That is why P02 can "take this information into account" without the evidential collapse.

3. [guess] "Shutdown cannot be an equilibrium" is a Definition 16 statement: the procedure makes the shutdown-indicating event inconsistent.

### P03 — Smoking Lesion Steelman III: Revenge of the Tickle Defense (2017-10-05; karma 6; 2 comments) — `03-smoking-lesion-steelman-iii-revenge-of-the-tickle-defense`

**Thesis.** "my 'CEDT' is just the ratifiability condition" (Jeffrey's; Skyrms's *Ratifiability and the Logic of Decision* shows "a reasonable interpretation of ratifiability … makes Jeffrey's EDT select actions which are CDT-optimal when CDT chooses via a probability distribution which has been updated on knowledge of which action is to be selected"); since pure ratifiable actions may not exist (matching pennies against a skilled predictor), choose **mixed strategies** — "the best-response function is a Kakutani function of the probabilities" — and, following Joyce's *Regret and Instability in Causal Decision Theory*, take "The 'decision', as such, [to be] the formation of the epistemic state about the action". Imposing the same self-knowledge on ε-exploring EDT ("EDT's prior knows what policy EDT selects — IE, it knows what it will do up to the randomness involved in exploration") "will remove most of the correlations which allow it to make EDT-like decisions" — "a very strong version of the tickle defense" that "makes EDT act as CDT advises in all but the most extreme cases, where predictors are perfect" — whence the **Law of Logical Causality**.

**Definitions / rules.** Ratifiability (the chosen action must be *among* the maximal ones after evidential conditioning on the choice); mixed-strategy ratifiability; **Law of Logical Causality (LLC)**: "If conditioning on any event changes the probability an agent assigns to its own action, that event **must** be treated as causally downstream." Consequences: Death and Omega downstream; the steelman's lesion stays upstream "because an agent obeying ratifiability will know its own decision perfectly, screening off the action from such influences"; but "in a different version of Smoking Lesion, where the increased tendency to smoke is more like a trembling hand after the decision is made, the influence is downstream rather than upstream."

**Examples.** Self-hating smoke-lover ($-1000$ on being a smoke-lover) showing EV-selection of equilibria hides information — "we require CDT at a higher level"; matching pennies; Death in Damascus ("CDT still proposes to flee half the time (as if frantically randomizing in the hope that Death will fail)"); Newcomb with a perfect predictor (CDT two-boxes, EDT one-boxes); PD against a copy using different random bits (evidential choice defects under self-knowledge).

**Assumptions.** ε-exploration keeps EDT's conditionals defined; knowledge of own policy up to exploration randomness; Skyrms-style convergent deliberation reaching the equilibrium state ("Skyrms raises interesting issues of the Dutch-bookability of such a process").

**Status.** "The whole structure is far from watertight, and is badly in need of formalization. However, it feels like progress to me."

**Comments.** Diffractor (2017-10-15): what does the LLC say about $\mathrm{CON}(PA)$ in Sam's probabilistic Troll Bridge — "the agent would think CON(PA) would be causally downstream of itself"; the author (2017-10-19): "my intuition is that LLC asserts that the troll, and even CON(PA), is downstream. And, it seems to get into trouble because it treats it as downstream. I also suspect that Troll Bridge will end up formally outside the realm where LLC can be justified by the desire to make ratifiability imply CDT=EDT." — first appearance of Troll Bridge as the LLC's limit (→ P11).

### P04 — Comparing LICDT and LIEDT (2017-10-21; karma 4; 2 comments) — `04-comparing-licdt-and-liedt`

**Thesis.** LIEDT (conditional expectation on the action) two-boxes against an Omega that predicts with the agent's own inductor (Theorem: $E_n(U_n\mid A_n{=}1) = P_n(A_n{=}1) - 1 < P_n(A_n{=}1) = E_n(U_n\mid A_n{=}2)$ [checked]), and LICDT (conditional on *exploring* into the action) pays XOR blackmail with a perfect predictor (Theorem); both one-box/refuse iff the predictor is perfect, "They appear to be following my 'Law of Logical Causality'", because logical induction "implements a version of the ratifiability condition" and exploration-learned counterfactuals "treat predictors who have more information about their own actions than they themselves do as causally downstream". Fairness is defined (same limiting action probabilities ⇒ same limiting per-action expectations, and no difference between exploration and plain actions), LICDT and LIEDT are conjectured to share equilibrium sets on continuous deterministically fair problems, and a third condition — (3) "the environment's behavior doesn't depend on whether you explore" — joins (1) ratifiability and (2) the LLC as the route to CDT=EDT "in a formal analysis which is yet-to-be"; exploration is tied to the chicken rule, proof-length counterfactuals and Troll Bridge.

**Definitions / rules.** Exploration sentences $S_n \leftrightarrow P_n(S_n) < \varepsilon$; LIEDT $:= \arg\max_a E_n(U_n\mid A_n{=}a)$; LICDT $:= \arg\max_a E_n(U_n\mid A_n{=}a \wedge S_n)$ ("Judging actions by what would happen if you took those actions randomly, rather than reliably"); *decision problem* = "a function $U_n(A_n)$ which takes an agent and a step number, and yields a LUV"; *fair*, *continuous fair*, *deterministically fair* ("the payout on instance n are only a function of the action probabilities according to $P_n$ and of the actual action taken"); conditions (1)–(3); the "law of logical counterfactuals" (= LLC); the "boring theorem" (action-only utilities → same limit).

**Examples.** Newcomb with $U_n = 10P_n(A_n{=}1) - I(A_n{=}1)$; XOR blackmail with $U_n = I(D_n)(-I(A_n)-2) + I(\neg D_n)(-I(A_n))$, $L_n = \mathrm{XOR}(D_n, A_n)$; Agent Simulates Predictor; Troll Bridge (proof-length counterfactuals).

**Assumptions.** "Both LICDT and LIEDT include any observations in their deductive state … So, they are updateful decision theories"; of fairness: "I don't expect that this definition is particularly good"; (3) is "a sort of 'randomized controlled trials' assumption, which seems to justify (2)".

**Status.** No header; conjectural throughout ("I wasn't able to prove this").

**Comments.** Tetraspace (2020-07-24): is the LLC "just because anything that's upstream gets screened off, because the agent knows what action it's going to take"? The author (2020-07-28, karma 6): "Not quite" — with a predictor "You don't know what you're going to do, because your deliberation process is unstable … So what screens off everything else is knowledge of the state of your deliberation, not the action itself. In the case of a mixed strategy, you know that you are balanced on a razor's edge"; and a later realization: LICDT/LIEDT "can succeed in XOR Blackmail (failing to send the money), but for an absolutely terrible reason" — under p-chicken with the disaster rarer than $\varepsilon$, "The agent refuses because it thinks it responds to the letter"; in transparent Newcomb "the agent only sees a full box finitely often before being relegated to empty boxes forever … Moral of the story: p-chicken is pretty awful in perfect-predictor cases, particularly when the predictor is interested in what you do conditional on a particular observation."

### P05 — Mixed-Strategy Ratifiability Implies CDT=EDT (2017-10-31; karma 12; 2 comments) — `05-mixed-strategy-ratifiability-implies-cdt-edt-1`

**Thesis.** In a Bayes net handed identically to both theories ("CDT takes the network to encode causal information, while EDT ignores that and just uses the probability distribution encoded by the network"), under the *maximization constraints* (mixed strategies put $P(a) > \varepsilon$ only on maximizers of the respective expectation), the *exploration constraint* (all action probabilities $\ge \varepsilon$), *mixed-strategy ratifiability* (a decision node $D$, parent of the action node $A$, whose state the agent knows) and *mixed-strategy implementability* ($A$ has no parent but $D$), "the consistent choices of mixed strategy are the same for CDT and EDT" (Theorem 1), because "$D$ screens $A$ off from any un-observed parents of $D$". Case 2 (approximate ratifiability, continuous selection functions, Brouwer) drops exploration; Case 3 replaces mixed strategies by "the agent's own estimate of its action probabilities" (deterministic $D \to A$; approximate self-knowledge); moral: "there is basically one notion of counterfactual available … My conclusion is that these are not problems to try and solve by counterfactual reasoning. They are problems to solve with updateless reasoning, bargaining, cooperative oracles, predictable exploration, and so on."

**Definitions / rules.** The four assumptions (P07 later numbers them #1 compatibility, #2 exploration, #3 ratifiability, #4 implementability); Theorem 1; *approximate ratifiability*: $|\ln d(a) - \ln d^*(a)| > \varepsilon$ for some $a$ ⇒ $P(D{=}d) = 0$; *continuous EDT/CDT* selection functions agreeing with max-EU when utility gaps exceed $\varepsilon$. Footnote 1, on choosing actions rather than distributions over actions: "the reasoning in this paragraph is much too fast".

**Examples.** Newcomb and PD (two-box/defect under the theorem); rock-paper-scissors between Alice and Bob ("'mixed strategies' can emerge naturally from following incentives against predictability").

**Assumptions.** As listed; "It's easy to see that CDT=EDT if all the causal parents of an agent's decision are observed"; the "agent's abstract algorithm" node as the standard hidden parent; "the ratifiability constraint is something which CDTers have argued for independent reasons … So denial of this assumption seems to be an unavailable response for CDTers."

**Status / edits.** Edited later to record reduced optimism (P06: "I edited my previous post to note that I'm now much less optimistic about the direction I was going in") — absent from the fetched LessWrong body [checked by grep]; [reconstructed] that the note was on the IAFF copy. Self-assessment: "The proofs above are just versions of the tickle defense for EDT … Philosophers seem to have a fairly good understanding of how CDT equals EDT when introspection is possible; see SEP on objections to CDT."

**Comments.** habryka (LaTeX support). Charlie Steiner (2017-11-02, karma 3): "This seems to be a bigger modification than just 'be introspective.' An EDT agent that starts out by searching through different Actions … upon introspection, might construct a causal graph that includes its Decision. Without further modification, when it sets an Action, this is then excellent knowledge about the Decision node, which is then also evidence about the parents of the Decision node, and we still have ordinary EDT … it seems like this ratification modification is doing something unusual with the Decision node that involves setting it but not conditioning on it!" ### P12 — Dutch-Booking CDT: Revised Argument (posted 2020-06-11, reposted 2020-10-27; karma 51; 24 comments) — `12-dutch-booking-cdt-revised-argument`

**Header.** "This post has benefited greatly from discussion with Sam Eisenstat, Caspar Oesterheld, and Daniel Kokotajlo." Reposted by habryka after a LessWrong API bug hid the June version.

**Thesis.** For any action $a$ with $P(a) > 0$ and $\mathrm{edt}(a) := E(U\mid\mathrm{Act}{=}a) \ne \mathrm{cdt}(a) := E(U\mid do(\mathrm{Act}{=}a))$, a two-step modification Dutch-books "CDT" in the broad sense ("using counterfactuals to evaluate expected value of actions … TDT counts as 'a CDT' in this sense"): *immediately before* the decision the bookie sells the bet $B$ — "In the case that $A=a$, the seller of this certificate owes the purchaser of this certificate $(\mathrm{cdt}(a)-U)\cdot s$", $s$ the sign of $\mathrm{cdt}(a)-\mathrm{edt}(a)$ — for $2d$, which the agent values at $P(\mathrm{Act}{=}a)\cdot|\mathrm{cdt}(a)-\mathrm{edt}(a)|$ "because the agent is betting ahead of time"; *as the agent is making the decision* the bookie offers $d$ to buy $B$ back, and "since the agent is reasoning during its action, it is evaluating possible actions according to $\mathrm{cdt}(a)$", under which $B$ is worth $0$ whether $a$ or any other action is considered — "So the agent is down $d$ utilons for no gain!" [checked]. Interpreted as "the two had better agree", not as EDT over CDT: "the Troll Bridge problem strongly favors CDT whose expectations agree with EDT over EDT", so the book is "a strong constraint on a theory of (logical) counterfactuals". The **current take** (four theses): (1) "We should think of counterfactuals as having real, independent truth … $\mathrm{cdt}(a)$ does not reduce to $\mathrm{edt}(a)$"; (2) "Counterfactuals are subjective in the same way that probabilities are subjective … there are many different subjectively valid beliefs"; (3) "counterfactual beliefs should almost always equal conditional beliefs, at least when the antecedent has positive probability"; (4) "conditional beliefs act a whole lot more like stereotypical CDT counterfactuals than most people seem to give them credit for. Something can't correlate with your action unless it contains information you don't have about your action." Also: an EDT dynamic-consistency argument for problems where "the payoff depends only on actions actually taken, not on your policy" (rules out counterfactual mugging, transparent Newcomb, Parfit's hitchhiker, XOR; "does not rule out the original Newcomb problem"); for probability-zero actions only "CDT strictly prefers that the agent be Dutch Booked rather than just do $a$"; and "TDT either two-boxes, or, is vulnerable to the Dutch Book I construct" against a strong-but-not-omniscient predictor. Quotes Paul Christiano's "simple argument" and "There are many tricky questions in decision theory. In this post, I'll argue that the choice between CDT and EDT isn't one of them." [checked against the live post, 2026-09-03]. "it only represents my view as of November or early December of 2019."

**Assumptions (stated).** Nonzero probability of $a$ ("a truly important requirement, rather than an artefact of the argument"; justifications: ε-exploration — "I don't like invoking that as much as I used to" — the chicken rule plus non-dogmatism, or "a free-will principle"); action probabilities preserved when actions are split ("part of the problem setup … a bookie who can offer these bets without changing anything"; only $P(a)+P(a') = P_{\text{old}}(a)$ matters); counterfacting on accepting the bet changes no other probabilities ("the argument definitely assumes 'reasonable counterfactual evaluations' in some sense"). Objections addressed: the bookie does not cheat ("The agent knows everything the bookie knows, from the beginning"); nothing is learned between the steps ("As the agent is evaluating a particular action, it does not 'know' it will carry out that action — it is only considering what would happen if it carried out that action!"); "agents WILL know what they'll do" — conceded: "if the agent really does know exactly what it will do, then yeah, the argument really does collapse to saying its evidential expectations should equal its counterfactual expectations for that one action … if $P(A)=1$, then we had better have $P(X|do(A))=P(X)$"; and the decision-theory / decision-procedure distinction — "A decision theory is a normative theory about what an agent should do. This means that CDT, as a normative theory, can produce recommendations for non-CDT agents" (MacAskill's bomb; Soares's "decisions are for making bad outcomes inconsistent") — which "offers further support for the argument I'm making."

### P13 — My Current Take on Counterfactuals (2021-04-09; karma 56; 57 comments) — `13-my-current-take-on-counterfactuals`

**Header.** "[Epistemic status: somewhat lower confidence based on the fact that I haven't worked out a detailed theory along the lines I've suggested, yet.]" "I've felt like the problem of counterfactuals is 'mostly settled' (modulo some math working out) for about a year"; "It's a brain-dump … Think of it as a frozen snapshot of a work in progress."

**The post's own summary.** "1. I can Dutch-book any agent whose subjective counterfactual expectations don't equal their conditional expectations. I conclude that counterfactual expectations should equal conditional probabilities. IE, evidential decision theory (EDT) gives the correct counterfactuals. 2. However, the Troll Bridge problem is real and concerning: EDT agents are doing silly things here. 3. Fortunately, there appear to be ways out. One way out is to maintain that subjective counterfactual expectations should equal conditional expectations while also maintaining a distinction between those two things: counterfactuals are not computed from conditionals. As we shall see, this allows us to ensure that the two are always equal in real situations, while strategically allowing them to differ in some hypothetical situations (such as Troll Bridge). This seems to solve all the problems! 4. However, I have not yet concretely constructed any way out."

**Thesis.** The *subjective theory of counterfactuals* (Sam Eisenstat's: "starting from the assumption that a very broad range of counterfactuals can be subjectively valid, we should seek the few 'coherence' constraints which distinguish rational counterfactual beliefs from irrational"); **Permissive CDT (PCDT)** — a basic counterfactual $C(A|B)$, the axiom $C(A|B)\,\&\,B \to A$, further axioms "weak enough to allow 2-boxing in Newcomb as subjectively valid", no chicken rule or forced exploration, VOI-exploration instead — "I claim PCDT is wrong" (the Dutch book; "Sam … eventually … came to agree"); the **Inferential Theory** ("hypothetical reasoning and counterfactual reasoning are one and the same" — "the core intuition behind EDT") "is probably wrong" because "the inference [action a implies bad stuff] & [action a] ⊢ [bad stuff] is valid" and Troll Bridge goes through; the way between: "counterfactual reasoning should match hypothetical reasoning **in the real world**, but shouldn't necessarily match it **hypothetically**" — **RCDT** (restrictive counterfactual DT): constraints "sufficient to guarantee that the counterfactuals we eventually learn will be in line with the conditional probabilities we eventually learn", but which "must not force counterfactuals to 'respect logic' in the sense that would force failure on Troll Bridge. For example, If A→B implies A↦B, then a proof that crossing the bridge is bad could stop us from crossing it. We can't let RCDT do that." The Newcomb learning example — PCH ("physical causality hypothesis": $C(U|\text{1-box}) = 10\,P(\text{1-box})$, $C(U|\text{2-box}) = 1 + 10\,P(\text{1-box})$) versus LCH ($10$ vs $1$) — shows PCH can dominate forever because "both of those propositions stick around": after observing 1-boxing and $+10$, "the other counterfactual — the one on 2-boxing — moves to +11 … the belief about what reward we could have gotten can stay around 11 forever." Options: more axioms, or "conditional contracts" not responsive to proofs of $B\to A$ ("a radical rejection of the ratio formula for conditional probability"), with the chicken rule "most likely" still needed. Alignment applications (human feedback on counterfactuals; the human as a logical inductor).
