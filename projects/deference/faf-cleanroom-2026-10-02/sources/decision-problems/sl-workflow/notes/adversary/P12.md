### 1.1 P12-5 — "under Definition 6 the book condemns the deviation-calibrated (one-boxing) cf and spares classical CDT; under shared seed the reverse" — WOUNDED (holds only in the normative-advice reading, at labels the CDT agent with that cf never produces; at CDT's own fixed points the asymmetry is carried by the *residue*, not by the book)

*Recomputed* [checked, `adv_p12.out` §A]. Opaque Newcomb $p = \tfrac34$, $L = 4$, $S = 1$, label $q = C(\mathrm{one})$. Definition 6: conditioning $= (1+2q,\ 2+2q)$ = forcing (R2-real); deviation (R1) $= (3, 2)$. Shared seed: conditioning $= (3,2)$; forcing $= (1+2q, 2+2q)$. The notes' numbers all reproduce ($\Delta = \tfrac12$ at $q = \tfrac12$, $\tfrac38$ at $q = \tfrac14$, on the side the notes say).

*What the notes did not compute*: whether the booked label is one the procedure $\mathrm{CDT}$ with that cf would itself output — i.e. $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$ approval (Definition 18: $\mathrm{supp}\,C(d) \subseteq \arg\max_a V^a_{s_d}(a)$). Both cf's have *strict* argmaxes at every $q$ (classical: two; deviation: one), so every properly mixed label is $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$-disapproved under both. Table (both semantics): a label is bookable at a positive act **iff** it is not $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$-approved under the cf in question. At the two fixed points — classical $q = 0$, deviation $q = 1$ — the taken act has $\Delta = 0$ under **both** semantics: at $q=0$, conditioning(two) $= 2$ = forcing(two) $= 2 + 2\cdot 0$ under shared seed as under Definition 6; at $q=1$, conditioning(one) $= 3$ = deviation(one) under both. So **no CDT agent with either cf is Dutch-booked at its own calibrated fixed point in opaque Newcomb, under either run semantics** — the post's own collapse concession ("for that one action") plus Remark 3.6, which the notes state as P12-11 but do not carry into P12-5.

*Where the asymmetry actually lives* [checked, §A′]: in the probability-zero residue (P12-15). Under Definition 6 at the deviation cf's fixed point $q = 1$: $\mathrm{R3}(\mathrm{two}) = \lim_\varepsilon (2 + 2q_\varepsilon) = 4 \neq c_{\mathrm{two}} = 2$ — the residue bites the one-boxing cf; at classical CDT's fixed point $q = 0$: $\mathrm{R3}(\mathrm{one}) = 1 = c_{\mathrm{one}}$ — no residue. Under shared seed the mirror: classical at $q=0$ has $\mathrm{R3}(\mathrm{one}) = 3 \neq 1$ (residue bites), deviation at $q=1$ has $\mathrm{R3}(\mathrm{two}) = 2 = c_{\mathrm{two}}$ (none). So the run-semantics relativity is real, but at fixed points it is the "moderately damning" dominated preference, not a sure loss.

*Verdict.* WOUNDED. The headline must read: "under Definition 6, at a properly mixed label that $\mathrm{CDT}$-with-the-deviation-cf would not itself produce, the book (in the post's normative reading, 'CDT recommends a Dutch book to a non-CDT agent') runs against the deviation cf and not against the forcing cf; under shared seed the reverse; at either evaluator's own deterministic fixed point neither is booked under either semantics, and the asymmetry survives only as the residue." The notes' sentence "classical CDT has $\Delta = 0$ (not bookable) while the deviation-calibrated cf … bookable" is true and misleading: the deviation-calibrated *agent* is never bookable in Newcomb. The Import 1 text ("classical CDT is spared and a deviation-calibrated cf is booked") must carry the mixed-label/advice clause.

### 1.2 P12-4 — "the book is one procedure ($\mathrm{CDT}$) at two points; 'CDT recommends a Dutch book to a non-CDT agent' is $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}(C^{\mathrm{book}}, B')$; numerically = $\mathrm{EDT}$ at $d_B$ under label honesty" — the rendering KILLED, the reading WOUNDED

(i) *$T_{\mathrm{CDT}}(C^{\mathrm{book}}, B')$ is false exactly where the book runs.* $C^{\mathrm{book}}(d')(x\text{-sell}) = C(d)(x)$ by (A2). At $d'$, $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$ requires $\mathrm{supp}\,C^{\mathrm{book}}(d') \subseteq \arg\max_{(x,\sigma)} V^{(x,\sigma)}(x,\sigma) = \{x^\ast\text{-sell} : x^\ast \in \arg\max_x c_x\}$. For a properly mixed base label and a cf with a strict argmax (every case in §1.1), the base support is not contained in the argmax, so $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}(C^{\mathrm{book}}, B')$ fails [derived from Definition 18; instance: deviation cf at $q = \tfrac12$, $\arg\max = \{\mathrm{one}\text{-sell}\}$, support $\{\mathrm{one}\text{-sell}, \mathrm{two}\text{-sell}\}$]. What the post means by "CDT recommends" is *per-coordinate*: at $d_B$, buy is $\mathrm{CDT}$'s maximizer; at $d'$, sell dominates keep for every base act (P12-2). That is a statement about the bet/side coordinates given the agent's base behaviour, not the advocacy predicate on the whole procedure. It is expressible in v2 as: for every $x$ in the base support, $\mathrm{CDT}$'s maximizer over $\{x\text{-keep}, x\text{-sell}\}$ is $x\text{-sell}$, and $\mathrm{CDT}(d_B) = \mathrm{buy}$. The notes' formula should be replaced by that.

(ii) *"Numerically = EDT at $d_B$" holds only for the myopic state.* Bet-locality (A3) fixes the supposed law of (act, side, world) at the state's actual law. Under strict or locally masked calibration at $d_B$ the actual law has $P(\mathrm{sell}) = 1$ (label honesty puts all mass on sell copies), so $V^{\mathrm{buy}}_{s_{d_B}}(\mathrm{buy}) = \mathbb E[r] - 2\delta + \delta = \mathbb E[r] - \delta$ [checked, §G] — the *sophisticated* price, and $\mathrm{EDT}$ at that state gives the same. The post's Step-1 number $\mathbb E[r] - 2\delta + \Delta$ requires the state's law of the side coordinate to be "keep" on the $a$-branch — a state *not* calibrated under the true continuation (global masking, or an uncalibrated $\mathrm{cf}_{s_{d_B}}$; C2-16 says the same, `threads/C2.md:179`). So the identity "bet-local CDT at $d_B$ = EDT at $d_B$" is right, but which number both give depends on whether the $d_B$ state knows the continuation; the notes' P12-3 knows this and P12-4 does not say it. WOUNDED with the hypothesis "for a $d_B$ state whose law of the side coordinate is myopic (keep)".

### 1.3 P12-3 — "as written the book binds only a myopic bettor; with P09's 'regardless' reverse bet a sophisticated bettor buys iff $\Delta > 2\delta$ and loses $\delta$ surely relative to decline-both" — SURVIVES (one textual over-reading noted)

*Recomputed* [checked, §G]: with $R$ (receive $\delta$, owe $(c-r)s$ on base $= a$) taken at $d'$ regardless — $\mathrm{CDT}$ takes it, supposed value $\delta$ under every base act since $(c_a - c_a)s = 0$ — the sophisticated $d_B$ state compares buy ($\mathbb E[r] - \delta$, the $B$ and $R$ settlements cancelling leaf-wise) with decline ($\mathbb E[r] + \delta - \Delta$): buy iff $\Delta > 2\delta$; decline-both is available and pays $r$ on every leaf. The literal offer ("I will buy the bet $B$ from you") gives buy $= \mathbb E[r] - \delta < \mathbb E[r]$ = decline: the sophisticated bettor declines. The Ahmed-via-O&C citation is verbatim (`:147–153`), and the "regardless" clause is indeed P09's ("we offer the second transaction whether the agent agrees to the first or not", confirmed against `threads/P09.md:25, :39`). *Over-reading:* the notes say the post's sentence "If that were the case (as it is in my scenario), the agent should not have accepted the bet in the first place" "concedes the sophisticated agent declines". Read in place (`:126`), it is the standard normative gloss of a diachronic book — the acceptance was irrational *because* predictably resold — and says nothing about what a CDT agent that foresees the resale *does*. Drop that gloss; the verdict stands without it.

### 1.4 P12-6 — "on the miniature, book + residue ⇒ CDT's calibrated fixed points = EDT's tremble-limit fixed point; exactly three unbookable $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$-approved pairs before the residue" — SURVIVES on the miniature; the "exactly three" is a grid artefact; the general "theorem" is definitional

*Recomputed* [checked, §C], cf grid in sixths on $[-1, 2]$ instead of the notes' $[0,1]$ (`p12_dutchbook.py:251`, `range(0, 7)`): at $q = \tfrac23$ the unique survivor is $(\tfrac23, \tfrac23)$ ✓; at $q = 1$ every $(0, c_b)$ with $c_b \le 0$ survives (seven on my grid), at $q = 0$ every $(c_a, 0)$ with $c_a \le 0$ — the notes' "exactly $\{(0,0)\}$" at the vertices is an artefact of a non-negative grid; v2 puts no sign constraint on supposed desirabilities. The residue (pin the null act to R3: $\mathrm{R3}(b) = 1$ at $q=1$, $\mathrm{R3}(a) = 2$ at $q = 0$) kills all of them, so the *conclusion* ($\tfrac23$ alone) is unaffected. *The general statement is a definition unpacked:* "unbookable at every positive act" **is** Definition 20's evidential criterion restricted to $A_d^+$ (the book's hypothesis is $V^a_{s_d}(a) \ne V_{s_d}(a)$ at some $a \in A_d^+$); given $\mathrm{supp}\,C(d) \subseteq A_d^+$ (strict calibration at a recorded point), $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$-approval with $c = e$ on $A_d^+$ implies $\mathrm{supp}\,C(d) \subseteq \arg\max_{A_d^+} e$ = $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$-approval, and conversely any $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$-approved $C$ is $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$-approved for $c := e$ on $A_d^+$ and $c \le \max e$ off it [derived]. So "book + residue ⇒ CDT's fixed points = EDT's" needs no enumeration and no C2 theorem: the book characterizes the evidential criterion at positive acts (the post's claim), the residue at null acts. The handoff to C2 should say "definitional once unbookability is unpacked; the content is the two pricing arguments", not "theorem candidate".

### 1.5 P12-14 — "the EDT dynamic-consistency theorem is the recording/veridicality regime (Definition 7 + Proposition 3); its Newcomb clause holds under shared seed only — under Definition 6 with masked/limit states EDT two-boxes while the prior-level evaluator one-boxes, so EDT is dynamically inconsistent in Newcomb under independent redraws" — refinement (b) KILLED; headline WOUNDED

*The kill* [checked, `adv_p12.out` §B]. The post's theorem is about one evidential evaluator at two times, both conditioning on *actions* ("thinking about possible choices ahead of time, the contribution to early expected utility is exactly the expected utility that action will be assigned later", `:170`). In v2 the early evaluator is a prior/earlier state conditioning on the live act event: $\mathbb E_\nu[r \mid \mathrm{live} = a]$. Under Definition 6 in opaque Newcomb that is $(1+2q,\ 2+2q)$ — the live draw is independent of the simulation's — and the late strictly calibrated state at the real node gives the same $(1+2q, 2+2q)$; under shared seed both give $(3,2)$. Same ranking at both times, both semantics: **act-conditioning EDT is dynamically consistent in opaque Newcomb under Definition 6 as under shared seed** (it two-boxes at both times under Definition 6 and one-boxes at both under shared seed). The evaluator that one-boxes under Definition 6 is the *disposition* one — $V_B(C[d\mapsto a]) = (3,2)$, i.e. $\mathrm{UDT}_{s^\circ,\rho}$ on $\rho$-events or Theorem 2's all-instance deviation — which is not "EDT ahead of time" but the standard EDT/UDT split (Remark 3.10's OC-vs-SSC divergence at a simulation-bearing point). Calling that divergence "EDT is dynamically inconsistent" mislabels it. The post's Newcomb clause therefore SURVIVES in v2 under both semantics; the notes' (b) does not.

*The wound on the headline.* The notes identify the theorem's hypothesis with Definition 7 recording and then observe that opaque Newcomb fails recording (the simulation node carries $d$ on every run — true, Definition 7's exactly-one clause) "although the post's criterion holds". That is a sign the identification is too strong, not that the post is wrong: the argument needs Proposition 3's bridge ($\mathrm{occ}(d) = \{\lambda \models O_d\}$, trivially true at $O_d = \top$) and that the early and late evaluators condition on the same act event (the live draw), which Definition 6 supplies whether or not a simulation node is present. Recording's exactly-one and action-veridicality clauses are what CA-12′ needs for *label-independence of act values*, a different theorem. Restate P12-14 as: "the post's condition is Proposition 3's bridge plus act-event = live draw; Definition 7 recording is sufficient, not necessary, and Newcomb is the witness of the gap." The (a) refinement (CA-1′/CA-2′: the strong form needs trembles or downstream-faithful masking off-path) is consistent with the ledger texts I read and SURVIVES as derived.

### 1.6 P12-17 — "P03's Law of Logical Causality (predictors downstream) is the deviation/shared-seed referent; under Definition 6 the book penalizes an LLC-obeying cf; the sequence's strong tickle defence and its Dutch book conflict under independent redraws" — KILLED (misreads the LLC's antecedent)

P03 states the law conditionally (`posts/03-…md:65`, verbatim): "*If conditioning on any event changes the probability an agent assigns to its own action, that event must be treated as causally downstream.*" And `:71`: "It does not mean that you must treat all things correlated with your decision as downstream. In Smoking Lesion Steelman, the lesion is still upstream of your decision. The Law of Logical Causality does not touch it, because an agent obeying ratifiability will know its own decision perfectly, screening off the action from such influences." The notes render the LLC as "predictors downstream" unconditionally. Under Definition 6 at a strictly calibrated point with a properly mixed label the prediction is an independent sample of $C(d)$, so conditioning on it does **not** change $P_{s_d}(a) = C(d)(a)$ (Lemma 3; the state knows the mixture): the LLC's antecedent fails, the law puts the predictor *upstream*, and the LLC-obeying cf is the forcing cf $(1+2q, 2+2q)$ = the calibrated conditionals — **unbookable**. Under shared seed conditioning on the prediction pins the act, the LLC puts the predictor downstream, the cf is the deviation $(3,2)$ = the shared-seed conditionals — again unbookable. The LLC is, by construction, "downstream iff evidentially relevant to the act": it is the evidential criterion in causal-graph clothing, and it *agrees* with the book under both semantics. P03's "you must treat Omega as causally downstream of you in the perfect-predictor version of Newcomb" (`:69`) is the shared-seed / deterministic case where the antecedent holds. So there is no LLC-vs-book tension in the sequence at the level P12-17 asserts; the sequence-map's Q-D remains a question about *referents*, not about the LLC. The corresponding sentence inside P12-13 ("Consequence for the sequence: P03's Law of Logical Causality … and P12's Dutch book pull against each other under Definition 6") dies with it, and the handoff of P12-17 to C2/L2 is misaddressed (§4).
