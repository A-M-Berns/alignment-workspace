**Located error 2 — the Definition-6 column is not XOR Blackmail as stipulated; "false under Definition 6" is a change of question.** Levinstein–Soares (READ, `:158–172`): the letter says "I have sent you this letter iff exactly one of the following is true: (i) the rumor is false, and you are going to pay me \$1,000 upon receiving this letter; or (ii) the rumor is true, and you will not pay me upon receiving this letter … The predictor then predicts what the agent would do upon receiving the letter, and sends the agent the letter iff exactly one of (i) or (ii) is true. **Thus, the claim made by the letter is true.**" The FDT paper (READ, PDF p. 24–25) has the same sentence and footnote 13: "To simplify exposition, we will assume here that the predictor is infallible." Under Definition 6 the letter's claim is true on a letter-run iff the live draw equals the simulation's draw. I computed [checked, `adv_p06.out`]:
$P(\text{claim false}\mid L{=}1) = \dfrac{q(1-q)}{\delta(1-q)+(1-\delta)q}$ ($=\tfrac12$ at $q=\tfrac12$);
$P(\text{claim false}\mid L{=}1,\mathrm{act}{=}\mathrm{pay}) = p(q) = P(D{=}1\mid L{=}1,\mathrm{pay})$ — **identically**: given the letter and a live "pay", the disaster is present exactly when the simulation said "refuse", i.e. exactly when the honest predictor's letter lied;
for a trembling refuser ($q = \varepsilon/2$): $P(\text{claim false}\mid L{=}1,\mathrm{pay}) \to 1$ as $\varepsilon\to0$.
So the Definition-6 quantity that drives the column — "given the letter, paying is no evidence against termites" — is computed on runs where the letter's claim is false, and in the tremble limit around the refusing policy it is computed *only* on such runs. Definition 14 gives the precise statement: the abstract problem $\Sigma_{\mathrm{XOR}}$ carries the stipulation "the letter's claim is true" (equivalently "the predictor is infallible"), and no Definition-6 tree with a stochastic label is an instantiation of it; for deterministic labels the two semantics coincide on every tree (SE-1, "same run"), so the Def-6 column has *distinctive* content only where it is not XOR Blackmail. The thread half-concedes this in P06-A2 ("the literature's XOR Blackmail is the first reading") but frames the abstract problem as "underspecified for stochastic procedures"; it is not underspecified — the truthfulness clause decides it. Nor is the Def-6 simulator the FDT paper's "fallible predictor": a fixed-accuracy predictor of the realized act ($6′$ plus an independent flip with accuracy $p$) still makes evidential choice pay — at $p = 0.99$, $\delta = \tfrac1{100}$, $q=\tfrac12$: $P(D{=}1\mid L,\mathrm{pay}) = \tfrac1{9802}$, $P(D{=}1\mid L,\mathrm{refuse}) = \tfrac12$, pay minus refuse $\approx +4.99\times10^5$ [checked, `adv_p06b.out`], exactly Soares's "the problem goes through fine if the prediction is only correct 99% of the time" (READ, `soares-2017-…:150`) — whereas the Def-6 simulator's accuracy about the realized act is $\tfrac12$ at $q = \tfrac12$ and, *given* act $=$ pay, $1 - p(q) \to 0$ as $q\to0$: its errors are concentrated on the act being evaluated. Three predictors, three abstract problems; only (A) and (B) are the literature's XOR Blackmail. Added hypothesis under which P06-1's "false under Definition 6" survives as written: "XOR Blackmail" read as the *disposition-sampling* problem whose letter is false with probability $p(q)$ on pay-runs — which is P05-A2's/P06-A2's "perfect simulator", a different Definition-14 object. Verdict: WOUNDED (change of question), consistent with `adversary/P05.md` §3 on P05-19.

**What survives of P06-1.** "True under Definition 6′" — CONFIRMED, and 6′ is the literature's problem (both stipulation clauses point to it; the FDT paper's Fiona graph, PDF p. 25, has one node FDT$(P^F,G^F,\text{letter})$ parenting both Predictor and Act — literally the post's $A'$).
