## 1. Claim 1 — P04-A and everything hung on it

### 1.1 What the thread claims

P04-A (§3.1 of the notes): on every concrete problem, under Definition 6 and under Definition 6′, at every $\varepsilon>0$ and every label, $\mathbb E[r\mid O_d,\text{live drew }a,\text{tremble}]=\mathbb E[r\mid O_d,\text{live drew }a]$. Consequences drawn there and repeated in §3.6, §4 (Import 1), §7, §8 and the Handoffs: P04-7 "CONFIRMED and strengthened … the exception is *unrepresentable*, not merely 'unfair'"; P04-10/11 "CONFIRMED trivially on every tree"; "P04's LICDT is not a CDT; it is LIEDT with a redundant conditioning clause"; the sequence-map's hypothesis 2 "REFUTED as a rendering"; P04-L′ "LICDT $=$ LIEDT: always, with no hypothesis"; Import 1 proposes a v2 Remark 3.2′: "an environment that pays an explored action differently from a chosen one is unrepresentable."

### 1.2 What is right

The identity as literally stated — for the two seed statuses named, with the tremble folded into $C^\varepsilon(d)$ as one draw — is correct and I reproduced it on the probe-Newcomb and XOR under both semantics [checked, `adv_p04.out` §A, §B: `equal: True` on every row; the thread's five-tree battery I did not rerun beyond these two]. The proof in the notes is sound *for those two statuses*: the flag and the action are one draw, so the flag's seed status is the action's.

### 1.3 The refutation of the generality: the deliberate-shared status [checked]

P04's own stipulation is that exploration is pseudorandom "in the sense that the logical inductor cannot predict it much better than to assign probability $\epsilon$ to exploration (and therefore, neither can any poly-time computable predictor)" (post, §"Both LICDT and LIEDT use a logical inductor"), while the deliberate action is a deterministic function of the shared inductor that a predictor may well compute — the post's own phrase is "predictors who have more information about their own actions than they themselves do". That is a *two-status* randomness: the deliberate draw is potentially shared with a predictor, the exploration coin is fresh to everyone. The thread's rendering fuses both into one seed and can therefore only offer "both fresh" (Definition 6) or "both shared" (Definition 6′).

The two-status case is a v2 tree under Definition 6′ **as literally stated** in P2:seeds ("a run draws once and independently for each queried point $d$ a seed $a_d\sim C(d)$"), with no new machinery: take two points $d=(s,\top,\{1,2\})$ and $d'=(s',\top,\{1,2\})$ with $C(d')=\mathrm{Unif}$ fixed; tree: root $=$ hypothetical query of $d$ (Omega's simulation) $\to$ chance node with $\beta=(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)$: the $\varepsilon$-edge queries $d'$ (the exploration action, fresh), the $(1-\varepsilon)$-edge queries $d$ (the deliberate action — the same seed as the simulation's); leaf payoff $10\cdot\mathbf 1[\text{sim}{=}1]-\mathbf 1[\text{act}{=}1]$ with the act coordinate recording whichever of $d,d'$ was drawn. The leaf-world can carry an "explored" coordinate (the chance edge) — so the tree *does* read the flag, contrary to Import 1. (Equivalently in the thread's own (flag, action) notation: probe $=$ the deliberate draw $D\sim C(d)$; live $=(0,D)$ w.p. $1-\varepsilon$, $(1,\mathrm{Unif})$ w.p. $\varepsilon$ — my `delib` semantics.)

Values at the state strictly calibrated for the run law ($q:=C(d)(1)$ the deliberate label; `adv_p04.out` §A):

$$\mathrm{LICDT}(1)=10q-1,\qquad \mathrm{LICDT}(2)=10q\qquad(\text{two-box at every }q,\varepsilon:\ \text{the RCT counterfactual, FA-19′'s classical CDT}),$$
$$\mathrm{LIEDT}(1)=\frac{9(1-\tfrac\varepsilon2)q-\tfrac\varepsilon2(1-q)}{(1-\varepsilon)q+\tfrac\varepsilon2},\qquad \mathrm{LIEDT}(2)=\frac{5\varepsilon q}{(1-\varepsilon)(1-q)+\tfrac\varepsilon2}.$$

At $q=\tfrac12,\varepsilon=\tfrac1{10}$: LIEDT $=(\tfrac{17}2,\tfrac12)$ — one-box; LICDT $=(4,5)$ — two-box. **Different evaluators, opposite verdicts, on P04's Newcomb.** Fixed points (D2 at fixed $\varepsilon$, supp $\subseteq$ argmax): LICDT has the unique fixed point $q=0$ (two-box; the difference is $-1$ identically). LIEDT has three: $q=0$ (two-box, stable), an unstable root at $q\approx0.0058$ ($\varepsilon=\tfrac1{10}$; $0.00056$ at $\tfrac1{100}$) and a **stable mostly-one-boxing** root at $q\approx0.9942$ ($0.99944$ at $\varepsilon=\tfrac1{100}$), where the sign of LIEDT$(1)-$LIEDT$(2)$ is $+$ below and $-$ above ($+8$ at $q=\tfrac12$, $-1$ at $q\in\{0,1\}$). So on this tree LIEDT has a one-boxing equilibrium LICDT lacks — the classical CDT/EDT split, realized by exactly the mechanism P04 designed LICDT for ("Judging actions by what would happen if you took those actions randomly, rather than reliably, is supposed to remove the kind of correlation which makes EDT … one-box in Newcomb").

XOR under the same status (`§B`): LICDT's pay$-$refuse is $-c$ at every $q$; LIEDT's differs at interior $q$ but equals $-c$ at $q\in\{0,1\}$, so both fixed points refuse — P04-3's theorem indeed needs the *fully* shared seed (the thread's P04-D stands, §3 below); but the evaluators are again distinct functionals.

A defensible replacement: "Under Definition 6, and under Definition 6′ with the tremble folded into $C^\varepsilon(d)$, the tree reads only the drawn action, so exploration-conditional and act-conditional evaluators coincide. An exploration mechanism whose randomness a predictor does not share — P04's stipulation — is modelled as a chance node routing to a separate point, and there the two evaluators separate (LICDT: the RCT/forcing counterfactual; LIEDT: a mixture)."

### 3.1 Claim 5 — P04-H / P04-I (P04-8/9): the type of "decision problem" and fairness
