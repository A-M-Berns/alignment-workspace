## Claim A — FR-11 (+ FR-13, FR-14): tremble-EDT-consistency on strongly fair, fully recorded trees implies $V$-optimality ("EDT = UDT, no Newcomblike problems"). **VERDICT: WOUNDED.**

**A.1 The kill shot on the letter [checked].** The theorem as stated is false; the hole is queried points whose observation is realized by *no* leaf-world.

Counterexample. $\mathcal{E}$ generated by $\mathrm{act} \in \{a, b, \bot\}$ and $\mathrm{coin} \in \{H, T\}$. Tree: a single decision node $q$ with point $d = (s, O_d, \{a, b\})$, $O_d = \{\mathrm{coin} = T\}$; leaves $a \to (\omega_a, 0)$, $b \to (\omega_b, 1)$ with $\omega_a \models a \wedge H$, $\omega_b \models b \wedge H$. No chance nodes (EC and pruning vacuous). $B$ is strongly fair (singleton fiber). $B$ **records at $d$ for every procedure** — Definition 7's clauses all begin "every run whose leaf-world satisfies $O_d$…", and no run's leaf-world satisfies $O_d$, so recording holds vacuously; FRec holds.

Take $C = \delta_a$. For every $\varepsilon$: $\nu_{B, C^\varepsilon}(O_d) = 0$, so Definition 8's parenthetical ("queried points with $\nu(O_d) = 0$ are unconstrained") leaves $s_d$ free. Choose $P_{s_d}$ = the true world-marginal ($\tfrac12$ on each of $\omega_a, \omega_b$ — the *beliefs* need not even be fantasy) and the desirability $V_{s_d}$ with atom values $V(\omega_a) = 7$, $V(\omega_b) = 0$ (any atom values satisfy the averaging axiom). Then $B$ is strictly OC-calibrated for $C^\varepsilon$ (vacuously at $d$, and $d$ is the only point), $A_d^+ = \{a, b\}$, $\arg\max V_{s_d} = \{a\} \supseteq \mathrm{supp}\, C(d)$, so $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}(C, B_\varepsilon)$ holds: $C$ is tremble-EDT-consistent in exactly FR-11's sense. But $V_B(C) = 0 < 1 = \max_{C'} V_B(C')$. This simultaneously refutes FR-14's "N1 fails" on the fair + FRec class ($\delta_a$ and $\delta_b$ are both tremble-EDT-consistent here, one non-optimal).

Note the counterexample is the $\varepsilon$-uniform twin of the notes' own §3.2 fantasy-state device; trembles rescue their example only because $O_2$ there is tremble-reachable. Nothing in FRec forbids tremble-unreachable observations.

**Exact repair.** Add the hypothesis: *every queried point's observation is satisfied by some leaf-world* (with zero-probability chance edges pruned and full-support trembles, this yields $\nu_{B, C^\varepsilon}(O_d) > 0$ at every queried $d$). Under the repaired hypotheses I attacked Steps 1–3 and failed:

- Step 1 [derived, verified]: with $O_d$ realized, recording makes the node met on $O_d$-runs subtree-veridical, and strong fairness (the isomorphism preserves leaf-worlds) propagates subtree-veridicality to the *whole* fiber; coverage then gives $\mathrm{occ}(d) = \{\lambda \models O_d\}$ a.s.; recording's action clauses give $\{\lambda \models a \wedge O_d\} = $ "reached a $d$-node, drew $a$" a.s.; the occurrence weights multiply the node-independent continuation value (FR-1(ii), which I verified for trembled procedures — Definition 6's draws depend only on labels) and cancel.
- Step 2 [verified]: $\nu_\varepsilon(a \mid O_d) = C^\varepsilon(d)(a) > 0$ (Prop 7's recording argument), so $A_d^+ = A_d$; rational-in-$\varepsilon$ leaf probabilities give $Q_C(d, a^*) \ge Q_C(d, a)$ for $a^* \in \mathrm{supp}\, C(d)$ in the limit (weak inequalities survive; the notes correctly avoid claiming argmax convergence).
- Step 3 [verified]: height induction $W_C(q) = M_q$; at a fiber, node-independence plus Step 2 gives $W_C(q) = \max_a M_{q_a} = M_q$; the fair-tree assignment lemma ($\max_C V = \max_\pi V$ on strongly fair trees, via fiber-constant leaves-up argmaxes) is sound — the isomorphism equalizes subtree optima across a fiber, so a fiber-constant optimal assignment exists and is a deterministic procedure.

**A.2 A false lemma to strike [checked].** The notes' §1.1 (fair-repair) asserts "FRec implies subtree-veridicality of every decision node." False: Counterfactual Mugging $B_1$ (Prop 6) records at its only point for *every* procedure — every $O_T$-run passes exactly one $d$-node (the T-node), which is subtree-veridical and action-veridical — while the H-node (the simulation) is not subtree-veridical. Remark 3.4 says simulations are *deliberately tolerated* by recording. The theorem's proof does not actually need the false lemma (fairness + realized-$O_d$ does the propagation), but the lemma must not survive into the synthesis.

**A.3 Satellites.** FR-12 (optimal $\not\Rightarrow$ tremble-EDT) SURVIVES: recomputed — out $\to 0$; in $\to$ ($x \to 0$, $y \to -1$); $C = $ (out, y) has $V = 0 = \max$; under $C^\varepsilon$, $v_\varepsilon(d_2, x) = 0 > -1 = v_\varepsilon(d_2, y)$ exactly (children are leaves), so $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ rejects $y$ at every $\varepsilon$. FR-13 (existence) SURVIVES as existence (free states at null observations only make existence easier), but its inclusion $\{\text{tremble-EDT}\} \subseteq \{V\text{-opt}\}$ inherits A.1's wound. FR-14: N1/N3 claims inherit the wound; N2-as-refinement stands once A.1's hypothesis is added.

## Claim B — Definition R2 + FR-4: joint relocation outputs a strongly fair tree. **VERDICT: KILLED as stated** (explicit counterexample); survives only under a *redefinition* of fairness the notes never state.

**The clash [checked].** R2 step 3 stamps *every* leaf-world in branch $T_a$ with $\mathrm{pol}_d = a$ (this stamping is not optional — action-veridicality at $\hat q$, hence FR-8's "records at $\hat d$", requires it). Strong fairness as the notes define it demands fiber isomorphisms preserving leaf pairs $(\lambda, r)$. A surviving point $e \notin U$ inside the relocated scope is copied into every branch; the copies' leaf-worlds differ in the $\mathrm{pol}_d$ coordinate. Hence $F_e$ is **unfair in the letter of the notes' own definition**, and FR-4's proof step (b) ("all copies are isomorphic to the original") is wrong — it forgot the stamping that step 3 of the same construction mandates.

Concrete witness: root chance $\tfrac12 H / \tfrac12 T$ (evented); T-branch: $d$-node, and below each of $d$'s answers an $e$-node (leaves recording $\mathrm{act}_e$); H-branch: a hypothetical $d$-node over plain leaves. $F_d$ is unfair (T-side subtrees contain $e$-nodes, H-side ones don't), so $d \in U$; $e$ has no $U$-node below, so $e \notin U$. After $\mathrm{Rel}_U$: two $e$-copies, one per $\mathrm{pol}_d$-branch, leaf-worlds $\models \mathrm{pol}_d{=}\mathrm{pay}$ vs $\models \mathrm{pol}_d{=}\mathrm{refuse}$. No $(\lambda, r)$-preserving isomorphism exists. FR-2 and FR-5 escape only because their outputs have no surviving points in the scope.

**A deeper structural fact the notes missed [derived].** Under full EC + FRec with all observations realized (exactly the repaired hypotheses of Claim A), strong fairness with $(\lambda,r)$-preserving isomorphism forces **every fiber to be a singleton**. Proof: two non-nested members $q, q'$ diverge at a unique branching node $n$. If $n$ is chance, EC gives disjoint edge-events entailed by the leaf-worlds below each edge, contradicting the isomorphism's leaf-world equality. If $n$ is a decision node carrying $e$: with $O_e$ realized, fairness propagates subtree-veridicality to $n$ (as in A.1), so all runs below $n$ are $O_e$-runs, and recording's action clauses make leaf-worlds below different action-edges of $n$ entail disjoint act-events — contradiction again. Consequence: on the fully-evented, fully-recorded trees where FR-11 lives, the fiber-summation machinery of Step 1 is vacuous (one term), and multi-instance "perfect-copy simulation" fibers — the anthropically interesting case, and FR-1's own advertised example — exist **only** where the algebra is blind to which instance is which (un-evented chance) or where recording fails. That is philosophically apt (a "perfect copy" is exactly instance-blindness of the world-algebra) but is nowhere stated, and it means the enriched-algebra fairness definition cannot serve the relocated outputs.

**Exact repair.** Define fairness of $\mathrm{Rel}_U(B)$ over the pre-enrichment algebra: isomorphism preserving $(\lambda|_{\mathcal{E}}, r)$, fresh $\mathrm{pol}$-coordinates exempted. I checked that Claim A's proof survives this weakening on relocated outputs (continuation $(\lambda|_{\mathcal{E}}, r)$-laws still match across copies; the act-value conditioning at $e$ pools copies with equal payoff laws; recording at $e$ is unaffected). Alternatively E1-refine *every* copied survivor (not just relocated nodes, as E1 currently says) — but then E2 is no longer canonical as claimed.

## Claim C — FR-9 / FR-10 / FR-15's AMD sentence: the equivalence relation $\sim$ and its invariance claims. **VERDICT: FR-10 SURVIVES; FR-9's invariance claim WOUNDED; FR-15's AMD sentence KILLED (internal contradiction).**

**C.1 The contradiction [checked].** $\sim$ is adopted as: equality of realizable **pure-profile** law sets ($\mathrm{law}_B(\pi)$, enrichment coordinates marginalized out). FR-7(a) — which I verified — says resolution-style relocation preserves pure-profile laws **always, nested fibers included**. Therefore relocation is $\sim$-preserving on *every* problem. Compute the AMD (Prop 5(c)): pure laws $\{\delta_{(\lambda_1, 0)}, \delta_{(\lambda_3, 1)}\}$ (the payoff-4 leaf needs different answers at one point — unreachable purely); the collapsed tree $\mathrm{pol}{=}a \to 0$, $\mathrm{pol}{=}b \to 1$ has the same two laws. So $\mathrm{AMD} \sim \mathrm{Rel}(\mathrm{AMD})$, while $\max_C V = \tfrac43$ vs $1$. Hence:

- FR-9(iii)'s "$T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ is $\sim$-invariant" is **false on the general class** (the AMD pair is a counterexample: optimal values differ across a $\sim$-class). It holds restricted to almost-fair problems, where each point occurs $\le$ once per path, $V$ is multiaffine, and the mixed sup is a function of the pure-law set. State the restriction.
- FR-15's "it [the AMD] is not almost fair and FR-7(c) shows no $\sim$-preserving repair exists" is **self-contradictory**: under the adopted $\sim$, relocation *is* a $\sim$-preserving repair of the AMD — it just destroys the mixed optimum, which $\sim$ cannot see. What FR-7(c) actually proves (and this part is sound): no strongly fair tree matches the AMD's **mixed value function** under a point/action-bijection — a fair tree's $V$ is multiaffine, the AMD's $V(x) = (1-x)(3x+1)$ is quadratic in one coordinate. "Mixed-value-preserving" and "$\sim$-preserving" are different predicates and the notes trade on the conflation at three load-bearing places (FR-9(i)'s restriction, FR-15's AMD sentence, Task 5(a)).

**C.2 The squeeze [derived].** Refining $\sim$ to mixed-law-set equality does not save it: FR-9(iv) itself notes joint relocation *extends* the realizable correlated laws (the product-root node mixes jointly), so the finer relation would rule joint relocation of $\ge 2$ points unsound. So the coarse relation blesses the banned repair (AMD) and the fine relation bans the blessed one. There is no single relation on offer under which "equivalent" does the licensing work; every use of "$\sim$-equivalent" in Tasks 2/4/5 silently means the two-part certificate "law-equal *and the input was almost fair*", where the second conjunct — not $\sim$ — carries the argument against face-value individuation.

**C.3 What survives.** FR-10's example is correct [checked]: $T_{d,e}$ and $T_{e,d}$ both strongly fair, both reduce to profiles $\leftrightarrow$ pairs $(a,b)$ with laws $\delta_{(a,b)} \otimes \delta_{v(a,b)}$; mixing separates nothing (each realizes all joint laws over $(a,b)$); the $18 \ne 24$ profile count at $|A_d| = 2, |A_e| = 3$ checks ($2 \cdot 3^2 = 18$, $3 \cdot 2^3 = 24$). The "dichotomy" is sound but nearly tautological — relocation moves decisions by construction, so any location-pinning relation unblesses it. Minor flag: FR-9's cited witness for EDT's non-invariance ("Prop 6's $B_1/B_2$") is wrong — $B_1 \not\sim B_2$ (the pay-law of $B_2$, $\tfrac12\delta_{(T,\mathrm{pay},0),-x} + \tfrac12\delta_{(H,\bot,0),0}$, is in neither of $B_1$'s laws). The correct, trivial witness is one tree under two state-labelings (law sets equal, EDT verdicts differ).

## Claim D — the Cartesian-frames correspondence. **VERDICT: Claim 2.1 recheck SURVIVES; "relocation does not change the frame" KILLED as defined; the counterexample bounding slop Claim 2.2 KILLED.**

**D.1 Claim 2.1 [checked, survives].** Against the actual conditional-policies definition ("for all $f : V \to A$ there is $a_f$ with $f(v(a_f \cdot e)) \cdot e = a_f \cdot e$ for all $e$"): in the mugging frame, $f(S_T) = \mathrm{pay}, f(S_H) = \mathrm{refuse}$ has no witness — $a = \mathrm{pay}$ fails at $e = H$ ($(H, y) \ne (H, 0)$), $a = \mathrm{refuse}$ fails at $e = T$ ($(T, 0) \ne (T, -x)$). Decoupling ($\mathrm{pay} \cdot H := (H, 0)$) restores both conditionals. The Eight-Definitions §4.4 quote and the cf-paper conclusion quote ("It would be useful to understand how the introduction of a probability measure over environments interacts…", p. 18 of the extracted text) are verbatim [checked].

**D.2 Frame invariance under relocation [checked, killed].** The notes define $E$ = chance-outcome profiles, *one outcome per chance node*. Relocation copies every chance node in the scope into each branch. Mugging: original frame $2 \times 2$ with columns $c_H = ((H,y),(H,0))$, $c_T = ((T,-x),(T,0))$. Relocated tree has two coin-nodes (one per branch), so $E = \{H,T\}^2$ and the matrix is
$$\begin{array}{c|cccc} & (H,H) & (H,T) & (T,H) & (T,T) \\ \hline \mathrm{pay} & (H,y) & (H,y) & (T,-x) & (T,-x) \\ \mathrm{refuse} & (H,0) & (T,0) & (H,0) & (T,0) \end{array}$$
All four columns are distinct, so the biextensional collapse is $2 \times 4 \ne 2 \times 2$: **not biextensionally equivalent** — the environment gained genuinely new possibilities (the two coin-copies decorrelated, e.g. column $(H,T)$). Also "built from $\sim$-invariants" is false for the $E$-component: $\sim$ is blind to tree shape (FR-9(ii) says so), and chance profiles are tree shape. Exact repair: identify copied chance nodes — one $E$-coordinate per *original* chance node (a shared-seed convention for chance across policy branches). With that identification the relocated frame equals the original; without it the headline "the frame is the $\sim$-class's invariant content" is false twice over.

**D.3 The straddle counterexample [checked, killed].** FR-16 claims [checked counterexample]: "a fair tree may straddle a chance branch with one point whose perfect-copy instances sit on both sides — fair, yet the branch partition is unobservable." This configuration does not exist. If the chance branch is evented (EC — required for "the branch partition" to be a partition of $W$ at all; CF observability is defined on partitions of $W$), the leaf-worlds below the two edges entail disjoint events, while the notes' strong fairness requires the fiber isomorphism to preserve leaf-worlds — contradiction. If the branch is not evented, both sides carry the same worlds and there is no two-cell world-partition to fail observability. Either way the [checked] mark is wrong; slop Claim 2.2's right-to-left direction reverts to **open**, and the whole biconditional stays [guess] in both directions (the notes' final sentence happens to say [guess], but the claimed bound above it is broken).

## The single most damaging objection

**The pipeline's licensing concept — "equivalent" — is not yet a coherent single relation, and the notes' philosophy leans on it being one.** The adopted $\sim$ (pure-profile law sets) blesses *every* resolution-style relocation, including the AMD collapse it is supposed to ban ($\mathrm{AMD} \sim \mathrm{Rel}(\mathrm{AMD})$ with $\max_C V$ dropping $\tfrac43 \to 1$), so $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ is not $\sim$-invariant and "$\sim$-equivalent to a fair problem" cannot by itself distinguish repair from damage. Refining to mixed-law equality reverses the failure: joint relocation *adds* correlated laws (FR-9(iv)'s own admission) and becomes unsound. So every Task-2/4/5 conclusion phrased as "$\sim$-equivalent" is really the two-part certificate "law-equal **and** the input was almost fair", where the almost-fairness conjunct — a structural condition on descriptions, exactly the thing a critic is free to reject — is doing all the anti-compatibilist work. Presenting $\sim$ as the invariant content of a problem (and the CF frame as "the $\sim$-class's invariant content", itself killed at D.2) overstates what was proved: the theorems are fine after repair, but the individuation argument is circular as packaged — the class restriction that licenses the repair *is* the disputed individuation claim.

## The one thing to tell the synthesis agent not to repeat

Do not repeat **"FR-7(c) shows no $\sim$-preserving repair [of the AMD/self-succession] exists."** It is self-contradicted by the notes' own FR-7(a) plus their own definition of $\sim$: relocation *is* $\sim$-preserving on the AMD; what FR-7(c) proves is that no **mixed-value-preserving** point/action-bijective repair exists. Always name the grade (pure-law vs mixed-value) when invoking preservation or equivalence — and, while at it, also drop the false parenthetical "FRec implies subtree-veridicality of every decision node" ($B_1$ refutes it; Remark 3.4 tolerates simulations by design).
