### 2.4 GR-10 — main theorem SURVIVES; sharpening KILLED

**Main theorem.** I checked each step against Def 7 / Def 8 / Def 18. Step 1: under FRec $+$ non-nesting, an $O_d$-run passes exactly one $d$-node, which recording makes subtree-veridical (hence "live" in GR-D2) — so predictor nodes carry no $O_d$-leaves at all (a run through a predictor node with an $O_d$-leaf would pass a non-veridical $d$-node as its only $d$-node, contradicting recording); conditioning on $\lambda\models a\wedge O_d$ is conditioning on "reached a live node and drew $a$" (recording's action clauses), and the draw is independent of the reach (Def 6), so $V_{s^\varepsilon_d}(a)=\sum_{q\ \mathrm{live}}\Pr_\varepsilon(q\mid O_d)\,G_q(C^\varepsilon,a)$ — a convex combination of live members' $Q$-values, hence within $D_V(B,C^\varepsilon)$ of *every* member's (live or predictor) — this needs $D_V$ to range over all members, which it does. Step 2: full-support trembles $+$ realized observation $+$ recording give $A_d^+=A_d$ and $\nu_\varepsilon(a\wedge O_d)>0$, so $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ reads $V_{s^\varepsilon_d}(a^\ast)\ge V_{s^\varepsilon_d}(a)$ and yields $Q_q(C^\varepsilon,a^\ast)\ge Q_q(C^\varepsilon,a)-2D_V$ at every member; weak inequalities pass to $\varepsilon\to0$ (rational functions). Step 3: the induction uses only $M'_q\le\max_aM'_{q_a}$ (a procedure's value at $q$ is a mixture of its children's values) and $M'_n\le\sum\beta_cM'_c$ at chance nodes — both inequalities in the right direction with *procedure* optima, so no assignment lemma is needed, which is exactly what lets isomorphism go. The chain $W_C(q)\ge\min_{a^\ast\in\mathrm{supp}}W_C(q_{a^\ast})\ge W_C(q_{a'})-2D_V\ge M'_{q_{a'}}-2D_V(h(q)-1)-2D_V$ is right. Stress test [checked, `adv_gr10_sharpening.py` (B)]: 250 random two-point trees ($d$ at the root with $O_d=\top$, an evented coin below each action, $e$ live on $T$ and simulated on $H$ with independent random integer payoffs; $H=2$; FRec, EC, realized observations, almost fair by construction), all deterministic tremble-EDT-consistent procedures (504 pairs), $D_V$ taken as $\max$ over $\varepsilon\in\{0,\tfrac1{10},\tfrac1{100},\tfrac1{1000}\}$ of $D_V(B,C^\varepsilon)$: **zero violations** of loss $\le 2HD_V$; the worst observed loss$/D_V$ was $52/45\approx1.16$ against the allowed $4$. Verdict: **SURVIVES** (at the pure/mixed grade the proof covers; under independent redraws; with the toolkit's TWC event-conditioned tremble-EDT, which is the form the theorem states).

**Sharpening.** The notes claim, for a single queried point, loss $\le(\sum_{q\ \mathrm{sim}}R_q)\,D_V$, marked [derived], and call it "asymptotically tight" on the near-copy family. Counterexample [checked, `adv_gr10_sharpening.py` (A)]: evented coin $\tfrac12/\tfrac12$; point $d$, $O_d=\{\mathrm{coin}=T\}$, actions $a,b$; $T$-branch live node $a\to0$, $b\to-t$ (worlds record the act); $H$-branch simulation node $a\to-D$, $b\to+D$ (worlds have act $=\bot$). FRec, almost fair, EC, realized observations all hold (toolkit-verified). Face-value tremble-EDT approves only $a$ ($0>-t$ at the live node); $V(a)=-\tfrac D2$, $V(b)=\tfrac{D-t}2$, loss $=D-\tfrac t2$; $D_V=\max(D,D+t)=D+t$; sim-share bound $\tfrac12(D+t)$. At $D=1$: $t=\tfrac1{10}$: loss $\tfrac{19}{20}$ vs bound $\tfrac{11}{20}$; $t=\tfrac1{1000}$: $\tfrac{1999}{2000}$ vs $\tfrac{1001}{2000}$, ratio $1.997$. The general $2HD_V$ holds ($\tfrac{11}5\ge\tfrac{19}{20}$). Diagnosis: the notes' argument bounds the simulation share by $D_V$ per member, but a simulation member's $Q_q(a')-Q_q(a^\ast)$ can be $2D_V$ ($a'$ is $D_V$ above the live average, $a^\ast$ is $D_V$ below it, the live average itself ordering them the other way); the near-copy family hides this because refuse pays $0$ at both members, so only one action disagrees. Correct single-fiber sharpening [derived]: loss $\le2(\sum_{q\ \mathrm{sim}}R_q)D_V$, tight as $R_{\mathrm{sim}}\to1$ (which also shows the factor $2$ in $2HD_V$ is tight at $H=1$). Verdict: **KILLED** as stated; the factor-2 version survives. The table row "loose by a factor 4" should read "by a factor 2".

### 2.8 GR-14 — transitions SURVIVE; $f(\varepsilon,\rho)$ WOUNDED

Transitions and stationary law re-derived: drift-then-fill for a true one-boxer gives $P(1B\to2B)=\rho(1-\varepsilon)$, $P(2B\to1B)=\rho+(1-\rho)\varepsilon$, $\pi_A(2B)=\rho(1-\varepsilon)/(2\rho+\varepsilon-2\rho\varepsilon)$; at $\rho=\varepsilon=\tfrac1{100}$, $99/298$, odds $99/199$ ✓; the sibling's $\rho:(\rho+\varepsilon)$ is the first-order odds ✓. **The realized value is mis-timed.** The chain state is the start-of-episode verdict; under drift-then-fill the box is filled according to the *post-drift* verdict, whose stationary law is $\pi'(2B)=\pi_A(1B)\rho+\pi_A(2B)(1-\rho)=\rho/(2\rho+\varepsilon-2\rho\varepsilon)$ (which is the fill-then-drift stationary law — the two orderings agree on the verdict in force at fill time). ### 2.9 GR-15 — WOUNDED

The identification "the refuser trap is the gated-learning chain with $\varepsilon\mapsto r_b$, forgetting $\rho$ in place of drift; stationary sealed probability $=$ GR-14's formula" fails on the drift structure. GR-14's drift is a *symmetric* flip (the verdict moves $1B\to2B$ and $2B\to1B$ w.p. $\rho$). Verdict: **WOUNDED** — the two bearers share the hazard/gate structure but not the drift structure (symmetric verdict drift vs one-way credence reset); this belongs on the list of differences as a sixth item, and the "stationary odds bad:good $\approx\rho:(\rho+r_b)$" clause of the identification must be dropped for the mugging side.
