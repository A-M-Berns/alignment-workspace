## B. DY-3 — the sequence form exists; limit $\{\tfrac23\}$; dynamics agree. SURVIVES on existence; WOUNDED on the dynamics leg.

That is not a return-driven learner; it is exactly v2 Remark 5.3's Skyrms **deliberational dynamics** ("probability-of-one's-own-action evolving within a single decision to a fixed point"), and its convergence is of the *time-average* $f_t$, not of the played policy (which is pure at every step). ## E. DY-8/DY-9 — dynamic FR-11 and the GLIE instance. WOUNDED.

**The counterexample [checked; `adv_glie_depth2.py`, `adv_glie_depth2_exact.py`].** Tree: $d_1$ ($O=\top$): out $\to1$; in $\to d_2$ ($O_{d_2}=\{a_1{=}\mathrm{in}\}$): x $\to4$, y $\to\tfrac12$. Toolkit: `validate()` = [], strongly fair (singleton fibers), `records_for_all` True at both points, both observations realized; optimum (in, x) $=4$; $V(\mathrm{out},\mathrm{y})=1$. Event-credited Monte-Carlo control exactly as in `dy_fair_learner.py` (init 0, sample averages, $\varepsilon$-greedy) but with the thread's canonical schedule $\varepsilon_t=\min(1,1/t)$ — $\varepsilon_t\to0$, $\sum_t\varepsilon_t=\infty$. Once the learner has seen (in, y) (return $\tfrac12<1$) with out already valued at 1, its greedy is (out, y) and $(d_2,\mathrm{x})$ is reached only by exploring at *both* levels, probability $\varepsilon_t^2/4$ per episode; $\sum_t\varepsilon_t^2/4<\infty$, so with positive probability $(d_2,\mathrm{x})$ is never credited. Simulation, 1000 runs $\times$ 3000 episodes: greedy limit (out, y) in **35.9%** of runs ($\varepsilon_t=1/t$), 29.3% ($2/t$), 7.7% ($1/\sqrt t$, decaying like $T^{-1/4}$ since $\sum1/(4t)=\infty$). Exact four-state recursion for $T\to\infty$: **0.366** ($1/t$), 0.299 ($2/t$), 0.195 ($3/t$) — the trap is permanent, value $1<4$.

**Diagnosis.** The notes' Def (DY-8) says GLIE = "every action at every point explored infinitely often, exploration rate $\to0$" and then asserts that the Def-10 tremble schedule with $\sum\varepsilon_t=\infty$ "is the canonical instance". The instance claim is false whenever some $(d,a)$ is reachable only through $k\ge2$ exploratory draws: the visit probability is $\prod$ of the $\varepsilon_t$'s, and $\sum_t\varepsilon_t^{k}$ can converge. The thread's own Step 2 sentence — "each point is reached with probability bounded below by a product of pruned chance weights and exploration rates" — contains the product and misses that its sum can be finite. Singh–Jaakkola–Littman–Szepesvári's GLIE is a *visitation* property ("every action is executed infinitely often in every state that is visited infinitely often"), which the $1/t$ learner also violates here ($d_2$ is visited infinitely often, x at $d_2$ finitely often). **Exact added hypothesis:** the learner visits every $(d,a)$ infinitely often a.s. — e.g. $\sum_t\varepsilon_t^{k}=\infty$ with $k$ the maximal number of decision nodes on a root-to-leaf path (so $1/\sqrt t$ suffices at depth 2, fails at depth 3), or per-point visit-count schedules $\varepsilon(d)=c/n(d)$, or optimistic initialization. Under that hypothesis the leaves-up argument of Step 3 (SLLN at the lowest fibers; Cesàro for the sample averages above them as the policy below converges; strict maxima stabilize, ties are value-neutral) is sound on finite trees — I could not break it. One citation note: Sutton–Barto call the convergence of Monte-Carlo ES control "one of the most fundamental open theoretical questions in RL"; the thread's [reconstructed] leans on SARSA(0)'s GLIE theorem, and what actually carries the finite-tree case is the thread's own induction — mark it [derived], not [reconstructed].

**Simulations are not GLIE.** Every Monte-Carlo script in `dynamic-scratch/` floors exploration (`max(0.02, …)`, `max(0.01, …)`) or temperature (`max(0.02, …)`). The reported "greedy limits" are those of $\varepsilon$-soft learners after finite $T$; on rows (i)–(vi) this is qualitatively harmless, on the AMD it is not (F).

**Verdict: WOUNDED.** The theorem holds with "every $(d,a)$ visited infinitely often" as a hypothesis; the four-hypothesis mapping (fairness = Markov, recording = unbiased credit, realized = the event occurs, trembles = exploration) survives, but the fourth entry must read "exploration that visits every point–action pair infinitely often", not "$\sum\varepsilon_t=\infty$".

## F. DY-10 — the AMD is $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{occ}}$-learning-fair; $\kappa=\mathrm{pol}$ always holds. KILLED as stated; WOUNDED to smooth learners.

**Exact part [checked, SURVIVES].** $Q(a)=\frac{4(1-q)}{2-q}$, $Q(b)=\frac{2+2q}{2-q}$ recomputed from the per-occurrence bookkeeping (node-1 $a$-draws $q$ per episode with return 0, node-2 $a$-draws $(1-q)q$ with return 4, so $Q(a)=\frac{4(1-q)q}{q+(1-q)q}$; the $q$ cancels), tie at $q=\tfrac13$, $=\sum_qR_qG_q/\mathbb E[\#_d]$ with $\mathbb E[\#_d]=2-q$; at the pure points $Q(a)=2>Q(b)=1$ ($q=0$) and $Q(b)=4>Q(a)=0$ ($q=1$), so neither pure procedure satisfies Theorem 1's condition and $S_{\mathrm{occ}}(\mathrm{AMD})=\{\tfrac13\}=T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ statically. Fine.

**The dynamic headline fails on the thread's own data.** The thread's definition of $\mathcal L$-learning-fair quantifies over *every* GLIE learner in the class and asks for convergence "in the greedy/limiting policy". An $\varepsilon$-greedy per-occurrence MC-control learner (a member of $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{occ}}$ by DY-8) has a *pure* greedy policy at every time and therefore cannot converge to $q=\tfrac13$; the only question is where it goes. `dy_amd_dynamics.py`'s own $\varepsilon$-greedy run reports late-time $P(a)\approx0.030$ with $Q(a)=Q(b)=1.015$ — i.e. greedy $b$ — and `dy_fair_learner.py` row (vii) reports the occurrence learner's greedy limit as $b$, $V=1$; the notes gloss this as "chatters around the tie" and headline the softmax result. I re-ran it as a genuinely GLIE learner ($\varepsilon_t=1/\sqrt t$, no floor, $4\times10^5$ episodes, two seeds) [checked; `adv_amd_greedy_chatter.py`]: greedy $=a$ in **0.1%** of the last 40 000 episodes, overall per-occurrence $a$-frequency **0.003**, $Q(a)=Q(b)=1.002$ (the estimates equalize at the value of pure $b$ while the policy sits at $b$; with $\varepsilon_t=5/t$: 2 greedy switches in the second half, $a$-frequency 0.000). So this learner's effective limit is the pure Def-22 fixed point $b$ (value $1<\tfrac43$), which **violates Theorem 1** ($Q(a)=2>Q(b)=1$ there) — neither the greedy policy nor its time-average approaches $\tfrac13$. Smooth learners do approach the tie: softmax with $\tau_t=1/\ln(t+2)$ (no floor) gives late $P(a)\in[0.346,0.352]$, $\tau_t=1/\sqrt t$ gives $[0.30,0.38]$ with mean $0.340$; the thread's REINFORCE gives $0.344$ — approximate, finite-$T$, and only for learners whose policy class contains interior points.

**Why the greedy learner lands on $b$ [derived].** With sample-average estimates the process is fictitious-play-like in the *estimates*, not in the policy: whenever $Q(a)$ edges above $Q(b)$ the greedy flips to $a$, node 1 then draws $a$ almost surely with return 0, and $Q(a)$ is pulled back below $Q(b)$; the rest point of this discrete dynamic is "estimates equal at $V(\delta_b)=1$, policy at $b$ with vanishing excursions". The continuous best-response map has its rest point at the tie; the discrete one does not. This is exactly the greedy-vs-smooth distinction the notes never draw.

**$\kappa=\mathrm{pol}$ "always holds" — KILLED [checked; `adv_family_and_pol.py` (B)].** DY-8 puts both finite bandits over whole procedures *and* policy gradient in $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pol}}$, and DY-10/DY-12/DY-19 assert $S_{\mathrm{pol}}=T_{\mathrm{opt}}$. Two counterexamples, both the thread's own trees. (i) The AMD with a bandit over *pure* procedures gets $\max(0,1)=1<\tfrac43$ — the thread states this number itself (DY-10, row (vii)), so its "for $\kappa=\mathrm{pol}$ it always holds" contradicts its own table; the finite-arm bandit is learning-fair only when the arm set contains an optimal procedure, i.e. when the optimum is pure (Def 21: no path with two nodes of one point). (ii) Convex 3-fold mugging ($x=2$, $y=\tfrac32$): $V(q)=\frac{q(3q^2-4)}{4}$, $V'(q)=\frac{9q^2-4}{4}>0$ for $q>\tfrac23$, so $q=1$ ($V=-\tfrac14$) is a boundary *local maximum* of $V$ on $[0,1]$ besides the global one $q=0$ ($V=0$). REINFORCE on $q=\sigma(\theta)$ (per-episode return, the thread's L4 construction): from $q_0\in\{0.27,0.50\}$ it converges to $q=0$; from $q_0\in\{0.73,0.82,0.92\}$ it converges to $q=1$, value $-\tfrac14<0$. So $S_{\mathrm{pol}}$ for policy gradient is the set of *local* maxima of $V$ on the product of simplices, equal to $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ only when $V$ has no non-global local maxima — a property of $B$, not automatic. This also corrects the zoo handoff "the convex 3-fold's all-pay basin is unreachable": unreachable for $\varepsilon$-greedy sample-average learners from uniform starts (I confirmed the mechanism: the early high-exploration phase pushes $q$ below $\tfrac23$ and a single flip to refuse is absorbing, since at $q\approx0$ the per-occurrence $Q(\mathrm{pay})\to\frac{-x}{4}<0$), reached by policy gradient from $q_0>\tfrac23$.

**Verdict.** "The AMD is $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{occ}}$-learning-fair" is **KILLED** for the class as the thread defines it and **survives only as** "learning-fair for smooth per-occurrence learners (softmax with vanishing temperature, policy gradient), whose limiting policies can be interior". The two-way divergence claim survives in that weakened form (mugging: repairable yet $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ev}}$-unfair ✓; AMD: structurally irreparable yet learnable by *smooth* per-occurrence learners). "For $\kappa=\mathrm{pol}$ it always holds" and "$S_{\mathrm{pol}}=T_{\mathrm{opt}}$" are **KILLED**. The "[checked]" mark on DY-10's headline covers only the fixed-point algebra; the convergence sentence was [guess] and is now refuted for half the class.

## G. DY-7 / DY-12(1) — the family result and the vacuity claim. WOUNDED.

## H. DY-19 — the Galois pair and the shadow theorem. KILLED in its first half; the pair survives as a programme.

The shadow theorem's first half: "every limit point $C$ of the greedy (or softmax with temperature $\to0$) policy of a GLIE per-occurrence Monte-Carlo control learner satisfies Theorem 1's condition at every queried point". **Counterexample: the AMD, the thread's own headline evidence** (F). The greedy sequence of the $\varepsilon$-greedy learner has limit point $\delta_b$ (and possibly $\delta_a$ along a vanishing-frequency subsequence); at $\delta_b$, $\sum_qR_qG_q(a)=1\cdot0+1\cdot4=4>\sum_qR_qG_q(b)=1\cdot1+1\cdot1=2$, so $\mathrm{supp}\,\delta_b\not\subseteq\arg\max$ — Theorem 1 fails. What is true, and what the stochastic-approximation route can hope to prove, is a statement about the *smooth* learner's policy parameter (or the time-average of a fictitious-play-like process), not about greedy iterates.
