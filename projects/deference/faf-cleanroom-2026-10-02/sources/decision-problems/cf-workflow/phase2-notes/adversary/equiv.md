## 1. EQ-3 (coincidence theorem) and the C1 adjudication — SURVIVES

**What I tried.** (a) Case audit of the proof. (Leaf, leaf) and the two impossibility cases are right because a chance-normal node has two inequivalent children each of which emits a visible event or a distinct leaf label, so its trace law is not a point mass at a bare label. (Decision, decision): the first event forces the same point; the continuation after $(d,a)$ is $\mathrm{tr}_C(\mathrm{child}_a)$, which does not depend on $C(d)$ because a $d$-node below the root would be a nested pair, and nested pairs are inequivalent under $\simeq_\Delta$ (EQ-4's height argument, which I verified: normalization deletes no decision node, so $N(T_{q'})$ sits strictly below $q'$'s ancestor in $N(T_q)$) — hence excluded by the inner-fairness hypothesis. (Chance, decision): all children of the chance root would be $d$-nodes, pairwise inequivalent in normal form — an unfair inner fiber. (Chance, chance): children are leaves with distinct labels or decision nodes with distinct points (equal ones merged, inequivalent same-point ones excluded); the trace law's mass on an immediate label / first event identifies each child's probability; per-action continuations are children of smaller height. No gap found. The corollary's induction on height is sound: any same-point pair inside $T_{q_1}$ has strictly smaller height (a nested pair $(q_1, q')$ would contradict $\mathrm{Fair}_{\approx_{\mathrm{tr}}}$ by EQ-4's trace argument, so none exists).

(b) Random battery [checked; `check_eq3_random.py`]: 198 random two-point trees (depth $\le 3$, chance probabilities in $\{\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\tfrac23\}$, three leaf labels, half of them with a $\Delta$-rewritten duplicate subtree — splits into equal copies, probability-one wrappers, zero-probability branches — to populate non-singleton fibers). Result: `fair_tr 70 = fair_bis 70, mismatch 0`; of 349 trace-equivalent same-point pairs, 349 were $\simeq_\Delta$; no pair was trace-equivalent with fair inner fibers and non-bisimilar. E4-type gaps did not arise randomly (recombination needs a designed tree), which is consistent with the theorem: they require an unfair inner fiber.

(c) Is the adjudication a quiet favour to one brief? No: E1 (`bis:n bisΔ:Y`) and E3 (`tr:n law:Y`) are computed and both briefs' *literal* relations are separated from the chosen one — brief B's plain bisimulation by E1, brief A's value-level $\sim$ by E3 and by Smoking Lesion $\beta=0$ (value-fair, law-unfair: `values: m | m`, world laws $\gamma_1 = \tfrac34$ vs $\gamma_0 = \tfrac14$). The honest reading of the headline is therefore: **C1 is a coincidence only after both briefs are amended** (brief A to trace level, brief B modulo the chance laws); as stated by their authors they differ. The notes say this; the synthesis must keep the "provided".

(d) One register remark, not a kill. The notes call the trace level "forced by Definition 13" (§9 dead end 1). What Definition 13 forces is that a *spurious* $e$-query changes $\mathrm{occ}(e)$ and $\#_e$ — statistics of point $e$, not of the fiber's point $d$. For the purposes fairness at $d$ serves in this program — a node-independent downtree law $\theta_d$ under every trembled procedure, closure of the anthropic fork (EQ-5), the assignment lemma and FR-11's Step 1 — the notes' own EQ-5 shows $\mathrm{Fair}_{\approx_{\mathrm{law}}}$ already suffices. What the trace level buys beyond that is (i) invariance of Def-13 verdicts at *every* point of the subtree and (ii) a functional-bisimulation quotient. That is a defensible design choice, and E3 is the right separating tree, but "forced" overstates it: adding the oracle law "a decision node all of whose children are equivalent is its child" to the normal form would keep canonicity (bottom-up rewriting) and make E3 fair.
