## 1. CFF-2 — "relocation preserves the identified frame literally, for every $B$ and every $U$, nested fibers included". **VERDICT: WOUNDED** (literal equality needs non-nested $U$; in general only $\simeq$, i.e. UN-7).

**The proof's gap.** "Every copied chance node reads its original's coordinate … both environments are the set of original chance profiles." False when a relocated fiber is nested: a chance node reachable only under an *inconsistent* assignment of the relocated point (answer $b$ at the upper node, $a$ at the lower) is never copied — resolution answers every occurrence alike — so the relocated tree has strictly fewer chance nodes than $B$ and the identified environment is a proper coarsening of $\mathbf E_B$, not equal to it.

**Minimal witness [checked `adv6_cff2_nested.py`].** Point $d$, actions $a/b$: root $d$: $a \to$ leaf $(w_0, 0)$; $b \to d$ again: $a \to$ coin $\to (w_4,4)/(w_5,5)$; $b \to (w_1, 1)$. Nested fiber (AMD shape) with a coin below the inconsistent path $b{,}a$. $\mathrm{Fr}(B)$ is $2 \times 2$ (columns $H, T$; the coin coordinate is inert for pure profiles); $\mathrm{Rel}_d(B)$ has **no chance node**, so $\mathrm{Fr}_\kappa(\mathrm{Rel}_d B)$ coarsened is $2 \times 1$. `same_matrix_via` (the thread's own equality test): **False**; `biext_equivalent`: **True**; collapses $2 \times 1 = 2 \times 1$.

**Random test [checked `adv5_cff2.py`, `adv6_cff2_nested.py`]:** 400 random trees, relocating a random nonempty set of multi-member fibers (closure applied, `obs=TOP`): non-nested relocations — literal equality **51/51**; nested relocations — literal equality 156, **$\simeq$-only 49**, neither 0. So: $p_*\mathrm{Fr}_\kappa(\mathrm{Rel}_U B) = \mathrm{Fr}(B)$ as matrices **iff every chance node in the relocated scope lies on a path answered consistently at the relocated points** (automatic when no fiber in $U$ is nested); otherwise $\simeq$ with the original's inert coordinates deleted. The thread's three nested checks (TN-V2, AMD, 3-fold concave) are degenerate for this question: TN-V2's chance nodes sit under distinct points $(x,y)$ (consistent), AMD has no chance, and the concave $k$-fold mugging has $b_j \in \{0,1\}$ hence **no chance node below the simulations**.

**Consequences.** (i) "Strengthens UN-7 from $\simeq$ to $=$" holds on non-nested $U$ only (the universal thread's UN-7 is the correct general statement). (ii) CFF-30's "square commutes *on the nose*" inherits the wound as a matrix statement; the collapse square $\mathrm{Col}(p_*\mathrm{Fr}_\kappa \mathrm{Rel}_U B) = \mathrm{Col}(\mathrm{Fr} B)$ still holds (biextensional equivalence gives isomorphic collapses) — CFF-30 SURVIVES at the level it is named for. (iii) CFF-10's ledger entry "relocation does not change the frame — now a theorem" should read "up to $\simeq$; literally for almost-fair inputs". (iv) CFF-23(i) ("relocation is invisible to CF time") is unaffected: $\mathrm{External}_T$ is $\simeq$-invariant (paper: "well-defined up to biextensional equivalence").

What I tried that failed: survivors inside the scope (copies keep the point; rows biject), multi-member relocated fibers with chance inside the scope, recorded and unrecorded coins, joint relocation of two fibers — all literally equal when non-nested.

## 3. CFF-11 / CFF-12 / CFF-13 — the Crown Lemma, the Straddle Theorem, and "open problem 1 proved in the lazy frame with fairness weakened to outcome-fairness". **VERDICT: CFF-13's headline for STRONGLY FAIR trees SURVIVES; CFF-11 as stated WOUNDED (two definitional errors); CFF-12 SURVIVES once those are fixed; CFF-13's outcome-fair GENERALIZATION KILLED as stated (explicit nested counterexample), WOUNDED to "outcome-fair with realizable outcome sets, or no nesting".**

**3.1 Error one in Definition CFF-B: "pre-decision = union of exit classes".** The (⇐) direction of the Crown Lemma needs each exit class *uniformly* inside or outside $S$; a union of exit classes need not be, because exit classes can overlap (two exits sharing an outcome). [checked `adv1_crown.py` (A)] Unrecorded root coin; $H \to d$: $a \to (w_1,1)$, $b \to (w_2,0)$; $T \to d'$: $a \to (w_1,1)$, $b \to (w_3,0)$. Exit classes $W_H = \{(w_1,1),(w_2,0)\}$, $W_T = \{(w_1,1),(w_3,0)\}$. $S := W_H$ is a union of exit classes and is **not** column-determined: at column $T$ the rows $(\cdot, a) \mapsto (w_1,1) \in S$ and $(\cdot, b) \mapsto (w_3,0) \notin S$. The thread's `CEU=>CD True` on 21 trees holds only because no battery tree has overlapping exit classes.

**3.2 Error two in Definition CFF-B: $W_x$ = "outcomes of leaves below $x$".** With a nested fiber, leaves below an inconsistent assignment are realized by no pure profile, and the Crown Lemma's (⇒) direction ("column-determined ⇒ union of exit classes") fails: [checked `adv1_crown.py` (B)] 27 random trees where `column_determined` disagrees with "uniform on all-leaf exit classes"; in every case the culprit outcome sits on an inconsistent path (one example is printed in the script output). With $W_x$ redefined as the set of outcomes **realizable** below $x$ by pure profiles, and "pre-decision" as "each exit's realizable class uniformly in or out of $S$", the corrected Crown Lemma and the Straddle Theorem hold on **297/297 random trees (182 with nested fibers)** [checked `adv7_crown_realizable.py`: `lemma_fail_real 0`, `straddle_fail_real 0`]. Re-derivation of the corrected (⇒): the policy avoiding $n$ makes $m$ independent of non-crown coordinates; $m$ is constant on $R_x$; every *realizable* outcome below $x$ is realized by some $(\pi, \varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon \in R_x$, hence has membership $m(x)$. $\square$ (⇐): the exit is a function of $\varepsilon$ and every row's outcome at $\varepsilon$ lies in that exit's realizable class. $\square$ On almost-fair trees (no nesting) every leaf is realizable and the two definitions coincide, so nothing changes there.

**3.3 The strongly-fair headline SURVIVES.** Strong fairness excludes nesting (FR-1(i)), so all leaves are realizable and outcome sets are the realizable ones; a fiber with nodes below an $S$-exit and an $N$-exit would have disjoint outcome sets, contradicting equal nonempty sets; no straddling ⇒ observable by the Straddle Theorem (re-derived: classify points by side; $\pi_f(d) := f(\text{side})(d)$; a run at an $S$-exit meets only $S$-points, so the $\pi_f$- and $f(S)$-runs coincide). Stress tests: 297 random trees (100 outcome-fair) — `cff13_fail 0`; **132 trees with forced multi-member fibers** (isomorphic copies, and copies mutated by permuting action-subtrees and changing $\beta$, with and without an extra point above the coin; 90 strongly fair, 42 outcome-fair-but-not-strongly-fair; 38 column-determined partitions) — **0 failures** [checked `adv7_crown_realizable.py`]. The thread's own tree $M$ recomputed: crown $\{c_1, c_2\}$, exits $\{e\text{-node}, d_L, d_R\}$, coin partition observable.

## 13. Closing items

**The single most damaging objection to the thread as a whole.** The frame functor the thread works with is not a function of a v2 problem. Definition 5 carries no chance seeding, and the thread's own CFF-8(a) shows the same tree gets opposite verdicts about the same partition under the two natural seedings (the coin below a fresh point: not even column-determined lazily, observable identified). So "CF-observability of a partition of $B$'s outcomes" is not a property of $B$; it is a property of $(B, \kappa)$, and the thread's headline resolution of Seam 3 — "fairness has a frame-side shadow lazily and loses it under identification" — is a statement about which representation datum one adds, not about fairness. The program's canonical example makes the point sharply: $\mathrm{Rel}_d(B_1)$ is a witness *for* the lazy theorem (no nontrivial chance-determined partition) and *against* the identified one. Until the seeding is in the type (the thread's own Q12 amendment), no frame-side statement about fairness — phase 1's CF-12/13/15 included — is a theorem about problems, and the claim that fair problems correspond to Cartesian frames is not merely half-true, it is not yet well-posed. The genuinely convention-free mathematics in the thread is the lazy Crown/Straddle/strongly-fair chain (§3), which survives; everything under "identification" (CFF-2, 3, 15, 30) is true by the construction that defines the convention.

**The one thing to tell the synthesis agent not to repeat.** Do not repeat CFF-19(ii)/CFF-23(iii) in the form "the later self is the product of the committed frames iff no action-straddling" or "$\mathrm{External}_T(\mathrm{Fr}) \simeq \mathrm{Fr}_{\geq k}$": both fail on the first tree with two stage-1 points (tree $R$, §5), where $\mathrm{External}_T \simeq \&_{a_1}\mathrm{Commit}^{a_1}$ is the *conditional* later self and $\mathrm{Fr}_{>1}$ is strictly smaller. State the decomposition as "peel observation cells, then commit within each cell on the single live point", and state $\mathrm{External}_T$'s equivalent as the conditional later self. More generally: do not upgrade a phase-1 "$\simeq$" to "$=$", or "strongly fair" to "outcome-fair", on the strength of a battery whose members are all almost-fair single-stage trees — every one of the thread's three definitional slips (all-leaf vs realizable outcomes, literal vs $\simeq$, edge vs profile straddling) is invisible on exactly that battery.

**Handoffs I believe are misaddressed or mis-stated.** (i) **dynamic**: the CFF-23(iii) handoff ("$\mathrm{External}_T(\mathrm{Fr}) \simeq$ the stage team $\mathrm{Fr}_{\geq k}$, not $\mathrm{Loc}_d$") is false on $R$; the correct message is "$\mathrm{External}_T \simeq \&_{a_1}\mathrm{Commit}^{a_1}$ (conditional later self); $\simeq \mathrm{Fr}_{\ge k}$ only when every later point is consulted under one earlier profile; DY-17 fails on $R$ for that reason and on $T_2$ for the same-stage-teammates reason". (ii) **universal**: "CFF-2 strengthens UN-7 from $\simeq$ to $=$" only for non-nested $U$; UN-7 is the right general statement and the universal thread should keep it. (iii) **equiv**: "outcome-fairness (equal outcome sets) is the coarsest fiber relation the frame can see" must be stated with *realizable* outcome sets, else it is refuted on nested trees (§3.4). (iv) **seeds / literalist**: the seeding is representation data, as the notes say — but the handoff should add that the two seedings give opposite observability verdicts on one tree (CFF-8(a)), which is the strongest reason the seeding must enter the type rather than stay a convention.
