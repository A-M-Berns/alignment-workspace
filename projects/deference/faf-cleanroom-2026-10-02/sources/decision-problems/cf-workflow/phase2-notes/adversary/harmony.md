## 1. The attacks

### HA-4 — SC Theorem 11.1 is false. SURVIVES.

What I tried. (i) Re-implemented SC Defs 10.2–10.4 from the SC text (not from the thread's code): 32 proposals per player, outcome = $W$-argmax of the intersection else the batna profile. (ii) Checked the hypotheses: $W = U_1 + 2U_2 + $ tie-break is injective and Pareto-consistent — the only subjectively dominated outcome is $ad$ (by $ac$, $bc$, $bd$) and $W(ad) = \tfrac1{1000}$ is the minimum [checked, `bg_check.py` (a)]. (iii) Nash at $\varepsilon = 0$: against $s^\ast_2 = (d,\{bd\})$ *every* proposal of player 1 yields $0$ (any deal is $bd$, worth $0$; any batna profile is $ad$ or $bd$, worth $0$), and symmetrically for player 2 — so both players are indifferent at order $\varepsilon^0$ and the order-$\varepsilon^1$ totals decide. (iv) Totals against all 32 opponent proposals: $T_1(s^\ast_1) = 72$: $ac$ is realized whenever $ac \in \mathcal A_2$ ($16$ proposals, $48$) or, with no deal, when $b_2 = c$ ($8$ more, $24$); $T_1(\text{add } bc) = 68$; $T_2$ likewise $72$ vs $68$ [checked]. Maximum of $T_i$ over all 32 proposals is $72$ for both players, so $s^\ast_i$ is a best response to the uniformly $\varepsilon$-trembled opponent for every small $\varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon < \tfrac1{32}$); explicit perturbed-game payoffs $\tfrac{18}{25}, \tfrac9{125}, \tfrac{9}{125000}$ equal the maxima at $\varepsilon = 10^{-2}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-6}$ [checked]. (v) The second-order decomposition for player 1: gain $+8$ (four proposals $(d, \mathcal A_2)$ with $bc \in \mathcal A_2 \not\ni ac$: batna $ad$ replaced by $bc$), mechanism (a) $-4$ (four proposals $(c, \mathcal A_2)$ with $bc \in \mathcal A_2 \not\ni ac$: batna $ac = 3$ replaced by $bc = 2$), mechanism (b) $-8$ (eight proposals accepting both $ac$ and $bc$: sel displaced from $ac$ to $bc$); net $-4$ [checked] — the thread's "$-8 + 4$" is the same number grouped differently. (vi) Selten step: in a perturbed game with minimum weight $\varepsilon$ on each pure strategy, the constrained strategy $\sigma^\varepsilon_i = (1 - 31\varepsilon)\delta_{s^\ast_i} + \varepsilon \sum_{s \ne s^\ast_i} \delta_s$ is a best response iff its free mass sits on pure best responses, i.e. iff $s^\ast_i$ is a pure best response to $\sigma^\varepsilon_{-i}$ — exactly what (iv) verifies; $\sigma^\varepsilon \to \sigma^\ast$; SC Def 10.4 is existential over perturbation sequences, so the uniform sequence suffices. Verdict: the outcome $ad$, $U = (0,0)$, of a trembling-hand equilibrium in SC Def 10.4's sense is strictly dominated by $bc$, $U = (2,1)$. **The theorem is false as stated; the proof's "additional terms are $O(\varepsilon)$" is the error — for $n = 2$ they are of the same order as the gain and can be negative.**

Escape hatch, for the sibling's benefit [derived]: the counterexample dies under a *strictly perfect* reading ("for every perturbation sequence") — a perturbation concentrating player 2's trembles on $(d,\{bc\})$ makes "add $bc$" strictly profitable for player 1. But SC Def 10.4 says "there exists a sequence", and strictly perfect equilibria need not exist in finite games (Okada 1981 [reconstructed: standard game-theory reference, not read this session]), so that repair costs Selten existence. My independent enumeration also shows the game has a second uniform-THPE outcome, $bc$ (one profile) — the theorem fails, not the game.

### HA-5 — homogeneous harmony $=$ optimality. (i) SURVIVES; (iii) and the "$=$ maximizers" restatement KILLED → WOUNDED with the safe-batna hypothesis.

(i) I re-read the proof clause by clause. The lemma — for each optimal $O^\dagger$ and each player $j$, the proposal $s_j' = (b_j, \mathcal A_j \cup \{O^\dagger\})$ weakly dominates $s_j = (b_j, \mathcal A_j)$ profile-by-profile in value and strictly improves against every completely mixed $\sigma_{-j}$ unless $b_j$ is a safe batna (every $(b_j, \cdot)$ optimal) — is correct: when the others accept exactly $\{O^\dagger\}$ the outcome under $s_j$ is the batna profile $(b_j, b_{-j})$ for arbitrary $b_{-j}$, so non-safety produces a strict gain at a positive-probability profile; hence a non-accepting, non-safe proposal is never a constrained best response and carries only its minimum weight in every perturbed equilibrium, so it is absent from the limit's support. The case analysis on a support profile (all accept $O^\dagger$ ⇒ deal is $W$-max of a set containing $O^\dagger$ ⇒ optimal, since Pareto-consistency with common utility puts every non-optimum below every optimum; else a safe-batna player $j$ either sees no deal ⇒ optimal batna profile, or a deal $O \notin M$ ⇒ $j$'s deviation to $(b_j, \emptyset)$ yields $\max$ in every profile, contradicting Nash of the limit) is complete. Sanity: 120 random $2{\times}2$ and 8 random $3{\times}2$ common-payoff games, every uniform-THPE outcome optimal [checked, `bg_check.py` (f), `sharpening_check.py`]. The 43/0 and 337/4 Stag Hunt counts and the FR-12 tree's three optima under each $W$-order reproduce exactly [checked].

(iii) and the restatement of SC Thm 13.1(2) as "the surviving outcomes are exactly the maximizers, unique iff the maximizer is" — **false**. Counterexample [checked, `bg_check.py` (b)]: common payoff $V(ac) = V(bd) = 1$, $V(ad) = V(bc) = 0$; $W$ ranks $bd > ac > ad > bc$. Uniform-THPE outcomes $= \{bd\}$: $bd$ 337 Nash profiles / 4 pass, $ac$ 273 / 0. With $W$ reversed, $\{ac\}$. Why it holds under *every* perturbation sequence, not just uniform trembles [derived]: no player has a safe batna ($a$ would need $ad$ optimal, $b$ would need $bc$, etc.), so by the thread's own lemma every support strategy of every player accepts *every* optimum; the intersection then contains $M$ in every support profile and sel returns $\arg\max_W M$. I verified the dominance step exhaustively: for both players, adding the $W$-max optimum to any acceptable set lacking it weakly dominates profile-by-profile and is strictly better at some opponent proposal [checked]. So:

**Proposition S (sharpening of HA-5) [derived; checked on 84 random no-safe-batna games].** In the homogeneous game $\mathcal G(B)$: (a) every harmonious outcome is $V_B$-optimal (HA-5(i)); (b) if *no* player has a safe batna, the harmonious outcome is **unique** and equals $\arg\max_W M$ — $W$ *does* select among optima, and uniqueness holds even with $|M| > 1$; (c) if some player has a safe batna, other optima can arise (the batna channel and safe-batna-restricted deals): FR-12 realizes all three. The FR-12 tree is exactly the degenerate case where a whole row (out) and a whole column ($x$) of the profile table are optimal. HA-14(i) ("$W$ does not pin the outcome even homogeneously — SC Remark 11.4's non-uniqueness is not a heterogeneity phenomenon") and HA-5(iii) are therefore true only on trees with safe batnas; the generic homogeneous verdict is the opposite. Theorem H(a) as written ("elements of $M$, at least one harmonious") is fine; the "exactly the maximizers" gloss in HA-5, the condensed notes and the handoff to the final phase is not.

## 4. Closing
