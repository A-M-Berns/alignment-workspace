## 2. Task 2 — learning-first fairness, made precise

### 2.1 The interaction model — DY-7

Three candidates, as the mandate lists them.

(a) **Repeated independent episodes of one problem $B$.** Episode $t$ is a fresh run of $B$ (Def 6) under the learner's current procedure $C_t$; between episodes the learner updates $C_t$ from what it experienced. This is the frequency reading of calibration and of Remark 5.4 ("repeated interaction is repeated probing … sampling estimates dispositions, so branching swallows labels in the limit"), and it is the only model in which "converges to the optimum *on $B$*" is even well-typed. It is the definition's home.

(b) **A distribution over problems.** Each episode draws $B\sim\pi$ from a family; the learner's action is a function of the point it is consulted at (Def 4: procedures see points, not trees) and of its own history. This is the first-personal model (brief A, Sharpening 5: the procedure is handed states, not trees) and the grounding thread's "family access (ii)" (gap1 §2; bridge dossier row T10 — vocabulary flagged, not resolved here). Prop 6's pair decides what it does [checked; `dy_family.py`]: $B_1$ and $B_2$ carry the *same* point $d$ with opposite optima (pay / refuse); on the mixture, $V_{\mathrm{mix}}(q)=\pi\cdot\tfrac{q(y-x)}{2}+(1-\pi)\cdot\tfrac{y-q(x+y)}{2}$, so $\partial V_{\mathrm{mix}}/\partial q=\pi y-\tfrac{x+y}{2}$ and the mixture-optimal disposition is **pay iff $\pi>\frac{x+y}{2y}$** (at $x=1,y=3$: $\pi>\tfrac23$). No point-based learner converges to the per-problem optimum on both members (it cannot tell them apart); every consistent one converges to the mixture optimum, which is $\mathrm{UDT}_{s^\circ}$ with $s^\circ$ the family prior — Remark 6.2's "the $B$-dependence must be built in — the prior", derived as a limit object rather than posited. So under (b) *family*-learning-fairness holds iff the point determines the optimal act across the family, which is Remark 6.3's richness condition (Q1) in learning clothes.

(c) **Within-run repetition (self-succession).** The AMD phenomenon. It is a property of $B$ (nested fibers), not of the interaction model; it is what the learner-class index sees differently (DY-10). Not a candidate for the definition's home.

**Non-triviality lives in the learner class, not the model.** Under (a) with an unrestricted class, every finite $B$ is learning-fair: a bandit over the finitely many deterministic procedures (or the compact behavioural simplex) with a GLIE rule converges to the optimum of $V_B$ on any finite tree, because from the policy level $B$ is just a finite-armed bandit with bounded rewards (P1's assignment/procedure distinction only says the *value* of the best arm may be below the assignment optimum). So a learning-first definition is vacuous until the class is restricted to learners that see *decision points* and assign credit *per point* — and then everything turns on how they assign it.

### 2.2 The learner classes — DY-8 (definitions)

Fix $B$, model (a), GLIE exploration (every action at every point explored infinitely often, exploration rate $\to0$; Def 10's uniform tremble $C_t^{\varepsilon_t}$ with $\varepsilon_t\to0$, $\sum\varepsilon_t=\infty$ is the canonical instance — Remark 3.9's "evidential learner with vanishing exploration"). A learner is indexed by its **credit scheme** $\kappa$, i.e. by which experience it lets update its estimate for $(d,a)$:

- $\kappa=\mathrm{ev}$ (**event-conditioned; face-value EDT; FR-11's tremble-EDT read dynamically**): a consultation at a $d$-node with draw $a$ is credited with the episode return iff the leaf-world satisfies $a\wedge O_d$ — the state at $d$ is the empirical version of Def 8's conditional under the explored policy.
- $\kappa=\mathrm{occ}$ (**occurrence-conditioned, per occurrence; SIA; Theorem 1**): every consultation on the path is credited with the return; under independent redraws this is the Monte-Carlo estimate of the forcing value $G_q(C,a)$ weighted by reach, i.e. of $\sum_q R_qG_q(C,a)/\mathbb E[\#_d]$ (P1 item 15: tremble-EDT under independent redraws is Theorem 1's evaluator).
- $\kappa=\mathrm{dev}$ (**deviation-conditioned, per run; SSA; Theorem 2 / Def 22**): the learner credits "my policy answered $a$ at $d$ this episode" with the return, one credit per run however often $d$ occurred — which is what a shared-seed agent (Q12) experiences, and what estimates $V_B(C[d\mapsto a])$; P1 item 17's hypothesis (full-support seed at $d$; no other stochastically-answered nested point) is where this identification holds.
- $\kappa=\mathrm{pol}$ (**policy-level**): arms are whole procedures; also policy gradient on the behavioural parameters with the episode return.

**Definition (learning-fair).** $B$ is **$\mathcal L$-learning-fair** if every GLIE learner in $\mathcal L$ converges (a.s., in the greedy/limiting policy) to a $V_B$-optimal procedure (Def 21's optimality, $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$). Which coherence sense: the mandate's three are *type-level* ("same decision at action and policy level" — automatic for any procedure, since Def 4 already makes a procedure a policy-level object; P1 item 25 comment), *Nash* (Def 22, whose fixed points include TN-V2's $(2,2)$) and *global optimum*. Only the third makes learning-fairness a substantive property of $B$; the first is the type, the second is a fixed-point set that some learner classes converge *into* (below) rather than a target.

**Definition (static shadow).** For a credit scheme $\kappa$ let $S_\kappa(B)$ be the set of procedures stable under $\kappa$'s best-response map at $\varepsilon\to0$: $S_{\mathrm{ev}}$ = the tremble-EDT-consistent set (FR-11's concrete form, sequence version per DY-3); $S_{\mathrm{occ}}$ = Theorem 1's fixed points; $S_{\mathrm{dev}}$ = Def-22-coherent procedures with the deviation quantified over $\Delta(A_d)$ (P1 item 25's repair); $S_{\mathrm{pol}}=T_{\mathrm{opt}}$. PC-12's slogan "the static criterion is a theorem about the limit" is then the family of statements "limit points of $\mathcal L_\kappa$-learners lie in $S_\kappa(B)$" — one per $\kappa$ (§6).

### 3.1 (a) Strongly fair ⇒ learning-fair for tremble-EDT learners — DY-9

**Theorem (dynamic FR-11) [derived, with one standard convergence result reconstructed].** Let $B$ be strongly fair, fully recorded (FRec) for every procedure, with zero-probability chance edges pruned and every queried observation satisfied by some leaf-world — P1 item 20's hypotheses (the last one is adversary-repair Claim A's addition). Then $B$ is $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ev}}$-learning-fair.

*Proof.* Step 1 (the tree is a finite episodic MDP whose states are the points). Under strong fairness no fiber is nested ($\#_d\le1$, P1 FR-1(i)) and the continuation $(\lambda,r)$-law after drawing $a$ at any $d$-node is the same at every member of the fiber under every procedure, trembled included (FR-1(ii)). So "the agent is at $d$" is a Markov state for the purpose of value estimation: the return after $(d,a)$ depends on $(d,a)$ and on the procedure below, never on which node of the fiber was reached. Step 2 (the learner's data are the MDP's transitions). Recording plus realized observations make the event $\{\lambda\models a\wedge O_d\}$ coincide a.s. with "reached a $d$-node and drew $a$" (P1 adversary-repair Claim A, step 1; Prop 7's recording argument gives $\nu_\varepsilon(a\mid O_d)=C^\varepsilon(d)(a)>0$), so $\kappa=\mathrm{ev}$ credits exactly the reach-and-draw returns — no selection bias — and every $(d,a)$ is credited infinitely often under GLIE (each point is reached with probability bounded below by a product of pruned chance weights and exploration rates). Step 3 (convergence). GLIE Monte-Carlo control on a finite episodic MDP converges to an optimal greedy policy [reconstructed: the GLIE convergence theorems for on-policy control — Singh, Jaakkola, Littman & Szepesvári 2000 for SARSA(0); the finite-horizon Monte-Carlo version is the textbook statement (Sutton & Barto) and follows by leaves-up induction: the lowest fibers' estimates converge to their true continuation values (SLLN), their greedy actions stabilize at strict maxima (any tie-break is optimal at ties), then the next level, exactly FR-11's Step 3 run forward in time]. $\square$

## 4. Task 4 — exploration and the self-confirming refuser trap

### 4.1 Formalization — DY-15 first, so the objects are named

Iterated mugging: episodes are independent runs of $B_1$; the environment is one of two instantiations of the abstract problem (Def 14): $h_1$ = Omega couples (heads transfer iff the agent's disposition is pay — $B_1$ itself), $h_0$ = the heads branch is inert (no transfer ever). The agent's coupling credence is $\pi_t=P_t(h_1)$; the agent observes each episode's coin, its own action on tails, and whether a transfer arrived on heads. Per-episode expected return of the disposition "pay" at credence $\pi$: $\pi\cdot\tfrac{y-x}{2}+(1-\pi)\cdot\tfrac{-x}{2}=\tfrac{\pi y-x}{2}$; of "refuse": $0$. Myopic Bayes: **pay iff $\pi>x/y$** (slop §6's formula; arithmetic confirmed P1:verify G2).

**The trap [derived].** A payer generates decisive evidence on every heads episode (transfer $\Rightarrow h_1$, none $\Rightarrow h_0$; under $h_0$ a transfer has probability $0$); expected time to the first heads is 2 episodes. A refuser generates no transfers under either hypothesis — the likelihood ratio of its whole data stream is 1 — so $\pi_t\equiv\pi_0$ forever: refusing at $\pi_0\le x/y$ is *self-confirming*. In the vocabulary of the static picture: the refuser's state is masked-OC-calibrated (Def 9) for every procedure (P1 item 1) and mixture-calibrated (Appendix B item 4) on the data it generates; what is uncalibrated is its *deviation* statistic — what would happen if its disposition were pay — which is exactly the counterfactual calibration Remark 3.11 declines to impose by default (and Q6 asks about). So the static picture can **name** the trap: it is a self-confirming equilibrium [reconstructed: Fudenberg–Levine's notion — beliefs correct on-path, unconstrained off-path], i.e. an on-path-calibrated, off-path-uncalibrated fixed point. What it cannot do is **price** the escape, because escaping means generating off-path data at a cost, and cost-of-data is a statement about a sequence of runs. That is the one place in this thread where the dynamic picture is not optional.

### 4.2 Which $(\pi_0,\ \text{exploration schedule})$ pairs converge — DY-13

**[checked; `dy_trap.py`, $x=1$, $y=3$.]**

(A) *Bayes-optimal exploration is a threshold, and the myopic trap is a discounting artifact in the two-hypothesis model.* With discount $\beta$, paying reveals the truth on the next heads (probability $\tfrac12$ per episode); $W(1)=\tfrac{(y-x)/2}{1-\beta}$, $W(0)=0$; while paying, $W(\pi)=\tfrac{\pi y-x}{2}+\beta\big[\tfrac12W(\pi)+\tfrac12\pi W(1)\big]$, so paying is optimal iff
$$\pi\;>\;\pi^*(\beta)\;=\;\frac{x}{\,y+\dfrac{\beta(y-x)}{2(1-\beta)}\,}.$$
$\pi^*(0)=x/y=\tfrac13$ (myopic), $\pi^*(0.5)=\tfrac14$, $\pi^*(0.9)=\tfrac1{12}$, $\pi^*(0.99)=\tfrac1{102}$; value iteration on a 2000-point grid reproduces $0.3335,\ 0.2505,\ 0.0835,\ 0.0100$. As $\beta\to1$ every $\pi_0>0$ explores: a patient Bayesian with two hypotheses is never trapped. The exploration itself costs: a trapped refuser who switches to "pay until the first heads" pays $x$ on each tails episode before that heads — expected cost $x$ (one expected tails before the first heads) — against an expected gain of $\pi_0\cdot\tfrac{y-x}{2}$ per remaining episode if $h_1$ is true.

(B) *Convergence of a myopic refuser with an exploration schedule $\varepsilon_t$* (pay with probability $\varepsilon_t$ when Bayes says refuse): it learns $h_1$ iff some exploratory pay coincides with a heads episode, probability $\varepsilon_t/2$ per episode, so $P(\text{never learn})=\prod_t(1-\varepsilon_t/2)\to0$ **iff $\sum_t\varepsilon_t=\infty$**; under $h_0$ the exploration costs $\varepsilon_t\cdot x/2$ per episode, so the played policy converges to the optimal (refuse) iff $\varepsilon_t\to0$. Hence: $(\pi_0,\varepsilon_\cdot)$ converges to the optimal policy under both hypotheses iff [$\pi_0>x/y$] or [$\sum\varepsilon_t=\infty$ and $\varepsilon_t\to0$]. Regret, $T=10^5$:

| schedule | $P(\text{not learned by }T)$ | $\mathbb E[\text{regret}\mid h_1]$ (forgone $\tfrac{y-x}{2}$ per refusing episode) | $\mathbb E[\text{exploration cost}\mid h_0]$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\varepsilon=0.1$ | $\approx0$ | $18.0$ | $5000$ (linear) |
| $\varepsilon=0.01$ | $\approx0$ | $198$ | $500$ (linear) |
| $\varepsilon_t=1/t$ | $1.8\times10^{-3}$ | $355$ | $6.05$ |
| $\varepsilon_t=2/t$ | $5\times10^{-6}$ | $5.05$ | $11.6$ |
| $\varepsilon_t=4/t$ | $7.5\times10^{-11}$ | $0.12$ | $22.0$ |
| $\varepsilon_t=1/\sqrt t$ | $\approx0$ | $1.10$ | $316$ |

Analytically, $\varepsilon_t=c/t$ gives $P(\text{not learned by }T)\sim T^{-c/2}$, so the expected learning time is finite iff $c>2$, and the exploration cost under $h_0$ is $\sim\tfrac{cx}{2}\ln T$ — logarithmic regret, the bandit-optimal order; constant $\varepsilon$ buys fast escape at linear cost. The trade-off is the standard one-armed-bandit trade-off with the safe arm (refuse) known and the informative arm (pay) revealing itself only on heads.

### 4.3 The Beta-rate refinement: incomplete learning bites payers too — DY-14

**[checked; `dy_trap.py` (B), `dy_trap2.py`.]** Replace the two hypotheses by a coupling *rate* $\theta$ (on a heads episode with a paying disposition the transfer arrives with probability $\theta$), prior $\mathrm{Beta}(a_0,b_0)$, posterior mean $\frac{a_0+k}{a_0+b_0+n}$ after $n$ informative episodes with $k$ transfers; myopic rule pay iff mean $>x/y$; optimal policy pay iff $\theta>x/y$. Probability of ending in permanent refusal ("trap") when paying is optimal:

| prior | $\theta=0.8$ | $\theta=0.6$ | $\theta=0.4$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beta(1,1), myopic | $0.207$ | $0.457$ | $0.823$ |
| Beta(1,1), $\beta=0.9$ | $0.009$ | $0.096$ | $0.557$ |
| Beta(1,1), $\beta=0.99$ | $0.000$ | $0.017$ | $0.316$ |
| Beta(2,1), myopic | $0.009$ | $0.096$ | $0.557$ |
| Beta(1,2), myopic | $1$ (never pays) | $1$ | $1$ |
| Beta(1,2), $\beta=0.99$ | $0.002$ | $0.039$ | $0.396$ |
| Beta(5,5), $\beta=0.99$ | $0.000$ | $0.000$ | $0.122$ |

(The patient rows solve the Bellman equation $W(n,k)=\max\{0,\ \tfrac{my-x}{2}+\beta[\tfrac12W(n,k)+\tfrac12(mW(n{+}1,k{+}1)+(1-m)W(n{+}1,k))]\}$ by backward induction over 300 informative episodes.) This makes a lot of sense when we think of $W$ as a set of possible complete world histories. For any given time, this gives a partition where world histories are in the same subset if they agree on the world history up to that point in time. Further, this gives us a sequence of nested partitions, since the partition associated with one time is always a refinement of the partition associated with an earlier time. **Note that this is a multiplicative/updateless view of time. There is also an additive/updateful view of time, in which time is a nested sequence of subsets.** In the additive view, possible worlds are eliminated as you pass through time. In the multiplicative view, possible worlds are distinguished from each other as you pass through time. We will focus on the multiplicative view, which I consider better-motivated."
- Nested subagents: "This gives a nested sequence of multiplicative superagents $C_{T_n}\lhd_\times\cdots\lhd_\times C_{T_0}$, where $C_{T_i}=\mathrm{External}_{T_i}(C)$." Lemma: "if $U$ and $V$ are partitions of $W$ and $U$ is a refinement of $V$, then $\mathrm{External}_U(C)\lhd_\times\mathrm{External}_V(C)$." And: "The sequence $C_{T_0},\dots,C_{T_n}$ represents the agent persisting across time, but each subagent $C_{T_i}$ does not really represent a single time-slice of the agent. Instead, $C_{T_i}$ represents an agent persisting across time starting at the time $T_i$."
- Monotonicity: "for $i\le j$ we have $\mathrm{Obs}(C_{T_i})\subseteq\mathrm{Obs}(C_{T_j})$ and $\mathrm{Ctrl}(C_{T_i})\supseteq\mathrm{Ctrl}(C_{T_j})$", via the two lemmas (refinement shrinks controllables, grows observables); gloss: "This result allows us to think of time as a sort of ritual in which control of the world is sacrificed in exchange for ability to condition on the world."
- Future work: a list of comment-linked directions (frames as partitions into rectangles, generalizing observability, preferences and goals, subagents, logical time, logical uncertainty, formalizing time, computational complexity, time and coarse world models, category-theory-first approaches), with "I've erred on the side of inclusion in these comments: some may point to dead ends."

So PC-12's "Scott's own time construction is, in his words, the updateless view of time" is accurate to the source: the words are "a multiplicative/updateless view of time". Note the correction to my prior expectation and to the Eight Definitions teaser: the final post does *not* formalize agents that learn; it formalizes the *ordering* of an agent's boundaries by partitions of world histories.

### 5.2 Does the construction bear on relocation? — DY-17

**[derived] direction; [guess] exact identification (→ cf-frontier).** Phase 1's dictionary (P1 items 4–5, CF-5 verified by adversary-cf ADV-1): $\mathrm{Fr}(B)$ has agent $\prod_dA_d$ (the whole policy), and the action-level frame $\mathrm{Loc}_d(B)$ — the $d$-decision with the other points externalized into the environment — satisfies $\mathrm{Loc}_d(B)\lhd_\times\mathrm{Fr}(B)$ on the nose, by the externalizing definition. In the time post's terms a multiplicative subagent obtained by externalizing is a *later* self ($C_{T_j}\lhd_\times C_{T_i}$ for $j\ge i$): fewer controllables, more observables. $\mathrm{Loc}_d$ is exactly that — it controls only $A_d$, and can (locally) observe what the earlier points did — so the action-level description sits *later* in CF time than the policy-level one, and relocation (replacing the $\mathrm{Loc}_d$'s by the superagent $\mathrm{Fr}$, or, in the frame calculus, un-assuming and coarsening per CF-24) is a move **backwards** along CF time: the agent's boundary shifts earlier, to a frame with more controllables and fewer observables. This is the formal content of [scrubbed] and of brief B PC-1's "relocate until everything you don't know at $d$ is downstream of $d$": in CF time, downstream = finer partition = later externalization. Two qualifications. (i) CF time indexes by partitions $T$ of $W$ (world histories), whereas $\mathrm{Loc}_d$ externalizes the *other points' answers*, a partition of the agent set $A$ (Def 32's $\mathrm{External}_B$ for a partition $B$ of $A$); the two coincide only when upstream answers are world events — recording plus evented chance (P1's EC + FRec) — so the statement "$\mathrm{Loc}_d\simeq\mathrm{External}_T(\mathrm{Fr}(B))$ for the partition $T$ of $W$ by the recorded upstream context" is a [guess] with those hypotheses, for the cf-frontier thread. (ii) The relocation site (the fiber's lca, P1 R2) is then the coarsest time $T$ at which the coupled cluster is not yet distinguished, i.e. the latest time at which the decision can be made *once* — CF time gives the lca a meaning ("the last moment before Omega's simulation and the live choice come apart") but no new mathematics beyond P1's.

### 5.3 The division-of-labour verdict — DY-18

**[derived].** PC-12: "Cartesian frames will help with the static half and not the dynamic one." Confirmed, with the reason PC-12 does not give. CF time is a *preorder on frames* — nested externalizations indexed by refinements of a partition of world histories — and the post contains no probability and no learning; its dynamics is the passage from a coarser to a finer partition of *one* history space, i.e. the structure of a single interaction's information order, not a sequence of interactions. Learning-fairness (§2) is a statement about sequences of run distributions $\mu_{B,C_t}$ and about which returns are credited to which points; the frame functor forgets $\mu$ and $\beta$ (P1 item 4; adversary-cf's closing point). So CF cannot state learning-fairness, exploration cost, basins, or the trap — everything in §§3–4. What CF time *does* give the pair is its static leg in pure form: "Obs increases, Ctrl decreases" is the possibilistic shadow of "information accumulates across time and options are consumed", and the multiplicative reading ("possible worlds are distinguished from each other as you pass through time", not eliminated) is precisely the updateless bookkeeping that keeps the other branch in the frame — the frame-side twin of relocation keeping the heads branch in the value function. Verdict: CF is the right formalism for the *order* of boundaries (which decision is earlier, what is downstream of what) and the wrong one for *convergence*; the multi-round decomposition (cf-frontier, P1 open problem 2) is static CF time, and nothing in it will produce a learning theorem.

## 6. Task 6 — recommendation: a Galois pair, and the first theorem

### 6.1 Why not invert (learning primary)

(1) Vacuity or stance: unrestricted learner classes make every finite problem learning-fair; restricted ones import the conditioning fork as an architectural choice (DY-12). (2) The learning picture's verdicts inherit multiplicity as basins (DY-4) — it does not select either. (3) Its convergence theorems are, class by class, v2's §8: the limit sets of $\kappa$-learners are $S_\kappa$, and v2 already has $S_{\mathrm{occ}}$ (Theorem 1), $S_{\mathrm{dev}}$ (Theorem 2 / Def 22) and $S_{\mathrm{ev}}$ (Defs 8–10 + $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$, sequence form). Inverting would re-derive the static theorems as corollaries of stochastic approximation and lose the ex-ante checkability that a fairness *property of the description* (brief A, Sharpening 1) provides.

### 6.2 Why not stay static-first with learning as a check

(1) v2's own procedure-side existence device fails in fixed-$C$ form on v2's own miniature and is repaired only by the sequence form, which is a convergence statement (DY-2, DY-3). (2) Selection off the fair class has no static answer (Q4); E-stability (Q5) is a dynamic notion in disguise and has no content until a best-response map is named — the credit-scheme-indexed one (DY-4). (3) The trap is nameable statically but priceable only dynamically (DY-15): "which prior?" has a regret-theoretic answer ($\sum\varepsilon_t=\infty$, $\varepsilon_t=c/t$ with $c>2$, or $\pi^*(\beta)$) that no calibration equation contains.

### 6.3 The pair

Let $\Lambda$ be the class of GLIE learners with a credit scheme, ordered by inclusion, and $\Sigma$ the static solution concepts (sets of procedures per problem), ordered by inclusion. $\mathrm{Sh}:\Lambda\to\Sigma$ sends a learner class to its set of limit points (its *shadow*); $\mathrm{Dyn}:\Sigma\to\Lambda$ sends a solution concept to the largest learner class whose limits lie in it. On the fair + recorded + realized class, $\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ev}})=\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{occ}})=\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dev}})=T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ (DY-9; the three static conditions coincide there — P1 item 20's Step 1 is the coincidence); off it they separate (AMD, TN-V2, mugging: DY-10, DY-11, DY-4). The pair's two directions are the two research programs: statics supplies *checkable-at-the-fixed-point* conditions and the fair class where they are sufficient; dynamics supplies existence in sequence form, basins, and the price of counterfactual content. Each constrains the other: a static condition with no learner class beneath it is a stipulation (the fixed-$C$ Remark 3.12 is the example); a learner class with no static shadow is unanalysable (its verdicts are initial-condition facts).

### 6.4 The first theorem to attempt — DY-19

**Shadow theorem (per-occurrence).** On a finite tree $B$ with independent redraws, let $C_t$ be the greedy (or softmax with temperature $\to0$) policy of a GLIE per-occurrence Monte-Carlo control learner. Then every limit point $C$ of $(C_t)$ satisfies Theorem 1's condition at every queried $d$ — $\mathrm{supp}\,C(d)\subseteq\arg\max_a\sum_{q:d_q=d}R_q(C)G_q(C,a)$ — and, conversely, an isolated Theorem-1 fixed point at which the per-occurrence best-response map is locally contracting is reached with positive probability from a neighbourhood (E-stability, Q5, *defined* as this local attraction). Evidence: the AMD (unique fixed point $\tfrac13$, attracting, reached: DY-10), TN-V2 (two fixed points, both reached: DY-4), the convex $k$-fold mugging (two fixed points, one with a basin too small to be reached from uniform starts: DY-4). Proof route: stochastic approximation — the per-occurrence $Q$-estimates track $\sum_qR_qG_q/\mathbb E[\#_d]$ of the current policy, and GLIE control with those estimates is a best-response dynamic whose rest points are exactly Theorem 1's condition (P1 item 15's identity). Its dual for deviation-credited (shared-seed) learners and Def 22 (mixed) is the second target; the event-credited version is DY-9. This theorem gives Q5 its definition, converts Remark 5.2's multiplicity into basin measures, and is falsifiable by the toolkit (every step is a finite computation on a named tree).
