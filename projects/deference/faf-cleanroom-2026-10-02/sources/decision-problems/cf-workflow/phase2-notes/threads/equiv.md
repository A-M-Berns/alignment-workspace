## 0. The answer in one paragraph (register: derived, with the separating examples checked)

On finite trees there is one natural equivalence of subtrees once one fixes what must be preserved — namely everything v2's semantics consumes: the run distribution over *traces* (which points are consulted in which order, which actions are drawn, which leaf label is reached), for every procedure. It has three presentations that coincide on the stratified fair class: (i) inductive — chance-normal form plus labelled isomorphism; (ii) coinductive — probabilistic bisimulation *modulo the laws of the probabilistic-choice effect* (flatten chance-of-chance, delete probability-one chance nodes and zero-probability branches, merge bisimilar children); (iii) semantic — trace equivalence for every procedure. Brief A's stratified recursion is (i)/(iii) and brief B's bisimulation is (ii), so C1 is a coincidence *provided* brief A's $\sim$ is read at the trace level (not the value or law level) and brief B's bisimulation is taken modulo the chance laws. The two residues are real and go opposite ways: plain bisimulation keeps chance nodes nothing can see (E1, E2 below), and a value- or law-level $\sim$ erases spurious oracle queries that Definition 13's occurrence structure treats as real (E3) and, at the value level, erases world structure that calibration clause 1 consumes (Smoking Lesion with $\beta = 0$). The chosen relation excludes nesting outright, closes the anthropic fork, admits a canonical *terminal* DAG quotient (unique minimal DAG; fairness is exactly the existence of a quotient in which each point labels one node), is decidable in polynomial time, and changes no quantity in Definitions 6–13, 17–22 or Theorems 1–2 [checked on E1/E2]. For problem-equivalence $\sim$ the right object is not one relation but a graded family of realizable-law sets — pure, shared-seed, behavioral (Def 6), correlated — and the repair-licensing concept is an asymmetric two-inclusion certificate $B \sqsubseteq B'$ whose one-line consequence is optimum preservation; it blesses joint relocation of almost-fair inputs and refuses the absent-minded-driver collapse, which is what phase 1's Seam 1 needed.

**EQ-1 [checked; `grep` on `decision-problems-v2.md`].** Brief B's two citation flags are both hyphenation/LaTeX artifacts, not errors. v2 line 71 (Remark 3.1) reads verbatim: "The standard repair is to make the calls constructors, and it returns Definition 5 as an inductive type, $\mathrm{Probl} = (\Omega \times \mathbb{R}) + \Delta(\mathrm{Probl}) + (d : \mathcal{D},\; A_d \to \mathrm{Probl})$ — leaf, chance, decision-with-continuation — read as the least solution: every problem is finitely many applications of the three constructors. This is free-monad syntax for two effects, probabilistic choice and consulting an oracle of type $(d : \mathcal{D}) \to \Delta(A_d)$; Definition 6 is the *handler* discharging both, the fold from syntax to semantics, which the shadow discussion shows is neither injective nor surjective." The grep for "free monad" (with a space) returns 0 hits; "free-monad" is there. Likewise `cf_s` returns 0 hits because v2 writes `\mathrm{cf}_s` (23 occurrences; Definition 2 clause 3). So PC-2's typing citation stands: v2 does present the tree as the free monad on the two-effect signature, with Definition 6 the fold.

What the type does *not* do is hand over bisimulation by itself. Register: derived. An inductive type's native equality is *structural equality* (the initial algebra); read as a coalgebra for $F(X) = (\Omega\times\mathbb R) + \Delta(X) + \sum_d (A_d \to X)$ it also carries *bisimilarity* (largest relation with the zig-zag property, with the Larsen–Skou lifting of $\Delta$ — a choice); and the fold of Definition 6 carries its *kernel* (equality of extensional shadows). These are three different relations on finite trees, strictly ordered (EQ-2). Structural equality and plain bisimilarity are too fine — they distinguish chance nodes that no semantics of v2 sees — and the kernel of the fold is too coarse — it identifies a spurious oracle query with no query, which v2's own extensional-shadow paragraph (the sentence before Remark 3.1: "Inserting a query of $d$ whose branches lead to identical subtrees leaves $\widehat B$ unchanged while changing $\mathrm{occ}(d)$ and $\#_d$") deliberately refuses. The relation the *semantics* selects is the one that keeps the oracle effect syntactic and quotients the probabilistic effect by its equational theory: bisimulation modulo the $\Delta$-monad laws, equivalently trace equivalence (EQ-3). PC-2's "that type hands you its equivalence" is therefore right about the family and wrong about which member; the argument that picks the member is in §4.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Trees, fibers, normal form

$B$ is a concrete decision problem (Definition 5), $F_d := \{q : d_q = d\}$ the fiber of a queried point (fair-repair §0), $T_q$ the subtree at node $q$ read as a problem in its own right (Definition 6 runs it; its points are those it carries). A fiber is *nested* if some member is a proper ancestor of another. *Pruning* deletes zero-probability chance edges (v2's $\beta_n \in \Delta(\mathrm{children})$ allows them; Definition 6 never follows them; fair-repair already prunes).

**Definition (chance-normal form $N$).** Bottom-up: normalize the children; at a chance node collect the pairs (probability, non-chance descendant) reached through chance-only paths, multiplying probabilities (flattening); drop zero probabilities; merge descendants that are $\simeq_\Delta$-equivalent (defined below, on the already-normalized children) by summing their probabilities; if a single descendant of probability one remains, return it (the chance node disappears); otherwise return the chance node over the merged pairs. In normal form every chance node has at least two pairwise inequivalent non-chance children with positive probabilities. These are exactly the equations of the probabilistic-choice effect — the $\Delta$-monad laws — imposed on the free-monad syntax, with the oracle effect left free.

### 2.2 Six relations on subtrees

- $T \cong T'$ (**strict labelled isomorphism**, phase 1's working notion): rooted-tree isomorphism matching node type, chance probabilities (branches matched by probability, branch labels ignored), decision points (identity per Remark 2.2), action edges, and leaf pairs $(\lambda, r)$. Toolkit: `labelled_isomorphic`; my `iso_key` agrees with it on every pair tested [checked, `run_c1.py`, `run_stress.py`].
- $T \simeq T'$ (**strong probabilistic bisimulation**): the largest relation $R$ such that related leaves have equal labels, related decision nodes carry the same point and have $R$-related children action by action, related chance nodes induce the same distribution on $R$-classes of children, and no leaf/chance/decision node is related to a node of another type. On finite trees it is computed exactly by a bottom-up canonical key (`key_strong`): the key of a chance node is the multiset of (class key, summed probability) pairs.
- $T \simeq_\Delta T'$ (**bisimulation modulo the chance laws**): $N(T) \simeq N(T')$; canonical key `key_delta`.
- $T \approx_{\mathrm{tr}} T'$ (**trace equivalence**): for every procedure $C$ (over the points of both), the distributions of $\big((d_1,a_1),\dots,(d_k,a_k),(\lambda,r)\big)$ — the consulted points in order, the drawn actions, the leaf label; chance steps invisible — coincide. Toolkit-derived: `trace_dist` pushes `Run.mu` through `LeafInfo.decisions`.
- $T \approx_{\mathrm{law}} T'$ (**law equivalence**): $\widehat{T}(C) = \widehat{T'}(C)$ for every $C$ — equality of the extensional shadows of the subtrees (`dp.law`).
- $T \approx_{\mathrm{val}} T'$ (**value equivalence**): $V_T(C) = V_{T'}(C)$ for every $C$ (brief A's literal "same value function").

The semantic three are checked under a symbolic full-support procedure (`Procedure.symbolic`, stick-breaking) *and* under every pure profile.

### 2.3 Fairness under a relation

For a relation $E$ on subtrees, $\mathrm{Fair}_E(B)$: for every queried $d$, the subtrees of the members of $F_d$ are pairwise $E$-related. $\mathrm{Fair}_{\cong}$ is phase 1's **strong (structural) fairness** (P1:synthesis §1, adopted by all threads). Nothing here quantifies over procedures except through the semantic relations, and those quantify over *all* procedures, so every $\mathrm{Fair}_E$ is a property of the tree alone (brief B's PC-2 "automatically procedure-independent" holds for all six).

### 2.4 Grades of problem-equivalence: realizable-law sets

For a problem $B$ over algebra $\mathcal E$ (enrichment coordinates marginalized out when comparing with a relocated output), with pure profiles $\pi$ and procedures $C$:
- $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pure}}(B) := \{\mathrm{law}_B(\pi)\}$ — fair-repair §2.1's reduced-profile invariant; `law_set`.
- $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{mixed}}(B) := \{\mathrm{law}_B(C) : C \text{ product-mixed, Definition 6 independent redraws}\}$ — brief A's *behavioral, point-indexed* policies.
- $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{seed}}(B) := \{\mathrm{law}^{\mathrm{shared}}_B(C)\}$ — Q12's shared-seed semantics (one draw per point per run); `dp.law(..., semantics='shared')`.
- $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{corr}}(B) := \mathrm{conv}\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pure}}(B)$ — arbitrary distributions over pure profiles (correlation devices across points).

$B \sim_g B'$ iff $\mathcal L_g(B) = \mathcal L_g(B')$, for $g \in \{\mathrm{pure}, \mathrm{seed}, \mathrm{mixed}, \mathrm{corr}\}$. These are the *image* relations (no shared policy space needed, so they type across relocation, which changes the point set). The *function* relations — equality of $C \mapsto \mathrm{law}_B(C)$ on a shared policy space via a point/action correspondence — are used only where a correspondence exists (fiber subtrees within one tree; a problem and its atomic relocation).

**Definition (the repair certificate $\sqsubseteq$).** $B \sqsubseteq B'$ iff (i) $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pure}}(B) = \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pure}}(B')$ and (ii) $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{mixed}}(B) \subseteq \mathcal L_{\mathrm{mixed}}(B') \subseteq \mathcal L_{\mathrm{corr}}(B)$. Read: the repair adds no pure outcome, loses no behavioral law, and adds at most correlation.

### 2.5 Quotients

A *quotient* of $B$ is a surjection $\pi$ from the nodes of $N(B)$ onto a labelled DAG $D$ that is a *functional bisimulation*: leaves to leaves with equal labels; decision nodes to decision nodes with the same point and $\pi(\mathrm{child}_a(q)) = \mathrm{child}_a(\pi q)$; chance nodes to chance nodes with $\beta_{\pi n}(c') = \sum_{c : \pi c = c'} \beta_n(c)$. It is *fairness-witnessing* if every point labels exactly one node of $D$. $Q(B) := N(B)/{\simeq}$ denotes the bisimilarity quotient (`quotient(B, 'delta')`), and `unroll` maps a DAG back to a tree.

**EQ-4 [derived; nesting witnesses checked].** Which relations exclude nesting (self-succession), hence make $\#_d \le 1$ and put a fair tree in the almost-fair class:
- $\cong$: a finite tree is not isomorphic to a proper subtree (node count) — phase 1's argument (P1:fair-repair FR-1(i)).
- $\simeq$: bisimilar nodes have equal height after pruning (induction: a leaf has height 0; a decision node's height is $1 +$ the maximum over its children's class heights; a chance node's height is $1 +$ the maximum over the positive-probability children's class heights, and class heights are well defined by induction). A proper subtree has strictly smaller height.
- $\simeq_\Delta$: same argument on normal forms; normalization never deletes a decision node, so $N(T_{q'})$ is a proper subtree of $N(T_q)$ strictly below its root decision node.
- $\approx_{\mathrm{tr}}$: let $q'$ be a deepest $d$-node of a nested fiber and $q$ a proper $d$-ancestor; under any full-support $C$ (positive chance edges) $T_q$ has positive mass on traces with at least two $d$-events (the path through $q'$) while $T_{q'}$ has none — not trace-equivalent. E5 below shows it concretely: the top subtree's traces are $\{(d,a)\}: q$, $\{(d,b),(d,a)\}: q - q^2$, $\{(d,b),(d,b)\}: (1-q)^2$ against the bottom's $\{(d,a)\}: q$, $\{(d,b)\}: 1-q$ [checked, `run_c1.py`].
- $\approx_{\mathrm{law}}$: excludes nesting except where it is *law-spurious* — every alternative to reaching the lower member has the same law as the lower member under every $C$ (then the upper member is a spurious query in the law sense). E5 is *not* law-spurious: the top subtree puts mass $2q - q^2$ on the shared leaf and the bottom puts $q$, difference $q(1-q)$ [checked].
- $\approx_{\mathrm{val}}$ and the **pure-grade** law relation: do not exclude nesting. E5 — one point $d$ at two nested nodes, top: $a \to (o{=}1, 0)$, $b \to$ bottom; bottom: $a \to (o{=}1, 0)$, $b \to (o{=}2, 1)$ — has top and bottom subtrees with *identical laws at both pure profiles* (`E5 pure (d=a) law top == law bottom: True`, same for $b$) but different behavioral laws. ## 4. Conflict C1 adjudicated (task 2)

**EQ-3 (coincidence theorem) [derived].** Let $T, T'$ be chance-normal (§2.1) finite trees such that within each, every two nodes carrying the same point have $\simeq_\Delta$-equivalent subtrees ("inner fibers fair" — the stratification hypothesis). Then $T \approx_{\mathrm{tr}} T'$ implies $T \simeq T'$ (strong bisimilarity of the normal forms, i.e. $\simeq_\Delta$ of the originals). Consequently $\mathrm{Fair}_{\approx_{\mathrm{tr}}}(B) \iff \mathrm{Fair}_{\simeq_\Delta}(B)$ for *every* finite pruned $B$.

*Proof.* Induction on the maximum height. Write $\mathrm{tr}_C(T)$ for the trace distribution under $C$; equality is assumed for every $C$, and every $C$ may be taken full-support (full-support procedures determine the rest by continuity of the polynomials in $C$; more simply, all identities below are polynomial identities in $C$). Case on the root constructors. (Leaf, leaf): equal trace distributions are point masses at the two labels, so the labels agree. (Leaf, chance) and (leaf, decision): impossible — a normal-form chance node has two inequivalent children, which are either two leaves with different labels or a decision node emitting a visible event, so its trace distribution is not a point mass at an empty-trace leaf; a decision node emits a visible event. (Decision $d$, decision $d''$): the first visible event is $(d, \cdot)$ with probability one on one side and $(d'', \cdot)$ on the other, so $d = d''$; the mass of traces beginning $(d,a)$ is $C(d)(a)$ on both sides and the conditional continuation is $\mathrm{tr}_C(\mathrm{child}_a)$ resp. $\mathrm{tr}_C(\mathrm{child}'_a)$; these agree for every full-support $C$; the children contain no $d$-node (a $d$-node below the root $d$-node would be a nested, hence inequivalent — EQ-4 — same-point pair, excluded by the hypothesis), so their trace distributions do not depend on $C(d)$ and agree for every $C$; the induction hypothesis makes them bisimilar. (Chance, decision $d$): every child of the chance root must emit $(d,\cdot)$ first with probability one, so all its children are $d$-nodes; there are at least two and they are pairwise inequivalent (normal form) — an unfair inner fiber, excluded. (Chance, chance): the children of each root are leaves with pairwise distinct labels (equivalent leaves were merged) or decision nodes with pairwise distinct points (two inequivalent same-point children form an unfair inner fiber; equivalent ones were merged). The trace distribution's mass on "immediate leaf with label $L$" identifies the probability of the leaf child $L$ on each side; its mass on "first event $(e,\cdot)$" identifies the probability of the $e$-child; for each $e$ the conditional continuations after $(e,a)$ agree for every $C$ and are the $a$-children of the two $e$-nodes, bisimilar by induction (their heights are smaller); hence the $e$-children are bisimilar, and the two roots induce the same distribution on classes. $\square$

*Proof of the corollary.* $\mathrm{Fair}_{\simeq_\Delta} \Rightarrow \mathrm{Fair}_{\approx_{\mathrm{tr}}}$ by EQ-2. Conversely assume $\mathrm{Fair}_{\approx_{\mathrm{tr}}}(B)$ and prove, by induction on height, that every pair of same-point nodes has $\simeq_\Delta$-equivalent subtrees: for such a pair $q_1, q_2$, every pair of same-point nodes *inside* $T_{q_1}$ (or $T_{q_2}$) is a pair of members of one of $B$'s fibers with smaller subtrees, trace-equivalent by assumption, hence $\simeq_\Delta$-equivalent by induction; normalization preserves traces and this inner-fairness; apply EQ-3 to $N(T_{q_1}), N(T_{q_2})$. $\square$

*Reading.* Brief A's "stratification on height" (Sharpening 2, rider ii) is exactly the induction hypothesis that makes the semantic and the structural presentations agree; without it, E4 separates them (two trace-equivalent subtrees whose inner $d'$-fibers are unfair). Brief B's "presented inductively vs coinductively" (C1) is right for the *fair* class and for the *trace* reading of $\sim$; the honest statement of C1 is: **(c) $=$ (d) on the stratified fair class iff (c)'s $\sim$ is trace-level and (d) is taken modulo the chance laws; otherwise they differ, with E1 (chance residue: bis-unfair, trace-fair) and E3 (spurious query: law-fair, trace-unfair) as the two separating trees, both encoded** (`eqexamples.split_vs_leaf`, `eqexamples.spurious_query`).

**EQ-8 [checked; `run_c1.py`, `run_quotient.py`].** PC-2's three claimed properties, for the chosen $\simeq_\Delta$:
1. *Automatically procedure-independent*: yes — the definition mentions no procedure (§2.2); its semantic characterization "same traces for every $C$" is a theorem (EQ-3), not the definition.

**EQ-10 [derived; separations checked in `run_c1.py`, `run_grades.py`].** $\sim_{\mathrm{pure}}$ versus $\sim_{\mathrm{beh}}$ ($= \sim_{\mathrm{mixed}}$, point-indexed product mixtures under Definition 6). *Function version* (a correspondence $\varphi$ of points and actions, "the shared policy space"): $B \sim^{f}_{\mathrm{pure}} B'$ iff $\mathrm{law}_B(\pi) = \mathrm{law}_{B'}(\varphi\pi)$ for every pure $\pi$; $B \sim^{f}_{\mathrm{beh}} B'$ iff $\mathrm{law}_B(C) = \mathrm{law}_{B'}(\varphi C)$ for every product-mixed $C$ (value versions replace law by $V$).

## Toolkit issues

1. `fairness` / `labelled_isomorphic` implement strict isomorphism only; there is no bisimulation or chance-normalization. Reference implementations: `eqlib.key_strong`, `eqlib.key_delta`, `eqlib.quotient`, `eqlib.unroll` (exact canonical keys; bottom-up, no fixpoint needed on finite trees). Suggested API: `bisim_fair(B, delta=True)`.
2. Point identity is by name: `Problem.validate()` checks same-name-same-triple but not different-names-same-triple, which under Remark 2.2 is one point; after `calibrated_state`/`with_states` this hides merges (E9). `eqlib.detect_merges` is a stopgap.
3. `law_equivalent` is pure-grade only (documented); `eqlib.in_hull_of` / `single_point_mixed_membership` give the mixed grade for single-root-point outputs (linear membership, exact).
4. No disagreement between any toolkit result and my hand computations in `fairness`, `relocate`, `law`, `calibrated_state`, `theorem1_condition`, `theorem2_evaluator`, `records_for_all`, `Fr`, `biext_equivalent`, `max_mixed`, `value_polynomial`.
