## 1. The category (Task 1)

### 1.1 The criterion, and why every honest category is location-blind

An agent handed the description $B$ and choosing to act as $T(B)$'s agent uses exactly two things. (i) A translation of $T(B)$-policies into things it can do at $B$'s points: for pure policies the component restriction $\pi\mapsto(\pi(d))_{d\in U}$, inverse (on reduced profiles) to FR-6's lift $C\mapsto\tilde C$; for mixed ones only the *product* mixtures are executable, because under Definition 6 distinct points draw independently and cannot correlate (the E2 node's correlated mixtures need Q12's correlation devices). (ii) The guarantee that what then happens is what $T(B)$ said would happen — law equality at the grade of policies actually used. The tree shape of $T(B)$, where its nodes sit, and its stipulated states are not used by the agent at all. So the honest morphism notion is a law-preserving translation of policies at a declared grade, and any structure the agent does not use must be invisible to the category. This is why every category proposed below is location-blind; §3 shows the blindness is forced (UN-8), not a modelling choice.

**Definition UN-A (marked problems).** A *marked problem* is a pair $(B,M)$: a concrete decision problem (Definition 5, evented chance) together with a set $M$ of coordinates of its algebra declared to be disposition stamps (the fresh $\mathrm{pol}_d$-coordinates of Definition R1). Fairness of $(B,M)$ is strong structural fairness with $M$ exempted (Claim B's pre-enrichment-marginal sense). $\mathrm{Rel}_U(B,M):=(\mathrm{Rel}_UB,\ M\cup\{\mathrm{pol}_d:d\in U\})$.

**UN-1 [checked, `s5_idempotence.py`].** Fair is a class of *marked* problems, not of bare trees. Claim B's witness (root coin $H/T$ evented; $T$-branch a $d$-node with an $e$-node below each answer, leaves recording coin and $\mathrm{act}_e$ only; $H$-branch a hypothetical $d$-node over plain leaves with transfer $1$ on pay): $\mathrm{Rel}_{\{d\}}B$ is fair with $\mathrm{pol}_d$ exempted (`is_strongly_fair(..., exempt=['pol_d'])` True) and unfair without (`unfair_fibers` = `['e']`: the two $e$-copies differ only in the stamp). So "Fair as a full subcategory" must be read on marked objects and morphisms must respect markings (stamps to stamps, unmarked coordinates to unmarked coordinates). Everything below is about marked problems; I drop the $M$ from the notation.

### 1.2 Three law preorders

**Definition UN-B (graded law preorders).** For a grade $\gamma\in\{\mathrm{pure},\mathrm{mix},\mathrm{corr}\}$ let $\mathrm{Law}_\gamma(B)$ be the set of $\mathcal E$-marginal (unmarked-coordinate) joint world–payoff laws $\widehat B(C)$ realized by, respectively, pure profiles; product-mixed procedures under independent redraws (Definition 4); arbitrary mixtures of pure profiles (Q12's correlation devices). Put $B\preceq_\gamma B'$ iff $\mathrm{Law}_\gamma(B)\subseteq\mathrm{Law}_\gamma(B')$. $\mathbf{Law}_\gamma$ is the preorder viewed as a thin category; $B\cong_\gamma B'$ iff both inclusions hold; $\cong_{\mathrm{pure}}$ is P1:fair-repair §2.1's $\sim$. Fair is the full sub-preorder on fair marked problems. In a preorder, $F$ is a *reflection* of $B$ into Fair iff $F$ is fair, $B\preceq F$, and $F\preceq F'$ for every fair $F'\succeq B$ — a least element of the fair up-set, unique up to $\cong$; the universal property "every morphism $B\to F'$ factors uniquely through $\eta_B$" is automatic once $F$ is least (hom-sets are singletons).

**UN-2 (pure grade: a reflection exists for every problem, and it is vacuous) [derived; checked `s1`].** In $\mathbf{Law}_{\mathrm{pure}}$ every $B$ has a reflection into Fair, namely any fair $F\cong_{\mathrm{pure}}B$; $\mathrm{Rel}_U(B)$ with $U$ the closure of all unfair fibers is one for every $B$, nested fibers included, by FR-7(a). The universal property holds and says nothing beyond $\sim$: the reflection of the AMD is its collapse ($\max_CV$ falls $\tfrac43\to1$), and $T_{d,e}\cong T_{e,d}\cong T_\times$. Adjudication of brief A Sharpening 3's fear ("if two strongly fair trees can be $\sim$-equivalent yet disagree about where the decision sits … the reflection fails as stated"): the reflection does not fail. FR-10's pair is an *isomorphism* of this category, and the universal property is exactly as blind as the relation it is built on. What fails is the expectation that isomorphism would be finer than $\sim$.

**UN-3 (mixed grade: the unit becomes a genuine morphism, the AMD is no longer reflected by its collapse, location stays invisible) [checked `s1`; minimality [guess]].** (i) $\eta_B:B\preceq_{\mathrm{mix}}\mathrm{Rel}_U(B)$ holds iff no fiber in $U$ is nested (FR-6; FR-7(b)); for the AMD it fails — the law at $\tfrac13$ above is outside the collapse's family $\{x\delta_{(\mathrm{exit}=1),0}+(1-x)\delta_{(\mathrm{exit}=0),1}\}$. (ii) Let $B_{\mathrm{cpl}}$ be the coupled two-point problem: root $d$-node ($A_d=\{1,2\}$), the *same* point $e$ ($A_e=\{1,2\}$) below each answer, leaves recording $(\mathrm{act}_d,\mathrm{act}_e)$ with payoff $v(1,1)=2$, $v(2,2)=1$, $v=0$ off the diagonal. `unfair_fibers` = `['e']`, no nesting, closure of $\{e\}$ is $\{d,e\}$, and $\mathrm{Rel}_{\{d,e\}}B_{\mathrm{cpl}}$ is FR-10's $T_\times$ (root point $d{\times}e$ with four stamp-events; fair in both senses; law sets on $(\mathrm{act}_d,\mathrm{act}_e)$ equal to those of $T_\times$ and $T_{d,e}$). At the mixed grade $\mathrm{Law}_{\mathrm{mix}}(B_{\mathrm{cpl}})=\{x\otimes y\}$ (checked at $x=\tfrac13,y=\tfrac14$: $(\tfrac1{12},\tfrac14,\tfrac16,\tfrac12)$, reproduced by the lifted procedure on the relocation), while $T_\times$, $T_{d,e}$, $T_{e,d}$ each realize *every* joint law on $\{1,2\}^2$ — e.g. $\tfrac12\delta_{(1,1)}+\tfrac12\delta_{(2,2)}$, realized on $T_\times$ by a joint mixture and on $T_{d,e}$ by mixing $d$ at $\tfrac12$ and answering $e|1\mapsto1$, $e|2\mapsto2$, and realizable by no product ($xy=\tfrac12$ and $x(1-y)=0$ force $y=1$, then $(1-x)(1-y)=0\ne\tfrac12$). Hence $B_{\mathrm{cpl}}\prec_{\mathrm{mix}}T_\times\cong_{\mathrm{mix}}T_{d,e}\cong_{\mathrm{mix}}T_{e,d}$: the unit is a strict inclusion, so the reflection — if it exists — is a genuine one, and it is still location-blind. (iii) [derived] No fair tree has mixed law set exactly the product family: a fair tree consulting both $d$ and $e$ on every run nests one point below all answers of the other; fairness of the lower fiber (in any sense that compares payoff labels) forces the lower subtrees' payoffs to be independent of the upper answer, contradicting $v$'s coupling; refining the lower copies into distinct points (the E1 form) yields all joint laws. So at the mixed grade *relocation is never an equivalence for a coupled multi-point problem*: the fair forms are strictly richer, optimum-innocently (multiaffine, vertex-attained; FR-9(iv)). Whether $T_\times$'s class is the *least* fair upper bound of $B_{\mathrm{cpl}}$ is [guess]; I could not exclude a fair tree whose mixed law set lies strictly between the Segre surface and the simplex. (iv) The AMD has a fair $\preceq_{\mathrm{mix}}$-upper bound — the perfect-recall unrolling, whose behavioral laws fill the 3-simplex — and its least one is open; the collapse is not it.

The moral matches P1 Seam 1: at the pure grade "reflection" is $\sim$ with C.1's defect; at the mixed grade the category refuses the AMD collapse and records that fair forms of coupled problems are richer than the problems. In both, the licensing concept is the two-part certificate, with the almost-fairness conjunct now visible as "$\eta_B$ is a $\preceq_{\mathrm{mix}}$-morphism".

### 1.3 The Chu category

**Definition UN-C.** Objects: marked problems. A morphism $B\to B'$ is a Chu morphism $(g,h)$ between the induced frames $\mathrm{Fr}(B)\to\mathrm{Fr}(B')$ (paper Def 2: $g$ forward on pure policies, $h$ backward on lazy chance profiles, $\pi\cdot h(\varepsilon')=g(\pi)\star\varepsilon'$), with worlds coarsened to the unmarked coordinates (CF-24's coarsening — without it two frames with different stamp sets never share an outcome and the question is empty). $\mathbf{Chu}$ is the strict version; $\mathbf{Chu}/\!\simeq$ takes morphisms up to homotopy (paper Def 36), where isomorphism is biextensional equivalence. This is the agent-entitlement criterion in CF's own vocabulary: $g$ interprets a $B$-policy as a $B'$-policy, $h$ reads $B'$'s environment back, and adjointness is the outcome guarantee. Results: UN-6 – UN-8 in §3.

## 2. The refined relocation (Task 2)

Brief B, PC-3, verbatim: "relocate to the LCA of the fiber, with the relocated action set being conditional policies on the CF-observable events between the LCA and the fiber. Update on observables, stay updateless across the coupling. … the LCA relocation is the least relocation that makes your property hold, which is what makes it a solution rather than a stipulation — and it makes the required prior the posterior at the last observable".

Making it precise. Fix an unfair non-nested fiber $F_d$ with $\ell=\mathrm{lca}(F_d)$. Between $\ell$ and the members lie chance edges (and possibly other decision nodes). A partition of the routes $\ell\to F_d$ is *agent-registered* iff its cells are distinguished by the agent's points (members under different cells carry different points); by Remark 2.2 nothing else is available to a procedure. Two readings of "conditional policies on the CF-observable events":

- (R-a) events registered by points. Then $F_d$ is not one fiber but several — one per cell, each its own point with its own LCA — and "conditional policies on the cells" is the product $\prod A$ over those points: E2's action set (or E1's sequenced form).
- (R-b) events the points do *not* register (a coin between $\ell$ and $F_d$ whose outcome the members' point does not record). Granting conditional policies on it is CF-22's N1 ("un-commit to $\mathrm{Hon}_d$"), legitimate only when the cells are agent-distinguishable — which by hypothesis they are not. Remark 2.2 forbids the procedure.

**UN-4 (PC-3 collapses to R2 step 4 + E2/E1) [checked `s3_pc3_and_least.py`; derived].** Under (R-a) PC-3's site is the LCA, its action set is E2's product over the fibers of the component (or E1's sequenced product), its observation is R2 step 4's upstream context, and "the required prior is the posterior at the last observable" is FR-8 verbatim: strict OC at $\hat d$ pins $P_{\hat s}=\nu_{\mathrm{Rel}B,\tilde C}(\cdot\mid O_{\hat d})$, the posterior on the chance events above the site, which at the root is Definition 11's prior calibration. **UN-5 (what "least relocation" is a theorem of) [checked `s3`, `s4_sequenced_pingpong.py`; derived].** A *site* for $F_d$ is a node at which one new node carrying one point can be inserted so that every member is resolved below it; since one point means one node (Remark 2.2), legitimate sites are the common ancestors of $F_d$, and $\mathrm{lca}(F_d)$ is the unique least site — for a single fiber, PC-3's "least relocation … a solution rather than a stipulation" is a theorem. For several fibers: (i) phase 1's E2 relocates *all* unfair fibers at one node at $\mathrm{lca}(\bigcup F_d)$, which over-relocates when the closure splits into **scope-components** — call $d,e$ scope-entangled iff $\mathrm{scope}(F_d)$ contains an $e$-node or $\mathrm{scope}(F_e)$ a $d$-node; after phase 1's closure, the components of this relation have pairwise disjoint scopes (two scopes that meet are nested, and the outer contains the inner's LCA and members), so the surgeries commute and each component can be relocated at its own LCA with chance-only ancestors (a decision node above a component's LCA has that component's members below it, hence is scope-entangled with it and already inside). The two-muggings example is the witness: componentwise (depths $1,1$; observations $c'{=}i$) versus joint (depth $0$; $\top$), both fair, both $\sim$. (ii) Within a component the E1 sequenced forms are finer than the E2 product: on FR-3's ping-pong tree (`ping_pong()`; payoffs $1..8$), E2 gives one root node with four actions; the hand-built $T_{d,e}$ (root $\hat d$, then $\hat e|a$ with observation $\mathrm{pol}_d{=}a$, then the side coin) and $T_{e,d}$ are fair with the second point at depth $1$; all four trees are $\sim$ on (side, act$_d$, act$_e$) with $4$ reduced laws and optimum $6$; raw profile counts $4,4,8,8$; the naive per-fiber iteration does not terminate within $6$ steps (FR-3). (iii) Hence the least relocation exists and is unique iff no two fibers of a component share a site; when sites coincide (mutual entanglement) the least relocations are the linear extensions — $|U|!$ of them in the fully entangled case — and E2 is the least *order-free* form, one step coarser. In poset form, under "refines" (= relocates less): root form (UDT) $\le$ E2 per component $\le$ E1 forms; the bottom is unique, the tops are not. This is FR-10 restated as a fact about the relocation poset, and it is where PC-3's "least" stops being a theorem.

## 3. Reflection (Task 3)

**UN-6 (no unit in $\mathbf{Chu}$ without chance identification; the counit exists) [checked `s2_chu_morphisms.py`; Lean candidate `lean/universal-no-chu-unit.lean`, STATUS UNCHECKED].** Mugging $B_1$ ($x{=}1$, $y{=}2$): $\mathrm{Fr}(B_1)$ is $2\times2$ — rows pay/refuse, columns $H/T$, outcomes $((H,\bot,1),2)$, $((T,\mathrm{pay},0),-1)$, $((H,\bot,0),0)$, $((T,\mathrm{refuse},0),0)$. $\mathrm{Fr}(\mathrm{Rel}_dB_1)$ coarsened by $\mathrm{pol}_d$ is $2\times4$: columns $(c_{\mathrm{pay}},c_{\mathrm{refuse}})\in\{H,T\}^2$ (one lazy copy of the coin per branch), the pay row reads $c_{\mathrm{pay}}$, the refuse row reads $c_{\mathrm{refuse}}$; all four columns distinct, collapse $2\times4$. Exhaustive search over all $2^2\cdot 2^4=64$ table pairs: **zero** Chu morphisms $\mathrm{Fr}(B_1)\to\mathrm{Fr}(\mathrm{Rel}B_1)$; **exactly one** morphism $\mathrm{Fr}(\mathrm{Rel}B_1)\to\mathrm{Fr}(B_1)$, namely $g=\mathrm{id}$ on rows and $h(c)=(c,c)$ — CF-24's diagonal Assume. The obstruction by hand: at the column $(H,T)$ the pay row lands in an $H$-world and the refuse row in a $T$-world, so no single original coin $h(H,T)$ satisfies $\pi\cdot h(H,T)=g(\pi)\star(H,T)$ for both rows. Consequences: in the CF-native category relocation is *counit-shaped* — $T(B)\to B$ exists, $B\to T(B)$ does not — so a unit-shaped reflection is ill-posed there; if relocation has a universal property in $\mathbf{Chu}$ it is a coreflection ("the most general fair frame enactable in $B$"), which I did not settle (handoff cf-frontier; in strict $\mathbf{Chu}$ the uniqueness half looks false since off-diagonal columns are unconstrained, so it must be asked up to homotopy).

**UN-7 (frame invariance under chance identification) [derived; checked `s8_frame_square.py` on 11 trees].** With one lazy coordinate per *original* chance node (D.2's convention, `identify_chance=True`) and worlds coarsened by the stamps, $\mathrm{Fr}(\mathrm{Rel}_UB)\simeq\mathrm{Fr}(B)$ for every $B$ and every $U$, nested fibers included. Proof. Rows: FR-6's lift is a bijection between $B$'s reduced pure profiles and $\mathrm{Rel}_UB$'s. Columns: identification makes both environments the set of original chance profiles. Outcomes: for a frozen chance profile the deterministic run of $B$ under a pure profile answers every occurrence of $d\in U$ identically, which is exactly what resolution does; copied chance nodes read the original coordinate; so both runs end at the same leaf up to the stamps (FR-7(a) pointwise in the chance profile). $\square$ Checked as biextensional equivalence of collapses: mugging $B_1$, $B_2$, 3-fold concave mugging (nested), TN-V1, TN-V2, ping-pong, AMD, lesion S3, parallel fiber, look-decide over-relocation, CX1 — all True. Without identification the lazy frames differ exactly when a chance node shared across fiber members is copied (mugging $2\times4$ vs $2\times2$; ping-pong $4\times16$ vs $4\times2$; lesion $2\times64$ vs $2\times32$; parallel fiber $2\times4$ vs $2\times2$) and agree when the chance nodes were already per branch (TN-V1/V2, whose $n_x$/$n_{xy}$ sit one per hypothetical answer; AMD, CX1, look-decide, chance-free). So under the convention $\eta_B$ is an isomorphism of frames and the reflection in $\mathbf{Chu}/\!\simeq$ is trivial; the convention is what identifies a coin with its counterfactual copies — a shared-seed convention for chance (P1 Seam 2), owned by cf-frontier and load-bearing for every frame-side statement about relocation.

**UN-8 (the dichotomy, categorified) [derived; homotopy counts checked `s2`].** (i) In any category in which relocation's unit is an isomorphism ("the repair is an equivalence"), $T_{d,e}\cong T_{e,d}$: both are E1 outputs of $B_{\mathrm{cpl}}$ with unit isomorphisms. So no category makes relocation an equivalence and keeps decision location an invariant — FR-10's dichotomy in one line. (ii) In any *location-sensitive* category ($T_{d,e}\not\cong T_{e,d}$) that is symmetric under relabelling $d\leftrightarrow e$, neither E1 form can be a reflection of the symmetric $B_{\mathrm{cpl}}$: reflections are unique up to isomorphism and the relabelling carries one to the other; only a swap-invariant object such as $T_\times$ could be. (iii) In strict $\mathbf{Chu}$ the candidate $T_\times$ fails uniqueness: there are $16$ Chu morphisms $\mathrm{Fr}(T_\times)\to\mathrm{Fr}(T_{d,e})$ (the $e$-component at the unchosen $a'$ is free) against exactly $1$ back; all $16$ are homotopic, so in $\mathbf{Chu}/\!\simeq$ factorization is unique — but there $\mathrm{Fr}(T_\times)\simeq\mathrm{Fr}(T_{d,e})$ (both collapse to $4\times1$) and location-sensitivity is gone again. (iv) The failing axiom, named: in every location-blind category the universal property holds with nothing to say; in every location-sensitive one the *unit is not a morphism* — relocation moves decisions by construction, and in $\mathbf{Chu}$ without identification it decorrelates coins (UN-6). The minimal counterexample is FR-10's pair, and it is a counterexample not to the universal property but to the hope that isomorphism would be finer than $\sim$.

**UN-9 (C2 adjudicated) [derived].** Brief A's rationale (reflection) and brief B's (least relocation; data-cost comparability) are neither rivals nor complements at the level they were pitched. The reflection exists exactly where it is empty (UN-2, UN-7) and does not exist where it would be informative (UN-6, UN-8); "least relocation" is a theorem exactly where FR-10 does not bite (UN-5). Brief B's conditional-policy action set is neither what makes the reflection go through nor an alternative to it: it is phase 1's E2/E1 product (UN-4), the same reduced object, and its one real content — the LCA site with the upstream-context posterior — is R2 step 4 and FR-8. What survives of both rationales is a single object, the relocated joint law $\nu^\circ$ (§4): canonical up to refactoring, with its *site* pinned (component LCA) and its *sequencing* free.

## 4. What uniqueness is for (Task 4)

Remark 3.8(iii), verbatim: "The unequipped design is the default here because one job of the formalism is to make theories' data requirements comparable, and equipping every point with a prior equalizes those requirements by fiat — hiding exactly the cost of UDT that the framework should exhibit."

**UN-10 (refactoring invariance of the relocated prior) [checked `s6_datacost.py`].** On FR-10's three fair forms with the corresponding procedures ($x=\tfrac13$ at $d$, $y=\tfrac14$ at every $e$-copy; the product on $T_\times$): the strictly calibrated root state of $T_\times$ has $P=(\tfrac1{12},\tfrac14,\tfrac16,\tfrac12)$ on $(\mathrm{pol}_d,\mathrm{pol}_e)\in\{1,2\}^2$; the root state of $T_{d,e}$ equals it; the states at $e|a$ equal $P(\cdot\mid\mathrm{pol}_d{=}a)$ with $V(\mathrm{pol}_e{=}1)=2$ at $e|1$ and $0$ at $e|2$, matching the root's $V(\mathrm{pol}_d{=}a\wedge\mathrm{pol}_e{=}1)$; mirror at $T_{e,d}$; the value is $\tfrac23$ in all three. So every fair representative's calibrated states are conditionals of one law $\nu^\circ_{B,C}$ — the law of (world, payoff, stamps) under the lifted procedure — and the representative choice changes how the prior is *factored*, not what it contains. This is the sense in which "the prior UDT needs on $B$" is well-defined despite FR-10.

**Definition UN-D (data cost of UDT at $d$ on $B$).** Let $B$ be almost fair, $C$ a procedure, $\hat d$ the relocated point of $d$'s scope-component at its LCA, $\hat s$ its strictly calibrated state (masked at an interior self-model where strictness is self-certain, Remark 3.6). The *consulted state* is $\hat s$. The *extra data* UDT needs beyond EDT at $d$ is the off-observation part of $\hat s$:
$$\mathrm{Cost}(B,d):=\Big(P_{\hat s}(\neg O_d),\ \ (P_{\hat s},V_{\hat s})\big|_{\neg O_d}\Big),$$
the prior weight of the branches on which $d$'s observation is false together with the state restricted to them; numerical proxy: the number of $\hat s$-atoms outside $O_d$.

## 6. Structure (Task 6)

**UN-16 (idempotence) [checked `s5`].** In the pre-enrichment-marginal sense $T(T(B))=T(B)$ literally ($T(B)$ has no unfair fiber to relocate). In the enriched sense $T$ is not idempotent: on Claim B's witness the first pass leaves $e$ enriched-unfair (copies differ in $\mathrm{pol}_d$), the second pass relocates $\{\hat d,e\}$ jointly to the root, and the result is fair in both senses — iterating $T$ in the enriched sense climbs to the root/UDT form (points: $\{d{:}[1,1],e{:}[2,2]\}\to\{\hat d{:}[0],e{:}[2,2]\}\to\{\hat{\hat d}{\times}e{:}[0]\}$), all three trees $\sim$ on (coin, act$_e$, transfer). Claim B's convention is what makes $T$ a projection; the enriched reading makes it a climb toward UDT.

**UN-17 (order-independence) [checked `s3`, `s4`; derived].** Atomic relocations of scope-disjoint components commute literally (two-muggings); entangled fibers do not (FR-3's ping-pong: the naive iteration does not terminate) and their E1 sequencings are distinct fair trees. Categorical form: the rewriting system "relocate a component" is confluent modulo $\sim$ (every move preserves laws) and not confluent modulo tree isomorphism (two normal forms for a mutually entangled pair); E2 is the unique order-free normal form per component.

**UN-18 (functoriality) [checked `s9`; derived].** In the thin categories $T$ is trivially a functor with $\eta$ natural. Against structured maps: spurious-query insertion commutes with $T$ up to $\sim$ — $B_1$ with a fresh $d_0$ above the tails node has closure $\{d,d_0\}$, $d_0$ is swept in (its stamp is decision-irrelevant), and $T(B^+)\sim T(B)$; point-merging does not — CX2 is fair ($T$ = identity, $4$ laws, optimum $3$) while its $O$-free merge is unfair with $T$ producing $2$ laws and optimum $\tfrac52$, so the merge $B\to B'$ has no law-preserving lift $T(B)\to T(B')$. Fairness, hence $T$, is not functorial along coarsenings of point identity (identity thread's PC-6).

**UN-19 (the biextensional square) [checked `s8`; derived].** Strongest commuting statement: $\mathrm{Fr}\circ\mathrm{Rel}_U\simeq\mathrm{Fr}$ under chance identification plus stamp coarsening, for every $B$ (UN-7); since biextensional collapse is defined on frames, the collapses agree too. The exact gap to the unconventioned square is CF-24's: the lazy relocated frame has the off-diagonal columns (decorrelated counterfactual coins) and the stamp coordinate, and $\mathrm{Fr}(B)$ is recovered by Assume-diagonal then coarsen — an inclusion of environments, not an isomorphism (UN-6: the inclusion is the unique morphism, and there is none back). The extensional shadow is preserved by $T$ only at the pure grade (FR-7(a)) or for almost-fair inputs (FR-6), transverse to the frame statement (CF-23).

## Toolkit issues

None are fidelity errors against v2; three usability points:
1. `relocate(B, U)` relocates all of $U$ (after closure) at one node at $\mathrm{lca}(\bigcup F_d)$ even when the closure splits into scope-disjoint components — phase 1's E2 as specified, but it over-relocates (UN-5(i); the two-muggings example). A per-component mode would produce the least joint form.
2. `Fr`/`Loc` outcomes carry the enrichment coordinates, so a relocated frame never shares an outcome with the original; comparing frames across relocation (or checking observability on a relocated tree) requires CF-24's coarsening by hand (`common.coarsen`). Without it `biext_equivalent` and `observable` return false negatives (first run of `s7`). A `drop=` option would prevent this.
3. `entanglement_closure` sweeps in decision nodes above fiber members (correct per Claim E's remark), so a spurious point above a member is relocated with it (UN-18, `s9`); worth a docstring note since the survivor list then omits it.
