## 0. Setup: what point identity is, and the object under attack

v2 Def 3: a decision-point is $d = (s_d, O_d, A_d)$; Remark 2.2: "Identity of the full triple is what is required, observation included, so two locations coincide only under genuine subjective indistinguishability." Procedures are functions of points (Def 4), so two nodes carrying the same point receive the same mixed action, and "deviate at this occurrence only" is not an operation the type supports. The *fiber* of $d$ is $F_d = \{q : d_q = d\}$.

**The sloppy-Omega mugging $B_1'$ (the object PC-6 describes).** Start from Prop 6's $B_1$ — fair coin; on $T$ query $d$ (pay $\to (T,\mathrm{pay},0)$, $r=-x$; refuse $\to (T,\mathrm{refuse},0)$, $r=0$); on $H$ query $d$ hypothetically (pay $\to (H,\bot,1)$, $r=y$; refuse $\to (H,\bot,0)$, $r=0$); $0<x<y$; $P_s(T)=1$, $P_s(\mathrm{transfer}{=}0)=1$, $P_s(\mathrm{pay})=q_0$. Replace the $H$-node's label by a *different* point $d' = (s', O_T, \{\mathrm{pay},\mathrm{refuse}\})$ — same observation, same actions, $s' \ne s$ by one decision-irrelevant belief. Three ways to differ by one belief, all encoded:
- **(A) world-belief**: add a payoff-irrelevant coordinate $z\in\{0,1\}$ to $\mathcal E$ (every leaf-world has $z=0$); $P_s(z{=}0)=1$, $P_{s'}(z{=}1)=1$.
- **(A′) self-credence**: $P_{s'}(\mathrm{pay}) = q_1 \ne q_0 = P_s(\mathrm{pay})$, everything else equal.
- **(B) counterfactual structure**: $(P_{s'},V_{s'}) = (P_s,V_s)$, $\mathrm{cf}_{s'} \ne \mathrm{cf}_s$ (a different causal opinion; EDT never reads it).
Write $q := C(d)(\mathrm{pay})$, $q' := C(d')(\mathrm{pay})$.

## 1. Task 1 — the fragility attack (PC-6)

**ID-1 [checked; `two_point_mugging.py`].** In every version: $F_d=\{T\text{-node}\}$, $F_{d'}=\{H\text{-node}\}$ — both singletons — so $B_1'$ is strongly fair and almost fair with no unfair fiber; $V_{B_1'}(q,q') = \tfrac12(-xq + yq')$, separable; the optimum is $(q,q') = (0,1)$ — *refuse when real, pay when simulated* — with value $y/2$, exceeding the exact-sim optimum $\tfrac12(y-x)$ (Prop 6) by exactly $x/2$; the four pure profiles have values $\{(\mathrm{pay},\mathrm{pay}): \tfrac12(y-x),\ (\mathrm{refuse},\mathrm{pay}): \tfrac y2,\ (\mathrm{pay},\mathrm{refuse}): -\tfrac x2,\ (\mathrm{refuse},\mathrm{refuse}): 0\}$. So PC-6's three descriptive sentences — singleton fiber, structurally fair at the action level, refusing (at $d$) is $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$-correct — are all TRUE under Def-3 identity. Note the fourth fact PC-6 omits: $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ *pays at the simulation*. The correct policy against a detectably-sloppy Omega is to exploit it, and the type says so.

**ID-2 [checked + derived].** $B_1'$ **fails recording at $d'$ for every procedure, in every version**: coverage (Def 7) demands that a.s. every run whose leaf-world satisfies $O_{d'} = O_T$ pass a $d'$-node, and $O_T$-runs pass only the $d$-node (toolkit: `records_for_all(B, d_sim)['ok'] = False`, "T/…: passes 0 d-nodes"; `covers = False`); recording at $d$ holds for every procedure, exactly as in $B_1$ (P1:adversary-repair A.2 (checked)). General form [derived]: *a point that is queried only at nodes whose leaves falsify its observation — a pure simulation point — is uncovered, hence unrecorded, for every procedure*; and if instead its observation were true at the simulation's leaves ($O_{d'} = \{\mathrm{coin}=H\}$) it would no longer be a simulation of an agent who seems to see tails. Consequence: $B_1'$ lies **outside the fair class** of P1:synthesis item 20 (strongly fair ∧ fully recorded ∧ evented ∧ realized observations) — for the *honesty* conjunct, not the structural one. The exact-sim $B_1$ fails the structural conjunct (dislocated fiber) and passes recording; the sloppy-sim $B_1'$ passes the structural conjunct and fails recording. **The attack trades a structural defect for an honesty defect; the composite fair class catches both.** P1:adversary-repair Claim B (derived) — on fully evented, recorded, realized trees every fair fiber is a singleton — makes this sharper: in the fair class *all* fibers are singletons, so "the fiber is a singleton" is not what distinguishes the attack; the recording failure at the sim's point is.

**ID-3 [checked; `two_point_mugging.py`, `misc_checks.py`] — what every calibration sense says about the sim's point $d'$.**
- (A) world-belief: strict OC, limit OC, masked OC (any self-model), per-run SSC and per-occurrence SSC all reject $d'$ for **every** $C$: the $O_T$-conditional statistics are the $T$-branch's, which have $z=0$, and $P_{s'}(z{=}1)=1$. The labels block belief-level sloppiness on any world coordinate — this is Remark 4.4's "two calibrated states sharing $O$ must agree in their $P$ and $V$ components" in action (toolkit: strict-calibrated $(P,V)$ at $d$ and $d'$ are `equal_PV = True`).
- (A′) self-credence: strict and limit OC at $d'$ hold **iff $q = q_1$** — the sim's calibration is decided by the *decider's* pay-frequency, not by anything the sim does (`q = q1` → $d'$ calibrated, $d$ violated; `q = q0` → $d$ calibrated, $d'$ violated). Masked OC at $d'$: the self-model $C[d'\mapsto m]$ acts on the $H$-branch and cannot move the $O_T$-statistics, so **the mask is idle at a simulation point**: masked-calibrated iff $q = q_1$, for every $m$. Per-run / per-occurrence SSC: reject $d'$ for every $C$ (sim on heads, certain of tails: $\mu(T \mid \mathrm{occ}(d')) = 0 \ne 1$).
- (B) cf: strict, limit and masked OC at both points hold at $q = q_0$ (masked at $d'$ with a *free* full-support self-model, witness $\tfrac12$); SSC rejects $d'$ for every $C$. So under any OC grade the only fragility the labels admit is a $\mathrm{cf}$-difference — $\mathrm{cf}_s$ being uncalibrated by design (Remark 3.11) — and Remark 4.4 already names what closes it: a counterfactual theory (Def 20) pinning $\mathrm{cf}$ (e.g. the evidential criterion) makes same-observation calibrated points identical, i.e. memorylessness.
- The SSC verdict on the sim is version-independent (the sim is dishonest about its location whatever else it believes), and at $d$ per-run SSC = strict OC (Prop 3's bridge: the $T$-node is subtree-veridical and covered).

**ID-4 [checked; `two_point_mugging.py`] — what the procedures say.** In every version and at every OC grade including trembles (FR-11's concrete tremble-EDT), the *only* $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$-approved profile is $(\mathrm{refuse},\mathrm{refuse})$, value $0$: both points condition on $O_T$, so both value paying at $-x$ (`v_eps = {pay: -x, refuse: 0}` at $d$ and at $d'$). $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ approves only $(\mathrm{refuse},\mathrm{pay})$, value $y/2$. The two theories **agree at the decider's point** (refuse) and disagree only at the simulation's. Def-22 coherence and Theorem 2's evaluator side with $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$: at $d'$ the occurrence-conditioned value of pay is $y$ (it sees the heads transfer), so $(\mathrm{refuse},\mathrm{refuse})$ is *incoherent* — the exploit is Def-22-coherent, EDT's answer is not. FR-11's hypotheses are therefore load-bearing here in a new way: with recording failing at $d'$, observation-event conditioning at a simulation-only point reads the wrong branch, and tremble-EDT lands on a non-optimal profile — a second witness, alongside Told-You-So (P1:synthesis item 12), that the fair class needs recording and not only structure.

**ID-5 [checked; `two_point_mugging.py`] — frames.** The coin partition is CF-observable in $\mathrm{Fr}(B_1')$ (agent $A_d\times A_{d'}$: the row $(\mathrm{pay},\mathrm{refuse})$ *is* the conditional policy "pay if $T$, refuse if $H$") and not in $\mathrm{Fr}(B_1)$ (P1:cf-correspondence CF-15 (checked)). At $\mathrm{Loc}_d$: no simulation, powerless outside $S_T$, observable, $\mathrm{Loc}_d\simeq\mathrm{Hon}_d$ — the decider's tails observation is a **true Observation** in $B_1'$. At $\mathrm{Loc}_{d'}$: observable but *not* powerless outside $S_T$ and not no-simulation — an umbrella-type instance (P1:cf-correspondence CF-11(b)): the sim's point can "condition" on its claimed observation only because it has no power at all where that observation is true. Frame-side, a sloppy Omega turns the coin into a proper Observation, consistent with "refusing is correct".

**ID-6 [checked + derived] — relocation, and PC-10's escape.** There is nothing to relocate (`unfair_fibers = []`): the pipeline's fair form of $B_1'$ is $B_1'$. Forcing the joint relocation of $\{d,d'\}$ to a product root preserves $V$ and its prior-calibrated root EDT selects the tuple $(\mathrm{pol}_d{=}\mathrm{refuse},\ \mathrm{pol}_{d'}{=}\mathrm{pay})$ with value $y/2$: **policy-level relocation goes through unchanged and yields the exploit.** Whether the coupling "correlates policies rather than points" is then a fact about the prior's joint law on the two policy components: componentwise $\mathrm{UDT}_{s^\circ,\rho}$ with the *product* prior (the one the tree's chance labels supply, FR-15(iv)) refuses at $d$ ($V(\mathrm{pay}) = \tfrac12(q'y - x) < \tfrac12 q'y$) and pays at $d'$; with a *diagonal* prior ($\mathrm{pol}_d = \mathrm{pol}_{d'}$ a.s.) it pays at $d$ iff $y>x$ — the mugging verdict. So PC-10's "the policy reading doesn't care about the fragility" is half right: the policy reading does not dissolve the attack, it **relocates it into $s^\circ$**, as the question whether the policy components at two near-identical points are correlated. That is exactly the mandate's anticipated residue — "when do two points carry the same policy component" — and it is the diagonal-vs-product split of XC-14 (faithful thread) seen from the identity side. The tree cannot supply the diagonal (its two points are independent parameters of $C$); only an agent-side prior can — "which prior?" (email 3) bites here.

**ID-7 [derived] — verdict on "fairness collapses to triviality".** Three readings, three answers.
(i) *Structural fairness alone*: PC-6 is RIGHT — a sufficiently sloppy Omega makes every simulation-based problem strongly fair (singleton fibers), and P1:adversary-repair Claim B shows the honest fair class has singleton fibers anyway. Structure alone was never the fair class; that is why P1 conjoined recording and realized observations (item 20).
(ii) *The composite fair class*: PC-6 is WRONG — $B_1'$ is excluded by recording (ID-2), and belief-level sloppiness on world coordinates is excluded by every OC grade (ID-3(A)); self-credence sloppiness is pinned to the decider (ID-3(A′)); only $\mathrm{cf}$-sloppiness survives, and Remark 4.4 names its closure.
(iii) *The discontinuity*: the honest formal content of PC-6 is that the family $B_1^{(s')}$ has a verdict map $s' \mapsto$ (correct act at $d$) that jumps at $s' = s$ (pay) from refuse everywhere else, in any topology on states. This is a **scope boundary, not a collapse**: v2 declines counterpossibles (Def 2) and correlations between distinct points (§8's caveat after Def 22, "FDT territory"), so it can represent logical correlation between the agent and a near-copy only in the 0/1 form "same point / different point". On exact-sim problems fairness is non-trivial (the dislocated fiber is repaired, pay iff $y>x$); on sloppy-sim problems the exploit is *correct within the type*, because by Remark 2.2 any difference in the triple is one the procedure can read — the sim is introspectively detectable ("if $P(z{=}1)=1$, I am the sim"). PC-6 equivocates between "differs in one belief" (a state-level fact, hence detectable, hence a genuinely different problem) and "differs in a physical detail irrelevant to the agent" (not a state-level fact at all, hence the same point). A graceful treatment of near-copies — partial coupling — needs graded logical correlation, which is the out-of-scope question; v2's discreteness is the price of declining it, paid openly.

**ID-8 [derived] — which existing v2 structure blocks what.** *The coupling itself*: nothing — it is what the attack removes ($V$ separable, ID-1). *Prop 4's $k$-fold querying*: nothing — probing $d'$ realizes Bernstein functions of $q'$, not of $q$; the sloppy Omega's probes are of the wrong point. *The labels* (calibration): block (A) at every grade and pin (A′) to the decider's frequency (ID-3). *Remark 4.4's memorylessness-as-criterion*: blocks (B) and is the only thing that does. *Def 7 recording*: blocks the whole construction as a member of the fair class (ID-2). So the answer to the mandate's question is: v2 blocks the collapse, but not by the coupling — by honesty (recording, calibration) and by the memorylessness criterion it already demoted to a remark.

## 2. Task 2 — coarsenings of point identity

The question: is there an equivalence on points, coarser than Def 3's exact triple, under which (i) fairness stays non-trivial, (ii) the fragility attack is blocked, (iii) no full logical-correlation apparatus is imported? PC-6's cost claim to verify: "biextensional equivalence … reintroduces the logical-correlation question you put out of scope". What v2's scope exclusion actually says [checked against the text]: Def 2 excludes counterpossible supposition by type ("matching the scope decision that counterlogicals are not attempted here"); the caveat after Def 22 puts "correlations between *distinct* decision-points … unrepresentable by point-deviations … FDT territory and out of scope here by design". Nothing excludes deciding that two nodes carry *one* point.

**ID-9 [derived] — biextensional equivalence does not transport to points.** CF's Def 5 (cf-paper, p. 8) identifies two *agent options* $a_0\sim a_1$ iff $a_0\cdot e = a_1\cdot e$ for all $e$, and dually environments; collapse deletes duplicate rows and columns. In the tree's frame $\mathrm{Fr}(B)$ (P1:cf-correspondence CF-1) rows are policies $\prod_d A_d$, and the functor forgets states, observations, $\beta$ and $\mu$ (P1:synthesis item 4 (checked)). So biextensional collapse can identify two *policies* with equal outcome rows; it has no access to the points at all — the fibering (which nodes share a point) is visible only as the diagonal commitment $\mathrm{Fr}(B)\cong\mathrm{Commit}^\Delta(\mathrm{Fr}^{\mathrm{nd}}(B))$ (CF-4, the salvaged half). Read as "identify options the environment can't tell apart" *on states*, the proposal either (a) over-collapses — the tree reads no state; all same-$A$ points are indistinguishable to it, giving total amnesia — or (b) reduces to ID-11 (identity by Omega's discrimination). PC-6's suggestion is therefore not a candidate coarsening of point identity; the phrase "the environment's resolution" is cashed out correctly only by ID-14 (the environment presents the *observation*).

**ID-10 [derived] — behavioral equivalence is theory-relative.** "$d\approx_C d'$ iff $C(d)=C(d')$" merges the two points under $(\mathrm{refuse},\mathrm{refuse})$ (EDT's answer) and separates them under $(\mathrm{refuse},\mathrm{pay})$ ($T_{\mathrm{opt}}$'s answer): the identity would depend on the theory being evaluated. It is exactly the "statistical" variant brief A, Sharpening 1 warns against (fairness becomes a property of the problem–procedure pair), and P1:synthesis item 2 (checked) already killed its cousin (weak node-SSC selects the dominated AMD procedure). Not a fairness criterion.

**ID-11 [derived] — coupling-relative identity is the fibering choice, named.** "Same point iff Omega's predictor treats them alike" is, at the concrete level, circular (the tree *says* which nodes carry $d$); at the abstract level (Def 14) it is an underdetermined stipulation set $\Sigma = \{B_1\}\cup\{B_1^{(s')}\}$ whose Def-15 consistency is decided by the exact-sim member (masked-OC-consistent for every $C$ via $B_1$, Prop 6). Its *justification* — "Omega's algorithm on input $s'$ outputs what mine outputs on $s$" — is a logical-correlation judgement between two computations. PC-6 is right that this imports the scoped-out question, and wrong about the form: it enters as a 0/1 identity decision (a Q1-type modelling relativity, like Remark 6.3's choice of $\mathcal E$), not as a graded correlation between *distinct* points, which is what §8 excludes. The tree hosts logical correlation only as sameness.

**ID-12 [derived; XC-6 test] — the sibling's §14 criterion is the wrong level and idle here.** SC Def 12.1 (TRUSTED): $d_i,d_j$ are epistemically coherent iff $\mathbf X_j = \mathbf X_i(\cdot\mid\bar a_0)$ for an atom of a *calibrated* anticipation structure on $\hat{\mathbf X}_i$; SC §4.3 makes same-ontology calibration automatic, so with $(O,A)$ held equal the criterion reads: $d\approx d'$ iff $P_{s'} = P_s(\cdot\mid E)$ for some $E$ with $P_s(E)>0$, or conversely. Applied to $B_1'$: version (A) — $\delta_{z=0}$ vs $\delta_{z=1}$ — no conditioning relates them (not absolutely continuous): not merged; version (A′) — $P_{s'}$ has the same two atoms with weights $(q_1,1-q_1)$ vs $(q_0,1-q_0)$ — the only conditionings of $P_s$ available are on $\{\mathrm{pay}\}$, $\{\mathrm{refuse}\}$ or their union, giving weights $1$, $0$ or $q_0$, never $q_1\in(0,1)\setminus\{q_0\}$: not merged; version (B) — identical $P$: merged trivially, as under Def 3. So §14 coarsens Def 3 only along *sharpening* perturbations (uncertain → certain) and never along contradicting or re-weighting ones; on the attack it is idle (XC-6 confirmed). More importantly it answers a different question: SC §14 says "the same *procedure* at different informational states" — cluster membership (one $s^\circ$ per problem, Def 11; Def 23's families; bridge row T19) — not "the same *instance*". Adopted as point identity it would merge an agent with its own conditioned self at the same observation (with and without a private clue $E$), a decision-relevant conditioning: over-collapse. Anticipation thread owns the Def 6.2 / Prop 2 side (XC-4).

**ID-13 [derived] — functional identity is the diagonal prior.** topics/core-argument.md (careful + consistent): all instances run one algorithm $F$; self-knowledge makes "I take $a$ at $o$" entail "$F(o)=a$", propagating to all instances. In v2 all nodes already run one $C$ (Def 4); functional identity does not identify $d$ with $d'$ — $C(d')$ is the same function on a different input — and the correlation between $C(s)$ and $C(s')$ it is meant to deliver is the diagonal prior of ID-6 (XC-14's "UDT1.0 believes it is UDT1.1"). Its cost is the full logical-correlation belief ("$F$ answers alike on inputs that differ irrelevantly"), stored in $s^\circ$ — brief B's own PC-11 no-free-lunch point turned against PC-6's cure.

**ID-14 [derived; checked on the mugging] — observation-identity keeps fairness non-trivial without importing logical correlation.** Coarsen Def 3 to $(O_d, A_d)$: same observation and action set, same point — v1's memorylessness, which v2 demoted to Remark 4.4's normative criterion "queried points with equal $O_d$ carry equal states" (= Def 19's observation-functionality ∧ a counterfactual theory). Under it: $B_1'\mapsto B_1$ in every version — dislocated fiber, relocation, calibrated EDT at the relocated root pays iff $y>x$ (P1:fair-repair FR-2 (checked); reconfirmed in `misc_checks.py`: root values $\{\mathrm{pay}: \tfrac12(y-x),\ \mathrm{refuse}: 0\}$). Fairness stays non-trivial and the attack is blocked at the belief level uniformly (A, A′, B). Every worked example of v2 §§6–7 and phase 1 is unchanged (their same-$O$ points are already the same point). Cost: (i) under strict/limit OC, none for EDT or UDT — calibration already equalizes $(P,V)$ at equal $O$ (ID-3(A)) — and CDT loses the ability to distinguish same-$O$ points by causal opinion (Remark 4.4's change-log item 12 residue); under masked OC the per-point self-credence label is lost, which no verdict consults. (ii) No logical correlation is imported: identity by observation is a type-level identification; Conitzer-type "isomorphic situations under different observations" remain distinct points, so §8's FDT residue is untouched. (iii) What *is* imported is the sibling's decision-determination external-only clause — the agent's behaviour is a function of external observation alone (ID-26): the price is exactly PC-13's F1. (iv) The residual fragility moves to the observation: an Omega whose sim's $O'$ differs by one irrelevant detail. Then either no leaf-world satisfies $O'$ — excluded from the fair class by realized observations (P1:adversary-repair Claim A's added hypothesis) and vacuous under Defs 8–10 — or $O'$ is realized and readable, and exploiting is correct as in ID-7(iii). This is the right place for the boundary: the *environment* stages the observation (Omega controls what the agent is shown) while the *agent* supplies the state; sloppiness about the agent's internals is the natural kind, and observation-identity is exactly robust to it. This — not biextensional collapse — is what "make the information set the environment's resolution" comes to.

## 3. Task 3 — the consistency argument (PC-11, PC-14; the designated crux)

Brief B, PC-14: "is a state that's certain of tails while knowing this exact state occurs at Omega's heads-simulation consistent? I say no: in any self-locating logic, if my state occurs on $H$ then my state can't entail $T$."

**ID-15 [derived] — on v2's own algebra the state is consistent, and the missing axiom is anthropic, not logical.** Prop 6's $s$ is a probability on $\mathcal E$ (coin, choice, transfer) with $P_s(T)=1$: a perfectly consistent object (Def 2 asks only finite additivity and normalization). "Knows this exact state occurs at Omega's heads-simulation" is a *de dicto* fact about the tree (the point $d$ labels a node in the $H$-branch) that $\mathcal E$ cannot express — occurrence events do not exist there, which is precisely why Prop 2 lifts. What brief B's argument needs to turn the de dicto fact into a constraint on $P_s$ is a *de se* bridge: **the anthropic indifference axiom** — "if a node instantiating my exact state lies on runs where $X$ holds with positive probability, then $P_s(X)>0$", i.e. $P_s \ll \mu(\cdot\mid\mathrm{occ}(d))$ — the absolute-continuity half of Def 13's SSC equation (the Diaconis–Zabell bound XC-4 computes: on $B_1$, $\|dP_s/d\nu\|_\infty = 2 > 1/\mu(\mathrm{occ}(d)) = 1$, fails). v2 houses this as a *calibration* condition (Def 13, a normative equation between a label and a statistic) and explicitly declines to decide between its two forms (Def 13: "the anthropic fork is not decided here"; Q9). v2's states lack the axiom because observation-calibration conditions on the observation-*event* $O_d$ and Remark 3.4 deliberately tolerates simulation members of $\mathrm{occ}(d)\setminus\{\lambda\models O_d\}$. So, for v2's states as typed: outcome **(b)** — consistent; the axiom brief B needs is SSC's absolute-continuity clause, and it is a normative choice in v2, not "any self-locating logic".

**ID-16 [checked; `kfold_fork.py`] — on the lifted algebra the reading splits, and the fork reappears inside.** On $\mathcal E' = 2^{\mathrm{Leaves}(B_1)}$ (Prop 2), every leaf below the $H$-node has coin $=H$, so for any probability $P'$ on $\mathcal E'$: $P'(\lambda^{-1}T)=1 \Rightarrow P'(\text{H-sim leaves}) = 0$. Read *self-locatingly* ("this run is at the H-sim" = the H-leaves), a tails-certain lifted state must give the simulation probability $0$ — it is consistent, but only as a state that *denies being the sim*; positive credence in being the sim contradicts certainty of tails: outcome **(a)** for the self-locating reading. No Prop-2 (per-run) lifted state is tails-certain: $\mu(\text{H-leaves}\mid\mathrm{occ}(d)) = \tfrac12$. Under the per-occurrence lift the same holds on $B_1$ ($\#_d\equiv 1$). But the *magnitude* of the required self-location credence is lift-dependent: on the $k$-fold mugging (one real tails query, $k$ heads simulations; `examples.k_fold_mugging`) per-run SSC pins $P(T)=\tfrac12$ for every $k$ while per-occurrence SSC pins $P(T) = \tfrac{1/2}{(1+k)/2} = \tfrac{1}{k+1}$ (toolkit: $k=1,2,3,5 \mapsto \tfrac12,\tfrac13,\tfrac14,\tfrac16$). Outcome **(c)**: the consistency *verdict* (a tails-certain state that locates itself at the sim with positive probability is inconsistent; a calibrated self-located state must have $P(H)>0$) is lift-independent, while *how much* credence "knowing this state occurs at the heads-simulation" demands is exactly the anthropic fork (SSA-like $\tfrac12$ vs SIA-like $\tfrac{k}{k+1}$ on heads). The fork does not close inside the consistency question; it moves from "is the state incoherent?" (no, absent the axiom; yes, given it) to "incoherent by how much?".
