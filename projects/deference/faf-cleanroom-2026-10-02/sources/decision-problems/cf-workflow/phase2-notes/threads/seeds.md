## 1. Definition 6′, the seed-class generalization, and the vocabulary

**Definition 6′ (shared-seed run semantics).** Fix a concrete decision problem $B$ (Definition 5) and a procedure $C$ (Definition 4). A run draws, once and independently for each queried point $d$, a *seed* $a_d \sim C(d)$; it then walks from the root, sampling children at chance nodes from $\beta_n$ and following the edge $a_{d_q}$ at every decision node $q$ — the same edge at every node carrying $d_q$. Write $\mu'_{B,C} \in \Delta(\mathrm{Leaves}(B))$ for the resulting leaf law, $\nu'_{B,C}$ for its world-marginal, $V'_B(C) := \mathbb{E}_{\mu'_{B,C}}[r]$. The derived notions $\mathrm{occ}(d)$, $\#_d$ and the point-deviation $C[d \mapsto m]$ are unchanged (they are path-defined). The tremble $C^\varepsilon$ of Definition 10 is literal under 6′: one trembled draw per point per run.

**Definition 6″ (seed-class semantics; the "hybrid" of slop §8 Q3, P1:verify F/G territory).** Equip $B$ with a *seed-class labelling* $\sigma$ assigning to each decision node $q$ an index $\sigma(q) \in \mathbb{N}$. A run draws one seed $a_{d,i} \sim C(d)$ per pair (point $d$, class $i$ occurring on a $d$-node), independently, and follows $a_{d_q, \sigma(q)}$ at $q$. Definition 6 is the case $\sigma$ injective on every fiber; Definition 6′ the case $\sigma$ constant on every fiber. The labelling is a datum of the description beyond Definition 5's tree — "which instances Omega treats as one agent" (slop §8 Q3, verbatim: "seed-shared *within* the instances Omega treats as one agent and independent *across* the samples Omega learns from").

**Vocabulary (precise).**
- *Pure* procedure: deterministic $C$. Its run is the same under 6, 6′, 6″.
- *Behavioral* procedure: $C : d \mapsto \Delta(A_d)$ run under Definition 6 — Kuhn's behavioral strategy with information sets $=$ points (v2's official object).
- *Product-mixed* procedure: the same $C$ run under Definition 6′; equivalently the distribution $\bigotimes_d C(d)$ on pure procedures $\prod_d A_d$, drawn once per run.
- *Correlated-mixed* strategy: an arbitrary $m \in \Delta(\prod_d A_d)$ drawn once per run. Not a procedure in Definition 4's type; it is the general "distribution over the frame's agent set" (P1:cf-correspondence CF-3). Relocation's product root realizes exactly these (P1:fair-repair FR-9(iv)).

**SE-1 [derived; AMD numbers checked, `se1_semantics.py` (a)].** (a) $\mu'_{B,C} = \sum_{\pi \in \prod_d A_d} \big(\prod_d C(d)(\pi(d))\big)\, \mu_{B,\pi}$: the shared-seed law is the $\bigotimes_d C(d)$-mixture of the pure-procedure laws (immediate from the definition — condition on the seed tuple). Toolkit check on the AMD: `Run(B, C, 'shared').mu == q·Run(B, δ_a).mu + (1-q)·Run(B, δ_b).mu` → True. (b) Consequently $V'_B$ is *multiaffine* in $(C(d))_d$ for **every** tree — affine in each $C(d)$ separately, with $V'_B(C) = \sum_a C(d)(a)\, V'_B(C[d \mapsto a])$ — so $\max_C V'_B(C) = \max_{\pi \text{ pure}} V_B(\pi)$ is attained by a deterministic procedure on every tree (Proposition 5's deterministic maximum). On the AMD: $V'(q) = 1 - q$, $\max = 1$; toolkit `max_deterministic` = 1 vs `max_mixed` (Definition 6) = 4/3. (c) Lemma 1 (occurrence constancy) holds verbatim under 6′ and 6″: reaching a minimal $d$-node uses other points' seeds and chance only. (d) Under 6′ there is no single-instance forcing counterfactual: $G_q(C,a)$ of §8 ("force $a$ at $q$ only, other $d$-nodes still drawing from $C$") is not well-typed, because the other $d$-nodes reuse the seed; the toolkit's `cont_value`/`G` correctly refuse the shared semantics.

**SE-2 [derived; checked on the battery, `se1_semantics.py` (b),(c)].** **Theorem (when the two semantics agree).** For a concrete problem $B$ the following are equivalent: (i) $\mu_{B,C} = \mu'_{B,C}$ for every procedure $C$; (ii) no path all of whose chance edges have positive probability contains two decision nodes carrying the same point $d$ with $|A_d| \geq 2$.
*Proof.* (ii)⇒(i): a path with a zero-probability chance edge has probability $0$ under both semantics. On a path with all chance edges positive the decision nodes carry distinct points (single-action points contribute a factor $1$ under both), so under 6 its probability is $\prod \beta \cdot \prod_i C(d_i)(a_i)$ and under 6′ it is $\prod \beta \cdot \Pr(a_{d_1} = a_1, \ldots, a_{d_m} = a_m) = \prod\beta \cdot \prod_i C(d_i)(a_i)$ by independence of the seeds. (i)⇒(ii): suppose a positive path carries $d$-nodes $q_1$ above $q_2$ with $|A_d| \ge 2$, taking $a_1$ at $q_1$ and $a_2$ at $q_2$. Take $C$ uniform (full support). If $a_1 \neq a_2$ the path itself has positive probability under 6 and probability $0$ under 6′. If $a_1 = a_2 = a$, follow the path to $q_2$, take $b \neq a$ there, and continue along positive chance edges (every chance node has one, its probabilities summing to 1) to a leaf: positive under 6, zero under 6′. $\square$
*Corollary (almost fair ⇒ agreement).* On every almost-fair tree (no nested fiber, P1:fair-repair §1.3) Definitions 6 and 6′ induce the same leaf law for every procedure, hence the same $\nu$, $V_B$, and the same laws under every $C[d\mapsto m]$ and $C^\varepsilon$. Under nesting they differ for every full-support procedure on some leaf; on the AMD (Prop 5(c)) $V(q) = (1-q)(3q+1)$ vs $V'(q) = 1-q$, difference $3q(1-q)$; at $q = \tfrac13$: $\tfrac43$ vs $\tfrac23$ (P1:verify D1's numbers, re-derived).
*Battery [checked].* With a generic full-support procedure on all 35 problems of `examples.py`: `laws agree ⟺ is_almost_fair(B)` holds on every row; the nested rows are the six $k$-fold muggings, TN-V1, TN-V2, AMD, CX1 and Remark 4.3's miniature.
*Caveats to the "iff" (each checked).* (c1) Payoff/label degeneracy: a nested stochastic gadget whose leaves all pay $0$ has different leaf laws but equal values under 6 and 6′ — so value-level agreement is strictly weaker than law-level agreement (this reappears as SE-12's "missing case 1"). (c2) A zero-probability chance edge above a nested gadget: `nested_fibers ≠ {}` yet the laws agree — the pruning clause in (ii) is needed. (c3) A nested point with a single action: agree trivially.

**SE-3 [derived; the separating example checked, `se3_learnability.py`].** *Semantics-invariance of the normative layer on the almost-fair class.* Every calibration sense of Definitions 8–11 and 13 is a functional of $\mu_{B,C'}$ for $C' \in \{C,\ C[d\mapsto m],\ C^\varepsilon\}$ together with $O_d$ and $r$; every theory of Definition 18 reads states, $V_B$, or point-deviation values; Theorem 2's evaluator and Definition 22 read $\mu_{B,C[d\mapsto a]}$. By SE-2 all of these coincide under 6 and 6′ on almost-fair trees: *strict / masked / limit observation calibration, prior calibration, per-run and per-occurrence SSC, $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}, T_{\mathrm{CDT}}, T_{\mathrm{UDT}}, T_{\mathrm{opt}}$, Definition-22 coherence and Theorem 2's condition are semantics-invariant there.* Under nesting the senses can come apart, and Definition 10 does: take the AMD gadget on $d$ and let node 2's $a$-edge lead to a node of a second point $e$ with $O_e = \{\mathrm{exit} = 2\}$ (leaves $u \mapsto 4$, $v \mapsto 3$). Under $C = (d \mapsto b,\ e \mapsto v)^\varepsilon$: independent redraws give $\nu_\varepsilon(O_e) = \varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)/4 > 0$ and the limit conditional $\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\nu_\varepsilon(\cdot \mid O_e) = \delta_{(v,\,\mathrm{exit}=2)}$, so Definition 10 pins $e$'s state; under the shared seed $\nu_\varepsilon(O_e) \equiv 0$ for every $\varepsilon$ (reaching $e$ needs $b$ at node 1 and $a$ at node 2 — an inconsistent answer pattern), so Definition 10 leaves $e$ **unconstrained**. Register: *limit calibration under 6′ is strictly weaker than under 6 on nested trees and identical on almost-fair trees.*

## 2. Relocation under the shared seed (P1:fair-repair FR-5/FR-6/FR-7 redone)

**SE-5 [derived; checked battery-wide, `se2_relocation.py`].** **Theorem (relocation is Definition 6′ made structural).** Let $B$ be any concrete problem, $U$ any set of queried points (nested fibers allowed: every $U$-node in the scope is resolved to the chosen coordinate), $\tilde B := \mathrm{Rel}_U(B)$ the joint product relocation (P1:fair-repair E2 / toolkit `relocate`, entanglement closure applied), and for a procedure $C$ on $B$ let $\tilde C$ be the lifted procedure ($\tilde C(\hat d) = \bigotimes_{d \in U} C(d)$, $\tilde C = C$ on survivors). Then for every $C$ the canonical leaf map pushes $\mu'_{\tilde B, \tilde C}$ forward to $\mu'_{B,C}$; and if $U$ contains every nested fiber of $B$, $\mu_{\tilde B,\tilde C} = \mu'_{\tilde B,\tilde C}$ (the output is almost fair), so $\mu_{\tilde B,\tilde C}$ (Definition 6 on the output) pushes forward to $\mu'_{B,C}$ (Definition 6′ on the input). In particular $V_{\tilde B}(\tilde C) = V'_B(C)$ for every $C$, every grade, every tree.
*Proof.* Condition on the seed tuple $(a_d)_{d\in U}$, which under 6′ on $B$ is drawn once with law $\bigotimes_{d\in U} C(d)$ and at $\hat q$ is drawn once with the same law. Given the tuple, $B$'s run under 6′ follows $a_d$ at every $U$-node and the survivors' seeds elsewhere; $\tilde B$'s run in the $(a_d)$-branch follows the resolved edges (which are the $a_d$-edges) and the survivors' seeds at the copied survivor nodes, which carry the same points; the canonical leaf map identifies the leaf reached. Chance labels are copied. So the conditional laws agree branch by branch, and the mixtures agree. For the second clause: $\tilde B$'s fibers are the survivors' (copied) fibers and the singleton $\{\hat q\}$; a nested survivor fiber in $\tilde B$ comes from a nested survivor fiber in $B$, excluded by hypothesis; apply SE-2's corollary. $\square$
*Battery [checked, `se2_relocation.py`].* For every problem of the battery, both with $U =$ all queried points and with $U =$ the entanglement closure of the unfair fibers: `law(Rel_U(B), lift C) marginalized to B.coords == law(B, C, shared)` → **True on every problem**, nested fibers included (AMD, CX1, TN-V1, TN-V2, the six $k$-fold muggings, the miniature); `== law(B, C, independent)` → True exactly on the almost-fair rows plus the label-degenerate `k_fold_mugging(k, linear)` rows (whose $(\lambda,r)$-law is semantics-blind because the worlds do not record the individual simulated draws and the linear coupling makes the transfer probability affine); and the output is strongly fair in the pre-enrichment-marginal sense (`exempt=rel.pol_coords`, P1:adversary-repair Claim B) on every row, nesting or not. The two `sequenced_pair` rows fail for an encoding reason only (their worlds already carry coordinates named `pol_d`, `pol_e`, which `relocate` overwrites — Toolkit issue 1); rebuilt with fresh coordinate names (`se2b_sequenced.py`) they pass for $U = \{d\}$, $\{e|1, e|2\}$ and all three points.

**SE-7 [derived].** **Theorem (preservation, with exact hypotheses).** For every concrete problem $B$, every $U$, every procedure $C$: $V_{\mathrm{Rel}_U(B)}(\tilde C) - V_B(C) = V'_B(C) - V_B(C)$. Hence: (i) under Definition 6′ on the input, relocation is law- and value-preserving at every grade for every tree; (ii) under Definition 6 on the input, relocation is value-preserving for every $C$ and every payoff labelling iff $U$'s fibers meet no positive path twice (SE-2) — P1:fair-repair FR-7(b)'s mixed-grade obstruction *is* the 6/6′ gap, and P1:adversary-repair Claim F's payoff-degenerate exception is SE-2's caveat (c1); (iii) the output is strongly fair in the pre-enrichment-marginal sense irrespective of nesting (checked), so FR-4's "no nested fiber in $U$" hypothesis was needed only for preservation under Definition 6, not for fairness. ## 3. The learnability price (Proposition 4; Remark 5.4; the learning reading of Definition 10)

**SE-8 [derived; checked at $k = 3$, `se3_learnability.py`].** **Theorem (Prop 4 under Definition 6′).** Over concrete problems that query a binary point $d$ exactly $k$ times on every path and contain no other $d$-nodes, the functions $q \mapsto \Pr'(X)$ realizable under Definition 6′ are exactly $\{q \mapsto b_0(1-q) + b_1 q : b_0, b_1 \in [0,1]\}$ — Proposition 4's class at $k = 1$, for every $k$. *Proof.* Under 6′ the pattern of $a$-draws on a path is $\emptyset$ or $[k]$; conditioning on it, $\Pr'(X) = c_\emptyset (1-q) + c_{[k]} q$; backward, the $k = 1$ tree realizes any such pair. $\square$ More generally, for any tree whose only queried point is $d$, $\nu'_{B,C}(X) = (1-q)\,\nu_{B,\delta_{\bar a}}(X) + q\,\nu_{B,\delta_a}(X)$ (SE-1(a)). Check at $k = 3$: independent $\Pr(X) = \sum_j b_j \binom{3}{j} q^j (1-q)^{3-j}$ (Bernstein form verified), shared $\Pr'(X) = b_0(1-q) + b_3 q$ (verified). This confirms P1:verify E5 and makes it a theorem.

**SE-9 [derived; checked for $(n,g) \in \{(1,3),(3,1),(2,2),(2,3),(3,2)\}$, `se3_learnability.py`].** **Theorem (Prop 4 under the seed-class semantics).** In Definition 6″ with $n$ seed classes on the $k = ng$ queries ($g$ queries per class), the realizable class is exactly the degree-$n$ Bernstein class $\{q \mapsto \sum_{j=0}^{n} b'_j \binom{n}{j} q^j (1-q)^{n-j} : b'_j \in [0,1]\}$: the group size $g$ is expressively inert. *Proof.* Each class returns one independent $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(q)$ bit (its $g$ draws coincide); conditioning on the $n$ class bits gives $\sum_S c_S q^{|S|}(1-q)^{n-|S|}$ and grouping by $|S|$ averages the $c_S$ into $b'_j$; backward, route on the class pattern. $\square$ Toolkit emulation (each class as a separately named point with the same triple, run `'shared'`): degree in $q$ equals $n$ in all five configurations, and the polynomial equals the degree-$n$ Bernstein form with $b'_j = c_{jg}$ when the tree routes on the total count. In Omega's terms: *the expressive power of probing is the number of independent samples, not the number of instances.*

**SE-10 [derived].** *What this does to Remark 5.4 and to the learning reading of Definition 10.* (a) Remark 5.4's "branching swallows labels" runs at the rate of independent seed classes: under Definition 6′ within-run probing swallows nothing ($\Pr'(X)$ affine, SE-8); labels are approximated only across runs, each run being one class; under 6″ a $k$-fold simulator learns the disposition at Bernstein rate $n$ (its sample count), not $k$. The static-shadow story of §9 survives with "iterations" reinterpreted as seed classes. (b) Remark 3.9's learning reading ("an evidential learner with vanishing exploration is $\varepsilon$-calibrated throughout training, and Definition 10 is the $\varepsilon \to 0$ shadow of that process"): under 6′ the tremble is one draw per point per run, i.e. exploration at the *disposition* level, never at the instance level; the $\varepsilon \to 0$ shadow then constrains exactly the states at nodes reachable by consistent answers and leaves inconsistent-answer nodes free (SE-3's example) — on almost-fair trees this is no loss (SE-3), on nested trees it is the price of coherence. (c) The trade-off of slop §4 / P1:synthesis item 17 sharpens into two axes that the hybrid separates: *coherence* (SE-12: the live instance's own-draw conditioning equals the all-instance deviation) needs the live instance to share a class with the simulations it is coupled to; *learnability* (SE-9) needs many classes. Both can hold at once: share within a class, independent across classes. What is lost relative to Remark 5.4 as written: Omega's within-run sample of $k$ instances is worth $n \le k$ independent bits, and the coherence identity holds only for the class containing the live agent. (d) The labelling $\sigma$ is description data (slop Q3's "puts the agent's identity into the environment's labels"): two nodes with identical $(s, O, A)$ may still differ in class, so whether they "are one agent" is not recoverable from point identity — handed to `identity` and `literalist`.

## 4. Seam 5 — the 2×2 of own-draw evaluators, and CX1's exact boundary

**SE-11 [derived; every identification checked on six $k$-fold instances and CX1, `se4_cells.py`, `common.py`].** *Definitions.* Fix $B$, $C$ with $C(d)$ of full support, and a queried $d$. The *own-draw evaluator* in cell (semantics, weighting) is
$$E_{\mathrm{sem},w}(a) := \frac{\mathbb{E}_{w}\big[r \cdot \mathbf 1(\text{my draw} = a)\big]}{\Pr_{w}(\text{my draw} = a)},$$
where the expectation is over (run, instance) pairs with the run drawn from $\mu_{B,C}$ (sem $=$ 6) or $\mu'_{B,C}$ (sem $=$ 6′) restricted to $\mathrm{occ}(d)$, and the instance is one of the $\#_d(\ell)$ $d$-nodes on the run: **SIA** (per-occurrence) gives every instance weight $1$ (pair weight $\propto \mu(\ell)$); **SSA** (per-run, minimal reference class) gives the run weight $\mu(\ell \mid \mathrm{occ}(d))$ split uniformly over its instances. "My draw" is the action drawn at my instance. The four cells are, with the identifications proved below:

| | SIA / per-occurrence | SSA / per-run |
|---|---|---|
| Definition 6 (independent) | $\dfrac{\sum_q R_q\, G_q(C,a)}{\sum_q R_q}$ — **Theorem 1** (CDT+SIA), normalized by $\mathbb{E}[\#_d]$ | $\dfrac{\sum_q (R_q/n_q)\, G_q(C,a)}{\sum_q R_q/n_q}$ — **CDT+SSA**: Theorem 1 with fiber-*averaged* weights (the diluted evaluator) |
| Definition 6′ (shared seed) | $\dfrac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu'_{C[d\mapsto a]}}[\#_d\, r]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu'_{C[d\mapsto a]}}[\#_d]}$ — **EDT+SIA**: the all-instance deviation weighted by occurrence count (the inflated evaluator) | $\mathbb{E}_{\mu'_{C[d\mapsto a]}}[r \mid \mathrm{occ}(d)]$ — **Theorem 2′** ($=$ Definition-6′ coherence; $=$ Theorem 2 under SE-12's hypothesis) |

($n_q$ := the value of $\#_d$ on the paths through $q$, when constant; in general the SSA-indep cell is $\sum_\ell \mu(\ell)\, \#_d(\ell)^{-1} \sum_{i} \mathbf 1[a_i(\ell) = a]\, r(\ell)$ over $\sum_\ell \mu(\ell)\,\#_d(\ell)^{-1}\sum_i \mathbf 1[a_i(\ell)=a]$.)
*Proofs of the identifications.* (indep, SIA): under Definition 6 the draw at $q$ is an independent coordinate, so $\Pr(\text{at } q \wedge \text{draw } a) = R_q\, C(d)(a)$ and $\mathbb{E}[r \mid \text{at } q, \text{draw } a] = G_q(C,a)$ (P1:verify E1's argument, CONFIRMED); the SIA pair weights are $\propto R_q$, and the constant $C(d)(a)$ cancels in the ratio — Theorem 1's functional divided by $\mathbb{E}[\#_d] = \sum_q R_q$ (checked equal on every instance: `(indep,SIA) == Thm1 weights / E[#]: True`). (indep, SSA): same, with pair weights $R_q / n_q$. (shared, SSA): under 6′ "my draw $= a$" is "the seed $a_d = a$", whose conditional law is $\mu'_{B, C[d\mapsto a]}$ (other points untouched), and the per-run weight is $\mu'(\cdot\mid\mathrm{occ}(d))$, whose normalizer is seed-invariant by Lemma 1 (SE-1(c)); the SSA split over instances cancels exactly as in Theorem 2's proof. (shared, SIA): the same conditioning with weight $\#_d(\ell)$ per run. Checked: `(shared,SSA) == Thm2' (Def 6'): True` on every instance.

**SE-12 [derived; boundary cases checked, `se4_cells.py`].** **Theorem (exact boundary of the killed Claim 4.2; P1:adversary-verify R6, WOUNDED → repaired).** Let $C(d)$ have full support (so the own-draw conditioning is non-null). (i) The (shared, SSA) evaluator equals Theorem 2′'s $a \mapsto \mathbb{E}_{\mu'_{B,C[d\mapsto a]}}[r \mid \mathrm{occ}(d)]$, whose argmax is $\arg\max_a V'_B(C[d\mapsto a])$ — Definition-22 coherence *under Definition 6′* — for **every** tree. (ii) For the given tree shape and $C$, the identity "(shared, SSA) $=$ Theorem 2's Definition-6 evaluator $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{B,C[d\mapsto a]}}[r\mid\mathrm{occ}(d)]$" holds **for every payoff labelling $r$** iff for every $a \in A_d$, $\mu'_{B,C[d\mapsto a]}(\cdot \mid \mathrm{occ}(d)) = \mu_{B,C[d\mapsto a]}(\cdot\mid\mathrm{occ}(d))$, iff no $C[d\mapsto a]$-positive path through a $d$-node carries two nodes of a point $e \neq d$ with $|\mathrm{supp}\,C(e)| \geq 2$ (for some $a$). *Proof.* (i) is SE-11. (ii): both evaluators are linear in $r$ with coefficient vectors the two conditional laws on $\mathrm{occ}(d)$; two linear functionals agree on all $r$ iff their coefficients agree. ## 5. Kuhn at the law level (phase-1 open problem 7)

**SE-16 [derived; checked on 12 hand-built and 78 random instances, `se5_kuhn.py`].** **Theorem (outcome-equivalence at the $(\lambda,r)$-law level on strongly fair trees).** Let $B$ be strongly fair (every fiber's members have isomorphic labelled subtrees: chance labels $\beta$, points, edge actions, leaf pairs $(\lambda,r)$ — P1's adopted notion). For a correlated mixture $m \in \Delta(\prod_d A_d)$ let $\mu_m := \sum_\pi m(\pi)\,\mu_{B,\pi}$, and define the behavioral procedure
$$C_m(d)(a) := \frac{N_m(d,a)}{\sum_{a'} N_m(d,a')}, \qquad N_m(d,a) := \sum_\pi m(\pi)\, \mathbf 1[\pi(d) = a]\, \mu_{B,\pi}(\mathrm{occ}(d))$$
($C_m(d)$ arbitrary when the denominator vanishes). Then the pushforwards of $\mu_m$ and of $\mu_{B,C_m}$ (Definition 6) to $\Omega \times \mathbb{R}$ coincide. Hence on strongly fair trees the sets of realizable $(\lambda,r)$-laws of correlated mixtures, of product mixtures (Definition 6′) and of behavioral procedures (Definition 6) are equal. Leaf-level laws need not coincide.
*Proof.* Step 0. Strong fairness gives $\#_d \le 1$ on every path (P1:fair-repair FR-1(i)). Write $d \prec e$ if some $e$-node lies strictly below some $d$-node; $\prec$ is acyclic: from $d \prec e \prec d$, an $e$-node $q_e$ below a $d$-node $q_d$ and a $d$-node $q'_d$ below an $e$-node $q'_e$; the fiber isomorphism $\mathrm{sub}(q'_e) \cong \mathrm{sub}(q_e)$ carries $q'_d$ to a $d$-node strictly below $q_d$, whose subtree is isomorphic to $\mathrm{sub}(q_d)$ — a finite tree isomorphic to a proper subtree of itself, impossible.
Step 1 (route decomposition). Let $P$ be either law. For $q \in F_d$ the events "reach $q$" are pairwise disjoint (no path meets two $d$-nodes), so $N_P(d,a) := P(\text{reach some } d\text{-node and draw } a \text{ there}) = \sum_{q\in F_d} P(\text{reach } q \wedge \text{draw } a)$; for $P = \mu_m$ this is the displayed $N_m$, for $P = \mu_{B,C}$ it is $P(\text{reach } F_d)\,C(d)(a)$ (the draw at a node is independent of reaching it under Definition 6). For a leaf $\ell$ let $q(\ell)$ be the lowest decision node above it (if any), with point $d(\ell)$ and action $a(\ell)$ on the path, and $c(\ell)$ the product of the chance probabilities strictly below $q(\ell)$'s $a(\ell)$-child; then $P(\ell) = P(\text{reach } q(\ell) \wedge \text{draw } a(\ell))\cdot c(\ell)$, or $P(\ell) = c(\ell)$ if no decision node lies above $\ell$.
Step 2 (the $(\lambda,r)$-law is a function of the $N$'s). The fiber isomorphisms of $F_d$ put the leaves below the $a$-children of its members in bijection, preserving $c(\ell)$ and $(\lambda,r)(\ell)$. Summing Step 1 over such a class gives mass $N_P(d,a)\,c$ at one label. So $\mathrm{law}_P = \sum_{\text{chance-only } \ell} c(\ell)\,\delta_{(\lambda,r)(\ell)} + \sum_{d,a}\sum_{\text{classes}} N_P(d,a)\,c\,\delta_{\text{label}}$: two laws with equal $N$'s have equal $(\lambda,r)$-laws.
Step 3 ($N_{C_m} = N_m$, by $\prec$-induction). $P(\text{reach } F_d) = \sum_{q\in F_d} P(\text{reach } q)$, and $P(\text{reach } q) = P(\text{reach } q' \wedge \text{draw } a')\cdot c'$ where $q'$ is the lowest decision node above $q$ (point $e \prec d$), or $= c'$ if none. Grouping the members of $F_d$ by the class of their route $(e, a', \text{chance path})$ — well defined because the isomorphisms of $F_e$ carry the $d$-nodes below one member's $a'$-child bijectively onto those below another's — gives $P(\text{reach } F_d) = \sum_{\text{routes}} N_P(e,a')\,c' + \sum_{\text{chance-only routes}} c$, one function $\Phi_d$ of $\{N_P(e,\cdot) : e \prec d\}$ and $\beta$, the same for both laws. Base: $\prec$-minimal $d$ has chance-only routes. Induction: $N_{C_m}(d,a) = P_{C_m}(\text{reach } F_d)\,C_m(d)(a) = \Phi_d(N_m)\cdot N_m(d,a)/\sum_{a'}N_m(d,a') = N_m(d,a)$ when $\sum_{a'} N_m(d,a') = P_m(\text{reach } F_d) > 0$, and both sides vanish otherwise. $\square$
*The reverse inclusions.* On a strongly fair (hence almost fair) tree, Definition 6 $=$ Definition 6′ (SE-2), and a product mixture is a correlated mixture; so the three law-sets coincide. *What was used:* only the labelled isomorphism of fibers (probabilities, points, actions, leaf labels) — not veridicality, coverage or recording.
*Checks.* Hand-built trees with two-member fibers, sub-points shared across fibers and points reachable by several routes ($T_A$–$T_D$ in the script) and 39 random strongly fair trees (templates pasted at several sites, fibers up to size 8, up to 4 points) with random rational correlated mixtures: $(\lambda,r)$-laws equal in 90/90 tests; leaf-level laws differ in most.

**SE-17 [checked, `se5_kuhn.py` (1)].** *The adversary's four-leaf forget-your-own-action tree* (root $d_1 \in \{a,b\}$, then a $d_2 \in \{c,e\}$ node under each answer, four distinct leaf worlds): not strongly fair — `fairness` reports "fiber d2: 2 node(s), UNFAIR witness act1=a vs act1=b" — and there the correlated mixture $\tfrac12(a,c) + \tfrac12(b,e)$ has no product-realizable $(\lambda,r)$-law (product laws are $pr, p(1-r), (1-p)r, (1-p)(1-r)$; $\tfrac12,0,0,\tfrac12$ is not one). This is exactly P1:adversary-verify's sharpening ("the right open question is $(\lambda,r)$-law equivalence, not leaf-law") — now answered positively by SE-16. P1:verify D1's misattribution ("strong fairness implies perfect recall") stays killed: what strong fairness gives is no-absentmindedness plus label erasure, and SE-16 shows that this pair suffices for Kuhn's conclusion at the label level.

## 6. Procedures as correspondences (Q12's Kakutani intuition)

**SE-18 [derived].** *Retyping.* A *decision rule* is a correspondence $\Gamma : (d : \mathcal D) \to 2^{\Delta(A_d)}\setminus\{\emptyset\}$ with nonempty convex compact values; the advocacy theories of Definition 18 are already of this type — $\Gamma_T(d) = \{m : \mathrm{supp}\,m \subseteq \arg\max_{a \in A_d^{(+)}} v_T(d,a)\}$, a face of the simplex (v2's own hook: "the advocacy theories of Definition 18 return convex sets"). A procedure is a *selection* $C(d) \in \Gamma(d)$; Definition 17's uniform tie is the barycentric selection. The fixed-point problem of §5 becomes: with states freed ($\Sigma_B$), a calibration sense $\kappa$ maps a procedure to the calibrated states, $\kappa(C) = (s_d^C)_d$, and $\Phi := \Gamma_T \circ \kappa$ is a correspondence from $\prod_d \Delta(A_d)$ to itself; a *calibrated-and-approved* procedure (Definition 18's "$T$-consistency") is exactly a fixed point $C \in \Phi(C)$. **Run semantics as equilibrium selection:** the fixed point is chosen first, then run under 6 or 6′ (the two $\Phi$'s agree on almost-fair trees by SE-3 and can differ under nesting, SE-19).
**Existence theorem (Kakutani).** Let $B$ be a finite tree in which every queried observation is realized under every full-support procedure ($\nu_{B,C}(O_d) > 0$ whenever $C$ has full support — equivalently, some leaf-world satisfying $O_d$ lies below a positive chance path in the subtree of a $d$-node or elsewhere on a positive path, P1:adversary-repair A.1's hypothesis), $\kappa$ strict observation calibration (Definition 8) and $T$ an advocacy theory whose act value is a continuous function of the calibrated state where the state is defined (EDT's news value $V_{s_d}(a)$ with $A_d^+$ read as "all of $A_d$", which holds automatically under full support and recording; cUDT/CDT with a $\mathrm{cf}$ continuous in $(P_s,V_s)$). For $\varepsilon > 0$ let $\Delta_\varepsilon(A_d) := \{m : m(a) \ge \varepsilon/|A_d|\}$ and $\Phi_\varepsilon(C) := \Gamma_T^{\varepsilon}(\kappa(C))$, the $\Delta_\varepsilon$-constrained best-response face (maximal weight on maximizers). Then: (a) on $\prod_d \Delta_\varepsilon(A_d)$, $\kappa$ is continuous (leaf probabilities are polynomials in $C$; the conditioning denominators $\nu(O_d)$ and $\nu(a \wedge O_d)$ are bounded away from $0$ by full support and realized observations), the act values are continuous, and the best-response correspondence of a continuous function has closed graph and nonempty convex compact values; **Kakutani gives a fixed point $C_\varepsilon$** — an $\varepsilon$-calibrated-and-approved procedure, for every $\varepsilon$; (b) limit points $C_0$ of $(C_\varepsilon)$ exist by compactness, and $C_0$ is approved by $T$ at its Definition-10 (limit-calibrated) state: if $a \in \mathrm{supp}\,C_0(d)$ then $C_\varepsilon(d)(a) > \varepsilon/|A_d|$ for small $\varepsilon$, so $a$ maximizes $v_T^\varepsilon(d,\cdot)$, and weak inequalities pass to the limit of the (rational-in-$\varepsilon$) values, which is the Definition-10 value. (c) **Where it fails, and why trembles are forced.** Definition 17's EDT restricts the domain to $A_d^+ = \{a : P_{s_d}(a) > 0\}$; the correspondence $s \mapsto \mathrm{EDT}(s)$ is *not* upper hemicontinuous at states with a null act: with $P_{s_t}(a) = t$, $V_{s_t}(a) = 5 > 1 = V_{s_t}(b)$ for $t > 0$, $\mathrm{EDT}(s_t) = \{a\}$ for every $t \in (0,1]$ but $\mathrm{EDT}(s_0) = \{b\}$ — the graph is not closed at $t = 0$, and Kakutani does not apply to the untrembled composite (this is Remark 5.2's "discontinuous … can lack fixed points entirely", now located: the discontinuity is the domain restriction at null acts, not the argmax). Pinning the null-act values by their tremble limits (Remark 3.9's advice stance, the canonical evidential $\mathrm{cf}$ of Q6) restores closed graph — so the tremble device is exactly what the correspondence typing needs for existence, which is the calibration thread's fixed-point question on the fair class stated at the general level. Register: *existence holds for every finite tree with realized observations at every $\varepsilon > 0$ and in the tremble limit; without the tremble/pinning device it can fail at null acts.*

**SE-19 [checked, `se6_kakutani.py`].** *Remark 4.3's miniature as a Kakutani fixed point.* Under Definition 6 the strictly calibrated act values are $V(a) = 2(1-q)$, $V(b) = q$; the $\varepsilon$-restricted best response on $[\varepsilon/2, 1-\varepsilon/2]$ sends $q < \tfrac23$ to $1-\varepsilon/2$, $q > \tfrac23$ to $\varepsilon/2$, and at $q = \tfrac23$ returns the whole interval — so $q = \tfrac23$ is the unique fixed point for every $\varepsilon < \tfrac23$ (checked at $\varepsilon = \tfrac1{10}, \tfrac1{100}$; the vertices are not fixed), and the limit is Remark 4.3's $(\tfrac23,\tfrac13)$: *yes, the calibrated-and-approved mixture is the Kakutani fixed point, and it is reached through the $\varepsilon$-games.* Side fact: $V_B(C) = 3q(1-q)$, maximal $\tfrac34$ at $q = \tfrac12$, while the ratifiable point $\tfrac23$ has value $\tfrac23$ — a Q4 sufficiency-gap instance inside Remark 4.3 itself. Under Definition 6′ the sample equals the live draw, $r \equiv 0$, $V(a) = V(b) = 0$, and **every** $q$ is a fixed point: the miniature degenerates — a verdict change under the retyping-plus-semantics only at this (nested) example. On v2's worked examples §§6–7 nothing changes: their optima are strict (Told-You-So $10 > 5$; TN-V2 at $p = \tfrac34, L = 4, S = 1$: $3 > 2 > \tfrac74 > \tfrac54$; mugging $x=1,y=3$: $1 > 0$), so the face is a vertex and the selection is irrelevant.

**SE-20 [derived].** *What the retyping buys and costs.* Buys: the existence statement of SE-18 (the "tie-freedom is where equilibria live" of Remark 4.3 becomes an instance of Kakutani, with the tie face as the correspondence's value), a clean separation between recommendation (a face) and implementation (a selection plus a run semantics), and a precise diagnosis of the existence failure (domain restriction at null acts).
