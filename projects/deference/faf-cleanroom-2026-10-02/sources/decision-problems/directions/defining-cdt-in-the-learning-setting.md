## 0. Headline, with registers

1. **Every learning-CDT rendering on the menu is, at a recorded point under Definition 6, one of two things: conditioning on the act (renderings i–ii, on the act or on the draw), or an interventional supposition that provably equals conditioning on positive-probability acts (renderings iii–iv), so that classical CDT and EDT are one evaluator there and the contest has no content** [derived; Theorem 3 below; the (S1) case checked over all 25 DAGs on the lesion coordinates]. This is S9's coincidence stated for causal models rather than tree referents.
2. **Claim 9.1 of the boundaries note is true in the scope it was stated for and false as a general statement about "every graph prior"**: with latent confounders or reversed (post-query → act) structures admitted, observationally consistent causal models exist whose $P(k\mid do(m))$ differs from $P(k\mid m)$ at a recorded point (cancellation / faithfulness violation; reversed causation when the act is correlated with a post-query variable) [checked: script (B) and (D)]. The exact condition is an iff: $P^{G,do(a)} = P_s(\cdot\mid a)$ iff the act is independent of its $G$-parents under $P_s$ [derived].
3. **The exchange's "order δ" claim is right for the act the lab CDT takes and wrong for the one it does not take**: at the smoke label its $do(\mathrm{smoke})$ misses the actual cancer rate among its own smoking episodes by $\rho\rho_A\delta(\gamma_1-\gamma_0)/(1-\rho_A\delta) \approx 2.8\times 10^{-4}$, while its $do(\mathrm{abstain})$ misses the rate among its abstentions by $\rho(\gamma_1-\gamma_0) = 0.055$, an $O(1)$ gap on an $O(\delta)$-probability act; the Dutch-book exposure is $O(\delta)$ either way [checked]. The miss is a failure of neither clause of Definition 8: it is a $\mathrm{cf}_s$/$P_s$ disagreement that Definition 8 never inspects and Definition 20's evidential criterion does [derived].
4. **Non-responsiveness is the form of every CDT but not its content**: every interventional supposition is non-responsive on the algebra generated by the act's non-descendants, and at recorded points the evidential conditional is non-responsive on the pre-query algebra too (Lemma 3′), so the property separates CDT from EDT only where the act is correlated with what CDT holds fixed — reference-class and compulsion points, and the shared seed. The content of a CDT is the choice of the fixed algebra $\mathcal{E}_0$, exactly the pre-action subalgebra that v2.1 excised (change log item 22) [derived].
5. **v2's Definition 2 types $\mathrm{cf}_s$ as a belief (a single probability–desirability pair) and norms it as a choice rule (success only, no calibration)**; the doubled action set of the Dutch book forces the belief reading, because a choice-rule $\mathrm{cf}$ that prices in-act bets by $P_s(\cdot\mid a)$ and world-consequences by $P^a_s$ is not a probability on $\mathcal{E}$ [derived]. The author's constraint is best read as coherence for that single measure, hence as denying the choice-rule reading the status of a theory of counterfactuals — the exchange's suggestion, endorsed with the reason made explicit.
6. **The author's correction to the exchange holds and is checkable on the tree**: the tagged interventionist learner two-boxes exactly under Definition 6 (its assumption is physically right; policy value falls) and one-boxes exactly under Definition 6′ (its assumption is physically wrong; policy value rises) [checked]. What this says about the ideal deterministic agent: the "shadow of predecessors' exploration" is not a tremble rate but a *run semantics* — whether the environment reads the trembles — and Definition 10 must carry Q12's choice explicitly.
7. **Recommendation** (§7): define CDT in v3 as *interventional supposition under a causal structure observationally consistent with $P_s$* (an epistemology in Definition 19's sense, not a calibration), with Theorem 3 as its collapse theorem and Corollary 3.1 as the list of exactly where it parts from EDT — null acts, non-recorded points, the shared seed, and non-temporal or latent structures. The contest then reduces to four modelling parameters, and that is a relocation of the dispute with a dissolution on the recorded-point class.

## 2. Claim 9.1, proved in scope and refuted out of it (question b)

The boundaries note §9.2 stated: at a recorded point Lemma 3 gives $m \perp (\ell, k)$ under $P_s$, so any causal model observationally consistent with $P_s$ has $P(k\mid do(m)) = P(k\mid m)$ for every positive-probability $m$ and every graph prior. Here is what is actually true.

**Setting.** $\mathcal{E}$ finite, generated by coordinates $V = V_0 \sqcup \{m\} \sqcup V_1$: pre-query chance $V_0$ (the lesion $\ell$, the anti-lesion, a predictor's sample), the act coordinate $m$, post-query chance $V_1$ (cancer $k$). A *latent-free causal structure* for a state $s$ is a DAG $G$ on $V$ with $P_s$ Markov to $G$: $P_s(v) = \prod_i P_s(v_i \mid \mathrm{pa}_i(v))$. Its interventional law at $m = a$ is the truncated factorization $P^{G,a}(v_{-m}) := \prod_{i \neq m} P_s(v_i\mid \mathrm{pa}_i)\big|_{m=a}$ (Pearl's do-calculus; Meek–Glymour's reading of CDT [reconstructed from the CDT-foundations chats]). "Observationally consistent with $P_s$" is Markov compatibility; it is the only thing the record can test.

**Theorem 3 (collapse of interventional supposition at recorded points)** [derived]. Let $B$ record at $d$ (Definition 7) with $\nu(O_d) > 0$ and $O_d \in \langle V_0\rangle \cup \{\top\}$, under Definition 6, with $P_{s_d}$ strictly or limit observation-calibrated (Definitions 8, 10), and let $G$ be a latent-free causal structure for $s_d$. Then for every $a \in A_d^+$:
(i) $P^{G,a} = P_{s_d}(\cdot \mid a)$ on all of $\mathcal{E}$ **iff** $P_{s_d}(a \mid \mathrm{pa}_G(m)) = P_{s_d}(a)$ almost surely;
(ii) the identity holds whenever $\mathrm{pa}_G(m) \subseteq V_0$;
(iii) if moreover $m \perp \langle V_1\rangle$ under $P_{s_d}$ — post-query chance does not read the act, as under (S1) — the identity holds for **every** compatible $G$;
(iv) consequently, under supervenient payoffs $r = u \circ \lambda$, $V^{G,a}_{s_d}(a) = V_{s_d}(a)$ on $A_d^+$, and $T_{\mathrm{CDT}}$ with $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d} := \mathrm{cf}^G_{s_d}$ and $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ approve the same labels at $d$ whenever the causal argmax meets $A_d^+$.
*Proof.* Compatibility gives $P_s(v) = P_s(m \mid \mathrm{pa}_m)\prod_{i\neq m} P_s(v_i\mid \mathrm{pa}_i)$, so on $\{m = a\}$ the truncated product is $P^{G,a}(v_{-m}) = P_s(v_{-m}, a)/P_s(a \mid \mathrm{pa}_m(v))$, while $P_s(v_{-m}\mid a) = P_s(v_{-m}, a)/P_s(a)$; the two agree at every $v$ of positive probability iff $P_s(a\mid\mathrm{pa}_m(v)) = P_s(a)$, which is (i). For (ii): recording makes $m$ a function of the draw alone, and Definition 6 samples the draw independently of everything upstream, so $m \perp \langle V_0\rangle$ under $\nu(\cdot\mid O_d)$ for $O_d$ pre-query (Lemma 3, in S4's Lemma 3′ form — no hypothesis on post-query chance is needed); $\mathrm{pa}_G(m) \subseteq V_0$ then satisfies (i). For (iii): $m$ independent of every other coordinate satisfies (i) for any parent set. (iv): $V^{G,a}(a) = \mathbb{E}_{P^{G,a}}[u] = \mathbb{E}_{P_s}[u \mid a] = V_s(a)$. Limit calibration changes nothing, since Lemma 3 holds for $C^\varepsilon$ and the limit conditionals are R3. $\square$

**Check** [checked; script `claim91_dags.py`, §9]. On the (S1) recorded tree ($\rho = 0.1$, $\gamma = (0.05, 0.6)$, $q = \tfrac12$): 25 DAGs on $(\ell, m, k)$, 16 Markov-compatible, and the identity $P^{G}(k\mid do\,m) = P(k\mid m) = 0.105$ holds for all 16, reversed structures ($k \to m$, $k \to \ell$) included — clause (iii). With a direct effect $m \to k$ added ($\tau = 0.2$, still recorded): 7 compatible DAGs; the identity holds for both temporal ones and for the two reversed ones in which $m$ is a root, and **fails for the three in which $k$ is a parent of $m$** — there $P(k\mid do\,m) = P(k) = 0.205$ against $P(k\mid m{=}1) = 0.305$, $P(k\mid m{=}0) = 0.105$ — exactly clause (i)'s condition failing. So "every graph prior" was an overstatement: a prior that puts weight on reversed causation from a post-query variable to the act breaks the collapse at a recorded point whenever the act is correlated with that variable. In the double lesion it happens not to matter, because (S1) makes $m$ independent of everything (clause iii); in a problem where the act has real downstream effects it does.

**Corollary 3.1 (where interventional supposition parts from conditioning)** [derived]. (a) *Null acts*: for $a \notin A_d^+$ the truncated factorization needs $P_s(v_1 \mid v_0, m{=}a)$ on cells of probability zero, so $\mathrm{cf}^G(a)$ is whatever the parameter prior says on unvisited cells — the classical fill; EDT is silent there and R3 fills by trembles (Remark 3.9). (b) *Non-recorded points*: coverage failure (the bypass tree at $O_d = \top$, S2's reference class) and action-veridicality failure (the overwrite tree, S2's compulsion) both make $m$ a function of $\ell$'s forcing as well as the draw, $m \perp V_0$ fails, and the compatible temporal structure — with $\ell$ in the algebra it is unique — gives the population rate $0.105$ while conditioning gives S2's statistics ($0.600$ vs $0.0525$ at $q = 0$; script (C)); the backward-causal structures $\ell \leftarrow m$ reproduce conditioning, so a prior over structures is prior-relative here through them alone. (c) *Definition 6′*: the live draw shares its seed with an upstream simulation, Lemma 3 fails, and the classical Newcomb correlation $P(\mathrm{fill}\mid m)$ appears at a Definition-7-recorded point (S11): temporal $G$ two-boxes, $m \to$ sample one-boxes. (d) *Latent confounders*: a structure $U \to m$, $U \to k$, $m \to k$ can be observationally consistent with $m \perp k$ and have $P(k\mid do\,m{=}1) - P(k\mid do\,m{=}0) = -0.075$ (script (D): $P(k\mid m) = 0.38$ for both acts, $P(k\mid do\,m) = 0.500$ vs $0.575$) — the faithfulness violation; a prior giving such structures weight $w$ assigns the act an effect $-0.075\,w$ that no observational record removes. Admitting latents on the act is exactly the author's "not treating its own action … as an experimental intervention a priori" (exchange l. 47), so this is not a corner case for his first proposal but its defining feature.

**The doc's model-based paragraph, reconciled.** [two-lesions-doc-2026-09-18](../two-lesions-doc-2026-09-18.md) §6 says the untagged record "cannot discriminate the structure in which the lesion causes both smoking and cancer from the structure in which smoking causes cancer directly; the two are observationally equivalent while the lesion is unobserved." In v2's algebra the lesion is a coordinate and the OC-$\top$ state sees $\nu$ over $(\ell, m, k)$, so at the reference-class point the compatible temporal structure is unique and the prior-over-structures CDT restricted to temporal latent-free graphs is determinate (smokes); the doc's indeterminacy needs Definition 3's "neither kind of agent observes the state directly". Both are right in their algebras, and together they locate the prior's influence precisely: (b) through backward structures when the confounder is observed, through temporal structures too when it is latent; (d) always, if latents on the act are admitted; (a) always, at null acts. [derived]

**The draw as a coordinate.** On the overwrite tree with the draw $a$ recorded, the point records with respect to $A_d$ = draw-events and Theorem 3 applies to $a$: $P(k\mid a{=}1) = P(k\mid a{=}0) = 0.105$ at every label [checked]. The realized act $m$ is then a post-decision coordinate with parents $\{a, \ell\}$, the point does *not* record with respect to $m$, and the effect of $m$ on $k$ is identified by the instrumental-variable adjustment $[\mathbb{E}(k\mid a{=}1) - \mathbb{E}(k\mid a{=}0)]/[\mathbb{E}(m\mid a{=}1) - \mathbb{E}(m\mid a{=}0)] = 0$ under the exclusion restriction that $a$ reaches $k$ only through $m$ — a structural stipulation that "observationally consistent with $P_s$" does not supply (a structure $a \to k$ is compatible too). So rendering (ii) is rendering (iii) plus one exclusion axiom, and the compliance adjustment is where that axiom is spent [derived].

## 3. Non-responsiveness, the order-δ miss, and which clause fails (question c)

**The thesis.** The exchange (l. 55): what both proposals share, "and what I now think is the whole content of 'CDT' as a distinct theory, is that the causal model, once fixed, is not revised by information about the agent's own current act." In v2 terms: $\mathrm{cf}_s$ is *non-responsive on* $\mathcal{E}_0$ if $P^a_s(X) = P_s(X)$ for all $X \in \mathcal{E}_0$ and $a \in A_d$. Every interventional supposition $\mathrm{cf}^G$ is non-responsive on $\langle\mathrm{nondesc}_G(m)\rangle$ by the truncated factorization (Pearl: interventions leave non-descendants' marginals fixed). But so is the evidential conditional, on $\langle V_0\rangle$, at every recorded point under Definition 6 — that is Lemma 3′ (S4). So at recorded points non-responsiveness on the pre-query algebra is a *shared* property and separates nothing; it separates CDT from conditioning exactly where conditioning on the act moves a fixed-algebra event — the S2 homes and the shared seed. The thesis is therefore right about the *form* of every CDT and silent about its *content*, which is the choice of $\mathcal{E}_0$: classical CDT fixes the physical non-descendants (Omega's prediction included), the §9.3 "FDT learner" of the boundaries note fixes the logical state $\mathrm{Incon}$ but not the prediction, EDT fixes nothing. The exchange's own trio — "dispositional responsiveness to one's own logic gets Newcomb right and Troll Bridge wrong; non-responsiveness the other way round" (l. 59) — is two settings of $\mathcal{E}_0$, and the trio is resolved by a third: hold $\square\bot$ fixed, let the prediction move. That the same rule is called "a causal agent" $\mathfrak{B}$ in the doc (§7 Proposition 10) and an "FDT learner" in the boundaries note (§9.3) is this ambiguity in the naming [derived; naming claim reconstructed].

**Is every constraint-satisfying rendering "just EDT on a well-chosen conditioning variable"?** Yes, tautologously, at positive-probability acts: the author's constraint says $\mathrm{cf}_s(a) = P_s(\cdot\mid a)$ wherever the right side is defined, and the only freedom left is *which events are the acts* — Definition 3's $A_d \subseteq \mathcal{E}$, chosen by the problem. Act-events, draw-events and disposition-events ($\rho_d(a)$ of Definition 17) are the run's trichotomy (C1 Open 4), and rendering (ii) is the draw choice. And does CDT differ only by overriding defined conditionals? Yes, again by the constraint: two constraint-satisfying $\mathrm{cf}$'s agree on $A_d^+$ and differ only at null acts, so a CDT that differs on $A_d^+$ overrides a defined conditional there. Where the constraint is *not* imposed, the doc's §2 diagnosis applies — $D$ is ambiguous between own-act credence and the family of conditionals — and v2 already splits them (Remark 3.6, Definitions 9–10) [derived].

**The order-δ computation** [checked; script `lab_cdt_miss.py`, §1's parameters $\rho = \rho_A = 0.1$, $\gamma_0 = 0.05$, $\gamma_1 = 0.6$, $\delta = 0.05$]. The lab CDT's $do(m)$ gives the population rate $\rho\gamma_1 + (1-\rho)\gamma_0 = 0.105$ for both acts on both trees at OC-$\top$ (and among consulted runs, $0.10278$, at SSC on the bypass tree). The miss on abstaining is $\rho(\gamma_1 - \gamma_0) = 0.055$, $\delta$-independent — the same $O(1)$ number as the smoke-label evidential gap of the boundaries note §1 — because at $q = 1$ the only abstainers are anti-lesion-forced and cancer-free at rate $\gamma_0$. **Which clause fails?** Neither. Definition 8 constrains $P_{s_d}$ (clause 1) and $V_{s_d}$ (clause 2); at OC-$\top$ both hold with $P_{s_d} = \nu$ and $V_{s_d}(m{=}1) = \mathbb{E}[r\mid m{=}1]$. The lab CDT's $P_{s_d}(k\mid m{=}1) = 0.10528$ is correct; what says $0.105$ is $P^{m=1}_{s_d}(k)$, the *supposed* probability, and what CDT maximizes is $V^{m=1}_{s_d}(m{=}1)$, the *supposed* desirability — neither is in Definition 8's clauses. Remark 3.11 exempts $\mathrm{cf}_s$ from calibration, so the miss is not a calibration failure in v2 at all; the only v2 predicate that registers it is Definition 20's evidential criterion (agreement of $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d}(a)$ with $(P_{s_d}(\cdot\mid a), V_{s_d}(\cdot\wedge a))$), a *counterfactual theory*, or counterfactual calibration to an $O_d$-conditioned referent (S12, S24). The doc's Definition 1 has the same shape: it calibrates the act–outcome marginal $D$ and says nothing about a $do$-quantity, so the lab CDT is not miscalibrated by Definition 1 either. The exchange's "CDT's $do(\mathrm{smoke})$ … misses the actual cancer rate" is thus a true statement whose normative force depends entirely on the fork of §4 [derived].

## 4. The fork: belief or choice rule (question d)

**What Definition 2 commits to.** $\mathrm{cf}_s$ lives inside the subjective state, is total on non-$\bot$ events, and returns a *probability–desirability pair* under the averaging axiom — a Jeffrey–Bolker state under a supposition, Joyce's format (Remark 1.1). That is the type of a belief: a single measure on $\mathcal{E}$ answering "what is the case if $a$" for every event. Its *norms*, however, are the choice rule's: success only, no calibration (Remark 3.11), agreement with conditioning optional (Definition 20 is one theory among many; S24). So v2 has the belief reading's type with the choice-rule reading's freedom, and Remark 3.11's refusal means precisely: $\mathrm{cf}_s$ is belief-shaped but not answerable to the tree, because either tree-intrinsic referent would condemn classical CDT by fiat (Remark 3.11, Q6). The two answers to "what happens if I smoke" — $P_s(\cdot\mid\mathrm{smoke})$ and $P^{\mathrm{smoke}}_s$ — coexist in every v2 state, and Definition 8 inspects only the first [derived].

**The doc's §2 diagnosis is the same fork one level down.** [two-lesions-doc-2026-09-18](../two-lesions-doc-2026-09-18.md) §2 finds the presentation's $D$ "ambiguous in what D is": the agent's credence about its own acts, or the family of conditionals $D(\cdot\mid a)$ it consults; the basic argument "proves too much" because strict marginal calibration pins the first and so empties the second at any determinate solution. That is Remark 3.6's forced self-knowledge and Proposition 7's rigidity, and v2's answer is the doc's amendment: free the label (Definition 9) or take the tremble limit (Definition 10) while keeping the conditionals. The present fork adds a *third* object beside $P_s(a)$ and $P_s(\cdot\mid a)$ — $P^a_s$ — and asks the same question of it: is it a credence (then calibrate or book it) or a rule (then neither applies). The doc's Definition 1, like Definition 8, never reaches the third object [derived].

**What the Dutch book adds (S12).** The book prices the gap between a $\mathrm{cf}$ and conditioning at a positive act "whatever produced the gap"; which referent has a gap is fixed by run semantics and recording. Under Definition 6 at F3′ points the forcing referent equals conditioning (S9), so a $\mathrm{cf}$ calibrated to it — "classical CDT by construal, A11" (sl-critique C1) — is not bookable, while the deviation referent is; under the shared seed the roles reverse. Read through the fork: S12 is the statement that the book is the *belief* reading's test, and that under Definition 6 at recorded points the belief reading of classical CDT passes it, because Theorem 3 gives it nothing to disagree with conditioning about. Where it disagrees (Corollary 3.1 (b)–(d)), the book bites the belief reading and the choice-rule reading claims immunity by pricing the bet with $P_s$ instead [derived].

**Why the choice-rule reading does not survive the doubled action set.** P12's book (ll. 41–60) folds the second bet into the act: the action set at $d'$ is doubled to $\{a, a'\}$ with $a'$ = "$a$ and sell $B$". In v2 this is a decision point whose actions are compound events, and Definition 17's CDT evaluates $V^{a'}_{s}(a')$ — the supposed desirability of the *whole* compound act under the single measure $P^{a'}_s$. A choice-rule $\mathrm{cf}$ that wanted to price the bet's payoff by $P_s(\mathrm{fill}\mid a')$ while pricing the box by $P^{a'}_s(\mathrm{fill})$ would assign the one event $\mathrm{fill}$ two probabilities inside one supposition: not a probability on $\mathcal{E}$, hence not a value in Definition 2's codomain. So once bets can be bundled with acts — and Definition 3 lets any disjoint non-$\bot$ events be actions — the type forces one measure per supposition, and that is the belief reading. The choice-rule reading is coherent only for an agent that never faces a compound act, i.e. only on problems where the book cannot be posed; it is immune to the book by not being a theory of what compound acts bring about [derived].

**The author's constraint, read.** "To avoid Dutch books, counterfactual expectations must equal conditional probabilities whenever the latter are well-defined" (exchange l. 47) is then a coherence constraint on the one measure that both chooses and prices, binding on $A_d^+$ and silent elsewhere — the author's own scope in P12 ll. 124–138 ("if the agent really does know exactly what it will do, then … the argument really does collapse to saying its evidential expectations should equal its counterfactual expectations for that one action") and P09 l. 35. The exchange's suggestion (l. 61) that the constraint "is best read as denying that the second thing counts as a theory of counterfactuals at all" is right, and the reason is the previous paragraph: the second thing is not a supposition function. Two cautions carry over from the run: the book has force in P12's *advice* reading at mixed labels and only an $o(\varepsilon)$ residue at deterministic fixed points (S12; AlexMennen, P12 ll. 393–430), and a strictly calibrated bet point knows the continuation and declines the book as written (S12), so the constraint is a constraint on evaluators, not a sure loss for any calibrated agent [reconstructed from S12].

**The doc's $\mathfrak{B}$ sits exactly on the fork.** [two-lesions-doc-2026-09-18](../two-lesions-doc-2026-09-18.md) §7 Proposition 10: $\mathfrak{B}$ "carr[ies] the same credence function $P$" — T-respecting, so under the hypothesis $\square(\mathrm{Cross}_{\mathfrak{B}} \to \square\bot)$ its $P(\square\bot\mid\mathrm{Cross}_{\mathfrak{B}})$ would be $1$ — and crosses iff $10 - 20\,P_0(\square\bot) > 0$ with $P_0$ "formed without regard to its own act". Two answers to "what if I cross": the conditional ($1$, hypothetically) and $P_0$ (small). $\mathfrak{B}$ violates the author's constraint *dispositionally*, in a hypothetical that, by Proposition 10, its violation keeps from obtaining ($T \nvdash \mathrm{Cross}_{\mathfrak{B}} \to \square\bot$). This is the boundaries note §9.3's "counterfactual and conditional never actually disagree", and it is why no bookie can price $\mathfrak{B}$'s gap: the act at which the gap would be priced is one whose conditional is never actually $1$. So the belief reading's test (the book) and the choice-rule reading's privilege (immunity) coincide on Troll Bridge for a reason the double lesion does not have — the disagreement is self-preventing [derived; the doc's $\mathfrak{B}$ as read].

## 5. Determinism, inheritance, and the author's correction (question e)

**The exchange's account** (ll. 39, 41): a deterministic agent has no exogenous variation, so the ideal $\alpha_C$'s causal knowledge "has to be inherited from its bounded predecessors' randomization"; this "fails exactly where the environment responds to the current procedure rather than to the act, which is Newcomb". **The author's correction** (ll. 71–73): learning from tagged exploration alone "works in cases where Omega cannot predict the exploration, so that they're true interventions, but it doesn't work when Omega predicts those well, in which case the agent would learn an EDT-like picture. That is: the agent does poorly precisely when the interventionist assumption fits reality well."

**Checked on the tree** [checked; script `newcomb_semantics.py`, $L = 10$, $S = 1$, $p = 0.9$, tagged exploration $\eta = 0.05$]. Under Definition 6 (Omega samples the label): $P(\mathrm{fill}\mid \text{explore one}) = P(\mathrm{fill}\mid\text{explore two})$ at every label ($0.12$, $0.5$, $0.88$ at $q = 0, \tfrac12, 1$), so the tagged interventionist learns "no effect" — true as physics — two-boxes, and the policy value falls from $8.825$ at $q = 1$ to $2.175$ at $q = 0$. Under Definition 6′ (Omega reads the live draw): $P(\mathrm{fill}\mid\text{explore one}) = 0.9$, $P(\mathrm{fill}\mid\text{explore two}) = 0.1$ at every label, the learner reads its exploration as an intervention that moved the box — false as physics, Omega predicted it — one-boxes, and wins. The author's correction is confirmed, and its formal content is Q12: the tagged learner's verdict is a function of whether the environment reads the draw, not of anything about the agent. The exchange's account had the failure at "the environment responds to the procedure": under Definition 6 the environment does respond to the procedure (the label), and the tagged act-level data cannot see it — but *policy-level* variation can (Proposition 4's probing; the exchange's own first message, l. 13, the policy as an instrument), which is UDT's route, not CDT's. So the corrected account: act-level interventionism is right about acts and blind to labels under Definition 6, and wrong about acts but decision-correct under 6′; the inheritance story fails not because predecessors could not vary the right thing but because what they varied is read differently by the two run semantics [derived].

**Three implications for the ideal deterministic agent.** (1) *Definition 10's trembles are the shadow of exploration only with the run semantics named.* The doc's §3 amendment — "the ideal agent α is … the limit of a sequence of bounded agents … each of which assigns positive probability to every act … the conditionals $D(\cdot\mid a)$ that the ideal agent consults are the limits" — is Definition 10 in the doc's language, and the doc's §4 Corollary ("the limit of the fixed points is not the fixed point of the limit") is the v2 distinction between Definition 10 (fix the label, $\varepsilon \to 0$: the doc's *first reading*) and Remark 3.12's tremble-consistency along the sequence (the doc's *second reading*). Neither says whether the trembles are read by the environment; under Definition 6 the tremble limit is forcing and two-boxes, under 6′ it is the classical evidential conditional. v3 should index limit calibration by Q12's semantics, or the "shadow" is ambiguous at exactly the point Newcomb turns on [derived]. (2) *$\mathrm{cf}_s$ inherited as a parameter* is Definition 11's pattern — equip the point with a causal structure $G$ as it equips it with a prior state — and Theorem 3 says which part of the parameter the point's own statistics pin (the positive-act conditionals at recorded points) and which part is genuinely inherited (null-act cells; the fixed algebra; the structure at non-recorded points). "Inherited" and "left free by observational consistency" are the same set [derived]. (3) *A set of limits where the path matters*: the doc's Propositions 4–5 (recency learner $\to 0$ when $\eta_t/\delta_t \to 0$, cumulative learner $\to \hat p(\delta) \to 1$) and the boundaries note §1's two fixed points make the ideal object a set indexed by the learner class — the ratio of the agent's exploration to the environment's forcing, and the memory of the statistics — as §9.1 of the boundaries note and the doc's §9 moral two both conclude. v2 already has the static stand-ins: the $\exists/\forall$ of Definitions 15–16 as selection policies (Remark 5.2), credal calibration (Appendix B item 5), E-stability (Q5). What the double lesion adds is that the *environment's* vanishing parameter $\delta$ must enter the limit alongside the procedure's $\varepsilon$ — an abstract problem $\Sigma_\delta$ with a joint limit — which Definition 10 does not currently express [derived].

## 6. The two replacements the exchange proposed, against the doc's text

**Definition 4 → lab CDT.** The doc's Definition 4 is rendering (i); its Proposition 7 is a correct theorem and its Corollary transfers Propositions 1–6 to the causal learner verbatim. Replacing it by the lab CDT (rendering iv) changes the doc's conclusions as follows. §6's "on no construal does the causal learner outperform the evidential learner in the calibrated problem, except by being handed in advance what the calibration requirement forbids it to be handed" becomes false as stated — the lab CDT smokes at the untagged $\delta$-forcing fixed point where the untagged evidential learner abstains, and is right — unless "handed" is extended to cover "learned in a lab the field record did not contain", which is the exchange's restatement "non-responsiveness buys nothing that structural knowledge doesn't buy the responsive agent" (l. 63). That restatement is correct, and the exchange's own first message supplies the mechanism: a responsive agent with the override hypothesis in its model class identifies the forcing rates from policy variation alone (l. 13) and smokes calibrated. §9 moral three ("Smoking Lesion, made legitimate, is no longer an argument between the two decision theories") survives in the form: it is an argument between *knowledge states*, not decision rules — the lab CDT versus the untagged EDT is unfair in the calibration requirement's own sense, because the lab is a situation $S'$ the evidential agent was not placed in. Definition 1 can host this (a situation is a function from agents to measures over histories; the lab-then-field compound is such a function), so the replacement is representable in the doc's own terms; what it costs is Proposition 7 and the Corollary, which must be restated as "the act-level causal learner coincides with the evidential learner; the lab CDT does not, and differs by $O(\delta)$ on the act it takes and $O(1)$ on the act it forgoes" [derived; §3's numbers].

**Proposition 10 → non-responsiveness.** Here the exchange's restatement is already the doc's text. Proposition 10 defines $\mathfrak{B}$'s $P_0(\square\bot)$ as the credence "formed without regard to its own act — the credence of an agent whose causal model has $\square\bot$ as an exogenous root and its act as an intervention upon that model", and the remark after Lemma 8 says "Remove the responsiveness and the proof fails"; the remark after Proposition 10 gives the contrapositive: "The trap is … for any agent whose credence in the lesion answers to a theorem linking act to lesion." So "in Troll Bridge, non-responsiveness is the only thing that helps" is the doc's Proposition 10 plus its fragility remark, verbatim in substance. The addition "and it costs dispositional T-respect" (l. 63) needs one precision: $\mathfrak{B}$'s *credences* are T-respecting by stipulation ("the same credence function $P$"); what $\mathfrak{B}$ lacks is the (β)-type responsiveness of its *rule* to the conditional $P(\square\bot\mid\mathrm{Cross})$ — the choice-rule reading of §4. The cost is therefore dispositional non-compliance with the author's constraint, not with T-respect, and §4 explains why it is never priced. One further thing the doc's text settles that the exchange left open: $\mathfrak{B}$'s rule on $\mathrm{TB}(\theta)$ is exactly $\mathrm{CDT}_{G}$ with the temporal structure $\mathrm{bot} \to m$ (cross iff $\theta < \tfrac12$; §1), so the doc's "causal agent" is the lab CDT of the exchange with $\mathcal{E}_0 = \langle\square\bot\rangle$, and the boundaries note's "FDT learner" is the same object under a name chosen for its Newcomb behaviour, which the bridge does not test [derived].

## 7. Recommendation for v3 (question f)

**Definition 24 (causal structure; interventional supposition).** Let $\mathcal{E}$ be generated by coordinates $V$ with a distinguished act coordinate $m$, and $s$ a subjective state. A *causal structure for $s$* is a DAG $G$ on $V$, optionally extended by latent coordinates $U$, with Markov kernels whose $V$-marginal is $P_s$. Its *interventional supposition* of $a = \{m = a\}$ is $\mathrm{cf}^G_s(a) := (P^{G,a}, V^{G,a})$, where $P^{G,a}$ is the truncated factorization at $m = a$ and, for supervenient payoffs $r = u\circ\lambda$, $V^{G,a}(X) := \mathbb{E}_{P^{G,a}}[u\mid X]$ on $P^{G,a}$-non-null $X$ (non-supervenient payoffs: add the payoff coordinate to $V$, Remark 3.5). Success holds by construction. The *fixed algebra* of $G$ is $\mathcal{E}_0^G := \langle \mathrm{nondesc}_G(m)\rangle$, and $\mathrm{cf}^G_s$ is *non-responsive* on it: $P^{G,a}\restriction\mathcal{E}_0^G = P_s\restriction\mathcal{E}_0^G$ for every $a$.

**Definition 25 (observational causal consistency).** A decision theory $T_{\mathrm{OCC}}(C, B)$ holds iff at every reachable queried $d$ there is a probability $\pi_d$ over causal structures for $s_d$ with $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d} = \int \mathrm{cf}^G_{s_d}\, d\pi_d(G)$ (pairs mixed as Jeffrey states: $P = \int P^{G,a}d\pi$, $V(X) = \int P^{G,a}(X)V^{G,a}(X)d\pi / P(X)$). It consults the reachable states only, never $\mu$, $\nu$ or payoffs, so it is an *epistemology* (Definition 19) constraining $\mathrm{cf}_s$ relative to $P_s$ — not counterfactual calibration (Remark 3.11), and it does not beg Q6: the tree's own mechanism graph is Markov-compatible with every calibrated $P_{s_d}$ at pre-query observations, so classical CDT's supposition is always admissible. It is Stern's mixture over causal models / the Lewis–Skyrms K-partition with causal models as dependency hypotheses [reconstructed from the CDT-foundations chats], typed onto v2.

**Definition 26 (CDT in the learning setting).** $\mathrm{CDT}_\pi(d) := \mathrm{Unif}\arg\max_{a\in A_d} V^{\pi,a}_{s_d}(a)$ — Definition 17's causal choice with $\mathrm{cf}_{s_d} := \int\mathrm{cf}^G d\pi_d$ — and $T_{\mathrm{CDT}_\pi}$ as in Definition 18. Two named cases: the *lab CDT*, $\pi_d = \delta_{G^\ast}$ with $G^\ast$ the mechanism graph of $B$ (learned by interventional access, stipulated statically as a parameter of the point in Definition 11's pattern); and the *prior-over-structures CDT*, $\pi_d$ the posterior of a prior over structures conditioned on $P_{s_d}$. A rendering is *temporal* if $\pi_d$ is supported on latent-free $G$ with $\mathrm{pa}_G(m) \subseteq V_0$.

**Theorem 3** as in §2, with **Corollary 3.1**: $T_{\mathrm{CDT}_\pi}$ and $T_{\mathrm{EDT}}$ approve the same labels on $A_d^+$ at every point that records under Definition 6, for every temporal $\pi$ (and for every $\pi$ when the act is independent of everything, as under (S1)); they part (a) at null acts, where $\mathrm{CDT}_\pi$ is filled by the parameter prior and $\mathrm{EDT}$ by R3 or not at all; (b) at non-recorded points — S2's coverage failure and action-veridicality failure, where the compatible temporal structure gives the population statistic and conditioning the agent's; (c) under Definition 6′; (d) for non-temporal or latent-bearing $\pi$. Verdicts: Table of §1, rows (iii)–(iv).

**What the contest reduces to.** Four modelling parameters, each already in v2 or one step from it: which events are the actions ($A_d$: act, draw or disposition — the exchange's "which variable do you condition on", the boundaries note's channel 2 and the trichotomy; these are one question in two vocabularies); the run semantics (Definition 6 or 6′, Q12 — the axis the author's correction turns on and the exchange's reduction omits); the fixed algebra $\mathcal{E}_0$ (what the supposition holds still — the content of non-responsiveness, needing the pre-action subalgebra back in a targeted role); and the null-act fill (prior cells or tremble limits). On the recorded-point class under Definition 6 the contest is *dissolved* — Theorem 3 leaves it nothing to be about, which is S1's headline and P05's "basically one notion of counterfactual" (l. 104) with its parameters named (S9). Off that class it is *relocated*, not dissolved: to a choice of $A_d$ that Definition 3 leaves to the problem (Q1's relativity), to a semantics that Definition 6 fixes and Q12 questions, and to an $\mathcal{E}_0$ that no calibration pins (Remark 3.11). That is the honest statement: CDT versus EDT is not a theorem-shaped dispute in v3 but a specification-shaped one, decidable per problem once the four parameters are written down, and the framework's contribution is to make writing them down compulsory [derived].

**Open items, with diagnoses** (failure to prove is recorded, not read as "probably false"). (1) *Finite-sample Occam.* Whether a prior-over-structures CDT whose class contains both an unconfounded structure and the latent-cancellation structure of Corollary 3.1(d) converges to the collapse as the record grows: the unconfounded structure reproduces $m \perp k$ without fine-tuning and the confounded one only on a lower-dimensional parameter set, so the marginal likelihood should favour the former at a BIC-like rate — but the confounded model is singular (the cancellation set is a positive-dimensional submanifold), and I did not compute the rate. Direction of evidence: for the collapse, when an unconfounded structure is in the class; against it, when the class is confounded-only, where the posterior is the prior restricted to the observationally equivalent set [guess, with the argument stated]. (2) *SSC grades.* Theorem 3 is stated for observation calibration; S1 says the SSC clauses of the recorded-point coincidence need $H^\ast$ (every $d$-node on an $O_d$-run). I expect Theorem 3 to transfer to per-run SSC under $H^\ast$ by Proposition 3's bridge and to fail without it, as S1's mugging witness fails, but did not write the proof [derived for the expectation; not checked]. (3) *Definition 6′.* No shared-seed version of Theorem 3 exists here; Corollary 3.1(c) records only that its hypothesis fails. The natural conjecture is that under 6′ the collapse holds iff the act is independent of its $G$-parents *given the seed*, which would put the predictor's sample into $\mathcal{E}_0$ and make classical CDT's two-boxing the temporal verdict there — consistent with S11/S12 but unproved [guess]. (4) *Non-supervenient payoffs.* Definition 24's desirability clause assumed $r = u \circ \lambda$; with leaf-attached payoffs the supposed desirability must be read off the joint world–payoff law (Remark 3.5), and whether Theorem 3(iv) survives without adding the payoff coordinate to $V$ is unchecked.

**Two things not to do.** Do not define learning-CDT as rendering (i) and then report its coincidence with EDT as a finding (the doc's Proposition 7 is fine as a lemma about act-level estimation, not as the definition of CDT). Do not make Theorem 3's collapse a *calibration* condition on $\mathrm{cf}_s$ — that is the question-begging Remark 3.11 warns of; Definition 25 keeps it subjective, and the collapse is then a theorem about what observational consistency already forces.
