**Headline, with register.** A learner that evaluates whole policies by the marginal formula cf(U | do π) = Σ_w P(w) E(π, w) — with W (the exogenous set) decided by a policy-invariance test on observables, P(w) pooled over all episodes regardless of policy, E learned per (policy, w) cell, and proofs about the composite agent-plus-environment structurally inert — smokes in the double lesion on both trees and for every grip δ, one-boxes against every Omega that reads the policy (perfect, fallible r > 1/2 + K/2M, label-probe, shared-seed), two-boxes against an Omega whose box is policy-invariant, refuses XOR blackmail, pays the counterfactual mugging, crosses every troll bridge whose trigger is a fact about the environment, and takes the 10 [derived; all verdicts checked by simulation, 5 seeds each]. It mis-partitions exactly when the environment responds to a feature of the agent that is not a coordinate of the policy — an exploration flag, the agent's own estimate, the history of past policies at a slower timescale — and there its verdict is an artifact of its exploration schedule [checked for the flag-reading Omega and the slow Omega]. Relocation to the policy level dissolves the double lesion, Newcomb, XOR and the mugging by itself — policy-level EDT gets the same verdicts — but it does *not* dissolve Troll Bridge: the Löbian theorem asserts P(Incon | π = cross) = 1 for a w that is genuinely exogenous, and only the non-responsiveness clause resists it [derived; this is the one place the "FDT" in the name is load-bearing].

## 1. Specification (question a)

**1.1 Policy.** Fix the iterated setting: rounds t = 1, 2, …; in each round the agent faces one concrete problem B (Definition 5) drawn from a fixed situation. A *policy* is a Definition-4 procedure restricted to the decision-points the learner can occupy and quotiented by observation-functionality (Remark 4.4: points with equal O_d carry equal (P, V), and the learner's own state is common to all its points in a round), so a policy is a map π : O → Δ(A_o) from observations to mixed actions — one label per point, applied at *every* node carrying that point (Definition 6: each node redraws independently from the label, so Omega's simulation node and the live node draw i.i.d. from the same π(o)). The learner *varies* π by changing a label for a whole round: this is exactly the all-occurrence point-deviation C[d ↦ m] of Definition 22, never Theorem 1's single-instance forcing. In one-point problems a policy is a number q ∈ [0, 1]; the simulations use the grid Π = {0, 0.1, …, 1}. [derived]

**1.2 State.** (i) The policy set Π. (ii) A set V of observed per-round variables (realized acts, revealed chance such as the coin or the box, revealed outcomes such as cancer or the disaster), each with finite range, plus the payoff. (iii) A partition V = W ⊔ R of observed variables into *exogenous* and *responsive*, recomputed from data (1.5). (iv) A belief P over W-configurations, and for *latent* exogenous variables (Incon(PA)) a prior P₀ that the data can move only through the likelihood E. (v) A response function E(π, w) ∈ Δ(payoffs × R-values), stored as a histogram per cell (π, w). (vi) Admitted proofs: sentences of the forms "∀w E(π, w) = …" and "P(w) = …". The simulation's exploration state is the posterior itself (1.4). [derived; implemented]

**1.3 Decision rule.** cf(U | do π) := Σ_w P(w) · E_{E(π,w)}[U], maximized over Π. On visited policies with a correct partition this equals E[U | π_t = π], the policy-conditional expectation — so the FDT property (counterfactual = conditional wherever the latter is defined; post 12 ll. 424–428) holds *by construction* on the visited set, and the formula differs from policy-level EDT only (a) at unvisited policies (extrapolation through E), (b) at latent W, and (c) wherever something asserts P(w | π) ≠ P(w) for a w ∈ W. Case (c) is the whole of Troll Bridge (§4). [derived]

**1.4 Exploration rule.** VOI under a discount γ near 1: choose π_t to maximize discounted total payoff including the information value of resolving uncertainty about E and about the partition (post 13 ll. 81–83: PCDT "naturally chooses some actions which are suboptimal in the short term, due to the long-term value of information"; the convergence claim at l. 687). The simulation uses *Thompson sampling* over (P, E) — sample a P from the Dirichlet posterior over W-configurations and an E from the per-cell Dirichlet posteriors, adopt the argmax — as a stand-in: posterior sampling is probability-matching on being optimal, explores in proportion to residual disagreement, and vanishes as the posterior concentrates. It is not a discounted dynamic program, and the conjecture in §6 is stated for the VOI rule proper. The learner also computes a *greedy* policy (argmax of posterior means) so that environments which read whether the agent is exploring — a feature of the agent, not of π — can be modelled (§2.3). [implemented; the substitution is flagged]

**1.5 Update rules.** (a) Every observation of a W-variable updates P(w) as a *pooled* frequency over all rounds, whatever policy produced them: P(w) is never indexed by π. (b) Every (π_t, w_t, outcome) updates the cell E(π_t, w_t). (c) The partition: for each observed variable v not marked as an act, compare the Dirichlet-multinomial marginal likelihood of "one law for v pooled over policies" against "one law per visited policy"; v ∈ W iff the pooled model wins. A variable moves W → R when its law is found to vary with the policy and R → W when the pooled model regains the lead (the penalty for the per-policy model grows like |Π| log n, so under true invariance W is recovered with probability → 1; under true responsiveness the per-policy model wins linearly in n). Realized acts are always in R. (d) Admitted proofs enter as constraints on E or on P(w) (in the simulation, as the environment's stated payoff rule where it is stated). [derived; implemented]

**1.6 Non-responsiveness clause, precisely.** A theorem about the *composite* — any sentence of the form "π_t = π → φ(w)" or "□(π_t = π → φ(w))", i.e. a constraint on the joint of (π, w) derived from the agent's own code — constrains P(w | π), a quantity the formula of 1.3 never forms; it is therefore inert for the decision, whatever its provenance. A theorem about E ("∀w, E(cross, w) = −10": the bridge is out) or about P(w) ("P(bot) = θ") is admitted. This is §9.3's second consequence carried over with π for a: *the exogeneity of the policy is a policy, not a belief* — the agent does not believe its policy is uncorrelated with w; it declines to treat its policy as evidence about w. It is Definition 6 (the draw "independently of everything so far") adopted by the agent for channel 3 of [smoking-lesion-exploration-and-boundaries](../smoking-lesion-exploration-and-boundaries.md) §4, where Gödel makes it unavailable as a belief. [derived]

**1.7 Relation to the doc's 𝔅 and to §9.3.** The doc's Proposition 10 agent 𝔅 — crosses iff 10 − 20·P₀(□⊥) > 0, P₀ "the credence of an agent whose causal model has □⊥ as an exogenous root and its act as an intervention upon that model" — **is the act-level, one-shot, zero-data, latent-W instance of this learner** [derived]: W = {□⊥}, P = P₀, E supplied entirely by the troll's stated rule (a proof about E), Π = {cross, stay}. What the learner adds: E learned from cells instead of stipulated; P(w) for *observable* w decided by the invariance test instead of by fiat; the policy level. The exchange's Docs-Claude worry about 𝔅 — "holding P(□⊥) fixed against a theorem tying its act to □⊥ … isn't a belief experience could deliver … I don't see a learning path to it" — is answered halfway: for observable w the learning path is the partition test (exogeneity is *earned* from variation); for latent w it remains a design clause (1.6), and the doc's Remark after Proposition 10 says why no experience can supply it ("an agent causal about the bridge but evidential about itself … is afflicted like 𝔄"). §9.3's act-level learner is the one-point case of this learner with the *tag* doing the work the policy index does here: a tagged draw is a cell key; a policy is a cell key. [derived]

**2.1 Double lesion.** Take the doc's Definition 2 with its explicit forms (§3): P_p(smoke) = ε_Lδ + κp, P_p(cancer) = ε_L, κ = 1 − δ(ε_L + ε_A). The policy value is V(p) = π(ε_Lδ + κp) − Cε_L — *affine and increasing in p for every δ ∈ (0, 1]*, slope πκ > 0, unique maximizer p = 1, and as δ → 0 it converges uniformly to πp − Cε_L [derived; exact values printed in §8]. The learner's cf(p) estimates V(p) from the cells at p; both trees of [smoking-lesion-exploration-and-boundaries](../smoking-lesion-exploration-and-boundaries.md) §1 have the same (m, k) law, so the learner cannot tell them apart and does not need to — the bypass/overwrite distinction matters for conditioning on the realized act m at an SSC grade, and the learner conditions on the policy. Exposing the draw as an extra observable changes nothing (it is found responsive and unused). Concentration is slow because the slope (≈ 1 per unit q) is small against the payoff noise (cancer costs 100 at rate 0.2): greedy is correct from round 2000 but the fraction of rounds at q ≥ 0.9 rises 0.28 → 0.55 → 0.79 → 0.91 at t = 2000, 5000, 10000, 20000 (§8 item 2) — grid resolution, not verdict.

*Against the doc's §4–§5.* The doc's object is the act-conditional penalty Δ(p) = C[P_p(cancer | smoke) − P_p(cancer | abstain)], which has the singular limit of Proposition 3 and the order-of-limits Corollary; my reproduction of its numbers agrees (Δ(0) = 80.16, Δ(0.5) = 0.40, Δ(1) = 20.04 at δ = 0.01; recency learner → 0.0035 from every start; cumulative learner → 0.954 = p̂ from 0.5 and 0.95 and → 0 from 0.05 < p̌ ≈ 0.158; at δ = 0.1 > δ* everything → 0) [checked]. The policy-level learner never forms Δ(p).

**Is it a recency or a cumulative learner? Neither: it is policy-indexed.** Each cell pools every round produced by *that* policy, which is exact because the round law is a fixed function of the policy (the doc's own remark that P_p is affine in p is what makes within-policy pooling a homogeneous reference class), and it forgets nothing across policies because it never mixes them. The doc's memory distinction concerns the reference class of the act-conditional; the learner's reference class is the policy itself, so **its double-lesion verdict is memory-independent and grip-independent** [derived; checked at δ = 0.01 and 0.1].

**Which reading of the amendment does it implement?** Both, because for V(p) the limits commute: the fixed point of the limit (argmax of πp − Cε_L) and the limit of the fixed points (argmax of V_δ for each δ) are the same p = 1. The Corollary's non-commutation is a property of act-conditioning without a tag; the doc's Proposition 6 removes it with a per-episode tag, the policy relocation removes it with a per-round policy index, and the exchange's Docs-Claude already had the mechanism ("it has learned the tag as a parameter rather than observing it as a label"). [derived]

*Cost relative to the tag, and the act-level cousin.* The policy learner needs at least one visited round per candidate policy. Its untagged act-level cousin — the doc's Definition 4, which Proposition 7 identifies with untagged EDT — is a cumulative learner that lands at p̂ ≈ 0.79–0.94 for δ = 0.01 and at 0 for δ = 0.1 in my bandit implementation [checked, §8 item 1], exactly the doc's Proposition 5 pattern; so the policy relocation, not the decision formula, is what separates smoke from abstain here. Where the policy learner *does* inherit the cumulative learner's defect is non-stationarity (§2.4, §5.3): a within-policy cell pooled across an environment that has changed is a heterogeneous reference class.

**2.2 Newcomb.** With M = 1000, K = 1 and an Omega whose fill probability is f(π), cf(q) = q·M·f(q) + (1−q)(M·f(q) + K) = M·f(q) + (1−q)K. Against a *label-probe* (f(q) = q): cf = Mq + (1−q)K, maximized at q = 1 — the policy-level learner one-boxes against the very Omega against which post 04's LIEDT two-boxes, because LIEDT holds its label fixed while deliberating over acts and the policy learner sets the label [derived; checked]. Against a *threshold reader* (f = r if q > ½, 1−r if q < ½): every q > ½ fills the box with probability r, so cf is maximized at the smallest grid point above ½ — the learner sits at q = 0.6 and collects M·r + 0.4K, *more* than pure one-boxing; this is correct optimization against a predictor that reads a coarse feature of the policy, and a first instance of §3's point that the learner learns the predictor's level sets and exploits them [checked; payoff 1000.3 vs 1000]. One-box iff r > ½ + K/2M = 0.5005; at r = 0.4 it two-boxes. Against a *shared-seed* Omega (b = realized act, P04's "fully sharing predictor", Definition 6′) q = 1. **"Act-only fallible Omega" — my construal (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED as a reading of the brief):** an Omega whose prediction is correlated with the agent's act only through a channel the policy does not control — a cached guess, a population trait — so that P(b | π) is constant in π. The partition test puts b in W, cf(q) = M·P(b) + (1−q)K, and the learner two-boxes; this is the case in which FDT and CDT agree because Omega is not, in fact, predicting the policy [derived; checked, W = {b} in 5/5].

**2.3 Omega reading only an exploration flag — the first mis-partition.** Model: Omega reads the agent's act on rounds the agent flags as exploratory (adopted policy ≠ greedy) and fills at base rate β₀ otherwise. The flag is a feature of the *learner's relation to its policy*, not of the policy: no coordinate of π carries it. Dynamics [checked]: whenever greedy = two-box, flagged one-box rounds pay M; the learner switches greedy to one-box; now greedy rounds pay β₀M and flagged two-box rounds pay K, so E(two-box) is estimated from flagged rounds only and sinks toward K while E(one-box) sits at ≈ β₀M > K — the learner stays at one-box, forgoing K per greedy round. At β₀ = 0 the two estimates chase each other and the policy oscillates (greedy 0.8–1.0, frac at q ≥ 0.9 ≈ 0.47). Its realized payoff (≈ 740 at β₀ = 0.5) nevertheless *exceeds* deliberate two-boxing (≈ 501) because the environment rewards flagged one-boxing and Thompson sampling keeps flagging — the verdict is an artifact of the exploration schedule, and neither "one-box" nor "two-box" is stable. This is the policy-level echo of the author's correction: a learner whose exploration is read by Omega learns "an EDT-like picture" from those rounds, and here that picture is wrong for its deliberate rounds. It is post 04's unfair environment (fairness clause 2: expectations "do not differ between exploration actions and plain actions"; P04's hypothesis H) and the 2023 journal's "understandable that you might not be able to learn" case. No partition is even well-defined here, because the environment's law is not a function of (π, w). [derived; checked]

**2.4 Slowly-responding Omega — the second mis-partition.** Omega fills w.p. the exponential moving average of the agent's past one-boxing (rate λ ∈ {0.005, 0.05}). Round-to-round policy variation is fast relative to Omega's memory, so P(b | π_t) is flat on the visited set, the invariance test puts b in W, cf(q) = M·P̂(b) + (1−q)K, the learner two-boxes, and the box empties toward its residual exploration rate (payoff ≈ 450 vs 1000 for a persistent one-boxer) [checked, W = {b} in 5/5 at both λ]. The exogeneity finding is *true at the timescale tested* and false at Omega's: identifiability requires visitation at the environment's response timescale (block designs), i.e. a hypothesis class containing lagged or averaged policy dependence. With such a class the effect is identifiable by holding policies for blocks ≫ 1/λ; without it the learner cannot correct the partition from any amount of fast variation. [derived; the remedy not simulated]

**2.5 XOR, mugging, 5-and-10.** XOR (D ∼ Bern(0.01), predictor thresholds the policy, disaster 1000, payment 100): for p < ½ the letter comes iff D and cf(p) = −10 − p; for p > ½ it comes iff ¬D and cf(p) = −10 − 99p; refuse [derived; refuse side 99.8–100%, W = {D}]. Counterfactual mugging with Omega's simulation an independent draw from the tails-policy (Definition 6): cf(p) = ½(−xp) + ½(yp) = p(y−x)/2, pay iff y > x — the coin is in W, the transfer in R, and the policy label is exactly Remark 4.2's "event true in the worlds where the disposition holds" [derived; q = 1, payoff ≈ 1.0]. 5-and-10: E(take-10) = 10 from one visit or from the environment's stated rule; a spurious proof "π = take-10 → U = 0" is a composite sentence and inert (1.6); the learner never derives its own action because its action is a function of (P, E), which are functions of data and admitted proofs, not of sentences about π_t [derived; q = 1]. Post 07's point that COEDT gets 5-and-10 "by reasoning alone" holds for this learner iff E is admitted by proof; otherwise one exploratory round.

**2.6 Troll Bridge, statistically.** The simulation contains no logic; it checks the statistical half of §9.3 and §4 below. Fallible troll (trigger never fires because PA is consistent; spurious blow-up at rate ζ = 0.05): E(cross) → 10(1 − 2ζ) = 9, cross [checked]. TB(θ) (P11's forced-act tree, O_d = ⊤, forced crossing w.p. θ at −10): V(q) = −10θ + 10(1−θ)q, slope positive for every θ < 1, cross — the fixed forced mass that "swamps vanishing trembles" for every world-event-conditioned evaluator (P11-12′) is a *constant* at the policy level, so the separatrix θ/(1−θ) disappears [derived; checked at θ = 0.1, payoff ≈ 8]. The exploring-agent troll and the stricter troll are in §4.3–4.4; the pessimistic-prior runs confirm §9.3's asymmetry: the stricter troll traps the pessimistic prior (never crosses, 5/5), the original troll does not (crosses, 5/5) [checked].

## 3. The partition-learning problem (question c)

**What own-policy variation identifies** [derived]. For each observed v, the invariance of P(v | π) across the *visited* policy set; hence the reduced-form value E[U | π] on the visited set without any partition at all (the partition is a variance-reduction and extrapolation device for observables, and the sole carrier of exogeneity for latents). Four things it does not identify:

(i) Invariance off the visited set. A coarse-feature predictor is flat on each level set of its feature, so a learner whose visitation never crosses a level boundary finds b ∈ W and two-boxes; identifiable iff visitation crosses the boundary, and when it does the learner exploits the level set (§2.2).

(ii) Response at a slower timescale than the variation (§2.4); identifiable iff the hypothesis class has lags and the visitation has blocks.

(iii) Latent W. P(Incon) moves only through the likelihood E(cross, ·) on outcomes, never through P(Incon | π); its prior is a design input (5.4).

(iv) *Anything the environment reads that is not a coordinate of π*: an exploration flag (§2.3), the agent's current estimate (the stricter troll, 4.4), the agent's reasons (the 2023 journal's troll), the agent's source code beyond its input–output behaviour. Case (iv) is not a mis-partition but the absence of a partition: the environment's law is not a function F(π, w), so "responsive to policy" has no truth value. Its symptom in the data is unexplained non-stationarity of E(π, ·) across time at fixed π.

**The four cases named in the brief.** *Omega predicting exploration but not deliberation* — case (iv); the learner's verdict is an exploration-schedule artifact (§2.3). *Vice versa (deliberation, not exploration)* — at the policy level there is no such Omega: deliberation *is* the choice of π, and an Omega that reads π is the policy reader of §2.2, correctly partitioned. The act-level distinction LICDT lives on (post 04: condition on exploring-and-acting) has no policy-level referent. *A lesion reaching the act through the state* (channel 1 of [smoking-lesion-exploration-and-boundaries](../smoking-lesion-exploration-and-boundaries.md) §4) — the tickle becomes an observation coordinate o, the policy has a label per o, o ∈ W, and cf sums over o: representable and correctly partitioned (Proposition 12's pattern). A lesion that shifts the *learner's estimate* rather than an observation is channel 3 and case (iv). *A slowly-responding Omega* — case (ii). *A coarse-feature predictor* — case (i), benign once visited.

**Instrumental-variable framing** [derived]. Read the policy as an instrument Z for the act A on the outcome Y. IV needs relevance (Z moves A: true by construction), exclusion (Z affects Y only through A) and exogeneity (Z ⊥ the confounders of A and Y). In Newcomb exclusion *fails* — the policy affects Y through Omega's reading of the policy, not only through the act — which is exactly why estimating the *act's* effect by IV (the sibling direction "iv-design-draw-as-instrument" frames the draw as the instrument; I cite the framing without its output) gives the two-boxing answer, whereas the policy-level learner uses the *reduced form* Z → Y and needs no exclusion at all: it is estimating the total effect of the policy, which is what a policy chooser should want. What it does need is exogeneity of Z with respect to W — the partition test's content — and *stationarity of the reduced form*: that the environment reads Z and nothing else about the agent. Case (iv) is the IV textbook's "instrument correlated with an unmeasured cause of Y": the instrument the analyst manipulates (π) is not the variable the environment reads (the flag, the estimate, the history). P04's hypothesis H (exploration randomness unread by payoffs and worlds, and carrying no information about other draws given the act) is the formal name of the assumption that the *act-level* exploration bit is a clean instrument; the policy-level analogue is: the environment's law is a function of (π_t, w_t) alone. **Identifiability, theorem-shaped [derived, not proved]:** if the round law is F(π_t, w_t) with w_t i.i.d. and F fixed, then from own-policy variation on a visited set Π′ the partition restricted to Π′ and E on Π′ × W are identifiable (consistent estimators exist; the DM test is one); if the round law depends on any agent feature not determined by π_t, no partition is well-defined and every consistent estimator of "E(π)" is schedule-relative.

## 4. The Löbian argument at the policy level (question d)

**4.1 The argument and where it breaks.** Löb needs (L): □(π_t = cross → bad) → (π_t = cross → bad), proved inside T by: suppose □(cross → bad); suppose the agent adopts cross; examine the agent — the only way this agent adopts cross given □(cross → bad) is a misfire, i.e. Incon(PA); the troll fires on Incon; so bad. For the doc's 𝔄 the "examine the agent" step is its (β): Cross ↔ E_P[U | Cross] > E_P[U | Stay], and (α) makes P(□⊥ | Cross) = 1 under the hypothesis (Lemma 8).

**For this learner the inner step fails at "examine the agent"** [derived]. Its adoption of cross is the event cf_t(cross) > cf_t(stay), and cf_t is a function of (P_t, E_t), which are functions of the data and of admitted proofs about E and P(w). The hypothesis □(cross → bad) is a composite sentence; it does not change E_t (it is not of the form ∀w E(cross, w) = bad) and does not change P_t(w) (it constrains P(w | π), which is never formed). So under the hypothesis the agent adopts cross iff it would have anyway, i.e. iff P_t(Incon) < ½ (with E from the troll's rule); adopting cross therefore does not imply misfire, "cross → Incon" is not derivable inside the hypothetical, Löb's antecedent □L → L is not established, and L is unprovable — as it must be, since in the consistent world the agent crosses and collects 10, making L false and (T sound) unprovable.

*Carry-over from §9.3.* Consequence 1 (Löb's (L) fails) is the paragraph above with π for a; consequence 2 (composite proofs constrain P(w | π), never consulted) is 1.6 verbatim; consequence 3 (the learner is a Definition-4 procedure under Definition 6 while the proof-respecter's draw has a parent other than its state) transfers because the learner's policy is a function of (P_t, E_t) alone. The doc's Proposition 10 proof is the same stall stated for 𝔅, and its Remark ("the trap is … for any agent whose credence in the lesion answers to a theorem linking act to lesion") is the negative image of clause 1.6. [derived]

**4.2 Relocation alone does not save EDT** [derived]. Define policy-level EDT by cf_EDT(π) := E[U | π_t = π] = Σ_w P(w | π) E(π, w). On the double lesion, Newcomb, XOR and the mugging it agrees with the FDT learner, because there P(w | π) = P(w) for the exogenous variables and the responsive ones are inside E. In Troll Bridge the composite theorem asserts P(Incon | π = cross) = 1; policy-level EDT, being T-respecting, adopts it, its (β)-analogue holds at the policy level, and Lemma 8 runs with "π_t = cross" for "Cross". So the policy level buys this much — counterfactual mugging and transparent Newcomb become choices made at the policy level, before the branch is learned — and no more; the bridge needs the marginal formula. This sharpens [smoking-lesion-exploration-and-boundaries](../smoking-lesion-exploration-and-boundaries.md) §9.3's "Troll Bridge is a fact about the agent": it is a fact about the agent's *formula*, invariant under the act/policy relocation.

**4.3 The exploring-agent variant: what does the troll read?** Post 11 ll. 147–174: the troll fires on crossings due to the exploration clause or to P(cross) = 0; the agent's crossing given □(cross → bad) is "either an exploration round, or P(cross)=0", both punished, so (L) holds and Löb closes. The policy-level learner has no exploration clause and no chicken clause: every crossing under policy q is a draw from q, and exploration is the adoption of a q. Three readable transcriptions of "cross due to exploration" [derived; checked]: (a) *the policy is mixed* — a label probe; the learner learns that only q = 1 pays and adopts it (q = 1, payoff 10, 5/5); (b) *the realized act differs from the policy's mode* — same, since the mode of q = 1 is cross; (c) *the adopted policy differs from the learner's greedy policy* — this reads the agent's relation to its policy, not the policy; from a neutral prior with the not-cross tie-break every crossing is flagged and punished, E(cross) is learned as −10, and the learner never crosses (5/5); with the tie-break toward cross it would cross forever. (c) is case (iv) of §3 and is the stricter troll in disguise (4.4). In none of the three does the Löbian step go through — the troll's trigger in (a)–(b) is a fact about E (∀w E(mixed-cross, w) = −10), admitted and learned around; in (c) the failure is a prior-dependent trap, not a theorem.

**4.4 The stricter troll** (2023 journal ll. 33–35: blow up on crossing "for any other reason than high expected reward in the current instance"; here: on crossing against the agent's own posterior-mean estimate). Its trigger reads cf_t — the agent's internal estimate, which is a function of the agent's data, not of π_t. Verdicts [checked]: from a neutral prior the first crossing is *with* the estimate (a tie counts as not-against), pays 10, and confirms; from a pessimistic prior (cross cells loaded on −10) every crossing is against the estimate, punished, and confirms the prior; the original troll with the same pessimistic prior is crossed (VOI crossing is not punished). So the stricter troll traps exactly the learners whose prior says the bridge is out — the 2020-10-19 journal's second test ("100% failure while still having a sense that the agent has a way out" — it fails that test: the trapped agent has no way out) and its own verdict that this "would not be such a compelling philosophical dilemma". Reading of both journals ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED. Note the boundary this puts on the type: no v2 tree reads internal reasoning (P04:27; Definition 2's exclusion of counterpossibles), and the stricter troll reads exactly that; it is representable as an environment only if cf_t is made an observable coordinate — which is Definition 6's boundary crossed from the environment's side.

**4.5 The doc's fallible-troll paragraph and the bounded agent.** The doc lets the troll fire with probability q when triggered: for 𝔄, E[U | Cross] = 10 − 20q < 0 iff q > ½, so Lemma 8 runs for q > ½ and not below; and for bounded 𝔄_M, Löb fails for bounded provability, so 𝔄_M crosses safely and "its record fills with safe crossings" that do not help 𝔄 because Cross_M are sentences about other programs. Two consequences for this note [derived from the doc as read]: the FDT learner's cross/stay threshold is P(Incon)·q < ½ — it crosses against every troll 𝔄 crosses against and against the ones 𝔄 stays home from; and the negative half of §6's conjecture inherits a real subtlety: the iterated proof-respecter at round n is a bounded program, so "finds the Löbian proof" must mean that for large n the round-n agent respects the proof of L_n (length polynomial in n) — a fixed point between proof length and the agent's search bound that post 11 l. 174 asserts and omits.

## 5. Costs (question e)

**5.1 Composite shortcuts and bridges that are really out** [derived]. If the bridge is out as a fact about E — ∀w E(cross, w) = −10, provable from the troll's stated rule — the learner admits it and stays. If it is out only as a fact about the composite — this agent's code always trips the trigger, provable by examining the agent but not stated in E — the learner cannot use the proof and pays one crossing (−10 once) to learn E(cross) empirically; bounded, real. The proof-respecting agent gets this case right for free. Symmetrically, a composite theorem that is a *true* shortcut about a responsive variable the learner has mis-filed in W (e.g. "π = one-box → box full" against a policy reader whose box the learner has not yet seen vary) is ignored until visitation supplies it. The general statement: the learner trades the whole class of composite theorems for immunity to the Löbian one, and pays for it wherever a composite theorem was true and cheap.

**5.2 Traps and the Scary Door** (post 07 ll. 85–97). VOI exploration opens a door iff the information value exceeds the expected catastrophe; a prior that puts non-negligible mass on universe-destroying monsters keeps the door shut forever, and the learner "may never learn to perform optimally in the world you're in" — post 13's no-traps assumption is the price of every convergence statement in §6, in either its weak (the inductor is 1−ε confident the environment is trap-free, and it is) or strong (belief in traps is the only obstacle) form. The learner cannot use a composite proof "opening → monster" if it is only composite; a proof about E ("this door conceals a monster") it can. Post 07's moral survives: 5-and-10 is solvable by reasoning alone (1.6 admits E by proof), the Scary Door is not. [reconstructed + derived]

**5.3 Determinism in the limit, and the static shadow.** As exploration vanishes the learner sits at π* and its E(π, ·) for π ≠ π* stops being refreshed; its partition is the one established during the exploratory phase. Regime-switch check [checked, §8 item 3]: a perfect policy reader for 3000 rounds, then a policy-invariant box (P(full) = 0.9) forever. The learner's greedy first drops to the two-box side 4400–6100 rounds after the switch, and by round 12000 only 30–50% of post-switch rounds have a two-box greedy; the partition does eventually move b into W.

This is the exchange's "the ideal agent's causal knowledge has to be inherited from its bounded predecessors' randomization" made concrete: the deterministic limit carries a frozen E and a frozen W, correct iff the environment is stationary, and the doc's §9 moral two (limits do not commute; "to speak of the ideal agent's verdict without saying how the limit is taken is to speak of nothing in particular") applies to the *shadow* even where, as in §2.1, it does not apply to the verdict. [derived]

**Is Definition 10's limit calibration the right static shadow? Partly** [derived]. What matches: a label change π(d) → m for a round is, under Definition 6, exactly what a Definition-10 tremble is — every node carrying d redraws from the changed label — so for a label-probe predictor the trembled statistics ν_{B, C^ε} are what the policy learner's exploratory rounds produce.

What does not match: (i) Definition 10 trembles uniformly and at one rate ε for all acts, while VOI exploration is non-uniform and belief-directed, so the limit conditional at a null policy is the one along the learner's exploration *ray*, not the uniform ray (§9.1's "ray-dependence"; the run's test-sequence form is the repair); (ii) Definition 10 has each node redraw independently, so it cannot express a shared-seed predictor (Definition 6′; Q12's correlated draws), against which the learner's verdict differs from the label-probe verdict only in mixed policies; (iii) Definition 10 is a condition on *states*, whereas the object frozen in the limit is the *response function E* — the cf_s of Definition 2, Q6's uncalibrated slot.

Proposal: the static shadow of this learner is "limit calibration of P_s along the learner's exploration sequence, with cf_s at null policies pinned to the inherited E" — a test-sequence variant of Definition 10 plus a calibration rule for cf_s of the observational kind §9.2 called *observational causal calibration*. [guess as to whether this is the object v3 wants]

**5.4 Where a prior cannot be corrected** [derived]. (i) Latent W: P₀(Incon) moves only through outcomes via E; against a perfect troll one safe crossing is decisive, against a fallible one only partially, and the toy Bayesian treatment of a logical fact is a stand-in for the logical-inductor version. (ii) Unvisited policies: E there is extrapolation; VOI visits them iff the prior says they might win and are not traps. (iii) Traps (5.2). (iv) Agent features the environment reads that are not policy coordinates (§3 case iv): no coordinate, no correction; the effect appears as non-stationarity. (v) Timescale (§2.4): a class without lags cannot be corrected by fast variation. (vi) The partition of variables observed under too little variation (§3 case i).

## 6. Conjecture for the iterated setting (question f)

**Setting.** Rounds t = 1, 2, …; finite policy set Π; at each round the outcome law is F(π_t, w_t) with w_t i.i.d. ∼ P* over a finite observable exogenous set W* and possibly one fixed latent w₀ (Incon); payoffs bounded. *Policy-fairness*: F depends on the agent only through π_t (the policy-level form of P04's deterministically-fair problem and hypothesis H). Instances: the fallible troll (F(cross, w) = −10 w.p. q on Incon, spurious blow-up w.p. ζ, else +10; F(stay) = 0), the imperfect policy reader (fill w.p. r if π = one-box, 1−r otherwise), the double lesion. Discount γ → 1. *No-trap belief*: the prior puts probability 1 on bounded payoffs and the truth is bounded. *Visitation hypothesis*: the VOI rule adopts every π ∈ Π infinitely often while posterior disagreement about its value exceeds any fixed threshold (exploration decays but does not stop while it has something to resolve).

**Conjecture F (positive half) [guess; parts marked].** Almost surely:

(i) E_t(π, ·) → F(π, ·) for every π ∈ Π — consistency of the cell estimates on an infinitely-visited set [provable: frequency convergence on infinitely-visited cells; for a logical-inductor implementation this is calibration on an efficiently-computable subsequence, the LI paper's machinery, and post 12 ll. 424–428 states the VOI-driven version as a conjecture].

(ii) The partition converges to the true one on Π — v ∈ W iff P*(v | π) is constant [provable from (i) plus the Bayes-factor asymptotics of the DM test].

(iii) π_t = π* := argmax_π E_{w∼P*}[F(π, w)] for all but finitely many rounds, with o(T) regret — in the bridge, crosses at all but finitely many rounds iff P(blow | cross) = P*(Incon)·q + ζ (up to the overlap term) < ½; in Newcomb, one-boxes at all but finitely many rounds iff r > ½ + K/2M [the statistical part is a bandit-over-policies result and provable for Thompson sampling in a finite stationary class; the *VOI* part is exactly the "VOI-exploration result" post 13 ll. 84–86 asks for and does not have, and the 2023 journal l. 31 warns that "logical induction doesn't directly reason about the future via dynamic programming" — open].

(iv) cf_t(π) − E_t[U | π_t = π] → 0 on Π — the FDT property, self-vindicating [provable from (i)–(ii)].

(v) For every t, T ⊬ (π_t = cross → □⊥) — the learner's code never satisfies the analogue of the doc's (β), so Lemma 8 has no purchase [provable as a syntactic fact about the code, the doc's Proposition 10 argument iterated; requires that the troll's trigger be a fact about E and not about the agent's estimate, i.e. excludes the stricter troll].

Parts (iii) and (v) together are §9.3's conjecture with "positive initial crossing propensity" replaced by "prior P₀(Incon)·q + ζ < ½, or enough VOI to try once".

**Negative half [reconstructed from post 11 l. 172 and the doc §7].** In the same setting, a proof-respecting-conditional learner — any rule with cf(π) = E[U | π_t = π] whose P respects T (LIEDT; the p-chicken agent; policy-level EDT of 4.2) — has a round T_L after which P_t(□⊥ | π_t = cross) = 1 by the Löbian proof of L_t, so it crosses only on forced-exploration rounds (which the exploring-agent troll punishes) or never (without forced exploration), for linear regret; its record of earlier safe crossings does not help because, per the doc, those are sentences about earlier bounded programs. The existence of T_L is post 11's omitted proof; the fixed point of 4.5 (proof length vs search bound at round n) is the step that needs writing, and the doc's observation that Löb fails for bounded provability says the fixed point is not automatic. Failure to supply it here is recorded as a gap, not as evidence against the claim.

**Open items, with diagnoses.** (a) The VOI-exploration result (post 13's own open problem) — needed for (iii); Thompson sampling gives the statistical shape, not the discounted-optimality shape. (b) Identifiability under lags (§2.4) — needs a class with lagged policy dependence and a block-visitation hypothesis; not attempted. (c) The bounded-proof fixed point of the negative half. (d) Whether (v) survives a troll that reads the *statistics* of the agent's estimate rather than a theorem — e.g. fires when cf_t(cross) was computed from fewer than n rounds — which is the 2020-10-19 journal's "unjustified crossing" troll; my expectation is that it converts the theorem into a prior-dependent trap, as in 4.4 [guess].

## 9. Questions for the author

1. Is "policy-fair" (the round law is a function of (π_t, w_t) alone) the right fairness notion for the learning-theoretic version of Coherent Care, i.e. the policy-level form of P04's clause 2? It is what makes the partition well-defined, and every mis-partition found here is a violation of it.
2. Do you want the non-responsiveness clause stated as a property of the *formula* (cf never forms P(w | π)) rather than of beliefs — it is what makes 4.2's "relocation does not save EDT" a clean claim, and it is the doc's Proposition 10 with a learning path attached for observable w.
3. Should the doc's §5 acquire a third learner — the policy-indexed one — with the remark that for it the §4 Corollary is empty? The recency/cumulative fork then reappears for it only under non-stationarity (§2.4, §5.3).
4. Is the static shadow you want for v3 the test-sequence limit of 5.3 (P_s calibrated along the learner's own exploration sequence, cf_s pinned to the inherited response function), or Definition 10 kept with the ray-dependence acknowledged?
