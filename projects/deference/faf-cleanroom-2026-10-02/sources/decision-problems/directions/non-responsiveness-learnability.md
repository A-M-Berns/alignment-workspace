## 1. (a) What non-responsiveness is, precisely

**Three grades, in increasing strength** [derived].

**NR1 (formula-level).** The action-relevant expectation is cf(a) = Σ_w P(w)·E(a, w): a function of P's marginal over a designated set of exogenous coordinates w and of the response function E, never of P's act-conditionals P(· | a). This is Proposition 10's 𝔅 read literally, and it is what §9.3 states. Against the doc's (β): 𝔅 satisfies (β′) T ⊢ Cross_𝔅 ↔ (Σ_w P₀(w)E(cross,w) > Σ_w P₀(w)E(stay,w)) in place of (β), so Lemma 8's single use of (β) — from P(□⊥ | Cross) = 1 to ¬Cross — has no counterpart. Non-responsiveness is **not** the bare negation of (β) — a constant crosser, a constant stayer and a coin all negate (β) — but the replacement of (β) by (β′) with a specific P₀.

**NR2 (marginal shielding).** NR1 plus: the marginal P₀(w) is not moved by theorems whose statement fixes the agent's own act (sentences of the form A_t = a → ψ, or any sentence in which the agent's code appears). NR1 alone does not give this. If the marginal is the agent's ordinary coherent credence P over a language containing Cross, then a believed theorem Cross → □⊥ forces P(□⊥) ≥ P(Cross ∧ (Cross → □⊥)) ≥ P(Cross) − ε: the marginal is moved through the self-prediction. So §9.3's "it does not constrain P(w)" is true only if P(Cross) is not high or Cross is not in the language. The doc's remark after Proposition 10 has this exactly: "An agent causal about the bridge but evidential about itself — one that, told that crossing entails inconsistency and predicting that it will cross, raises its credence in inconsistency accordingly — satisfies the analogue of (β) and is afflicted like 𝔄." Refinement below (§8 B): without provably accurate self-prediction such an agent escapes the *original* troll, because under the hypothetical proof its every crossing is a self-mispredicted crossing (P(Cross) < 1/2 + ε), which is not □⊥; but a troll that fires on self-mispredicted crossing — the probabilistic cousin of the chicken-clause troll, item (C) of the (A)→(D) chain in [11-troll-bridge-5](../sl-workflow/posts/11-troll-bridge-5.md) — makes (L) hold for it and Löb fires [checked/derived]. So NR2 is the operative property, and the doc's "formed without regard to its own act" is the informal name for it.

**NR3 (anonymous marginal; derivation-free).** NR2 can be secured without inspecting derivations by an algebraic condition: P₀ is a credence (e.g. a logical inductor) over the sublanguage L_exo of sentences that do not contain the agent's code — the agent is not named in the language over which its marginal is formed. Then Cross → □⊥ is not a sentence of L_exo, P₀(□⊥) is a fact about PA alone, and no self-prediction exists in P₀ to carry the theorem's weight. The agent still has a full inductor P over the whole language — it assigns 1 to every theorem, including composite ones; it predicts its own act; it records tagged draws — and P is what learns Ê(a, w) from records (a_t, tag_t, w-observables, u_t) and what consumes proofs *about the environment's rule* (∀w E(cross, w) = bad is in L_exo since a is a variable there, not the agent's act). Only the marginal fed to cf is anonymous. This is a two-inductor architecture: the full inductor for self-knowledge and response learning, the anonymous inductor for the exogenous marginal.

**The sharp distinction from "not respecting logic."** In the author's 2020 sense — "updating to probability 1 (/0) when something is proven (/refuted)" (`Logsidian/journals/2020-10-19.md`) — the NR3 agent respects logic completely: P assigns 1 to Cross → □⊥ once proved, and P₀ assigns 1 to every theorem *of its language*. Nothing is disrespected; something is not consulted. Under the hypothetical proof the two inductors disagree about □⊥ — P(□⊥) ≈ P(Cross) ≈ 1, P₀(□⊥) small — which is the exact formal content of "can hypothetically disagree but never actually see the disagreement" (`13-my-current-take-on-counterfactuals` line 787); in the actual world the proof does not exist (Proposition 10) and they agree.

**What updates what** [derived]: (i) data about own draws → Ê (tagged, via P); (ii) proofs about E(a, ·) with a a variable → Ê (in L_exo, via either inductor); (iii) proofs about w alone (a direct proof of □⊥ or of Con) → P₀; (iv) composite proofs A_t = a → ψ → P only, never P₀ and never cf. The line between (ii)+(iii) and (iv) is syntactic — does the sentence name the agent — and needs no derivation inspection. The line does *not* separate self-created composite theorems (Löbian, false for the NR agent's world) from environment-created ones (a troll that reads the agent's code); both are excluded together (§3 below).

**Steelman symmetry.** The Smoking Lesion Steelman robots were built so "the epistemic module cannot watch the decision module" (`01-smoking-lesion-steelman` ll. 49–53), which *blocks* the tickle defence and hurts EDT. NR3 is the same blinding applied only to the marginal that feeds cf, and it *implements* the tickle defence as a policy where Gödel forbids it as a belief (§9.3). One device, opposite sign, depending on which module is blinded.

## 2. (b) Is there a learning path?

### 2.1 The 2023 conditional-contracts market

**Mechanics** [derived; payoff table checked, §8 C]. A bundle V(a) pays u if A = a and refunds its price c if not (`Logsidian/journals/2023-05-28.md` l. 20). The ordinary market also prices the decomposed conditional bet — one share of (A = a ∧ U) plus q shares of ¬(A = a) — at q = P(A=a ∧ U)/P(A=a). The enforcement trader the author names (l. 25) goes long the bundle and short the decomposed bet when c < q (reversed when c > q); its payoff is q − c if A = a is realized and exactly 0 otherwise, in every world. A trader with payoff ≥ 0 in every world and unbounded cumulative payoff would exploit the market, so logical induction forces Σ_{t : A_t = a} |c_t − q_t| < ∞.

**Claim CC-limit** [derived from the LI non-exploitability criterion; not in the sources as stated]. In an LI market with bundled conditional contracts, the bundle price equals the conditional expectation along the rounds on which the condition is realized, in the limit, for every prior; on rounds where the condition fails the bundle is unconstrained. This is RCDT's convergence desideratum — counterfactuals "in line with the conditional probabilities we eventually learn" (`13-my-current-take-on-counterfactuals` l. 183) — delivered by the market for free, and it sharpens the 2023 worry: the enforcer is not an optional wealthy trader whose presence is "a matter of prior"; every LI contains it, and its effect is prior-independent in the limit along realized rounds and prior-dependent only in rate. Whether the author already had the prior-independent form: not found in the sources read; ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED.

**Does the market make action-relevant prices non-responsive to composite theorems automatically?** No [derived]. A theorem cross → bad lets traders short (cross ∧ ¬bad) for free, so q → 0 wherever P(cross) > 0; the bundle price c is untouched at round t and moves only through realized crossings. Under the Löbian hypothetical the per-round step (L) fails: crossing at round t with c_t > 0 > q_t is consistent with the proof, the gap being finite-time; so Löb does not fire per round, the proof does not exist, and the CC agent passes the original troll by the same outside argument as 𝔅 (Proposition 10's Σ₁-soundness proof transfers verbatim, since the CC agent actually crosses). But the CC agent is *responsive with delay*. In the toy wealth market of §8 C, if the proven implication were true (u = −10 on crossing) crossing stops after 0, 5, 11, 17 rounds at physical-to-enforcer wealth ratios 1, 10, 100, 1000 [checked]; if it were false the enforcer bleeds to 0.05 of its wealth and the market overrides the "proof" [checked]. That is "learn counterfactuals in a way that respects reality, rather than trying to deduce them" (post 13 ll. 766–787) made concrete: the bundle answers to realized payouts, not to theorems.

**Asymptotic Löb** [guess; recorded as open]. For a margin rule (cross iff c_t > δ) the sentence ψ := ∀^∞t (cross_t → bad_t) looks Löb-eligible: □ψ → (q_t → 0 for large t) → (c_t → 0 along crossings, by CC-limit) → (finitely many crossings) → ψ vacuously. Gap: a stage-t belief P_t(cross_t → bad_t) need not be high because P_t(ψ) → 1 — ψ is Σ₂ and uniformity in t is not guaranteed for an LI. Failure to close this ≠ evidence it is closed; the diagnosis is that the CC market's shield against composite theorems is finite-horizon, and whether an asymptotic Löb undoes it is unknown. If it does, the CC agent is the author's own worry realized without any wealthy trader.

**VOI** [derived]. Agrees with §9.3: under the proof q = 0 pins the agent's *conditional*, so VOI is positive only if the action-relevant expectation is the bundle price c. The VOI escape of the 2023 journal (ll. 29–35) therefore presupposes the bundle as the action-relevant object — the market's form of the marginal formula — and is bounded by CC-limit: VOI crossing under the hypothetical is a finite delay, not a standing escape.

**Verdict on (i).** The conditional-contract market yields non-responsiveness to composite theorems exactly on rounds where the action does not occur — the rounds the Löbian hypothetical is not about. It is not a learning path to NR2. It is a distinct mechanism (finite-time gap plus Σ₁-soundness) that also passes the original troll, and it pays with delayed responsiveness whose closing time is the prior.

### 2.2 Dominance and regret

In the iterated fallible troll (fires w.p. θ = 0.05 on any crossing, □⊥ never obtains) the responsive learner that stops at the round it finds the proof earns 2620 over 2000 rounds and the non-responsive one 17520; both are calibrated on what they test (empirical P(bad | cross) = 0.063 and 0.062 on their own records) and the responsive agent's post-proof P(bad | cross) = 1 is never tested [checked, §8 E]. Regret against always-cross is O(1) for the non-responsive learner and linear for the responsive one — a learning-theoretic justification in form, and the standard one. But the benchmark class decides its force [derived]: on environments whose trigger factors through (a, w_exo) the non-responsive learner wins; on environments that read the agent (Newcomb; the doc's §8 "situations that read the agent") an anonymous-marginal learner that puts the box among the exogenous coordinates two-boxes and has linear regret against the one-boxer. Regret adjudicates class-relatively; choosing the class is the design stance. The doc's fallible-troll paragraph adds that data pushes the responsive agent the *wrong* way — the record of safe crossings by bounded predecessors "raises 𝔄's credence that T is consistent, whence, by Lemma 8, its credence that it will stay" — so there is no path from experience.

**The tagged-covariation refinement shrinks the design residue** [checked for Newcomb, §8 F; derived for Troll Bridge]. Let the exogenous coordinates be whatever the agent's own *deliberate-tagged* draws are empirically independent of, and let E absorb whatever covaries with them. In Newcomb with a predictor that reads the deliberate policy, box and deliberate act covary (cov 0.25 in the simulation) while coin-tagged interventions show the +1 two-boxing effect given the box; the rule files the box under E and one-boxes, and only an agent that used the coin data as its E would two-box — which is the author's correction to the Docs Claude in [two-lesions-exchange-2026-09-19](../two-lesions-exchange-2026-09-19.md) ("the agent does poorly precisely when the interventionist assumption fits reality well"), now with the fix visible: use deliberate tags for deliberate decisions. In Troll Bridge no tagged crossing ever covaries with □⊥, so □⊥ stays exogenous and the composite theorem about it is not in the marginal's language. What remains stipulated is only the composite-theorem exclusion, whose cost is bounded in §3.

### 2.3 Tiling and self-trust

**A responsive agent that has found the proof endorses installing a non-responsive successor** [derived]. Post-proof, 𝔄 values the modification M := "install 𝔅" by E_P[U | M]. Cross_𝔅 is a sentence about a different program, for which Lemma 8 does not hold (Proposition 10); P(Blow | Cross_𝔅) = P(□⊥), which stays small because P(Cross_𝔄) ≈ 0 post-proof carries no weight onto □⊥; so E_P[U | M] ≈ +10 per round against 0 for staying itself. 𝔄 modifies — by conditional expectation, with no counterfactual. This is the doc's own observation turned around: the record of Cross_M "is a record of sentences about other programs" is exactly why the successor is safe. It is the only path found here on which responsiveness produces non-responsiveness, and it is across agents, not within one. Pre-proof 𝔄 is indifferent unless it foresees the proof. A meta-troll that punishes the successor's crossings when the predecessor "modified for a dumb reason" restores (L) at the modification decision — 𝔄 modifies iff the conditional favours it or a chicken clause fires, and under □(M → bad) only the clause is left — and re-closes the trap; but that troll reads the predecessor's decision rule, so it belongs with the code-reading environments of §3. Two corpus hooks: post 13's "PCDT … should absolutely self-modify in some way" (l. 156), and Pi Rogers's remark that logical inductors endorse their future beliefs (`Logsidian/journals/2026-05-26.md`) — post-proof 𝔄 does *not* endorse its own future decisions; it prefers another agent's, a reflective-inconsistency signature.

### 2.4 A Dutch book on the composite?

No [derived]. Post-proof 𝔄 has cf = conditional, P(bad | cross) = 1 and P(cross) ≈ 0; every belief it tests is calibrated; a bookie cannot make it cross and its bets on □⊥ at a small credence are calibrated. 𝔅's cf equals its tagged conditional on its realized crossings, and its disparity with 𝔄's conditional is hypothetical only (post 13 l. 787). Neither agent is bookable. The Dutch book of `12-dutch-booking-cdt-revised-argument` says "the two had better agree" (l. 29); it constrains, it does not select. This matches the 2020 journal's diagnosis that there are "no epistemic principles pointing the other way" and that loose logic looks like "an unprincipled exception" (`Logsidian/journals/2020-10-19.md`): the justification of non-responsiveness is value (Definition 21), tiling (2.3) and class-relative regret (2.2), never coherence.

**Verdict on (b)** [derived]: no learning path within a single agent; a tiling path across agents; regret class-relative; coherence silent. Non-responsiveness is a stipulation — but one the responsive agent itself would make about its successor once it holds the proof.

## 3. (c) Dispositional T-respect: vacuous or genuine?

**In sound-T Troll Bridge the disposition is never exercised against a true, non-vacuous proof** [derived]. While 𝔅 crosses, no T-proof of Cross_𝔅 → □⊥ exists (Proposition 10). If P₀(□⊥) ≥ 1/2, 𝔅 stays, T ⊢ ¬Cross_𝔅 (a halting computation), hence T ⊢ Cross_𝔅 → □⊥ trivially — the theorem appears, is vacuous, and ignoring it changes nothing. So the exchange's phrase "disposed to ignore a proof about itself that, thanks to that very disposition, never materializes" is exact for this environment, and the cost is modal, not actual: the disposition's whole work is to make (L) false, i.e. to make the proof not exist.

**As a policy it is not vacuous** [derived]. NR3 excludes *every* sentence naming the agent, including true environment-created ones. Take the really-out bridge stated through the agent: the troll fires on any program with 𝔅's code. The full inductor P knows "my code is in S, S-programs are blown up on crossing, so Cross_𝔅 → bad" — a true composite theorem with a one-line derivation. 𝔅 ignores it, crosses once, and its tagged record then teaches Ê(cross, ·) = bad; the cost is one crossing (§9.3's stated cost), or everything if the first crossing is a trap. Contrast the really-out bridge stated through the environment — ∀w E(cross, w) = bad, with a a variable — which is in L_exo and which 𝔅 respects at once. So the disposition costs at most finitely many exploratory losses per environment where a code-reading theorem is true and not E-derivable without the code, and is fatal in trap versions of those; and it costs nothing exactly where the theorem is self-created, because there its truth is manufactured by the responsiveness it presupposes (Lemma 8's single use of (β); the doc's §7 remark "the lesion is self-created"; §9 moral four).

**Where the line is, and whether it needs derivations** [derived]. The line §9.3 calls structural *is* structural — syntactic, in fact: does the sentence name the agent's code or quantify over the act as a variable — and it needs no derivation inspection. But §9.3's stated mechanism ("composite proofs constrain P(w | a), which the formula never consults") is wrong as the reason: a believed composite proof a → w also constrains P(w), through P(a), under coherence (§1, §8 B). What shields the marginal is the language it is formed in, not the formula that reads it. And the syntactic line is coarser than the line that matters for cost: it does not separate the Löbian theorem (self-created, false for 𝔅's world) from the code-reading troll's theorem (environment-created, true); both name the agent. No derivation-free test separates them, and derivation inspection is unreliable too — "would the theorem survive replacing my decision rule by a constant crosser?" answers no for Löb and also no for a troll keyed to the whole code. The two kinds of proof are told apart only by the environment class, which is (b)'s design residue again.

**Relation to the doc's §8.** "In a formalism of frequencies there is nothing for a theorem to be but a frequency of 1" — NR3 is the learner-side rendering of exactly that formalism: the marginal that feeds cf is a body of credences about w in which the composite theorem cannot be written. The doc says what the calibration formalism cannot write down is "how the agent came by its conditional — by counting or by proving"; for 𝔅 the answer is that it never comes by that conditional at all.

## 4. (d) Bunthut's X-troll

**Reading 1 — the troll fires iff X is inconsistent, X the agent's counterfactual system** (`13-my-current-take-on-counterfactuals` ll. 740–760). For an NR3 agent there is no separate counterfactual logic; cf is a formula over P₀ and Ê, and X is the agent's whole theory. Bunthut's inner step — "examining the source code of the agent, because we're assuming the agent crosses, either X proved that crossing implies U=+10, or it proved that crossing implies U=0" — is false of 𝔅, whose code consults P₀(□_X⊥) < 1/2 and no X-proof about its own utility. Inside X, (L) fails. Outside, Proposition 10's proof transfers with X for T: Cross_𝔅 is a true Σ₁ sentence, X ⊢ Cross_𝔅, and X ⊢ Cross_𝔅 → □_X⊥ would give X ⊢ □_X⊥, a false Σ₁ sentence, against Σ₁-soundness. The trigger is inert, given X Σ₁-sound and P₀(□_X⊥) < 1/2 [derived]. §9.3's first half holds.

**Reading 2 — a trigger on what the rule consults.** (i) "P₀(□⊥) ≥ 1/2": the rule crosses only when P₀ < 1/2, so no crossing trips it; inert. (ii) "P₀(□⊥) < 1/2" (punish confident crossers): every crossing is punished, Ê learns it in one crossing, and no agent of the type crosses safely — a trap by the 2020 journal's second test, not a dilemma. (iii) "X ⊢ φ" for φ := ∀w E(cross, w) = bad — the §9.3 proposal. The environment's definition is φ ↔ □_X φ. Löb needs only ⊢ □φ → φ, the half "if X proves the bridge is out, it is out"; then ⊢ φ, and ⊢ □φ by necessitation (equivalently, necessitate the hypothesis to □(□φ → φ) and apply the Löb axiom to get □φ). The fixed point of p ↔ □p in GL is ⊤, confirmed on the chain model [checked, §8 A]. □φ is then a *true* Σ₁ sentence and φ is true: the bridge really is out, with no conflict with Σ₁-soundness (unlike a proof of □⊥). 𝔅 respects E-proofs — φ ∈ L_exo, a being a variable — so cf(cross) = −10 and it stays, correctly. The author's l. 177 ("it would be obvious that no one should cross… we lose the sense of a dilemma") applies. §9.3's second half holds, with the modal step verified: the direction Löb needs is □φ → φ; the converse is a consequence, not a premise. (iv) The self-misprediction troll of §1 is the X-troll that *does* bite — against the NR1-only agent whose marginal contains its self-prediction. It is the honest residue of Bunthut's "if the counterfactual reasoning system can understand [that its results affect your actions], Troll Bridge returns" (l. 762): the coherence channel P(w) ≥ P(a) − ε is exactly that understanding. NR3 severs it by construction, not by weakening X.

**Does the circularity objection survive?** Concede at the level of logic [derived]: no step of Lemma 8 is invalid, and 𝔅 makes no different *inference* — it makes a different *use* of inferences. The author's candidate "the inference from ¬(A∧B) to C(A|B)=0" (l. 812) is a step in setting the action-relevant expectation from a theorem, not a step within T; Bunthut is right that T's chain has no wrong link. Bunthut's "the alternative agent who believes crossing is −10 and sticks to that… is right, and the bridge would blow up if he were to cross it" (l. 872) is also right — Lemma 8 is a theorem for 𝔄 — and the run already has it formal: on TB(θ) both pure labels are calibrated, q = 0 on the merits and q = 1 vacuously (P11-11′, P11-19′). Calibration cannot adjudicate; Definition 21 does, and Definition 16 names what each rule does: 𝔅 makes Cross ∧ Blow P₀(□⊥)-inconsistent (ν(Cross ∧ Blow) = 0 in every calibrated instantiation for a consistent T), 𝔄 makes Cross ∧ ¬Blow inconsistent — the wrong event. That is Soares's "decisions are for making bad outcomes inconsistent" (the project aim recorded at `Logsidian/Research Ideas May 2026.md` l. 71) read against the doc's §9 moral four ("created by the decision rule"). Is "which counterfactual is true depends on which agent you are, and the non-responsive agent makes the good one true" satisfying? As a description it is provably so (Lemma 8 against Proposition 10). As an answer to "why be 𝔅" it relocates the question to §2, where the answer is value and tiling, not learning or coherence — and there Bunthut's residue stands: the architecture is not learned. What is inserted, though, is not a belief about the bridge ("insert the relevant takeaway… directly into its prior", l. 872): P₀(□⊥) is non-dogmatic and Ê is learned; what is inserted is a language boundary.

## 5. (e) The theorem-shaped claim, and what is provable now

**Setting.** Iterated bridge, rounds t ∈ ℕ; the troll fires on crossing iff w_t ∈ Bad, w_t exogenous — the original (Bad = □⊥) or the fallible troll (an independent θ-coin, together with □⊥); average or γ → 1 reward.

**E1 (positive half) [derived; provable with existing machinery].** Every NR3 learner with lim sup_t P₀_t(□⊥) < 1/2 and either one crossing (initial propensity or VOI) or an E-proof of the troll's rule crosses at all but finitely many rounds and averages 10(1 − 2θ). Shape: Ê_t(cross, ¬□⊥) → 10 from tagged data or from the rule; Ê(cross, □⊥) = −10 from the rule, which is in L_exo; cf_t(cross) → 10 − 20p > 0; the Löbian sentence for round t is unprovable by Proposition 10's argument. Machinery: LI convergence on observed frequencies, Σ₁-soundness. The hypothesis p < 1/2 is a hypothesis, not a theorem — LIs need not converge on a false Σ₁ sentence, and non-dogmatism gives only p > 0 — and it is the author's own "so long as it believes the probability of PA being inconsistent is low" ([11-troll-bridge-5](../sl-workflow/posts/11-troll-bridge-5.md), probabilistic version).

**E2 (negative half) [reconstructed — the author's; post 11 ll. 172–174 "I omit the proof here"; the Sep 15 email; the doc's fallible-troll paragraph].** Every learner satisfying (α) and (β) at each round — the chicken-completed or ε-explored proof-respecting conditional, the troll punishing the completion — stops crossing at every round after the one at which it finds the Löbian proof for that round. The delicate step is uniformity in t. The doc says Löb *fails* for bounded provability □_M ("T proves ¬□_M⊥ outright") and that the bounded 𝔄_M "crosses and, T being consistent, survives"; the author's reply to Sauer (post 11 l. 449) points to Critch's parametric bounded Löb for the case where the bound grows with the round. These are different objects — a fixed-M agent versus a sequence with growing bounds — and read together they say: fixed M safe, growing M eventually trapped; P11 Open 1 records the growing-bound case as unchecked. E2's LIDT form therefore rests on bounded Löb and is open here.

**E3 (separators) [derived].** The unshielded marginal learner (one coherent inductor) satisfies E1's conclusion on the original troll and is trapped by the self-misprediction troll (§1, §8 B). The margin-rule conditional-contract learner satisfies E1's conclusion on the original troll per round, with the asymptotic question of §2.1 open. The NR3 learner passes the original troll, the exploring-agent troll (fires on the coin or on □⊥: deliberate-tagged Ê separates coin crossings, and (L) fails for deliberate ones since they imply neither), and the self-misprediction troll. Against the stricter troll — fires on crossing "for any other reason than high expected reward in the current instance" (`Logsidian/journals/2023-05-28.md` l. 33) — it is trapped iff its prior has myopic cf(cross) < 0, which is a trap and not a dilemma by the 2020 journal's second test; §9.3 says the same. Against the φ ↔ □φ troll every agent that respects E-proofs stays, correctly, and E1 is not in play.

**E4 (the doc's iterated picture is E1's data half and E2's fixed-M form) [derived].** A sequence of bounded 𝔄_M each crossing safely, the limit agent 𝔄 stopping: the record helps 𝔅 (it is Ê's data) and hurts 𝔄 (it "raises 𝔄's credence that T is consistent, whence… its credence that it will stay").

**Provable now:** E1 under its hypothesis; Claim CC-limit (§2.1); E3's unshielded trap, given approximate coherence and a threshold with slack. **Open:** E2's LIDT form (bounded Löb); the asymptotic conditional-contract Löb; whether an LI over L_exo can still learn about w from observations — it can, since sensor reports name no code, but a proof is not attempted here [guess].

## 6. (f) A fourth axiom on cf_s for the next Coherent Care

**Axiom NR (exogenous rigidity)** [derived]. Designate a subalgebra 𝓔_exo ⊆ 𝓔 of exogenous coordinates. For every non-⊥ act a: (i) P^a_s(e) = P_s(e) for all e ∈ 𝓔_exo — supposing the act does not move exogenous beliefs; (ii) P^a_s(· | e) = P_s(· | a ∧ e) and V^a_s(X ∧ a ∧ e) = V_s(X ∧ a ∧ e) wherever P_s(a ∧ e) > 0 — the supposed world given a and e is what the state believes about worlds with a ∧ e. Hence P^a_s = Σ_e P_s(e)·P_s(· | a, e): the dependency-hypothesis / K-partition form of causal choice, on Remark 1.1's Jeffrey–Bolker chassis. Success holds automatically. Because 𝓔_exo is a parameter and not a tree-intrinsic referent, this is a *counterfactual theory* in Definition 20's sense (it consults the state only), not counterfactual calibration, and Remark 3.11's question-begging worry is not triggered: classical CDT is the choice that puts Newcomb's prediction in 𝓔_exo, and it is thereby located, not condemned.

**Consistency with "cf = conditional wherever the conditional is defined"** [derived; checked on TB(θ), §8 D]. Σ_e P(e)P(X | a, e) = P(X | a) for all X iff P(e | a) = P(e) for all e ∈ 𝓔_exo, i.e. iff a ⊥ 𝓔_exo under P_s. At a recording point (Definition 7) with 𝓔_exo the pre-query chance algebra, Lemma 3 gives that independence outright, so Axiom NR and Definition 20's evidential criterion coincide and §9.3's self-vindicating equality is enough. At a non-recording point on the act-event algebra they conflict: on TB(θ) at q = 0, O_d = ⊤, θ = 1/10, the act-conditional is defined (P_s(cross) = θ > 0) and equals −10, while NR-cf(cross) = 8. The conflict dissolves when the coordinate is the draw: E[U | draw = cross] = 8 = NR-cf, because the draw ⊥ bot by Definition 6. So the answer to "is the self-vindicating equality enough?" is: yes iff the act-events are the agent's draws; on non-recording trees the fourth axiom and the FDT constraint are jointly satisfiable only after re-coordinatizing on the draw — question 2 of the exploration note's §7 answered yes, and Steiner's fix (`12-dutch-booking-cdt-revised-argument` ll. 316–319) made official. Note also NR-cf = V_B(C[d ↦ cross]) = 8, the deviation referent, and ≠ the forcing referent G_q = 10: at O = ⊤ the axiom's P_s(e) is the population marginal, so it selects Theorem 2's counterfactual, not Theorem 1's.

**What fixes 𝓔_exo** is Q6 restated [derived]. Static side: pre-query chance, which Definition 6 makes independent of the draw. Learning side: what the agent's own deliberate-tagged draws are empirically independent of (§2.2, §8 F), which puts Newcomb's prediction in E and □⊥ in 𝓔_exo, so that one-boxing and crossing follow from one principle and the CDT/EDT dispute becomes a dispute over 𝓔_exo.

**What Remark 3.11 would become.** "cf_s is constrained by success and, relative to a designated exogenous subalgebra 𝓔_exo, by exogenous rigidity (Axiom NR); this is a counterfactual theory, not a calibration. At recording points it coincides with the evidential criterion (Lemma 3). At null acts it pins the exogenous marginal and leaves the response free — where a theory of counterfactuals does its work. Classical CDT is the theory with the prediction in 𝓔_exo. Q6 becomes: what fixes 𝓔_exo; the learning picture's answer is draw-independence in the agent's own record." Two costs to record: Remark 3.9's tremble-induced cf already satisfies NR on trees (trembles are Definition-6-independent of pre-query chance), so the axiom adds content only at acts trembles do not reach or when 𝓔_exo ≠ pre-query chance; and the Löbian motivation cannot be stated in v2's type — no provability, no language of sentences — so Axiom NR's static shadow is exogenous rigidity, and its Troll Bridge content lives in §9 (learning) and in channel 3 of the exploration note. Recommendation: state Axiom NR in v3 as a counterfactual theory with 𝓔_exo a parameter, prove the recording-point coincidence, add the learner's 𝓔_exo-selection as a conjecture beside §9's shadow theorem, and point the Löbian motivation here.
