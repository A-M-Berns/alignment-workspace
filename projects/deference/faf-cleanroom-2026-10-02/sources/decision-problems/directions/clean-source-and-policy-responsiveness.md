**Headline, with its register.** the author's correction is confirmed as a theorem about a two-parameter predictor: an agent that estimates the effect of its act from its exploration episodes alone one-boxes iff Omega's accuracy on the *exploration coin* exceeds the payoff threshold, whatever Omega knows about the policy or the deliberate draw; the policy that actually pays one-boxes iff Omega's accuracy on the *deliberate draw* exceeds the same threshold; so the learner fails exactly when its exploration is a genuine intervention that Omega cannot see [checked]. The criterion that splits the class is **policy-responsiveness**: respond to a correlate iff the all-instance deviation $C[d \mapsto a]$ moves its objective frequency $\nu_{B,C}$; Omega's prediction is responsive, Con(PA) and the lesion are not; the Two Lesions doc's Proposition 10 agent $\mathfrak{B}$ is the marginal-formula agent of the companion note's §9.3 and is this criterion's verdict on the bridge [derived]. The deviation counterfactual satisfies the criterion by construction and passes every problem in the battery, so it is the referent Q6 asks for *at the policy level*, with one flagged cost: at explored acts against a label-probe Omega it disagrees with the act-conditional, so whether it satisfies the author's "counterfactual = conditional where defined" depends on whether that constraint is read at the act or at the policy [derived; the reading is a question for the author].

## 0. Objects and notation

**The Newcomb model (for §1 and §3).** One point $d$ with actions $\{1, 2\}$ (one-box, two-box). Each round: deliberate draw $D \sim \mathrm{Bern}(p)$ ($D{=}1$ = one-box); exploration flag $E \sim \mathrm{Bern}(\varepsilon)$; coin $K \sim \mathrm{Unif}\{1,2\}$; realized act $A = K$ if $E$ else $D$. Omega's prediction $\Omega \in \{1,2\}$ fills the box iff $\Omega = 1$. Payoff $U = 10 \cdot \mathbf{1}[\Omega{=}1] - \mathbf{1}[A{=}1]$ (post 04's normalization; two-boxing forgoes nothing but the correlation). The predictor's information is the parameter: **label-probe** Omega fills w.p. the trembled label $q_\varepsilon = (1-\varepsilon)p + \varepsilon/2$, independently of $(D, E, K)$ — post 04's fallible Omega and, under v2's Definition 6, the only way a tree reads a label (Proposition 4); **$(r_D, r_K)$-Omega** matches $D$ w.p. $r_D$ on non-exploration rounds and matches $K$ w.p. $r_K$ on exploration rounds — $(1, \tfrac12)$ is the P04 ledger's *deliberate-sharing* predictor, $(1,1)$ the perfect predictor, $(\tfrac12, 1)$ a coin-only predictor, $(r, r)$ the uniformly fallible one.

**Five evaluators of "one-box minus two-box".** *LICDT* (post 04, l. 25): $\mathbb{E}[U \mid E{=}1, A{=}1] - \mathbb{E}[U \mid E{=}1, A{=}2]$ — the exploration-episode learner the author's correction is about. *ITT* (the exchange's "condition on the intention"; the doc's Definition 4 causal learner in its tagged form): $\mathbb{E}[U \mid D{=}1] - \mathbb{E}[U \mid D{=}2]$. *LIEDT*: $\mathbb{E}[U \mid A{=}1] - \mathbb{E}[U \mid A{=}2]$ on all rounds. *Forcing* at the live node (Theorem 1's $G_q$ at one instance; classical CDT per the P04 ledger's FA-19′): $-1$ always. *Deviation* (Definition 22 / Theorem 2): $V(p{=}1) - V(p{=}2)$ with $\varepsilon$ held.

**Vocabulary for §2–§6.** A *correlate* $X$ is any event whose frequency differs across the agent's acts. The act has *sources of variation*: the deliberate draw $D$ (the policy's output), the coin $K$ (exploration), and *forced writes* (the lesion, the `bot` branch). A source is *clean* w.r.t. $X$ if it is independent of $X$ at fixed policy, *dirty* otherwise. "Policy" means v2's Definition 4 object — a function on decision-points, the same function in every world — run under Definition 6; §3 says why this typing is load-bearing.

**The doc's two agents (§7).** $\mathfrak{A}$ is T-respecting ((α): $\square\varphi \to P(\varphi){=}1$) and responsive ((β): crosses iff $\mathbb{E}_P[U \mid \mathrm{Cross}] > \mathbb{E}_P[U \mid \mathrm{Stay}]$); Lemma 8 gives $T \vdash \mathrm{Cross} \to \square\bot$ and Proposition 9 has $\mathfrak{A}$ stay. $\mathfrak{B}$ "cross[es] if and only if $10 - 20 \cdot P_0(\square\bot) > 0$, where $P_0(\square\bot)$ is $\mathfrak{B}$'s credence in inconsistency formed without regard to its own act — the credence of an agent whose causal model has $\square\bot$ as an exogenous root and its act as an intervention upon that model" (Proposition 10, quoted); $T \nvdash \mathrm{Cross}_\mathfrak{B} \to \square\bot$.

## 1. The author's correction, verified (question a)

**Proposition A [checked; §9 rows A].** In the model of §0 with an $(r_D, r_K)$-Omega, for every $p \in (0,1)$ and $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$:
$$
\mathrm{LICDT\ gap} \;=\; 10\,(2 r_K - 1) - 1, \qquad \frac{dV}{dp} \;=\; (1-\varepsilon)\,\big(10\,(2 r_D - 1) - 1\big).
$$
So the exploration-episode learner one-boxes iff $r_K > 0.55$, independently of $r_D$, $p$, $\varepsilon$; the policy-optimal act is one-box iff $r_D > 0.55$; and the learner's best-response fixed point is $V$-optimal iff $(r_K > 0.55) \Leftrightarrow (r_D > 0.55)$. Against a label-probe Omega the LICDT gap is $-1$ at every $p$ while $V(p) = 9 q_\varepsilon$, so the learner two-boxes and earns $0.45$ where $8.55$ was available (at $\varepsilon = 0.1$).

*Proof sketch [derived].* On exploration rounds $A = K$ and $\Omega$ depends on $K$ alone, so $P(\Omega{=}1 \mid E, A{=}1) = r_K$ and $P(\Omega{=}1 \mid E, A{=}2) = 1 - r_K$; subtract. $V(p)$ is affine in $p$ because the policy enters only through $D$ on the $(1-\varepsilon)$ non-exploration mass, where $P(\Omega{=}1 \mid D) = r_D$ or $1 - r_D$. The label-probe row: $\Omega \perp (D, E, K)$ makes every act-conditional gap $-1$, while the label moves the box. $\square$

**The author's sentence, made exact [derived].** "This works in cases where Omega cannot predict the exploration, so that they're true interventions, but it doesn't work when Omega predicts those well" — with $r_K = \tfrac12$ (exploration unpredictable) the learner two-boxes; with $r_K$ high it one-boxes ("an EDT-like picture"). "The agent does poorly precisely when the interventionist assumption fits reality well" — the assumption fits when $r_K = \tfrac12$, and that is the row where, for any $r_D > 0.55$, the learner sits at the two-box fixed point ($p^\ast = 0$) and forgoes $(1-\varepsilon)(10(2r_D - 1) - 1)$ per round — $8.1$ at $r_D = 1$, $\varepsilon = 0.1$. Two riders the numbers add: (i) "predicts the exploration well" is necessary and sufficient for the learner to one-box, but the one-boxing is *correct* only if Omega also reads the deliberate draw — the coin-only Omega $(\tfrac12, 1)$ makes the learner one-box where two-boxing pays (§9: $V = 4.05$ vs $4.95$); the learner's verdict tracks the wrong parameter in both directions, not just the author's. (ii) The Monte-Carlo learner (§9 B) reproduces the fixed points; a learner that varies its *policy* across blocks and regresses payoff on $p$ adopts the optimal endpoint in all four predictor rows, at the cost of its exploration blocks.

**What was backwards in the exchange's "satisfying account" [derived; reading of the Docs text, ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED as to its author's intent].** The paragraph said learning-CDT's causal knowledge is inherited from randomization, "works in a stationary environment like the double lesion, and it fails exactly where the environment responds to the current procedure rather than to the act, which is Newcomb", and glossed the failure as "trusting experiments its predecessors ran, in a world that responds to something those experiments couldn't vary". Three things are wrong with this, in increasing order of importance. First, the account assumed exploration-based learning yields two-boxing; Proposition A says it yields two-boxing only when $r_K \approx \tfrac12$. Second, the account located the failure in the experiments' *invalidity* — an unvaried variable the world responds to, i.e. confounding. In the failing row the experiments are *maximally valid* interventions on the act: the coin is independent of everything, Omega included, and the learner correctly estimates $\partial U / \partial A$ at fixed policy as $-1$. It loses because a correct answer about the act is the wrong estimand; and in the row where the experiments are *invalid* as interventions — Omega reads the coin, so the randomization is not exogenous to the box — the learner one-boxes and wins. The learner's welfare is anti-correlated with the validity of its interventionist assumption, which is the sign the author says was flipped. Third, "something those experiments couldn't vary" is false of the policy-level version of the same learner: varying $p$ across blocks *does* move a policy-reading Omega, and the block-regression sees $V(p) = 9 q_\varepsilon$. The inheritance story is fine at the policy level and wrong only at the act level.

**The exchange's own construction one-boxes [checked].** The paragraph's learner "samples an intention from its policy, records the sample, and acts on it unless overridden. … Conditioning on the intention rather than the realized act" — that is ITT. Against the deliberate-sharing Omega $(1, \tfrac12)$ the ITT gap is $+8.1 = $ the deviation gap; against a $(0.9, \tfrac12)$-Omega, $+6.3$. The intention-conditioning learner does not two-box against any predictor that reads what it conditions on. It two-boxes ($-0.9$) against the label-probe Omega — whose dependence on the policy is *label*-dependence (Remark 5.1), which no conditioning on a draw can see. So the identity "ITT = deviation" holds exactly when every policy-dependence in the tree is branching-dependence on the live draw (Definition 6′ sharing $D$, or a predictor that reads $D$), and fails under Definition 6's independent redraws, where Omega's simulation is a label-probe — the P04 ledger's P04-B′ wedge [checked here on the one-point tree; the general statement is [derived] from Remark 5.1].

## 2. The clean-source principle and the Law of Logical Causality are duals (question b)

**Clean-source principle (CSP)** [reconstructed from the exchange's first message: "what matters is never which source is 'the agent' but which source is independent of the confounder … Condition on the clean source, then"]. Given a correlate $X$ treated as a confounder, estimate the act's effect from the source of variation in the act that is independent of $X$, and discard the correlation carried by the other sources.

*Double lesion* (doc Definition 2; the note's §1 bypass and overwrite trees): the deliberate draw is independent of the lesion by Definition 6 (Lemma 3); the forced writes are not. Condition on the draw: gap $0$ (doc Proposition 6), smoke. **Right** [checked, §9 D]. *Troll Bridge with a coin*: the coin is independent of Con(PA); the deliberation of a T-respecting responsive agent is tied to it (doc Lemma 8). Condition on coin-crossings: bridge safe, cross. **Right** [derived]. *Newcomb against a policy-reading Omega*: against the deliberate-sharing Omega the coin is the clean source and the deliberation the dirty one; condition on the coin: LICDT gap $-1$, two-box — **wrong** ($V = 9p$). Against the label-probe Omega both sources are clean at fixed policy; condition on either: gap $-1$, two-box — **wrong** again, for a different reason (no source carries the label). [checked, §9 A]

**Law of Logical Causality (LLC)** (post 03, l. 65, verbatim): "If conditioning on any event changes the probability an agent assigns to its own action, that event **must** be treated as causally downstream." Under self-knowledge of the policy (ratifiability), an event changes $P(\text{own action})$ iff it covaries with the draw at fixed policy — the 2020 comment's "knowledge of the state of your deliberation" (post 04, l. 149) locates the screening at the deliberation, not the realized act; MIRI-LOG l. 646 states the same law as "events … downstream of acts if they are correlated with exploration bits".

*Newcomb against the deliberate-sharing Omega*: $\Omega = D$, so conditioning on $\Omega$ moves $P(D)$; downstream; one-box. **Right** [checked: LIEDT $+8.0$, ITT $+8.1$]. *Troll Bridge*: for $\mathfrak{A}$, $P(\mathrm{Cross} \mid \square\bot) \neq P(\mathrm{Cross} \mid \neg\square\bot)$ (Diffractor, post 03 l. 84); the LLC puts Con(PA) downstream; treated as an effect of crossing it makes crossing "cause" inconsistency; stay. **Wrong**, and the author said so in 2017 (l. 89): "LLC asserts that the troll, and even CON(PA), is downstream. And, it seems to get into trouble *because* it treats it as downstream." Two riders. (i) Against the label-probe Omega the LLC's antecedent is *false* — at fixed policy $\Omega \perp D$ — so the LLC two-boxes there (P04-B′); the LLC gets Newcomb right only against a predictor that reads the draw. (ii) On the overwrite lesion read on the realized act, the lesion covaries with the act at fixed policy and the LLC misplaces it downstream; the author saw this too (MIRI-LOG l. 662: a lesion-biased trembling hand — "We definitely don't want to place it downstream of us"); the LLC has to be stated on the *draw*, and then it is right on the lesion. [derived; both quotations verbatim]

**The duality [derived].** Let $\mathrm{Cov}(D)$ be the set of correlates that covary with the deliberate draw at fixed policy. In the double lesion $\mathrm{Cov}(D) = \emptyset$ (the lesion reaches the act only through forced writes), so CSP and LLC agree: smoke. In Troll Bridge and in Newcomb-against-a-draw-reader, $\mathrm{Cov}(D)$ contains the decision-relevant correlate, and the two principles assign it *opposite* roles: CSP says it is a confounder — screen it off by conditioning on the source it does not touch; LLC says it is an effect — respond to it. Each principle is one rule applied to the whole class; Troll Bridge and Newcomb are the two problems on which the class has opposite membership, so each principle gets exactly one of them. Neither can split $\mathrm{Cov}(D)$, because both read only the *symmetric* fact of covariation with the deliberation; the split needs the *direction* — whether the correlate shapes the deliberation (Con(PA) → act, through (α) and (β); the lesion → deliberation in the note's channel 3) or is shaped by it (the policy → Omega's simulation). §3 reads the direction off the run law.

## 3. Policy-responsiveness (question c)

**Definition PR [derived from Definitions 4, 6, 22].** Fix a concrete problem $B$, a procedure $C$ and a queried point $d$. An event $X$ is *policy-responsive at $d$* if $\nu_{B, C[d \mapsto a]}(X)$ varies with $a \in A_d$ — the all-instance deviation of the label at $d$ moves $X$'s objective frequency. The **criterion**: an evaluator at $d$ should *respond to* $X$ (treat it as downstream, as an effect of what it chooses) iff $X$ is policy-responsive at $d$, and should hold $X$ at its marginal (treat it as an exogenous root) otherwise. The quantity it measures is a counterfactual on the run law — vary $C$, hold $B$ — not a credence conditional on the agent's own act; that is the whole of its content, and §3's last paragraph says why it has to be so.

**Against the doc's $\mathfrak{A}$ and $\mathfrak{B}$ [derived].** $\mathfrak{B}$'s rule is $\mathrm{cf}(\mathrm{cross}) = \sum_w P_0(w)\, E(\mathrm{cross}, w)$ with $w \in \{\square\bot, \neg\square\bot\}$ and $E$ read off the troll's stated rule ($+10$, $-10$): it *is* the marginal formula of the companion note's §9.3, with $P(\mathrm{Incon})$ there $= P_0(\square\bot)$ here and $E$ learned "from proofs about the environment's rule". So Proposition 10's $\mathfrak{B}$ is the §9.3 agent at the act level — the identification the coordinator asked for holds. PR agrees with $\mathfrak{B}$ on the bridge because $\square\bot$ is a root: no d-node is upstream of it, $\nu(\square\bot)$ is constant in $C$, hence non-responsive, hence marginalized. For $\mathfrak{A}$ the doc's Lemma 8 supplies $P(\square\bot \mid \mathrm{Cross}) = 1$ — a *credence conditional*, produced by (α) and (β), i.e. by $\mathfrak{A}$'s rule being a function of the theorem; it is not a dependence of $\square\bot$ on the policy, and PR does not read it. The doc's remark that "an agent causal about the bridge but evidential about itself … is afflicted like $\mathfrak{A}$" is PR's operative content stated negatively: an agent that lets its credence in a *non-responsive* variable answer to a theorem about its own act has treated a root as an effect. Where PR goes beyond $\mathfrak{B}$ is Newcomb: $\mathfrak{B}$'s recipe, applied with $w = \Omega$, two-boxes against every Omega it models as an exogenous root; PR puts $\Omega$ inside $E(\pi, \cdot)$ because $\Omega$ is responsive, and one-boxes. The PR agent is $\mathfrak{B}$ lifted to the policy with its root set fixed by responsiveness (§4).

**The objection: Löb runs at the policy level too [derived].** A T-respecting agent that conditions on its *policy* — $\mathrm{cross} \leftrightarrow \mathbb{E}_P[U \mid \pi{=}\mathrm{cross}] > \mathbb{E}_P[U \mid \pi{=}\mathrm{stay}]$ — satisfies a policy-level (β), Lemma 8 goes through verbatim with $\pi$ for Cross, and $P(\square\bot \mid \pi{=}\mathrm{cross}) = 1$. Does this make Con(PA) look policy-responsive? No, and the reason is the distinction between two relations that share the word "policy". *Correlation* is symmetric and lives in credence: for the proof-respecting rule, adopted policy and Con(PA) are correlated, because the rule's output at a point is a function of which theorems the state contains, and $\square\bot$ decides that (in a $\neg$Con world the agent proves everything, including its own inaction, and the chicken clause fires). *Responsiveness* is asymmetric and lives in the run law: vary $C$, hold $B$; $\nu_{B,C}(\square\bot)$ is constant. The correlation runs Con(PA) → state → act — the note's channel 1, state-mediated, which per-point calibration dissolves — never act → Con(PA). So "no policy choice changes Con(PA), whereas a proof-respecting rule's act is correlated with it" is stable, **given** three typing facts: (i) a policy is Definition 4's function on points, the same function in every world (an *input* to the run law, not an event in the world — Remark 4.2's $\rho_d(a)$ is the event, and conditioning on it is what the Löbian policy-level agent does); (ii) worlds depend on the policy only through queries (Remark 5.1's branch/label principle), so an event is responsive only if a $d$-node lies upstream of it — none lies upstream of a root; (iii) the agent's logic enters through the state $s_d$, so "PA inconsistent" is a different point, and Con(PA) selects the point rather than the draw. Without (i) — if "policy" meant the agent's realized behaviour as a function of the world — Con(PA) would covary with "the policy" and the distinction would collapse into the LLC's. One boundary [guess]: if the policy includes the *choice of T* (the agent picks the logic it will respect), then "Con($T_{\text{chosen}}$)" is responsive and PR says respond — pick a consistent $T$ — which is not a bug; Gödel makes it unverifiable, not wrong.

**Why the criterion must read the run law and not a conditional.** The doc's §8: "What cannot be written down, in a formalism of frequencies, is how the agent came by its conditional — by counting or by proving". PR concedes this and sidesteps it: it never asks for $P(X \mid \text{act})$ at all, by counting or by proving; it asks for $\nu_{B,C[d \mapsto a]}(X)$, which is a *deviation*, not a conditional, and is defined by the tree even where the conditional is undefined, vacuous, or a theorem. The separation the doc locates in "frequency vs theorem" can therefore also be stated inside a frequency formalism, provided the formalism has the deviation (the tree, not the shadow $\widehat{B}$ — the P04 ledger's P04-H′). This is not a refutation of the doc's claim about *conditionals*; it is the observation that Definition 22 is not a conditional.

## 4. v2's two counterfactuals, and the referent Q6 asks for (question d)

**Definition 22's evaluator satisfies PR by construction [derived].** "$X$ is policy-responsive at $d$" was defined as "$\nu_{B, C[d\mapsto a]}(X)$ varies with $a$" — the all-instance deviation's effect on $X$. An evaluator that ranks acts by $V_B(C[d \mapsto a])$ moves every responsive event and none of the non-responsive ones, so PR is less a test the deviation passes than the deviation's content stated as a rule about correlates. Its non-triviality is that it *disagrees* with LICDT, LIEDT, ITT, forcing, CSP and LLC on named problems (§3's table), and that it names which variable must be varied (the label) and where the dependence is measured (the run law).

*Newcomb with a simulation node* (Omega's node a $d$-node, Remark 3.3): $C[d \mapsto 1]$ moves Omega's draw too; $V(C[d\mapsto 1]) = 9 > 0 = V(C[d\mapsto 2])$; one-box [checked, P04 ledger Import 3′; §9 A's deviation column]. *TB($\theta$)* (root chance `bot` w.p. $\theta$ → forced cross at $-10$; else $d$: cross $+10$, stay $0$): `bot` is a root, unmoved; $V(\text{cross}) - V(\text{stay}) = 10(1-\theta) > 0$; cross at every $\theta < 1$ [checked, §9 C]. One discrepancy the numbers expose: Proposition 10's rule is $10 - 20\theta$, not $10(1-\theta)$, because TB($\theta$) is *bypass*-shaped — the inconsistent world writes "cross" without consulting $d$ (the run's own "forced crosser ≠ deliberator") — whereas the doc's bridge has the agent cross in the $\square\bot$ world by its own chicken clause. The doc-faithful tree consults $d$ in both worlds (payoff $-10$ on cross $\wedge\, \square\bot$, $+10$ on cross $\wedge\, \neg\square\bot$, $0$ on stay) and its deviation gap is exactly $10(1-2\theta)$ [derived; §9 C's exploring-agent row uses this shape]. Both trees classify $\square\bot$ as non-responsive; they differ only in whether the label controls the inconsistent world's act. Compare the act-conditional at the stay label: $P(\text{bad} \mid \text{cross}) = \theta / (\theta + (1-\theta)\varepsilon/2) \to 1$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$ — every tremble device (Definition 10's limit, the run's D2, LICDT) is swamped by the forced mass, which is the note's §9.2 observation, now with numbers; the deviation is not a tremble and is untouched.

**Theorem 1's forcing [derived; checked on the probe-Newcomb via the ledger's numbers].** Two objects share the name. *Single-instance forcing at the live node*, $G_q(C, a)$ with Omega's node still drawing $C(d)$: $10q - 1$ vs $10q$, two-box — the classical-CDT counterfactual (the ledger's R2-real), which holds a responsive variable fixed and so **fails** PR on Newcomb; on TB($\theta$) there is one $d$-node and forcing $=$ deviation, cross. *Theorem 1's condition* (the fiber sum $\sum_{q : d_q = d} R_q G_q$): counts Omega's simulation as an instance of $d$ and one-boxes, $9q + 9$ vs $9q$ (the ledger's P04-B′, "SIA over a coin"); it responds to Omega's prediction *as the agent's own act at another instance*, which on a tree where every policy-dependence is branching-dependence (all v2 trees, Remark 5.1) reaches the same events the deviation reaches, weighted per occurrence instead of all-at-once (Definition 22, comment (ii)). So Theorem 1's condition **satisfies** PR on both test trees; its single-node ingredient does not.

**Is the deviation counterfactual Q6's referent? [derived, with one flagged cost and one open reading].** Q6 asks whether $\mathrm{cf}_s$ should be calibrated to deviation or forcing "given that either choice condemns classical CDT by fiat (Remark 3.11)", and whether there is a neutral referent. PR's answer: (i) the deviation is the referent that passes the battery — one-box, refuse, pay-iff-$y>x$, smoke, cross, take 10 — and it is tree-intrinsic; (ii) it condemns classical CDT, and PR says this is the run law's verdict, not the framework's: classical CDT's counterfactual holds a responsive variable at its statistics, and the run law says the variable moves; (iii) the neutral-referent hope was misplaced because *any* referent must classify Omega's prediction, and there are only two classifications. The flagged cost: at a Definition-7-recorded point under Definition 6 the deviation agrees with conditioning at positive acts — setting the label to $a$ at the only $d$-node on each $O_d$-run yields exactly the subtree the conditional on $a$ selects, since the draw is independent of what precedes it (Lemma 3) [derived; the ledger's FA-20′(iii) concurs, [reconstructed]]; but against a label-probe Omega with $\varepsilon$-exploration, the deviation's value of two-boxing is $0$ while the conditional on the *realized* (explored) two-boxings is $10 q_\varepsilon \approx 10$. The author's constraint — "the counterfactual expectations equal the conditional probabilities whenever the latter are well-defined" (exchange) — is then violated at the act and satisfied at the policy (a block-experiment on $\pi = $ two-box does yield $\approx 0$). Post 12's book is stated at the act; the run's S12 (AlexMennen's $O(\varepsilon^2)$) says exploration-sourced divergence is not exploitable at first order; post 13's RCDT wants asymptotic agreement "in circumstances where we have good feedback". Which level the author's FDT constraint lives at decides whether the deviation is *the* referent or the policy-level referent only; §8 asks. Two further residues PR does not settle: the SSA/SIA weighting between Definition 22 and Theorem 1 (Q9) — both satisfy PR — and the updateless flavour (mugging: the deviation pays; every updateful evaluator refuses).

**What the learner that converges to it must be [derived; handed to [policy-level-fdt-learner](policy-level-fdt-learner.md), which builds it].** (L1) Its manipulated variable is the *label* at $d$, not the act: its experiments are policy epochs, and since $V$ is affine in the label two distinct values suffice (the Docs' identification argument, which is correct at this level; §9 B). (L2) It estimates $\nu_{B, C[d\mapsto a]}(X)$ for each candidate $X$ by regressing frequencies on the label; a variable whose frequency does not move is marginalized, one whose frequency moves goes into the response function $E(\pi, \cdot)$ — so it is $\mathfrak{B}$ with the root set *learned*, not stipulated. (L3) It never updates a non-responsive variable on its own policy — the companion note's "exogeneity of the draw is a policy, not a belief", lifted to the label; for logical variables this is a *structural prior* (one world, one value of Con(PA); no experiment varies it), and it is exactly the doc's $P_0$ "formed without regard to its own act". (L4) It composes: $\mathrm{cf}(\pi) = \sum_w P_0(w)\, E(\pi, w)$, $w$ the non-responsive coordinates, Omega's simulation inside $E$. (L5) For the deterministic ideal agent the estimate is inherited from the epochs in which the label varied; the author's correction says this inheritance is sound *because* a policy-reading Omega responded to exactly that variation. (L6) It must not be a tremble device: on TB($\theta$) every act-conditional tremble estimator is swamped (§9 C), the block estimator sees $10(1-\theta)$. Costs it inherits: the run's Q-traps (acts no epoch ever explores are prior-determined), Remark 5.2's multiplicity, and the stricter troll (§6) — its policy-level exploration is "crossing against its estimate" and is punished, a trap the doc's fallible-troll paragraph would classify with $\mathfrak{A}_M$'s records that "do not help".

## 5. The 2×2 (question e)

Columns: $X$ is **policy-responsive** or **not** (§3). The column reads the *direction* of a dirty covariation: dirty-and-responsive means $X$ reads the deliberation (an effect); dirty-and-non-responsive means $X$ shapes the deliberation (a confound). ## 6. The binding coin (question f)

**"Randomization-CDT passes Troll Bridge only through a binding coin" [derived; agrees with the exchange for the conditional agent].** In the original bridge the troll's rule is "blow up iff the agent crosses due to the $P(\mathrm{cross}){=}0$ clause" (post 11 l. 96), which under Con never fires (a consistent T-respecting agent cannot prove its own inaction), so the trigger is in effect $\square\bot$. A coin-crossing does not go through that clause; the coin is independent of Con(PA); so the coin **is a clean source** in the original bridge, and its crossings are safe [derived]. Why *binding*: for a T-respecting responsive conditional agent, an advisory coin the deliberation can veto is vetoed exactly when $\square(\mathrm{cross} \to \mathrm{bad})$ is in hand, so every actual crossing is deliberate, $\mathrm{cross} \to \square\bot$ discharges, and Löb fires. A binding coin at rate $\varepsilon$ makes the pooled event "cross" contain crossings that hold under Con, so from $\square(\mathrm{cross} \to \mathrm{bad})$ one gets only $\mathrm{cross} \to (\text{coin} \wedge \mathrm{Con}) \vee \square\bot$, not $\mathrm{cross} \to \mathrm{bad}$; the hypothesis does not discharge; the pooled conditional agent crosses deliberately too. So the exchange's first message was right that pooled conditioning with a clean binding coin stalls Löb, and its later "deliberate crossings remain Löb-bound" is right only for the agent that *separates* the sources (conditions on deliberate crossing alone) — which is the CSP agent [derived; no source states this split, so [reconstructed] as to the exchange's intent].

**"Redefining the troll to punish coin-crossings is just making the coin a dirty source" [derived].** The exploring-agent troll (post 11 l. 147: blow up on exploration *or* inconsistency) punishes every coin-crossing, so $\mathrm{cross} \to (\text{coin} \to \mathrm{bad}) \vee (\text{delib} \to \square\bot \to \mathrm{bad})$, the hypothesis discharges, Löb fires, and the pooled agent stays. Correct as stated. **Is it "real Troll Bridge"?** By the 2020 tests (2020-10-19 ll. 30, 35): *test 1* (100% failure regardless of prior) — yes for a proof-respecting exploring agent, since post 11's proof is prior-free; no for an RL agent, whose failure "depend[s] on the agent's starting beliefs" (l. 32). *Test 2* (a way out exists "if only it would take it") — yes: a deliberate crossing at the consistent point pays $+10$ and the troll does not punish it. So the exploring-agent troll is real Troll Bridge for proof-respecting agents and an unfair exploration-punisher for RL agents, which is post 11's own division (l. 176–178) [reconstructed]. The stricter troll — punish "unjustified" crossing, against the agent's own estimate — passes test 1 and fails test 2 (2020-10-19 l. 35: "there is nothing the agent can possibly do"; 2023-05-28 l. 33: "not … such a compelling philosophical dilemma"); it is the trap of §3's table, and it traps the policy-level learner of §4 too.

**Is the coin needed at all? The §9.3 alternative [derived].** The companion note's marginal-formula learner — the doc's $\mathfrak{B}$ with $E$ learned — crosses deliberately by $10 - 20 P_0(\square\bot)$ and never consults $P(\square\bot \mid \mathrm{cross})$; a deliberate crossing from that rule is not a "dumb reason", the exploring-agent troll's trigger is $\text{explored} \vee \square\bot$, so its deliberate crossings are unpunished; Löb stalls at (β) exactly as in the doc's Proposition 10 argument. No coin. The doc's fragility remark supplies the general form: the coin does for the conditional agent, on $\varepsilon$ of its acts, what non-responsiveness does for $\mathfrak{B}$ on all of them — a binding coin is $\mathfrak{B}$ for a fraction $\varepsilon$ of one's acts, a prosthetic for the disposition. So the exchange's "whether that counts as passing depends on whether you count the coin as the agent" resolves: the coin counts as the agent's iff it is binding, i.e. iff the deliberation is non-responsive to proofs about the coin's crossings; extend that non-responsiveness to the deliberation itself and the coin is idle. The residual asymmetry the exchange pressed — "I don't see a learning path to" $\mathfrak{B}$'s stipulation — is (L3) of §4: for logical roots the non-responsiveness is a structural prior, not learned; for the lesion and the disaster it is learned by (L2). [derived]

**A caution about TB($\theta$) as a stand-in [derived; §9 C].** In the actual bridge all crossings a consistent agent ever makes are safe, so a *tagged* binding-coin agent learns $P(\mathrm{bad} \mid \text{coin-cross}) = 0$ and the exchange's "learns that they are safe" is right. TB($\theta$) makes inconsistency a *frequency* — a `bot` branch that crosses and is blown up w.p. $\theta$ — and on the realized-act algebra pools those crossings with the coin's; then a coin at rate $\varepsilon \ll \theta$ is swamped ($P(\mathrm{bad} \mid \mathrm{cross}) \to 1$, §9 C) and the pooled tremble agent stays. That is the smoking-lesion crossover of the companion note's §8, not a fact about the bridge; it is the doc's §8 point — "there is nothing for a theorem to be but a frequency of 1" — biting the tree from the other side. The deviation evaluator does not see it either way.
