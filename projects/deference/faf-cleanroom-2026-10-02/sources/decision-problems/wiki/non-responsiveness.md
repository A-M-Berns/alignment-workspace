# Non-responsiveness: three grades, the leak, and whether it can be learned

Part of `two-lesions-index` · Part III, page 12 · Previous: [learning-cdt-renderings](learning-cdt-renderings.md) · Next: [newcomb-correction](newcomb-correction.md). Status per `two-lesions-provenance`. Source: [non-responsiveness-learnability](../directions/non-responsiveness-learnability.md) (all checks there); [two-lesions-doc-2026-09-18](../two-lesions-doc-2026-09-18.md) §7; [two-lesions-exchange-2026-09-19](../two-lesions-exchange-2026-09-19.md); [smoking-lesion-exploration-and-boundaries](../smoking-lesion-exploration-and-boundaries.md) §9.3–§9.4; the CDT=EDT? sequence post 13 and the 2023-05-28 journal.

**Headline.** Non-responsiveness — the property that protects the doc's causal agent from the Löbian lesion — comes in three grades, and only the strongest is stable: the action-relevant expectation must be formed over a *language that does not name the agent*, because under coherence a believed theorem about one's own act moves one's marginal through one's self-prediction [derived; checked]. No single-agent learning path produces it; what exists is a tiling path (a responsive agent that has found the proof endorses installing a non-responsive successor), a class-relative regret justification, and no coherence justification at all [derived]. The cost the exchange named "dispositional T-respect" is vacuous in a sound-theory Troll Bridge and genuine in code-reading trap environments, and no derivation-free test tells the two apart [derived]. Stated as an axiom on v2's counterfactual component it is *exogenous rigidity relative to a designated subalgebra*, a counterfactual theory rather than a calibration, and it is jointly satisfiable with the FDT constraint exactly when the act coordinate is the draw [derived; checked on TB($\theta$)].

## Where it comes from

The doc's Lemma 8 (`lobian-lesion`) uses the evidential agent's responsiveness once: from $P(\Box\bot \mid \mathrm{Cross}) = 1$ to $\neg\mathrm{Cross}$, the step licensed by

$$
(\beta)\qquad T \vdash \mathrm{Cross} \leftrightarrow \big(E_P[U \mid \mathrm{Cross}] > E_P[U \mid \mathrm{Stay}]\big).
$$

"Remove the responsiveness and the proof fails." Proposition 10's $\mathfrak{B}$ crosses iff $10 - 20 P_0(\Box\bot) > 0$ with $P_0$ "formed without regard to its own act — the credence of an agent whose causal model has $\Box\bot$ as an exogenous root and its act as an intervention upon that model." The exchange generalized: what every CDT shares "is that the causal model, once fixed, is not revised by information about the agent's own current act. Call that non-responsiveness."

## Three grades

**NR1 (formula-level).** The action-relevant expectation is $\mathrm{cf}(a) = \sum_w P(w)\, E(a, w)$: a function of $P$'s marginal over designated exogenous coordinates $w$ and of the response function $E$, never of the act-conditionals $P(\cdot \mid a)$. This is $\mathfrak{B}$ read literally and the session note's §9.3 learner. It replaces (β) by

$$
(\beta')\qquad T \vdash \mathrm{Cross}_\mathfrak{B} \leftrightarrow \Big(\textstyle\sum_w P_0(w) E(\mathrm{cross}, w) > \sum_w P_0(w) E(\mathrm{stay}, w)\Big),
$$

so Lemma 8's single use of (β) has no counterpart. Non-responsiveness is *not* the bare negation of (β) — a constant crosser, a constant stayer and a coin all negate (β) — but the replacement of (β) by (β′) with a specific $P_0$ [derived].

**NR2 (marginal shielding).** NR1 plus: the marginal $P_0(w)$ is not moved by theorems whose statement fixes the agent's own act. NR1 alone does not give this. **The leak** [derived; checked]: if the marginal is the agent's ordinary coherent credence $P$ over a language containing $\mathrm{Cross}$, a believed theorem $\mathrm{Cross} \to \Box\bot$ forces

$$
P(\Box\bot) \;\geq\; P(\mathrm{Cross} \wedge (\mathrm{Cross} \to \Box\bot)) \;\geq\; P(\mathrm{Cross}) - \varepsilon,
$$

so the marginal is moved through the self-prediction. The session note's §9.3 said composite proofs "constrain $P(w \mid a)$, which the formula never consults" and "do not constrain $P(w)$"; that is true only if $P(\mathrm{Cross})$ is not high or $\mathrm{Cross}$ is not in the language. The doc's own fragility remark has it: "An agent causal about the bridge but evidential about itself — one that, told that crossing entails inconsistency and predicting that it will cross, raises its credence in inconsistency accordingly — satisfies the analogue of (β) and is afflicted like $\mathfrak{A}$." Refinement: without provably accurate self-prediction such an agent escapes the *original* troll, because under the hypothetical proof its every crossing is a self-mispredicted one — the largest coherent $P(\mathrm{cross})$ at which it still crosses is below $\tfrac12 + \varepsilon$ [checked] — and self-misprediction is not $\Box\bot$; but a troll that fires on self-mispredicted crossing makes the lemma hold for it and Löb fires ([troll-variants](troll-variants.md)). So NR2 is the operative property, and the doc's "formed without regard to its own act" is its informal name.

**NR3 (anonymous marginal; derivation-free).** NR2 secured algebraically: $P_0$ is a credence — a logical inductor, say — over the sublanguage $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{exo}}$ of sentences that do not contain the agent's code. Then $\mathrm{Cross} \to \Box\bot$ is not a sentence of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{exo}}$, $P_0(\Box\bot)$ is a fact about PA alone, and no self-prediction exists in $P_0$ to carry the theorem's weight. The agent still has a full inductor $P$ over the whole language — it assigns $1$ to every theorem, including composite ones; it predicts its own act; it records tagged draws; it learns $\hat E(a, w)$ from records and consumes proofs about the environment's rule (∀w E(cross, w) = bad *is* in $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{exo}}$, since $a$ is a variable there). Only the marginal fed to $\mathrm{cf}$ is anonymous. A **two-inductor architecture**.

**Not "not respecting logic."** In the author's 2020 sense — updating to probability 1 when something is proven — the NR3 agent respects logic completely: $P$ assigns $1$ to $\mathrm{Cross} \to \Box\bot$ once proved, and $P_0$ assigns $1$ to every theorem *of its language*. Nothing is disrespected; something is not consulted. Under the hypothetical proof the two inductors disagree about $\Box\bot$ — $P(\Box\bot) \approx P(\mathrm{Cross}) \approx 1$, $P_0(\Box\bot)$ small — which is the exact content of "can hypothetically disagree but never actually see the disagreement" (`13-my-current-take-on-counterfactuals` l. 787); in the actual world the proof does not exist and they agree.

**What updates what** [derived]: data about own draws → $\hat E$; proofs about $E(a, \cdot)$ with $a$ a variable → $\hat E$; proofs about $w$ alone → $P_0$; composite proofs $A_t = a \to \psi$ → $P$ only, never $P_0$, never $\mathrm{cf}$. The line is syntactic — does the sentence name the agent — and needs no derivation inspection. It does *not* separate self-created composite theorems (Löbian, false for the NR agent's world) from environment-created ones (a troll that reads the agent's code); both are excluded together.

**Steelman symmetry.** The Smoking Lesion Steelman robots were built so that "the epistemic module cannot watch the decision module" (`01-smoking-lesion-steelman` ll. 49–53), which *blocks* the Tickle Defense and hurts EDT. NR3 is the same blinding applied only to the marginal that feeds $\mathrm{cf}$, and it *implements* the Tickle Defense as a policy where Gödel forbids it as a belief. One device, opposite sign, depending on which module is blinded.

## Form versus content

[learning-cdt-renderings](learning-cdt-renderings.md): every interventional supposition is non-responsive on the algebra of the act's non-descendants, and so is the evidential conditional on the pre-query algebra at every recorded point (Lemma 3′). Non-responsiveness is therefore the *form* of every CDT; its *content* is the fixed algebra $\mathcal{E}_0$. Classical CDT puts Omega's prediction in $\mathcal{E}_0$ and two-boxes; $\mathfrak{B}$ puts $\Box\bot$ in and the prediction out and crosses *and* one-boxes at the policy level ([policy-responsiveness](policy-responsiveness.md)); EDT puts nothing in. The exchange's tension — responsiveness gets Newcomb right and Troll Bridge wrong, non-responsiveness the reverse — is a tension between two settings of $\mathcal{E}_0$, resolved by the third.

## Is there a learning path?

The exchange: "That isn't a belief experience could deliver. It's a stipulation about which correlations count for decision, roughly 'ignore theorems about my own act,' and I don't see a learning path to it." Four candidates.

**(i) Conditional contracts** (the author's 2023-05-28 proposal: a market good for $P(U = u \mid A = a)$ that refunds when the condition fails; a wealthy enforcement trader making bundle price equal conditional). Mechanics [derived; toy checked]: the enforcer's payoff is $q - c$ if $A = a$ is realized and exactly $0$ otherwise, in every world, so logical-induction non-exploitability forces $\sum_{t : A_t = a} |c_t - q_t| < \infty$. **Claim CC-limit** [derived; not found in the sources as stated]: in an LI market with bundled conditional contracts, bundle price equals conditional expectation along realized-condition rounds, in the limit, for every prior — the enforcer is not optional, and the prior fixes only the rate. Does this give NR2? No. A proven $\mathrm{cross} \to \mathrm{bad}$ lets traders short $(\mathrm{cross} \wedge \neg\mathrm{bad})$ for free, so the conditional $q \to 0$; the bundle price is untouched at round $t$ and moves only through realized crossings. The per-round Löbian step fails (crossing with $c_t > 0 > q_t$ is consistent with the proof, the gap being finite-time), so the market passes the original troll — but it is *responsive with delay*: if the proven implication were true, crossing stops after $0, 5, 11, 17$ rounds at physical-to-enforcer wealth ratios $1, 10, 100, 1000$ [checked, toy market]; if false the enforcer bleeds and the market overrides the "proof." That is "learn counterfactuals in a way that respects reality, rather than trying to deduce them" (post 13 ll. 766–787) made concrete. The market shields on rounds where the act does not occur — the rounds the Löbian hypothetical is not about. Whether an *asymptotic* Löb over $\forall^\infty t\,(\mathrm{cross}_t \to \mathrm{bad}_t)$ undoes the finite-horizon shield is open [guess]. Verdict: not a learning path to NR2; a distinct mechanism (finite-time gap plus Σ₁-soundness) that also passes the original troll, paying with delayed responsiveness whose closing time is the prior. VOI exploration, as in the session note, presupposes the bundle as the action-relevant object and is bounded by CC-limit.

**(ii) Dominance and regret.** In the iterated fallible troll the responsive learner that stops at the proof earns $2620$ over $2000$ rounds and the non-responsive one $17520$; both are calibrated on what they test [checked]. Regret against always-cross is $O(1)$ versus linear — learning-theoretic in form. But the benchmark class decides its force: on environments whose trigger factors through $(a, w_{\mathrm{exo}})$ the non-responsive learner wins; on environments that read the agent (Newcomb) an anonymous-marginal learner that files the box among the exogenous coordinates two-boxes with linear regret. Regret adjudicates class-relatively; choosing the class is the design stance. And the doc's fallible-troll paragraph says data pushes the responsive agent the *wrong* way: the record of safe crossings "raises $\mathfrak{A}$'s credence that T is consistent, whence, by Lemma 8, its credence that it will stay." **The tagged-covariation refinement** shrinks the design residue [checked for Newcomb]: let the exogenous coordinates be whatever the agent's own *deliberate-tagged* draws are empirically independent of. In Newcomb against a policy-reading Omega, box and deliberate act covary while coin-tagged interventions show the $+1$ two-boxing effect given the box; the rule files the box under $E$ and one-boxes. In Troll Bridge no tagged crossing ever covaries with $\Box\bot$, so $\Box\bot$ stays exogenous. What remains stipulated is only the composite-theorem exclusion.

**(iii) Tiling.** A responsive agent that has found the proof endorses installing a non-responsive successor [derived]: post-proof, $\mathfrak{A}$ values "install $\mathfrak{B}$" by $E_P[U \mid M]$; $\mathrm{Cross}_\mathfrak{B}$ is a sentence about a *different program*, for which Lemma 8 does not hold (Proposition 10); so $E_P[U \mid M] \approx +10$ per round against $0$. $\mathfrak{A}$ modifies, by conditional expectation, with no counterfactual. The doc's observation that the predecessors' record "is a record of sentences about other programs" is exactly why the successor is safe. This is the only path found on which responsiveness produces non-responsiveness, and it is across agents, not within one. A meta-troll punishing the successor's crossings when the predecessor "modified for a dumb reason" re-closes the trap — but it reads the predecessor's decision rule, so it belongs with the code-reading environments below. Post-proof $\mathfrak{A}$ does not endorse its own future decisions; it prefers another agent's — a reflective-inconsistency signature.

**(iv) A Dutch book on the composite.** None [derived]. Post-proof $\mathfrak{A}$ has $\mathrm{cf}$ = conditional, $P(\mathrm{bad}\mid\mathrm{cross}) = 1$, $P(\mathrm{cross}) \approx 0$; every belief it tests is calibrated; a bookie cannot make it cross. $\mathfrak{B}$'s disparity with $\mathfrak{A}$'s conditional is hypothetical only. Neither agent is bookable. The book of `12-dutch-booking-cdt-revised-argument` says "the two had better agree" (l. 29); it constrains, it does not select. This matches the author's 2020 diagnosis that there are "no epistemic principles pointing the other way."

**Verdict.** No learning path within a single agent; a tiling path across agents; regret class-relative; coherence silent. Non-responsiveness is a stipulation — but one the responsive agent itself would make about its successor once it holds the proof.

## Dispositional T-respect: vacuous or genuine?

The exchange: "CDT is disposed to ignore a proof about itself that, thanks to that very disposition, never materializes." In a sound-theory Troll Bridge the disposition is never exercised against a true non-vacuous proof [derived]: while $\mathfrak{B}$ crosses no proof of $\mathrm{Cross}_\mathfrak{B} \to \Box\bot$ exists; if $P_0(\Box\bot) \geq \tfrac12$ it stays, $T \vdash \neg\mathrm{Cross}_\mathfrak{B}$, and the theorem appears vacuously. The cost is modal, not actual: the disposition's whole work is to make the lemma false, i.e. to make the proof not exist.

As a *policy* it is not vacuous. NR3 excludes every sentence naming the agent, including true environment-created ones. A bridge that is really out *as stated through the agent's code* — the troll fires on any program with $\mathfrak{B}$'s code — gives $P$ a true one-line composite theorem; $\mathfrak{B}$ ignores it, crosses once, and its tagged record then teaches $\hat E(\mathrm{cross}, \cdot) = \mathrm{bad}$. Cost: one crossing, or everything if the first crossing is a trap. A bridge that is out *as stated through the environment* — $\forall w\, E(\mathrm{cross}, w) = \mathrm{bad}$ — is in $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{exo}}$ and respected at once. So the disposition costs at most finitely many exploratory losses per environment where a code-reading theorem is true and not $E$-derivable, is fatal in trap versions, and costs nothing exactly where the theorem is self-created — because there its truth is manufactured by the responsiveness it presupposes (the doc's "the lesion is self-created," moral four). The syntactic line does not separate the two kinds of theorem, and derivation inspection does not either ("would the theorem survive replacing my decision rule by a constant crosser?" answers no for Löb and also no for a troll keyed to the whole code). They are told apart only by the environment class — the design residue again.

## Axiom NR for the next version

**Axiom NR (exogenous rigidity).** [derived] Designate a subalgebra $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}} \subseteq \mathcal{E}$. For every non-$\bot$ act $a$: (i) $P^a_s(e) = P_s(e)$ for all $e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$; (ii) $P^a_s(\cdot \mid e) = P_s(\cdot \mid a \wedge e)$ and $V^a_s(X \wedge a \wedge e) = V_s(X \wedge a \wedge e)$ wherever $P_s(a \wedge e) > 0$. Hence

$$
P^a_s \;=\; \sum_{e} P_s(e)\, P_s(\cdot \mid a, e),
$$

the dependency-hypothesis / K-partition form of causal choice on the Jeffrey–Bolker chassis. Success holds automatically. Because $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$ is a parameter and not a tree-intrinsic referent, this is a *counterfactual theory* in Definition 20's sense, not counterfactual calibration, and Remark 3.11's worry is not triggered: classical CDT is the choice that puts Newcomb's prediction in $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$, and it is thereby located, not condemned.

**Consistency with "cf = conditional wherever defined"** [derived; checked on TB($\theta$)]. $\sum_e P(e) P(X \mid a, e) = P(X \mid a)$ for all $X$ iff $P(e \mid a) = P(e)$ for all $e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$, i.e. iff $a \perp \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$ under $P_s$. At a recording point with $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$ the pre-query chance algebra, Lemma 3 gives that independence, so Axiom NR and Definition 20's evidential criterion coincide. At a non-recording point on the act-event algebra they conflict: on TB($\theta$) at the stay label, $O_d = \top$, $\theta = 0.1$, the act-conditional is defined and equals $-10$ while NR-cf(cross) $= 8$. The conflict dissolves when the coordinate is the draw: $E[U \mid \mathrm{draw} = \mathrm{cross}] = 8 = $ NR-cf, because the draw $\perp$ bot by Definition 6. And NR-cf $= V_B(C[d \mapsto \mathrm{cross}]) = 8$, Theorem 2's deviation, $\neq$ Theorem 1's forcing $G_q = 10$: at $O = \top$ the axiom's marginal is the population marginal, so it selects the deviation referent ([policy-responsiveness](policy-responsiveness.md)).

**What fixes $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$** is Q6 restated. Static side: pre-query chance, which Definition 6 makes independent of the draw. Learning side: what the agent's own deliberate-tagged draws are empirically independent of — which puts Newcomb's prediction in $E$ and $\Box\bot$ in $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$, so that one-boxing and crossing follow from one principle, and the CDT/EDT dispute becomes a dispute over $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$.

**What Remark 3.11 would become.** "$\mathrm{cf}_s$ is constrained by success and, relative to a designated exogenous subalgebra, by exogenous rigidity; this is a counterfactual theory, not a calibration. At recording points it coincides with the evidential criterion. At null acts it pins the exogenous marginal and leaves the response free — where a theory of counterfactuals does its work. Classical CDT is the theory with the prediction in $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$. Q6 becomes: what fixes $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$." Two costs: Remark 3.9's tremble-induced cf already satisfies NR on trees, so the axiom adds content only at acts trembles do not reach or when $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exo}}$ is not pre-query chance; and the Löbian motivation cannot be stated in v2's type — no provability, no sentences — so Axiom NR's static shadow is exogenous rigidity, and its Troll Bridge content lives in the learning story (`next-coherent-care`).
