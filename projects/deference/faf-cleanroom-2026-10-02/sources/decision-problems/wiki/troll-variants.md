# Troll variants: which are dilemmas and which are traps

Part of `two-lesions-index` · Part IV, page 19 · Previous: [marginal-formula-learner](marginal-formula-learner.md) · Next: `next-coherent-care`. Status per `two-lesions-provenance`. **Headline.** The trolls of the literature sort by the author's two 2020 tests — does the example make crossing impossible regardless of prior, and does the agent still have a way out if only it would take it — into dilemmas and traps [reconstructed]. The original and fallible trolls are dilemmas for proof-respecting agents and are passed by the marginal-formula agent from any prior that explores; the exploring-agent troll is a dilemma for proof-respecting agents and an unfair exploration-punisher for reinforcement learners; the stricter troll and the self-misprediction troll are traps that catch specific architectures by their priors or their self-prediction; Bunthut's X-troll is inert in one reading and a genuinely-out bridge in the other [derived; modal step checked].

## The two tests

`Logsidian/journals/2020-10-19.md` (the author, verbatim): "The first test of 'real Troll Bridge' is: does the example make crossing totally impossible? Or does it depend on the agent's starting beliefs?" And: "The second test is whether we can strengthen the issue to 100% failure **while still having a sense that the agent has a way out, if only it would take it**." A problem that fails the second test — the trapped agent has no way out — is a trap, not a dilemma; the 2023 journal says of one such troll that it "would not be such a compelling philosophical dilemma."

## Two readings of "crossing for a dumb reason"

The author's 2019 post: the troll blows up the bridge if the agent crosses "for a dumb reason — for example, due to unsound logic," and "random exploration falls into this category." The exploring-agent variant makes the trigger *exploration or inconsistency*; then for an ε-explorer given the proof, every crossing is either exploratory (punished) or deliberate (implying misfire, hence $\Box\bot$, hence punished), so the Löbian lemma holds and Löb closes. VOI crossing is not "random exploration": it is the argmax of total expected value including information. The 2023 journal's stricter troll is what punishes *it* — and that troll fails the second test. So the dilemma's force depends on VOI crossing being unpunished, which the original and exploring-agent trolls leave it.

## The binding coin, again

[clean-source-and-llc](clean-source-and-llc.md) §"The binding coin": in the original bridge a binding coin is a clean source and its crossings are safe; an advisory coin the deliberation can veto leaves deliberate crossings Löb-bound; the exploring-agent troll dirties the coin. The marginal-formula agent needs no coin — "a binding coin is $\mathfrak{B}$ for a fraction $\varepsilon$ of one's acts."

## Bunthut's circularity objection

Bunthut (`13-my-current-take-on-counterfactuals` l. 826, 872): learning is circular — "a good mechanism would have to learn to be susceptible to Troll Bridge, especially with the 'right' prior" — and "the alternative agent who believes that crossing is −10, and sticks to that … is right, and the bridge *would* blow up if *he* were to cross it." Conceded at the level of inference, answered at the level of use [derived]: no step of Lemma 8 is invalid, and $\mathfrak{B}$ makes no different *inference* — it makes a different *use* of inferences. Bunthut is right that the refuser is calibrated: on TB($\theta$) both pure labels are calibrated, the stay label on the merits. Calibration cannot adjudicate; value does (Definition 21), and Definition 16 names what each rule does: $\mathfrak{B}$ makes $\mathrm{Cross} \wedge \mathrm{Blow}$ inconsistent up to $P_0(\Box\bot)$; $\mathfrak{A}$ makes $\mathrm{Cross} \wedge \neg\mathrm{Blow}$ inconsistent — the wrong event. That is Soares's "decisions are for making bad outcomes inconsistent" read against the doc's moral four ("created by the decision rule"). As a description this is provable (Lemma 8 against Proposition 10); as an answer to "why be $\mathfrak{B}$" it relocates the question to [non-responsiveness](non-responsiveness.md)'s learning-path section, where the answer is value and tiling, not learning or coherence — and there Bunthut's residue stands: the architecture is not learned. What is inserted, though, is not a belief about the bridge ("insert the relevant takeaway directly into its prior"): $P_0(\Box\bot)$ is non-dogmatic and $E$ is learned; what is inserted is a language boundary.

## The author's concession and its answer

The author to Bunthut (ll. 801–803): "I'm not *blocking* Troll Bridge problems, I'm *making the definition of rational agent broad enough that crossing is permissible*. But if I think the Troll Bridge proof is actively irrational, I *should* be able to actually *rule it out*. IE, specify an X which is *inconsistent with* PA. I don't have any proposal for that." The wiki's answer is not a weaker $X$ but a *structured* one: the marginal-formula agent's $\mathrm{cf}$ respects proofs about $E$ and about $P(w)$ — in a language that does not name the agent — and not about the composite. Reading 2 of the X-troll shows the structure is robust: any troll that targets what the rule actually consults makes the bridge genuinely out.
