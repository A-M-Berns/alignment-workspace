# The clean-source principle and the Law of Logical Causality are duals

Part of `two-lesions-index` · Part III, page 14 · Previous: [newcomb-correction](newcomb-correction.md) · Next: [policy-responsiveness](policy-responsiveness.md). Status per `two-lesions-provenance`. Source: [clean-source-and-policy-responsiveness](../directions/clean-source-and-policy-responsiveness.md) §2, §5, §6; the CDT=EDT? sequence posts 03, 04, 05, 11; `Logsidian/pages/MIRI-LOG.md`; `Logsidian/journals/2020-10-19.md`.

**Headline.** Two rules for handling a correlate of one's act — screen it off by conditioning on the source of variation independent of it, or respond to it as an effect because it covaries with one's deliberation — classify the *same* class of correlates and assign it opposite roles, so each gets exactly one of Newcomb and Troll Bridge right [derived]. Neither reads *direction*: whether the correlate shapes the deliberation or is shaped by it. The criterion that does is [policy-responsiveness](policy-responsiveness.md). A binding coin is a clean source in the original bridge, the exploring-agent troll makes it dirty, and the agent of the doc's Proposition 10 needs no coin, because a binding coin is that agent on $\varepsilon$ of one's acts [derived].

## The clean-source principle (CSP)

[reconstructed from the exchange's first message: "what matters is never which source is 'the agent' but which source is independent of the confounder … Condition on the clean source, then"]. Given a correlate $X$ treated as a confounder, estimate the act's effect from the source of variation in the act that is independent of $X$, and discard the correlation carried by the other sources.

*Double lesion*: the deliberate draw is independent of the lesion by Definition 6, the forced writes are not; condition on the draw: gap $0$, smoke. **Right** [checked]. *Troll Bridge with a coin*: the coin is independent of Con(PA), the deliberation of a T-respecting responsive agent is tied to it (Lemma 8); condition on coin-crossings: bridge safe, cross. **Right** [derived]. *Newcomb against a policy-reading Omega*: against the deliberate-sharing Omega the coin is the clean source and the deliberation the dirty one; condition on the coin: LICDT gap $-1$, two-box — **wrong**. Against the label-probe Omega both sources are clean at fixed policy; condition on either: gap $-1$, two-box — **wrong** again, for a different reason: no source carries the label [checked].

## The Law of Logical Causality (LLC)

The author, 2017 (`03-smoking-lesion-steelman-iii-revenge-of-the-tickle-defense` l. 65, verbatim): "If conditioning on any event changes the probability an agent assigns to its own action, that event **must** be treated as causally downstream." Under self-knowledge of the policy, an event changes $P(\text{own action})$ iff it covaries with the draw at fixed policy — the 2020 comment's "knowledge of the state of your deliberation" (`04-comparing-licdt-and-liedt` l. 149) locates the screening at the deliberation, not the realized act, and MIRI-LOG l. 646 states the same law as "events … downstream of acts if they are correlated with exploration bits."

*Newcomb against the deliberate-sharing Omega*: $\Omega = D$, so conditioning on $\Omega$ moves $P(D)$; downstream; one-box. **Right** [checked: LIEDT $+8.0$, ITT $+8.1$]. *Troll Bridge*: for $\mathfrak{A}$, $P(\mathrm{Cross} \mid \Box\bot) \neq P(\mathrm{Cross} \mid \neg\Box\bot)$; the LLC puts Con(PA) downstream; treated as an effect of crossing it makes crossing "cause" inconsistency; stay. **Wrong** — and the author said so in 2017, replying to Diffractor (l. 89): "LLC asserts that the troll, and even CON(PA), is downstream. And, it seems to get into trouble *because* it treats it as downstream." Two riders: against the label-probe Omega the LLC's antecedent is *false* — at fixed policy $\Omega \perp D$ — so the LLC two-boxes there; and on the overwrite lesion read on the realized act the lesion covaries with the act at fixed policy and the LLC misplaces it downstream — the author saw this too (MIRI-LOG l. 662: a lesion-biased trembling hand, "We definitely don't want to place it downstream of us"). The LLC has to be stated on the *draw*, and then it is right on the lesion.

## The duality

Let $\mathrm{Cov}(D)$ be the set of correlates that covary with the deliberate draw at fixed policy. In the double lesion $\mathrm{Cov}(D) = \emptyset$ (the lesion reaches the act only through forced writes), so CSP and LLC agree: smoke. In Troll Bridge and in Newcomb against a draw-reader, $\mathrm{Cov}(D)$ contains the decision-relevant correlate, and the two principles assign it *opposite* roles: CSP says confounder — screen it off by conditioning on the source it does not touch; LLC says effect — respond to it. Each principle is one rule applied to the whole class; Troll Bridge and Newcomb are the two problems on which the class has opposite membership, so each principle gets exactly one of them. Neither can split $\mathrm{Cov}(D)$, because both read only the *symmetric* fact of covariation; the split needs the *direction* — whether the correlate shapes the deliberation (Con(PA) → act through (α) and (β); the lesion → deliberation in channel 3) or is shaped by it (the policy → Omega's simulation). [policy-responsiveness](policy-responsiveness.md) reads the direction off the run law. [derived]

## The 2×2

Rows: the agent's *deliberate* source of variation is **clean** or **dirty** with respect to the decision-relevant correlate $X$. Columns: $X$ is **policy-responsive** or not ([policy-responsiveness](policy-responsiveness.md)). The column reads the direction of a dirty covariation: dirty-and-responsive means $X$ reads the deliberation (an effect); dirty-and-non-responsive means $X$ shapes it (a confound). **Reading.** The label (Definition 22's deviation) is the right conditioning variable in all four cells; the act-level proxies each cover a pattern. Conditioning on the deliberate draw is right on the diagonal and wrong on the anti-diagonal. Conditioning on a coin is right in the non-responsive column (it screens a confound) and wrong in the responsive one (it screens an effect): CSP is "always screen the covariate." The LLC is right where "covaries with $D$" and "responsive" agree in truth value and wrong where they disagree — bottom-left (Con(PA)) and top-right (the label-probe). The anti-diagonal is where the sequence's two 2017 laws — implementability ("things correlated with our randomness must be downstream of it," `05-mixed-strategy-ratifiability-implies-cdt-edt-1` l. 46) and the LLC — each cover one cell and fail the other. Question for the author: retire both in favour of one directional rule, or keep them as the two act-level halves?

## The binding coin

The exchange: "randomization-CDT passes Troll Bridge in the sense that its coin-crossings are safe and it learns that they are, and fails in the sense that its deliberate crossings remain Löb-bound … Only variation the deliberation cannot veto breaks it." And: "redefining the troll to punish coin-crossings is in these terms just making the coin a dirty source."

**The coin is a clean source in the original bridge** [derived]. The troll's rule is "blow up iff the agent crosses due to the $P(\mathrm{cross}) = 0$ clause" (post 11 l. 96), which under consistency never fires for a T-respecting agent, so the trigger is in effect $\Box\bot$. A coin-crossing does not go through that clause; the coin is independent of Con(PA); its crossings are safe. Why *binding*: for a responsive conditional agent, an advisory coin the deliberation can veto is vetoed exactly when $\Box(\mathrm{cross} \to \mathrm{bad})$ is in hand, so every actual crossing is deliberate, $\mathrm{cross} \to \Box\bot$ discharges, and Löb fires. A binding coin at rate $\varepsilon$ makes the pooled event "cross" contain crossings that hold under consistency, so from $\Box(\mathrm{cross} \to \mathrm{bad})$ one gets only $\mathrm{cross} \to (\text{coin} \wedge \mathrm{Con}) \vee \Box\bot$, not $\mathrm{cross} \to \mathrm{bad}$; the hypothesis does not discharge; the *pooled* conditional agent crosses deliberately too. So the exchange's first message was right that pooled conditioning with a clean binding coin stalls Löb, and its later "deliberate crossings remain Löb-bound" is right only for the agent that *separates* the sources and conditions on deliberate crossing alone — which is the CSP agent.

**The exploring-agent troll dirties it** ([troll-variants](troll-variants.md)): blow up on exploration *or* inconsistency, so $\mathrm{cross} \to (\text{coin} \to \mathrm{bad}) \vee (\text{delib} \to \Box\bot \to \mathrm{bad})$, the hypothesis discharges, Löb fires, and the pooled agent stays. Is it "real Troll Bridge"? By the author's two 2020 tests (`Logsidian/journals/2020-10-19.md` ll. 30, 35): test 1, 100% failure regardless of prior — yes for a proof-respecting exploring agent (post 11's proof is prior-free), no for an RL agent; test 2, a way out exists "if only it would take it" — yes: a deliberate crossing at the consistent point pays $+10$ and is not punished. So it is real Troll Bridge for proof-respecting agents and an unfair exploration-punisher for RL agents, which is post 11's own division.

**Is the coin needed at all?** No [derived]. The marginal-formula agent ([marginal-formula-learner](marginal-formula-learner.md)) crosses deliberately by $10 - 20 P_0(\Box\bot)$ and never consults $P(\Box\bot \mid \mathrm{cross})$; a deliberate crossing from that rule is not a "dumb reason"; the exploring-agent troll's trigger is $\text{explored} \vee \Box\bot$, so its deliberate crossings are unpunished; Löb stalls at (β) exactly as in Proposition 10. The doc's fragility remark supplies the general form: the coin does for the conditional agent, on $\varepsilon$ of its acts, what non-responsiveness does for $\mathfrak{B}$ on all of them — **a binding coin is $\mathfrak{B}$ for a fraction $\varepsilon$ of one's acts**, a prosthetic for the disposition. The exchange's "whether that counts as passing depends on whether you count the coin as the agent" resolves: the coin counts as the agent's iff it is binding, i.e. iff the deliberation is non-responsive to proofs about the coin's crossings; extend that non-responsiveness to the deliberation itself and the coin is idle. What remains stipulated is the composite-theorem exclusion for *logical* roots ([non-responsiveness](non-responsiveness.md)).

**A caution about the tree.** In the actual bridge every crossing a consistent agent makes is safe, so a tagged binding-coin agent learns $P(\mathrm{bad} \mid \text{coin-cross}) = 0$. The run's TB($\theta$) makes inconsistency a *frequency* — a `bot` branch that crosses and is blown up w.p. $\theta$ — and on the realized-act algebra pools those crossings with the coin's; then a coin at rate $\varepsilon \ll \theta$ is swamped and the pooled tremble agent stays [checked]. That is the smoking-lesion crossover of [double-lesion-fixed-points](double-lesion-fixed-points.md), not a fact about the bridge; the deviation evaluator does not see it either way ([tb-theta-shadow](tb-theta-shadow.md)).
