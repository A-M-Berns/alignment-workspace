# Policy-responsiveness: the criterion, the battery, and the referent Q6 asks for

Part of `two-lesions-index` · Part III, page 15 · Previous: [clean-source-and-llc](clean-source-and-llc.md) · Next: `lobian-lesion`. Status per `two-lesions-provenance`. Source: [clean-source-and-policy-responsiveness](../directions/clean-source-and-policy-responsiveness.md) §3–§4, §9; `decision-problems-v2` §8 (Theorems 1–2, Definition 22), Q6; the run's P04 and P11 ledgers.

**Headline.** Respond to a correlate of your act — treat it as downstream, as an effect of what you choose — iff the all-instance point-deviation of your label moves its objective frequency; hold it at its marginal otherwise. This criterion passes a ten-problem battery on which the clean-source principle, the Law of Logical Causality, LICDT, intention-to-treat, LIEDT and single-node forcing each fail at least one named problem [derived; six rows checked]. It is v2's Definition 22 stated as a rule about correlates, and it reads the *run law* — vary the procedure, hold the tree — rather than any credence conditional, which is how it sidesteps the doc's "frequency versus theorem" point without refuting it [derived]. The doc's causal agent $\mathfrak{B}$ is this criterion's verdict on the bridge; lifted to the label with its exogenous roots fixed by responsiveness, it also one-boxes [derived]. One fork is left open: whether the author's FDT constraint, "counterfactual equals conditional wherever the conditional is defined," is meant at the act or at the policy.

## Definition

> **Definition PR.** [derived from Definitions 4, 6, 22] Fix a concrete problem $B$, a procedure $C$ and a queried point $d$. An event $X$ is *policy-responsive at $d$* if $\nu_{B, C[d \mapsto a]}(X)$ varies with $a \in A_d$ — the all-instance deviation of the label at $d$ moves $X$'s objective frequency. **The criterion:** an evaluator at $d$ should respond to $X$ iff $X$ is policy-responsive at $d$, and hold $X$ at its marginal (treat it as an exogenous root) otherwise.

The quantity measured is a counterfactual on the run law, not a credence conditional on the agent's own act. That is the whole of its content.

## Against the doc's two agents

$\mathfrak{B}$'s rule is $\mathrm{cf}(\mathrm{cross}) = \sum_w P_0(w)\, E(\mathrm{cross}, w)$ with $w \in \{\Box\bot, \neg\Box\bot\}$ and $E$ read off the troll's stated rule: it *is* the marginal formula of [marginal-formula-learner](marginal-formula-learner.md) at the act level. PR agrees with $\mathfrak{B}$ on the bridge because $\Box\bot$ is a root — no $d$-node lies upstream of it, $\nu(\Box\bot)$ is constant in $C$, hence non-responsive, hence marginalized. For $\mathfrak{A}$ the doc's Lemma 8 supplies $P(\Box\bot \mid \mathrm{Cross}) = 1$ — a *credence conditional*, produced by (α) and (β), i.e. by $\mathfrak{A}$'s rule being a function of the theorem; it is not a dependence of $\Box\bot$ on the policy, and PR does not read it. The doc's fragility remark — "an agent causal about the bridge but evidential about itself … is afflicted like $\mathfrak{A}$" — is PR's content stated negatively: an agent that lets its credence in a *non-responsive* variable answer to a theorem about its own act has treated a root as an effect. Where PR goes beyond $\mathfrak{B}$ is Newcomb: $\mathfrak{B}$'s recipe applied with $w = \Omega$ two-boxes against every Omega it models as a root; PR puts $\Omega$ inside $E(\pi, \cdot)$ because $\Omega$ is responsive, and one-boxes. **The PR agent is $\mathfrak{B}$ lifted to the label with its root set fixed by responsiveness.** [derived]

## The objection: Löb runs at the policy level too

A T-respecting agent that conditions on its *policy* — $\mathrm{cross} \leftrightarrow E_P[U \mid \pi{=}\mathrm{cross}] > E_P[U \mid \pi{=}\mathrm{stay}]$ — satisfies a policy-level (β), Lemma 8 goes through with $\pi$ for $\mathrm{Cross}$, and $P(\Box\bot \mid \pi{=}\mathrm{cross}) = 1$. Does this make Con(PA) look policy-responsive? No [derived], and the reason is a distinction between two relations sharing the word "policy." *Correlation* is symmetric and lives in credence: for the proof-respecting rule, adopted policy and Con(PA) are correlated because the rule's output is a function of which theorems the state contains, and $\Box\bot$ decides that. *Responsiveness* is asymmetric and lives in the run law: vary $C$, hold $B$; $\nu_{B,C}(\Box\bot)$ is constant. The correlation runs Con(PA) → state → act — channel 1 of `channels-and-agent-boundary`, state-mediated, dissolved by per-point calibration — never act → Con(PA).

So "no policy choice changes Con(PA), whereas a proof-respecting rule's act is correlated with it" is stable, **given** three typing facts: (i) a policy is Definition 4's function on points, the same function in every world — an *input* to the run law, not an event in the world (Remark 4.2's $\rho_d(a)$ is the event, and conditioning on it is what the Löbian policy-level agent does); (ii) worlds depend on the policy only through queries (Remark 5.1), so an event is responsive only if a $d$-node lies upstream of it — none lies upstream of a root; (iii) the agent's logic enters through the state, so "PA inconsistent" is a different point and Con(PA) selects the point rather than the draw. Without (i) — if "policy" meant the agent's realized behaviour as a function of the world — Con(PA) would covary with "the policy" and the distinction would collapse into the LLC's. One boundary [guess]: if the policy includes the *choice of theory*, then Con($T_{\text{chosen}}$) is responsive and PR says respond — pick a consistent theory — which is not a bug; Gödel makes it unverifiable, not wrong.

**Why the criterion must read the run law.** The doc's §8: "What cannot be written down, in a formalism of frequencies, is how the agent came by its conditional — by counting or by proving." PR concedes this and sidesteps it: it never asks for $P(X \mid \text{act})$ at all; it asks for $\nu_{B, C[d\mapsto a]}(X)$, a *deviation*, defined by the tree even where the conditional is undefined, vacuous, or a theorem. The separation the doc locates in "frequency versus theorem" can therefore also be stated inside a frequency formalism, provided the formalism has the deviation — the tree, not the extensional shadow. This is not a refutation of the doc's claim about conditionals; it is the observation that Definition 22 is not a conditional.

## v2's two counterfactuals under PR

**Definition 22's evaluator satisfies PR by construction** [derived]: an evaluator ranking acts by $V_B(C[d \mapsto a])$ moves every responsive event and none of the non-responsive ones. Its non-triviality is that it *disagrees* with LICDT, LIEDT, ITT, forcing, CSP and LLC on named problems, and that it names which variable must be varied (the label) and where the dependence is measured (the run law). Newcomb with a simulation node: the deviation moves Omega's draw too, $V(C[d\mapsto 1]) = 9 > 0$, one-box [checked]. TB($\theta$): `bot` is a root, unmoved; gap $10(1-\theta)$ on the run's tree, $10(1-2\theta)$ on the doc-faithful tree ([tb-theta-shadow](tb-theta-shadow.md)); cross [checked]. Compare the act-conditional at the stay label, $P(\mathrm{bad}\mid\mathrm{cross}) = \theta/(\theta + (1-\theta)\varepsilon/2) \to 1$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$: every tremble device is swamped by the forced mass; the deviation is not a tremble and is untouched.

**Theorem 1's forcing: two objects share the name** [derived; checked on the probe-Newcomb]. *Single-instance forcing at the live node*, $G_q(C, a)$ with Omega's node still drawing $C(d)$: $10q - 1$ vs $10q$, two-box — the classical-CDT counterfactual, which holds a responsive variable fixed and **fails** PR on Newcomb; on TB($\theta$) there is one node and forcing equals deviation. *Theorem 1's condition* (the fiber sum over all $d$-nodes, Omega's simulation included): $9q + 9$ vs $9q$, one-box — it responds to Omega's prediction *as the agent's own act at another instance*, and on a tree where every policy-dependence is branching-dependence it reaches the same events as the deviation, weighted per occurrence instead of all-at-once. So Theorem 1's condition **satisfies** PR on both test trees; its single-node ingredient does not.

## Is the deviation Q6's referent?

Q6 asks whether $\mathrm{cf}_s$ should be calibrated to deviation or forcing, "given that either choice condemns classical CDT by fiat (Remark 3.11)," and whether there is a neutral referent. PR's answer [derived]: (i) the deviation is the referent that passes the battery, and it is tree-intrinsic; (ii) it condemns classical CDT, and PR says this is the run law's verdict, not the framework's — classical CDT's counterfactual holds a responsive variable at its statistics, and the run law says the variable moves; (iii) the neutral-referent hope was misplaced because *any* referent must classify Omega's prediction, and there are only two classifications.

**The flagged cost — the level of the FDT constraint.** At a Definition-7-recorded point under Definition 6 the deviation agrees with conditioning at positive acts (setting the label at the only $d$-node on each run yields exactly the subtree the conditional selects, since the draw is independent of what precedes it). But against a label-probe Omega with $\varepsilon$-exploration, the deviation's value of two-boxing is $0$ while the conditional on the realized (explored) two-boxings is $10 q_\varepsilon \approx 10$. The author's constraint — "the counterfactual expectations equal the conditional probabilities whenever the latter are well-defined" — is then *violated at the act and satisfied at the policy* (a block experiment on $\pi = $ two-box does yield $\approx 0$). Post 12's book is stated at the act; the run's S12 says exploration-sourced divergence is not exploitable at first order; post 13's RCDT wants asymptotic agreement "in circumstances where we have good feedback." Which level the constraint lives at decides whether the deviation is *the* referent or the policy-level referent only. `two-lesions-open-questions` puts this first after the draw coordinate. Two residues PR does not settle: the SSA/SIA weighting between Definition 22 and Theorem 1 (Q9) — both satisfy PR — and the updateless flavour (the mugging: the deviation pays; every updateful evaluator refuses).

## What the learner that converges to it must be

Handed to [marginal-formula-learner](marginal-formula-learner.md), which builds it [derived]: (L1) its manipulated variable is the *label*, not the act — its experiments are policy epochs, and since $V$ is affine in the label two values suffice; (L2) it estimates $\nu_{B, C[d\mapsto a]}(X)$ by regressing frequencies on the label; a variable whose frequency does not move is marginalized, one whose frequency moves goes into the response function — $\mathfrak{B}$ with the root set *learned*; (L3) it never updates a non-responsive variable on its own policy — for logical variables a *structural prior*, exactly the doc's $P_0$; (L4) it composes $\mathrm{cf}(\pi) = \sum_w P_0(w) E(\pi, w)$ with Omega's simulation inside $E$; (L5) for the deterministic ideal agent the estimate is inherited from the epochs in which the label varied, and the author's correction says this inheritance is sound *because* a policy-reading Omega responded to that variation; (L6) it must not be a tremble device — on TB($\theta$) every act-conditional tremble estimator is swamped, the block estimator sees the gap. Costs it inherits: the run's Q-traps, Remark 5.2's multiplicity, and the stricter troll ([troll-variants](troll-variants.md)).
