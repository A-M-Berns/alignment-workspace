## 1. The corpus's "EDT ≠ UDT" was CDT ≠ UDT; the real separation is on the *full* branch of transparent Newcomb (blocking → resolved)

**Severity:** blocking the original conclusion of [when-udt-edt-diverge](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/when-udt-edt-diverge.md) (relabelled by the corpus on 2026-08-05; `EDTvsUDT.lean` header). **Resolved here** by `Transparent.edt_ne_udt_transparent`.

- The source's `EDTvsUDT.lean` holds the prediction fixed while varying the action; that is CDT's dominance argument. Re-founded as `Opaque.cdt_udt_differ` (T8), with the policy utility *derived* from `consistent` worlds rather than typed in.
- EDT one-boxes in the opaque model: `Opaque.edt_oneBoxes_opaque` (`E[U | A = one] = 10⁶ > E[U | A = two] = 1000` for any prior giving both policies positive weight). So the archived scorecard's "cross-situation dependence ⟹ UDT beats EDT" (`udt-rep-2-inventory` 2-031(b)) is not a theorem.
- The genuine separation needs (i) transparent Newcomb, (ii) a **prior over the agent's own policy** (the object the corpus never makes explicit), and (iii) a positive predictor error `ε`. `Transparent.edt_ne_udt_transparent`: for every full-support prior and `0 < ε < 999/1999`, the EDT rule (`argmax_a E[U | O = o, A = a]`, junk `−1`) strictly prefers `two` at *both* observations, while the UDT1.1 optimum is uniquely `(one, two)`. **The divergence is on the full branch** (`E[U | full, one] = 10⁶ < E[U | full, two] = 1001000`); on the empty branch both two-box (`edt_rule_twoBoxes_on_empty_of_pos_support`). The corpus's prose ([when-udt-edt-diverge](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/when-udt-edt-diverge.md) lines 84–88, [daniel-h-challenge](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/daniel-h-challenge.md) "Transparent Newcomb") tells the *empty*-branch story ("the empty box proves I'm not a one-boxer"), which does not separate the rules: the UDT policy also two-boxes there. Pointer: [when-udt-edt-diverge](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/when-udt-edt-diverge.md) line 86; [daniel-h-challenge](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/daniel-h-challenge.md) lines 25–29.

## 5. [formal-single-agent](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/formal-single-agent.md)'s definition of cross-situation dependence is non-constancy (imprecision)

**Severity:** imprecision. Lines 61–71: "there exists a policy `π` with `π(s) = a` … and another policy `π'` with `π'(s) = a` … such that `E[U | π] ≠ E[U | π'] despite agreeing on action at s`." Formalized literally as `FSADependence U := ∃ s π π', π s = π' s ∧ U π ≠ U π'`. Theorem `fsaDependence_iff_nonconstant`: on `|S| ≥ 2` this holds for **every non-constant `U`**, separable ones included (instance: `fsaDependence_sepIndicator` for `U π = [π 0 = 1] + [π 1 = 1]`). The intended notion is `topics/policy-types`'s ("`U(ē)` depends on both `Ä_ö` and `Ä_ö'`"), whose policy-utility form is non-separability; the two corpus definitions of *no* cross-situation dependence — the additive decomposition and the policy-dependence test — are related by `separable_of_rtest` / `Separable.exists_rtest`: they agree only given a presentation of `U` as a sum of per-situation rewards (every `U` has some such presentation, e.g. `R s π := U π / |S|`; it is the existence of an `RTest` presentation that separability amounts to). The refuted-definition row in the ledger quotes the sentence; the reading is the literal one.

## 6. [core-argument-draft](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/core-argument-draft.md) Step 4 "simplifies to" is false in general (local error)

**Severity:** local error. Lines 86–90: "The UDT formula: `a* = argmax_a E[U | π(s) = a, π is optimal elsewhere]`. For a coherent agent with a single policy, this simplifies to `a* = argmax_a E[U | π(s) = a]`." Under the only precise reading the source supports — the left side is `U (π*[s ↦ a])`, the right side is `E_μ[U | π(s) = a]` for a prior `μ` over policies (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED that this reading was intended) — the two argmaxes differ: `Simplification.rhs_argmax_ne_lhs_argmax` with `U (0,0) = 1`, `U (1,1) = 2`, `U (0,1) = U (1,0) = 0` and `μ (1,0) = 6/10`, `μ (0,0) = 3/10`, `μ (1,1) = μ (0,1) = 1/20`: `rhs 0 1 = 2/13 < rhs 0 0 = 6/7` while `lhs 0 1 = 2 > lhs 0 0 = 0`. The surviving neighbour (`rhs_eq_lhs_of_concentrated`): equality holds when the prior restricted to `{π s = a}` is concentrated on `π*[s ↦ a]` with positive weight. The point-mass form (`rhs_delta_eq`, `rhs_delta_junk`) gives equality at `a = π* s` only and the junk value `−1` at every other action — finding 7's phenomenon again. The same identification is assumed in [presentation-outline](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/presentation-outline.md) Part 5 and in [daniel-h-challenge](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/daniel-h-challenge.md)'s theorem; `UDTConnection.lean` asserts it in comments ("So: argmax_a E[U | π(o)=a] = argmax_a U'(π[o↦a])") with no proof.

## 7. The C&T `−1` convention is load-bearing: the separation vanishes under degenerate priors, and the C&T rule disagrees with UDT1.1 at `empty` (imprecision)

**Severity:** imprecision in the corpus (it never states the prior over the agent's own policy). Three theorems:

- `separation_vanishes_of_null_event` / `edt_rule_oneBoxes_on_full_of_eps_zero`: at `ε = 0`, or under a prior giving no mass to full-box two-boxers (`mass_full_two_eq_zero_of_prior_null`), `P(full, two) = 0`, so `E[U | full, two] = −1 < 10⁶ = E[U | full, one]`: the EDT rule "one-boxes" at `full` **by junk value**. The separation is convention-dependent under degenerate parameters; the `−1` convention makes every probability-zero action strictly dispreferred (udt-rep-090). With C&T's `[0,1]` payoffs and the earlier draft's `j = 2` ([communication-trust-translated](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/communication-trust-translated.md) line 269 footnote), the null action would instead be *preferred* — no theorem, one sentence.
- `ctScore_eq`: the C&T rule `argmax_a E[U | Π o = a]` (condition on the policy's action under the prior, no conditioning on the observation) reduces to the `μ`-average of the policy values `V ε π` over `{π : π o = a}`. It is a **third object**, distinct from both the EDT rule and the UDT1.1 optimum.
- `ctRule_oneBoxes_on_full_of_lt`: for every full-support prior and `ε < 999/2000` it one-boxes at `full` (the UDT1.1 threshold is `999/1999`; the two thresholds differ because the C&T rule averages over the `one`-group's minimum against the `two`-group's maximum). `ctRule_disagrees_with_udt11_on_empty`: under `½δ_{(one,one)} + ½δ_{(two,two)}` and `ε = 1/10`, the C&T rule strictly prefers `one` at `empty` (`E[U | Π empty = one] = 900000 > 101000`) while the UDT1.1 optimum `(one, two)` has `two` at `empty`. So the rule the corpus writes as "UDT" agrees with the policy optimum at `full` but can disagree at `empty`; the prior over one's own policy and the junk convention are load-bearing in both directions. This is the run's concrete form of udt-rep-090's bridge finding; `udt-ct-examples` should cite it.

## 8. The inventory's "encode `π` into a leaf" remark is wrong by cardinality (imprecision, in `udt-rep-2-inventory` 2-018, not in the source)

**Severity:** imprecision. `|Pol| = |A|^{|Node|}` with `|Node| = ∑_{k<n} |O|^k` (`Tree.card_pol`, `Tree.card_node`) exceeds `|Leaf| = |O|^n` for every nontrivial `n`, so no injective encoding of policies into terminal histories exists. The remark's *conclusion* — every policy utility with values in `[0,1]` arises from some environment — is true and is proved by randomizing the root: `Tree.exists_env_of_policyUtility` (mass `W π` on `o₀`, `1 − W π` on `o₁`, deterministic afterwards, `U l := [l 0 = o₀]`), for `n ≥ 1` and two distinct observations. `Tree.perverse_V` is the indicator case (udt-rep-2-019(a)).

## 9. [self-sealing-verdicts-and-trembles](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/self-sealing-verdicts-and-trembles.md) §2's decay variant does not fix the order of update and flip (imprecision)

**Severity:** imprecision. "If verdicts also drift (… spontaneous flip with probability `ρ` per episode)" leaves open whether the flip is applied before or after the episode's update. "Update then flip w.p. `ρ`" gives `P(1B→2B) = ρ`, `P(2B→1B) = ε(1−ρ) + (1−ε)ρ` and stationary `π(2B) = ρ / (2ρ + ε − 2ερ)` (formalized: `SelfSealing.stepDecay`, `statDecay_stationary`); "flip then update" gives `P(1B→2B) = ρ(1−ε)`, `P(2B→1B) = ρ + (1−ρ)ε` and stationary `π(2B) = ρ(1−ε) / (ρ(1−ε) + ρ + (1−ρ)ε)` (not formalized; recorded). Both give the source's odds `ρ : (ρ + ε)` to first order in small `ε, ρ`; the exact forms differ. The Coate–Loury citation is from model knowledge (the inventory's flag) and is not cited as verified here. §5 item 1's "exact stationary distribution" and "`f(ε, ρ)`" are `statDecay` and `perEpisodeValue` for the chosen order.

## 12. The mandate's T14 "policy-independent environment" would make the theorem vacuous (mandate imprecision, disclosed)

**Severity:** imprecision in `udt-policy-calc-mandate` T14 (not in a source). ## 14. T11 and 2-006: "why ain't'cha rich" is the ex-ante ordering; "decision problems can punish any procedure" needs a non-policy-determined environment (records)
