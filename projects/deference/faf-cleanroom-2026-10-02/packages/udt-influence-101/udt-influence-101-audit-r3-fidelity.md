**What the source says.** Post 8 l. 29: "μ is treated as an abbreviation for 'the algorithm which just selects an action from the probability distribution μ', and a … for 'the algorithm which just plays action a'" — the **algorithm construal**; Post 6 l. 115 likewise defines `Ā_{h,n}` as "the algorithm that, on any extension of h₁:ₙ, just plays action a with ℙ_{h₁:ₙ}(A(h, S̄_h) = a | h) probability". Under this construal F4 is correct: the Theorem 1 proof (Post 8 l. 199, "Use affineness of influence") applies Assumption 5 to `Ā_{h,n}`, a time-`n` precommitment that is not a constant algorithm; in `privModel` Assumptions 3, 4 and literal 5 hold and Theorem 1's identity fails. **But Post 8 l. 39 offers a second construal in the same passage**: "Or, you can get it to hold exactly in practice by just having beliefs about the influence of playing different actions, and treating 𝕀^ℙ_{h₁:ₙ}(μ, h, o) as an abbreviation for E_{a∼μ}[𝕀^ℙ_{h₁:ₙ}(a, h, o)]." Under this **abbreviation construal** Assumption 5 is definitional and cannot fail, and the content of the proof's `E_{a∼Ā_{h,n}}` step moves into Assumption 3, which then reads `𝕀(A, h, o) = E_{a∼Ā_{h,n}(h,S̄_h)}[𝕀(a, h, o)]`. In `privModel` **that fails** at `(0, ω₀, full)`: `𝕀^ℙ_∅(privA, hT, full) = 0` while `E_{a∼Ā_{hT,0}(hT,S̄)}[𝕀^ℙ_∅(a, hT, full)] = 𝕀^ℙ_∅(one-box, hT, full) = 1/2` — probe `PrivAvg.lean`, `not_A3_abbrev`, from the package's own `IP_privA`, `IP_ofAct_true`, `play_privA_ω₀` and the probe's `play_avg_privA`. So under the abbreviation construal `privModel` is not a counterexample to Theorem 1; it is a model in which Post 6's Assumption 3 is false — in precisely the scenario Post 6 l. 160 says Assumption 3 covers ("predictors which just look at your average behavior when you get into the situation they're thinking about").

**The finding that is construal-independent.** Diffractor's `Ā_{h,n}` is the *time-`n` conditional* average of `A` at `h`; a diffuse predictor that lacks the agent's time-`n` information responds to the *global* average over timelines reaching `h` (Post 7 l. 281, Sub-Assumption 4: "your average behavior when you get to the decision point, not which timeline you do it in"). The proof of Theorem 1 needs the two to coincide, and with private information at time `n` they do not. Whether this breaks Assumption 5's scope (algorithm construal: the stated A5 does not cover `Ā_{h,n}`, and Theorem 1 is false as stated) or Assumption 3's truth (abbreviation construal: A5 is a definition, A3 fails where Post 6 says it holds) depends on which of Post 8's two readings of `𝕀(μ, h, o)` one takes. The package's text commits to the first throughout without saying that it is a choice, and the `post8_theorem1_counterexample` docstring asserts the contrary ("not a matter of interpretation"). The r1 adversarial N3 objection ("interpretation-dependent") was rejected in r2 on the ground that the alternative would make Omega know the agent's private information; that disposes of a *different* alternative (what the predictor reads), not of Post 8 l. 39 (how the influence of a mixed action is defined). The opposite holds: `privA` is constant on each time-`0` atom (`constOnAtom_privA`), so its time-`0` conditional average at `hT` is itself.
