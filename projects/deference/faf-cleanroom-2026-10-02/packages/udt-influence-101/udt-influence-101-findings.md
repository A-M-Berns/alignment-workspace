# udt-influence-101 — findings about the sources

Severity scale ([STANDARDS](../../STANDARDS.md) §5): **blocking** (the note's conclusion does not follow) · **local error** · **imprecision** · **presentation**. Attribution claims are marked ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED. Lean names are in `Cleanroom.Udt.UdtInfluence101` unless qualified.

## F1 — Influences are two-sided derivatives of a signed ε-family (imprecision; mandate §3 vs [STANDARDS](../../STANDARDS.md) §3)

Post 6's Assumption 1 defines the influences as one-sided limits `lim_{ε→0} (1/ε)(… ε-played …)`. A `FinDist`-valued ε-family is only defined for `ε ∈ [0,1]`, so Mathlib's two-sided `deriv` would be junk on it. `PlaySpace.law` returns the polynomial extension of the mixture to all real `ε` (a signed weight outside `[0,1]`; mass one for every `ε`, `law_sum`), and the influence is the two-sided derivative of that extension, which equals the one-sided limit. Pointer: `Defs.lean` module docstring; report "Representation decisions" 1.

## F2 — Lemma 1 is false at a null observation that ε-play makes possible (local error in Post 6; silent division)

Post 6 Lemma 1 divides by `ℙ_{h₁:ₙ}(o)` for every `o`. If `ℙ_{h₁:ₙ}(o) = 0` but `ℙ^ε_{h₁:ₙ}(o) > 0` for `ε > 0`, the term `ℙ^ε(o)·𝔼^ε[U|o]` has derivative `𝕀^ℙ(o)·lim_ε 𝔼^ε[U|o]`, which is not `𝕀^ℙ(o)·(junk value of 𝔼[U|o])`. The identity holds under positivity of every `atom ∩ {o}` (`PlaySpace.PosObs`); `lemma1_split` carries it, `AllPosObs` is a hypothesis of Theorem 1. Pointer: `Calculus.lean`, `lemma1_split` docstring.

## F3 — Theorem 1's proof divides by `ℙ_{h₁:ₙ₊₁}(h)` on atoms that cannot reach `h` (local error in Post 8's proof; gap)

"Unpack the conditional expectation … `𝔼_{h₁:ₙ₊₁}[f·𝟙_h]/ℙ_{h₁:ₙ₊₁}(h)`" is `0/0` on a positive time-`(n+1)` atom below `h_{n+1}` from which `h` is unreachable, and on such an atom `𝕀^𝔼_{h₁:ₙ₊₁}(A,h)` need not vanish (a predictor elsewhere may read the profile at `h`). The theorem needs `PlaySpace.ReachPos h` (every positive atom along `h` can still reach `h`), used exactly once in the induction (`Theorem1.lean`, `theorem1`, Step D). The "fast feedback" step is, by contrast, a theorem (`not_reach_of_nextObs_ne`).

## F4 — Assumption 5 as stated does not cover the step that uses it, and Post 8's Theorem 1 is false under its stated assumptions (local error in Post 8; blocking for the general reading; refuted in Lean, repair round 2)

Post 8 states Assumption 5 for "the algorithm which just selects an action from μ" but applies it to `Ā_{h,n}` ("Use affineness of influence", `E_{a∼Ā_{h,n}}`), an algorithm that reads the unplannables up to time `n`. The two differ in profile-predictor environments: a node that reads the profile at `h` sees `prof Ā_{h,n} = prof A` (the global average), while Post 8's step uses the time-`n` conditional average `Ā_{h,n}(h, S̄_h)`; with a partially informative unplannable (a noisy signal of the latent world) these differ and the extended identity fails although the literal A5 holds (the literal one holds in every profile model by the product rule on affine factors — recorded as `ProfileModel.A5_ofDist_general`, OPEN). The package states `PlaySpace.A5` in the extended form (time-`n` precommitments along `h`), recovers Post 8's statement as `A5_ofDist`, and proves the extended form in the model under `OneEps h` and `ProfCoherent h` (`ProfileModel.A5_model`), both verified on the homework instance (`Home.hone`, `Home.hcoh`). Pointer: `Defs.lean` `A5` docstring; `ProfileA5.lean` module docstring.

**Repair round 1 (2026-10-02; superseded by repair round 2 below).** The separation claim was *argued, not formalized* (audit r1 fidelity N5, adversarial N3) and the severity was downgraded to "imprecision / interpretation-dependent" on the ground that whether Post 8's `Ā_{h,n}` "is" a constant algorithm depends on whether the predictor reads the global baseline profile or the time-`n` conditional average. Audit r2 (fidelity) B1 rejected the downgrade: the second horn means Omega knows the agent's private time-`n` information, the opposite of Post 7's diffuse predictor (Sub-Assumption 4, "the diffuse influence only depends on your average behavior"), and under the first horn Post 8's Theorem 1 is false as stated. The r1 text also noted that the Omega instance's `A5` holds only because its time-`0` atom is the whole support (`Omega.A5_asp` docstring).

**Repair round 2 (2026-10-02) — the failure is now in Lean** (`Priv.lean`, promoting and completing the audit's probe `A5Fails.lean`). The **private-signal model** `Priv.privModel` (`O = Act = Ξ = Bool`, `W = Unit`, horizon `2`; the root state `ξ₀` a fair coin the agent sees and the predictor does not; an always-smart Omega at the root fills the box iff the action it draws from the profile at `hT` is one-boxing; everything else fair; `U = [o₀ = true]`; all `128` worlds of weight `1/128`) and the algorithm `Priv.privA` (one-box at `hT` iff `ξ₀ = false`) give:

- `Priv.not_A5 : ¬ S.A5 hT`: the precommitment form fails — `privA` is a time-`0` precommitment at `ω₀` (`Priv.constOnAtom_privA`) with `𝕀^ℙ_∅(privA, hT, full) = 0` (`Priv.IP_privA`: its global profile is fair, `Priv.prof_privA`) while the `δ one-box`-average of the pure-action influences is `1/2` (`Priv.IP_ofAct_true`). ## F6 — The uniqueness glosses are false (refuted; the tie instance built in repair round 1)

[diffractor-synthesis](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/diffractor-synthesis.md) l. 67 "UDT1.01 is the unique algorithm where all instances agree to use it" and l. 194 "Under these axioms, `A = UDT1.01`", read as uniqueness of the time-0 influence maximizer: two distinct maximizers of `f^0_{h,S̄_h}` at one positive world give two distinct UDT1.01 algorithms (`PlaySpace.exists_two_isUDT101`, the conditional), and any algorithm supported on maximizers ties (`tie_iff`). **The tie is exhibited** (repair round 1, after audit r1 B1/B2 found the hypothesis uninhabited): in the sixteen-world profile model `Tie.tieModel` the depth-one node `hT` is inert — nobody reads its profile and its action enters neither the kernels nor the utility — so every influence at `hT` vanishes (`ProfileModel.IP_eq_zero_of_inert`, `IEo_eq_zero_of_inert`, `IE_eq_zero_of_inert`), `f^0_{hT,S̄} ≡ 0` (`fS_eq_zero_of_inert`, `Tie.score_hT`), both constant algorithms are UDT1.01 at `hT` (`Tie.isUDT101_ofAct_hT`) and `exists_two_isUDT101` instantiates (`Tie.two_udt101_hT`). N+: in the same model the score at the root is `f^0_root(a) = [a = true] − 1/2` (`Tie.score_root`), so `ofAct true` is UDT1.01 at the root and `ofAct false` is not (`Tie.isUDT101_ofAct_true_root`, `Tie.not_isUDT101_ofAct_false_root`). The homework instance (I) itself has a tie node: `hH` is inert in `Home.homeModel` (`HomeTie.inert_hH`, `HomeTie.ublind_hH`), so `f^0_{hH} ≡ 0` (`HomeTie.score_hH`) and `HomeTie.two_udt101_hH` gives two distinct UDT1.01 algorithms at `hH` — the audit's route (ii); its N+ companion (`f^0_{hT}` non-constant in `Home`) is machine-checked in the library since repair round 2 (`HomeScore.lean`, the audit r2 probe promoted: `HomeScore.IE_true = 1/8`, `HomeScore.IE_false = −1/8`, `HomeScore.score_hT_nonconst`). **The kind of tie** (audit r2 N3/N5): both ties are at *inert* nodes, where nothing depends on the action, so every algorithm — not only the two constants — is trivially optimal there; a tie between two payoff-relevant actions with equal non-zero `f^0` (and a third strictly worse) is not built. The refutation of uniqueness stands, by the weakest kind of tie. ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED: the synthesis may have meant "unique up to ties". Surviving neighbour: `theorem2_tiling` + `tie_iff`. **Prior flag:** [cleanup-audit-2026-08-05](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/cleanup-audit-2026-08-05.md) (plan item 5) and inventory udt-rep-083 already call the "A = UDT1.01" theorem fabricated ("not a uniqueness result"); the Lean confirms a known flag rather than discovering it (audit r1 fidelity N6). The l. 194 "theorem" presupposes uniqueness in its own axiom 5 ("A is *the* universally locally optimal algorithm"), which is the actual source of the overclaim.

## F10 — "Assumption 3 is decision-determination" and "Assumption 2 follows from DD" (imprecision; `topics/decision-determination` ll. 111–121)

Assumption 2 is the law of total expectation over the next observation and holds in every finite single-prior play space with no DD-flavoured hypothesis (`condExpJunk_eq_sum_nextObs`); an environment that reads the algorithm's code satisfies it too, so "A2 follows from DD" is vacuously true and wrongly explained. Assumption 3 is a first-order statement about derivatives; a DD-like environment (one that reads only the average behaviour) implies it (`ProfileModel.A3`: this is T8(a)'s forward direction), but "A3 *is* DD" is a category error. The converse separation (a play space with A3 whose law is not a function of the profile) was not built.

## F14 — The mandate's instance (II), with the transparent-Newcomb payoffs and a type-blind baseline, satisfies CEI (imprecision in `udt-influence-101-mandate` T6(b); repair round 1)

The mandate specifies instance (II) as "`W = {smart, dumb}`, the root kernel fills the box with probability `prof(h)(one-box)` if smart and `1/2` if dumb, `ξ₁` reveals `W`, `A` one-boxes iff `ξ₁ = smart`, `U` the transparent-Newcomb payoffs", and expects `¬ CEI 0 A h`. By `IEo_decomp`, CEI fails iff `Σ_α (d/dε ℙ^ε(α | full))·𝔼_α[U] ≠ 0` over the time-`1` atoms `α ∈ {smart, dumb}`. With the Newcomb payoffs `U = 1000·[full] + [two-box]` and a baseline that does not read `ξ₁`, `𝔼_α[U] = 1000 + ℙ_B(two-box)` is the *same* on both atoms, so the shift term is `c · d/dε Σ_α ℙ^ε(α | full) = c · 0 = 0`: **CEI holds**. Two further defeats: with a fair baseline predictor `π₀ h = 1/2` and `A` = "one-box iff smart", `prof A h (one-box) = ℙ_B(smart | full) = 1/2 = π₀ h (one-box)`, so the ε-mixed profile is constant and the acausal channel is silent (every time-`0` influence of `A` at `h` *through the profile* is `0`; the time-`1` influences are the causal `∓1/2` of the two-box bonus — on the smart atom `A` one-boxes and `𝔼^ε[U | atom] = 1000 + (1−ε)/2` — and CEI holds there as `0 = (1/2)(−1/2) + (1/2)(1/2)`; audit r2 B2 corrected the earlier "every influence of `A` at `h` is `0`"); and `A5` in the precommitment form needs the root state to be deterministic (F4): the Omega instance's `A5_asp` is true only because `ξ₀ = false` a.s., and with a fair private `ξ₀` the precommitment form fails (`Priv.not_A5`). The failure needs a payoff (or baseline behaviour) that depends on Omega's type given the box. The Omega instance of record (`Omega.aspModel`) therefore pays iff the type report says smart (`U = [ξ₁ = true]`, `ξ₁` a `3/4`/`1/4`-noisy report of the type), takes `A = ofAct true` (always one-box, profile `δ one-box ≠ π₀`), and gets `𝕀^𝔼_∅(A, hT, full) = 1/8 ≠ 0 = 𝔼_∅[𝕀^𝔼_{h₁}(A, hT) | full]` (`Omega.IEo_eq`, `Omega.rhs_eq_zero`, `Omega.not_CEI`) with `A1`, `A3`, `A4`, `AllPosObs`, `ReachPos hT` proved there and, since repair round 2, `A5` too (`Omega.A5_asp`, `OmegaA5.lean`): the failure sits inside Theorem 1's full hypothesis package. Severity: imprecision in the mandate (the mechanism it names is right; the payoffs it names do not exercise it). The by-hand claims in this paragraph (CEI under the Newcomb payoffs; the silent channel for the mandate's `A`) are not machine-checked.

## F15 — "Assumption 3 is decision-determination" is a category error; the honest sentence is one-directional (imprecision; `topics/decision-determination` ll. 111–121, udt-rep-086; repair round 1)

Forward: in every profile model the environment reads the algorithm only through its profile and realized actions, and that implies Assumption 3 (`Bridge.profile_env_implies_A3`, i.e. `ProfileModel.A3`). Converse: Assumption 3 does not say the environment is a function of the algorithm's behaviour. Precisely which "DD" is refuted (audit r2 N5): the claim "A3 implies that the ε-law is determined by the algorithm's realized behaviour (its `play` on every world, hence its profile)" — the corpus's and Diffractor's `Ā` notion of average behaviour. Not refuted, and not refutable: "the environment depends on the agent only through its policy as a function" — `A₂` and `ofAct true` are different functions (they differ at the unrealized state), and every law is a function of the algorithm. `Bridge.sqSpace` has the ε-law `1/2 + ε²·c(A)·(±1)` with `c(A)` read off the algorithm's action at an *unrealized* epistemic state (its code); two algorithms with identical actions on every world have different ε-laws at `ε = 1` (`Bridge.law_not_determined`), yet every influence is the derivative at `0` of a function with no first-order term, so Assumption 3 holds there (`Bridge.A3_sq`, all influences `0` — A3 holds in `sqSpace` for this degenerate reason; a separation with non-zero influences is not built). Assumption 3 is a first-order statement about derivatives; decision-determination is an exact conditional independence. Likewise "Assumption 2 is a consequence of DD" (l. 117) is vacuously true and wrongly explained: the law of total expectation over the next observation holds in `sqSpace` (`Bridge.a2_without_dd`) and in every play space, with no DD-flavoured hypothesis (F10).
