## F1 — The class-cut's weight clause is class-free (local error in the mandate's design; proved)

The mandate's `ClassReflective P 𝒞 Q̂` ("the class weights are `a`-invariant") cannot be indexed by the class. For the off-class terms `branchProb T Q a · condEU T Q a − branchProb T Q b · condEU T Q b` to vanish, every branch weight must be action-invariant; inertness alone leaves `(branchProb T Q a − branchProb T Q b) · condEU T Q a`, which is nonzero whenever an inert branch is re-weighted. So the weight clause is `ReflectiveAt P Q` (every `T`), and the only class-relative hypothesis is `ClassInert P 𝒞 Q`. This matches the source: bli-soto-a-071's `C(n,m)` has a global "no re-weighting" clause and a per-branch utility clause. Pointer: `Defs.lean` (`ReflectiveAt`, `classCut`); mandate §3.1, T1.

## F2 — The program's "general verdict" `100 μ(Rec) ρ > 10 μ(Ask)` is model-dependent (imprecision in the program; mandate §5.1; proved)

The display is the pinned-Ask model (`H_point` at the observed table, Omega's simulation weighted by `ρ̄^Rec`): `sist_hpoint` gives `EU Q give − EU Q refuse = V·recTerm' − c·μ(Ask) + resTerm`, i.e. pay iff `V·μ(Rec)·ρ̄^Rec > c·μ(Ask)` with an action-blind residual. The symmetric model of bli-soto-b-2-006 — Omega's pick distributed as the Ask class, which is what journal l. 291 describes — gives `(V·μ(Rec) − c·μ(Ask))·ρ̄` (`sist_symmetric`), whose sign is `ρ̄`-free for `ρ̄ > 0` (`sist_sign_symmetric`). Neither source fixes the model; both are shipped and the ledger names them. The general identity carries two profiles (`askTerm`, `recTerm`) and specializes to each.

## F3 — SIST's hypotheses "nearly restate its conclusion" is answered by the auditor's table, and the "noisy `U`" hole is not about the finite theorem (bli-soto-a-077's flag, bli-soto-b-2-011 (ii); mandate §5.2, §5.3; proved)

In `sist_identity` the only hypotheses are the utility's shape and two positivities; the branch beliefs (`−c`, `V`) are read off the utility by `EU_ref`, not assumed, and the same package yields *refuse* on `wRefuse = (93/100, 5/100, 2/100)` (`Sist3.refuse_instance`) and *pay* on `wPay` (`Sist3.pay_instance`); over the tent skeleton the branch masses are `trajLaw` fibers (`stateMass_sistSkel`) and faith is `ofSkeleton_faith`. The author's worry is about universal instantiation of a *subjective* belief ("`E(U) = −10`") in the encoded setting (`bli-rvc-ui`/M6), where it is real; it does not touch the finite theorem.

## F4 — The degenerate-kernel collapse and the delta kernel (mandate T2(g); partly proved, partly not built)

`collapse`/`collapse_iff`: a table of mass one makes every one-step value at it its updateful value (the Ask/Rec split carries no weight). The instance "`ofSkeleton` of a delta kernel at the mugging's base" is **not built**: a delta kernel `law t := [· = roundTo d t]` is not a `bli-finite` `Kernel`, because `Kernel.balanced` demands balance at *every* unit-cube table and a point mass at the rounded table is balanced only at grid-valued bases. A "delta on grid bases, tent elsewhere" kernel would be a legitimate `Kernel` (`IsProb` and balance both hold) and is the honest way to instantiate; deprioritized. Severity: presentation (the mandate's phrasing "balanced on grid tables; disclose that off-grid bases need rounding" is the disclosure of exactly this). Note also what the collapsed verdict *is*: with only the Other-class table positive, the SIST utility at the observed Ask table reads the residual, so the verdict is the residual's (`EU Qh give − EU Qh refuse = r₀ give − r₀ refuse`), "the updateful refuse" when the residual charges the cost.

## F6 — Balance is not used in U12 (presentation: the mandate's rendering adds a hypothesis the theorem does not need; proved)

The mandate's T7(b) renders the certainty theorem with "`stateMass` a `NonDegenerate` balanced probability on it (the finite `FS`)". `certain_of_branchClause` uses only full support on the face (`∀ T : ↥face, 0 < stateMass T`), positivity of the actions, faith (through the Bayes lemma) and `faceConst_prod`; balance of the state law never enters. The finite `FS` that matters is support = face. `bli-program` §3.9 U12 itself (l. 307: "under `FS` the coordinate is constant on the face, hence 0/1") and `bli-program-desiderata` I8 state the corollary without balance, so the program is not at fault — repair round 1 re-pointed this finding from the program to the mandate (audit B2/A3). Pointer: `Independence.lean`; mandate T7(b).

## F8 — Enforcing `C(n,m)` by editing conditionals breaks faith exactly where it bites (bli-soto-a-2-009 (ii), bli-soto-b-2-002 (iii) "suspicious"; mandate §5.9; proved)

`reweight_faith_iff`: Soto's `P'(q, a, u) := P(q)·P(a)·P(u | q, a)` keeps faith for the action sentence at a table `T` iff `T φ₀ = μ(act = a₀)`, i.e. iff the branch clause already held at `T` (the Bayes lemma read backwards). So the construction can only enforce `C(n,m)` where the tables already satisfy it, or at the price of self-trust — the flag was right. `reweight_joint`, `reweight_branchClause`, `reweight_condExp` establish the rest of the claim (a probability, the branch clause, within-cell conditionals preserved).

## F10 — The term reading and Soto's "mediocre payoff" claim (bli-soto-a-089; mandate §5.5; refuted-with-reading)

## F12 — F1 ("the Rec action is ignored") ties only for independent points (bli-soto-b-2-015; mandate T4; proved correction)

`EU_rec_diff_F1 = (V·w(Rec) − c·w(Ask))·ρ`: when the Rec action costs nothing and is ignored by the utility, the actions at Rec still differ in one-step value unless `ρ = 0` (or the stakes balance), because conditioning on `pp·Rec = give` moves the law of `pp·Ask`. The mandate's "at Rec every action ties" is the `ρ = 0` instance. Soto's F1 claim (journal l. 629: "if Receive(Q_m) is the case, this action is completely ignored. In this case … your argument works") concerns the **Ask** verdict, which is `ρ`-free under F1 — `EU Ask give − EU Ask refuse = w(Rec)·V − w(Ask)·c` for every `ρ ∈ [−1, 1]` (the fidelity audit's probe `audit-r1-probes/F1AskVerdict.lean`, `EU_ask_diff_F1`); only the Rec-point tie is `ρ`-dependent. (Repair round 1 removed an earlier sentence that mis-stated Soto's claim as "right only for independent points", audit B2/A3.) Severity: local error in the mandate's rendering; the source does not state the tie explicitly.

## F15 — The two senses of "good to update on" with ties (bli-soto-b-2-009; mandate T5(d); partly open)

`IndGood` (the crux hypotheses) and `EVGood` (every two-step policy ex-ante dominates every one-step policy) are defined; `evGood_of_indGood_tieFree` proves `IndGood` everywhere ⟹ `EVGood` when the one-step maximizer is unique at every table. With ties the implication fails in the form stated: under `IndGood` the one-step and two-step policy sets coincide (`twoStepPolicy_iff_oneStepPolicy`), but two one-step policies can have different ex-ante values (they are per-point maximizers), so `EVGood`'s universal comparison can fail between two equally one-step policies — the EV sense needs a tie-break or a "some selection" quantifier to be the intended notion. The converse `EVGood ⟹ IndGood` is not attempted; I conjecture it fails (a prior where updating on a correlated sentence happens to be ex-ante harmless while inertness fails), but no witness was built. Open.

## F17 — "Iterated SIST by the class-cut with `𝒞_k = {Ask_k, Rec_k}`" holds for parallel nodes, not for the sequential tree (mandate T6(a); local error in the mandate's recipe; the parallel case proved, the sequential case hand-computed)

The mandate's T6(a) asks for "a finite-horizon prior with `K` nodes, each a mugging, utilities additive over nodes: at every `Ask_k`, `IsOneStepChoice Ask_k give` — by `classCut` with `𝒞_k = {Ask_k, Rec_k}` and inertness of the other nodes' tables". In `FiniteBLIPrior` the state is one table, so "the agent at node `k`" has to be rendered, and the two renderings behave differently.

(i) **Parallel nodes** (`Iter.iterPrior`, built): each world is at one node `(k, branch)` and carries that node's payoff, read at `Ask_k`; the other nodes' tables read their own `Ask_j`, so under independent points `{Ask_k, Rec_k}` is inert at `Ask_k` (`Iter.classInert_node`, derived) and the per-node class lemma gives `EU Ask_k give − EU Ask_k refuse = w(Rec_k)·V − w(Ask_k)·c` at every `k` (`Iter.verdict`, `Iter.isOneStepChoice_pay_all`). This is exactly the mandate's recipe, and "additive over nodes" is the mixture.

(ii) **The sequential tree** (not built): one play visits all `K` nodes, `Ω₀ = plays × current depth`, the state is the table at the current node, and the world's utility is the sum of all `K` node payoffs, node `j`'s term reading `pp·Ask_j` (with `Ask_j` a history-indexed table). Then every world whose play passes through the ask at node `k` carries the term `−c·[pp·Ask_k = give]` or `V·[pp·Ask_k = give]` **whatever its current node is**: an ancestor's or a descendant's table on that play is not inert at `Ask_k`, so `ClassInert {Ask_k, Rec_k} Ask_k` is false and the class-cut identity with that class is false (the class sum is `μ(current = k)` times the true difference). The verdict `EU Ask_k give − EU Ask_k refuse = μ(play through node k)·(V·P(tails_k) − c·P(heads_k))` still holds — by the partition cut over the coin coordinate (`partitionCut` with `β := coin_k`), or by a class-cut whose class is the set of tables *compatible with the history at `k`*. Hand-computed, not machine-checked.

So the mandate's recipe is right for the object it names once "node" is read as "which mugging the prior finds itself in", and the sequential reading needs the `Y`-branch form of T6(b) (the class = the tables under a history predicate), not the two-table class. The Lean module docstring of `Iterated.lean` says which object it is. Severity: local error in the mandate's recipe for the sequential reading; no source claim is affected (bli-soto-b-2-012 (i) is about SIST applying at each node, which both renderings give).
