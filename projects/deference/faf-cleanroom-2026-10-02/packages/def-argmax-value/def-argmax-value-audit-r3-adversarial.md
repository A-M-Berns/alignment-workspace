## 3. Non-blocking issues

In value order.

**N1 — The OPEN's diagnosis names two limits and omits the third candidate.** `Open.lean`'s docstring, the open-list reason, the ledger row and the handoff item 1 all say "`p_n → 1` (`τ_n` eventually always true) and `p_n → 0` are each consistent with everything proved". Two remarks, neither machine-checked. (i) The parenthetical conflates two statements: `τ_n ↔ s_{f n} ≤ p_n`, so "`τ_n` eventually always true" is `p_n ≥ s_{f n}` eventually, which `p_n → s` from above also satisfies; "eventually always true" is not "price → 1". (ii) The obvious continuous trader — buy `τ_n` at day `f n` with weight `ctsInd δ (p_n − s_{f n}) 0`, sell with weight `ctsInd δ (s_{f n} − p_n) 0`, both `EF`-features of day-`f n` prices as `probeThreshold` is — has non-negative settled value on every day (`(1 − p_n)·w⁺` when `τ_n`, `p_n·w⁻` when `¬τ_n`), bounded away from `0` exactly when `p_n ∈ [c, s_{f n} − δ] ∪ [s_{f n} + δ, 1 − c]`; so, if the pending-settlement bookkeeping goes through as it does for FAF's `lic_paradox_resistance`, the criterion confines the limit points of `p_n` to `{0, s, 1}`, and the OPEN is equivalent to "is `s` a limit point of `P_{f n}(τ_n)`?" — with the price allowed to sit *at* the threshold (where the ramps vanish), which is the one regime a pinning engine cannot touch. That is a sharper statement of what the next attempt has to decide than "`→ 1` or `→ 0`", and it says the refutation route the handoff sketches ("the criterion forces `p_n` toward `{0, 1}` away from a neighbourhood of `s`") is the right shape but must leave `s` itself in. Heuristic; recorded for the handoff, not a defect in the Lean.
