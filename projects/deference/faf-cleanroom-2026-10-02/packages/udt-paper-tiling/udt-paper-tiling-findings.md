## F-1 — `eff` is assumed, not defined (bli-paper-001; `main.tex` 95–97) · local error

The paper defines `eff(π)` as "the limit of repeated applications of `elim`" and then *assumes* it "always exists and is uniquely defined", noting in a footnote that race conditions and loops must be excluded by "a notion of time, or causality". Three things can go wrong and the paper names none precisely: `mod(a)` may contain self-modifying actions (chains), two actions may force one point differently (disagreement), and forced actions may be ill-typed. The repair is `CausalStructure` (`Structure.lean`): a rank under which available actions only modify strictly later observations, no disagreement, forced actions non-modifying and well-typed; `effCausal` is then a definition and `main.tex` 97's two properties plus type preservation are theorems (`effCausal_nonMod`, `effCausal_idem`, `effCausal_wellTyped`). Paul's race conditions (bli-slides-009) are the witnesses that the hypotheses are needed (`StructureWitness.lean`: `Disagree.no_causalStructure`/`orders_differ`, `Cycle.no_causalStructure`/`orders_differ`); the third one — a modification issued at an impossible observation still rewrites — is a *feature* of any `eff` that is a function of the policy alone (`effCausal_fires_at_null`), which the working notes also accept (l5 b.166). Agreement of `effCausal` with iterated `elim` in an arbitrary rank-compatible order — bli-slides-036's "well-defined limit" *along such orders* — was open in round 1 and is proved in repair round 1 (`elimSeq_eq_effCausal`, `ElimOrder.lean`): one pass of `elim` over the self-modifying points, in any no-repeat, complete, rank-sorted enumeration, yields `effCausal π` for a well-typed `π`; such an enumeration always exists (`effCausal_reached`, repair round 2).

**Correction (repair round 2, from the adversarial audit's B1 and the fidelity audit's N-9).** This file said in round 1 that "under the three hypotheses the paper's assumed limit exists … the hypotheses are exactly what the race conditions violate". That is false as written: the three hypotheses make `effCausal` well-defined and reached by one rank-ordered pass, but they do **not** make the paper's *unordered* "limit of repeated applications of `elim`" unique. There is a fourth race condition beside Paul's three — a modification whose target is itself a self-modifying point — and it survives `rank_lt`, `no_disagree`, `mod_nonMod`/`mod_typed` and Limited Self-Modification: `Chain.limit_not_unique` (`StructureWitness.lean`, lifted from the auditors' probes) has `m₁ ∈ 𝒜_{X1}` forcing a non-modifying action at `X2` and `m₂ ∈ 𝒜_{X2}` forcing one at `X3` under a valid causal structure with rank `X1 < X2 < X3`; eliminating `(m₁, m₂, a)` in rank order overwrites `m₂` before it can fire and ends at `(a, a, a) = effCausal π`, while eliminating in reverse order lets `m₂` fire first and ends at `(a, a, b)` — both complete, repeat-free, non-modifying, and different. So bli-paper-062's question ("does the limit exist?") has the sharper answer: it exists and is order-independent *among rank-compatible orders*, and only there. `main.tex` 97's "uniquely defined" needs a fourth clause — elimination follows the causal order (equivalently, `eff` is *defined* as the rank-ordered result, which is what `effCausal` does) — and "a notion of time, or causality" in the footnote is exactly that clause, not a consequence of excluding the loops and same-entry races it names. The ledger's Fidelity for `elimSeq_eq_effCausal` is accordingly `variant`, not `exact`.

## F-2 — The extensional collapse: Theorem 3 has no content when the argmaxes are evaluated at the meta level (bli-paper-010–013, 017; `main.tex` 219–261, 267) · blocking for a naive formalization, not for the paper

Under the obvious finite-probability reading — `π*` a random variable, every `argmax` inside a conditional evaluated at the meta level — the assumptions of Theorem 3 lose their content, as theorems (`Collapse.lean`): with a constant argmax variable, Faith in Joint Argmax *is* "the constant is a meta-level joint maximizer" (`faithInJointArgmaxAt_const_iff`), and such a constant exists in every model with a positive cell (`exists_jointMaximizer`, `faithInJointArgmax_extensional`); the same for Faith in Argmax; Action Coordination and Naive Action Coordination become the equation between two constants (`actionCoordination_const_iff`, `naiveActionCoordination_const_iff`); Knowledge of Decision Procedure with a constant `αM ō` makes every point `pp·ō ≠ a‡` null, so for `ac aₘ ≠ a‡` the right side of Fine-Grained Fairness and every cell of Theorem 3's chain is the junk value `0` (`kdp_const_null`, `kdp_const_fairness_cell_null`); and KDP everywhere makes `pp` a point mass, so "strictly prefers" compares junk zeros (`kdp_all_const_pointMass`). The paper flags the issue itself (`main.tex` 267: nested expectations "apparently require a probability distribution to reason about itself"). The content is recovered in the **computation-output model** (`ArgmaxVars`, `Vingean.lean`): the argmax computations are coordinates of the prior, constrained only by the assumptions; Theorems 3 and 4 are then proved (`thm3_vingean_tiling`, `thm4_appendixA_tiling`), and the model's FJA is not a tautology (`VinSep.not_fja`). ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED that this model is what the authors intend (the inventory's own label).

**Addendum (repair round 1, from the adversarial audit's B1).** Inside Theorem 3's own hypothesis package the model is inert: Action Coordination at `(A', o, o')` plus Knowledge of Decision Procedure at `o'` pin `αJ` to `pp · o'` almost surely, so for *any* `V` Faith in Joint Argmax at the triple is equivalent to the `V`-free statement `FaithInJointArgmaxPrior` — every positive concrete cell `(a, a')`, `a ∈ A'`, is dominated by some positive marginal `EU o b`, `b ∈ A'` (`fja_pinned`); some `V` satisfies FJA ∧ AC ∧ KDP iff the bare prior satisfies that (`exists_vars_iff`); and Theorem 3 in the model is Theorem 3 over the bare prior with that hypothesis (`thm3_prior`: FGF followed by one instance of the prior-form hypothesis at the fairness cell). So what the computation-output model buys for Theorems 3/4 is nothing beyond the prior-level reading; what it buys is the ability to state the *constant* case and prove it empty (above). The N+ witness (`VinWit`, repaired: `sup U = 6 > 4 = EU o x`, so the conclusion is not closed by a global bound and `fja`/`fa` are proved from the cell values) exercises FGF and `FaithInJointArgmaxPrior`; its AC and KDP are a.s.-identities, which inside the package is all they can be. The earlier claim that the witness exhibits "a model the extensional reading cannot express" is withdrawn: what the extensional reading cannot express is a constant `αM`, and what the witness exhibits is a random, sometimes suboptimal `pp · ō`.

## F-4 — Theorem 2's proof needs `π*(o) = aₘ`, and the UDT 1.0 rule is not used in the inequality (bli-paper-007) · imprecision

The paper's proof derives `E(u | π*(o) = eff(π*)(o)) ≥ E(u | π*(o) = π*(o))` and says this contradicts the strict preference for `aₘ`; it does only if `aₘ = π*(o)`. The form the proof supports is the fixed-point form `thm2_udt10_tiling` (at every observation, `eff πstar o` is an available non-modifying action of positive point mass at least as good as `πstar o`), which uses Policy Fairness and Policy Coordination and **not** the decision rule; the paper's phrasing is the corollary `thm2_no_strict_selfMod`, where `IsUDT10` enters (if `aₘ` strictly beat every non-modifying action, so would the fixed point's own action, which is at least as good). `udt-bli-tiling` imports these two names for bli-paper-006/007.

## F-5 — Policy Coordination is a squeeze and lets one mix-and-match (bli-paper-006, 071, 072) · imprecision (the assumption is too strong)

The proof of Theorem 2 uses Policy Coordination at exactly one policy, `π = eff(π*)`, with the equality antecedent Policy Fairness supplies; that one instance, `PCAtEff`, is the conclusion's witness form (`thm2_of_pcAtEff`, Kind S). Any coordination assumption that fires at `eff π*` is this squeeze — the author's "abuses PC to switch between global optima" (l3 b.24–25) made exact. With the UDT 1.0 rule, Policy Coordination makes equally good positive policies pointwise equal (`pc_mix_and_match`), so any pointwise recombination of two global optima is a UDT 1.0 fixed point (`isUDT10_recombination`) — the source's "far too strong" (l3 b.30). **Sharpest form (repair round 1, from the fidelity audit's B-1):** under Theorem 2's full package *with the rule* the `≤` of the conclusion at `eff π*(o)` is an equality at every observation (`thm2_forced_tie`: PC at `eff π*` one way, the fixed point the other), so every witness of `thm2_no_strict_selfMod`'s package ties at the modifying points. "PC holds non-vacuously only in tie models" is therefore a theorem about the package when `π*` is a UDT 1.0 fixed point, and not an obstacle to a witness: `Thm2Tie.full_package` inhabits the full package non-degenerately (two fairness classes of different value, PC firing at `(x,y) ≠ π*`, `z` strictly dominated) with exactly the forced tie, and for the fixed-point form `thm2_udt10_tiling` (which does not take the rule) `Thm2Wit.thm2_full_package_Nplus` has PC firing at a strictly better policy and a strict conclusion `10 < 15` — there `π*` is not a fixed point (`Thm2Wit.not_isUDT10`), as it cannot be.

## F-6 — Fine-Grained Fairness's free `o` and positivity; KDP's `𝒜` versus `𝒜_o` (bli-paper-009, 012) · imprecision

Fine-Grained Fairness quantifies over `aₘ ∈ 𝒜^m` with `o` free; it is read with `o` bound by `aₘ ∈ 𝒜_o`, and with positivity guards on both the self-modifying point and the fairness cell (`FineGrainedFairness`), so it never equates junk values. Knowledge of Decision Procedure's argmax ranges over `𝒜` where the rule uses `𝒜_o`; in the computation-output model the variable `αM o` carries whichever is meant and the imprecision is harmless.

## F-9 — Naive communicative cloning is refuted even under Policy Fairness (bli-paper-075, 076(c)) · blocking for the naive form of 075's theorem (CC + PF alone)

## F-10 — 2-002(c) is near a squeeze; the bridge `PolicyFair ↔ MeanFair` is the content (bli-paper-2-002, 2-003) · imprecision, as the inventory says

`policyLevel_tiling` (`PolicyLevel.lean`) is the mixture identity (`mixture_of_meanFair`: the value of a chosen policy is the kernel mixture of the effective-policy values) plus `effEU π = chosenEU π` for non-modifying positive `π`, with Avoidability supplying that every reached policy is such a `π` (`avoidable_iff`: the implementing policy must be `π` itself). ## F-11 — 2-004 is ill-posed as written, and under Avoidability idempotence holds (bli-paper-2-004) · imprecision

"The kernel has to send `π'` to a stationary distribution of `e`" has two readings — the law of `π#` given `π* = π'` is `e`-invariant, or `e ∘ e = e` row-wise. Under Avoidability every reached policy is a fixed point of the kernel, so **idempotence holds** (`kerIdempotent_of_avoidable`), and the inventory's question "does CI-fairness imply idempotence?" needs `¬ Avoidable` for its countermodel: the two-step kernel `A → B → C`, `C → C` is CI-fair (constant utility), not Avoidable and not idempotent (`TwoStep.twoStep`). Both readings coincide row by row in the finite case.

## F-13 — NAC ⇏ PC needs neither ties nor correlation (bli-paper-018; against the mandate's expectation) · finding

The mandate expected that "ties or correlation" would be necessary for a model with Naive Action Coordination and without Policy Coordination. Neither is: with **three** observations, independent uniform points and the symmetric utility (`aaa ↦ 9`, one `b ↦ 10`, two `b`s `↦ 0`, `bbb ↦ 9`), extensional NAC holds at every pair and every conditioning action with *unique* maximizers, `aaa` is a UDT 1.0 fixed point, and PC fails because `aab` is better overall while its point at `o₃` is worse (`IncomparableWitness.Three.nac_not_pc`). What NAC's one-other-point conditioning cannot see is a third observation. **Sketch, not formalized** (audit r1): with two observations and unique maximizers, extensional NAC implies PC — with two observations the cell *is* the policy, and chaining the two NAC instances at `(a*, b*)` makes that cell dominate every other cell, so PC's antecedent fires only at `π*` itself; this is why the inventory's intuition pointed at ties. The other direction, PC ∧ ¬NAC, is Coordinated Buttons with `P(green) = 3/5` (`CB.pc_not_nac`), where PC holds only because nothing is as good as `(green, green)` — so PC ∧ ¬NAC-ext is established only with PC holding vacuously beyond `π*` (`CB.pc` is graded N−). PC firing at other policies is exhibited in `Thm2Wit.pc_fires` and `Thm2Tie.pcZ` (`Theorem2Witness.lean`), where NAC is not at issue.

## F-15 — Evidence classes with the same posterior merge (bli-paper-020; `main.tex` 299–303) · presentation

"The number of heads among three demonstration flips" takes four values, but one and two heads give the same posterior `1/2` on the official flip; since the policy is a function of the belief state (bli-paper-047), the agent cannot distinguish them and they are one observation (`mid`, prior mass `1/4`). Version A's threshold `10/11` is exact (`updateful_pay_iff`: pay iff `P(h ∣ e) ≤ 10/11`, from `homeEU e pay = 100 − 110·P(h ∣ e)`); the three classes give pay, pay, refuse (`versionA`). Version B's ex-ante value is `∑_e P(e)·(50 − 10·P(h ∣ e))·[pay at e]` (`exAnteValue_eq`; the `50` is `100·P(¬h)` with the average bias `1/2`), so constant pay is the unique optimum (`versionB_optimal`); `NoCrossBranch` holds in A and fails in B (`moral`).

## F-18 — Theorem 4's and Theorem 2's hypothesis packages force equalities the papers state as `≤` (bli-paper-016, 007; `main.tex` 185–199, 363–404) · finding for the paper (repair round 1, from the adversarial audit's N1 and the fidelity audit's B-1)

**Theorem 4.** With exactly the hypotheses of `thm4_appendixA_tiling` (Limited Self-Modification, Fine-Grained Fairness, Faith in Argmax at `(ob aₘ, o, âₘ)`, Naive Action Coordination there, Knowledge of Decision Procedure at `ob aₘ`, the two positivities), `EU o aₘ = EU o âₘ` (`thm4_forces_equality`). NAC and KDP pin the `αC`-cell to the twin's point `pp·o = âₘ`, so Faith in Argmax says every positive cell `(âₘ, a)` of the twin's row is `≤ EU o âₘ`; but `EU o âₘ` is the convex combination of exactly those cells (`EU_eq_sum_cells`), so every positive cell *equals* it (`eq_of_convex_le`); the fairness cell `(âₘ, ac aₘ)` is positive and FGF identifies `EU o aₘ` with it. Appendix A's package therefore forces the twin's conditional value to be independent of the `ō`-point, and the self-modification to be *exactly* as good as its twin; the paper's "at most as good" is weaker than its own hypotheses give. The Theorem 3 analogue: under the prior-form hypothesis `FaithInJointArgmaxPrior` the best positive non-modifying action's row is flat (`row_flat_of_fjaPrior`). Theorem 3's `≤` itself is forced to equality only when the twin is the best positive action of `nonModAt o` — then `row_flat_of_fjaPrior` plus FGF give `EU o aₘ = cellEU (twin, ac) = EU o twin`, which is what happens on `VinWit` (`4 = 4`; the witness's `EU o y = 1 < 4` is a different inequality and does not bear on this, as audit r2 N-4/N7 noted); when the twin is not the best action, `cellEU (twin, ac) ≤ EU o b*` can be strict, and such a model is not shipped.

**Theorem 2.** With exactly the hypotheses of `thm2_no_strict_selfMod` (Policy Fairness, Policy Coordination, the UDT 1.0 fixed point, the `eff` properties, the positivities), `chosenEU o (π* o) = chosenEU o (eff π* o)` at every observation (`thm2_forced_tie`); see F-5. Both are statements about what the assumption packages *imply*, and both say the packages are stronger than the theorems they are used for — the same shape as F-5's squeeze finding. Severity: finding (not an error in the papers' proofs).
