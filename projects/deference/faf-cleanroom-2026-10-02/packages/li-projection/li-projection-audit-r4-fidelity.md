## 2. Blocking issues

### B1. Findings F4 ("what the note needs") and the report's "modulus, not uniformity" framing: the per-sentence with-modulus form is not "trivially inhabited / empty of content" — it is "each limit is a computable real", open here; T6.2's impossibility needs both uniformity and the modulus

**Text.** `li-projection-findings.md` F4, last paragraph: "either state `𝓡(H)` with a computable modulus (then the uniform, with-modulus statement is T6.2 — no computable `f` recovers all the limits with a modulus; a *per-sentence* form with a modulus is trivially inhabited, since any single real has a rational sequence with a constant modulus classically, so the per-sentence version is empty of content either way), or drop the claim." Also F4 (iii): "**So the modulus, not uniformity, is the load-bearing difference between the source's notion and the Lean's**"; the T6.2 docstrings and ledger row: "the modulus is what carries the impossibility"; report §T6.2: "**The difference from the source is the modulus, not uniformity**"; report "Dependents": "Cite it as 'no computable approximant *with a computable modulus*'".

**What the source defines** (chat 11 L4590–4592, re-read): `𝓡(H) := {φ : ∃ e.c. Ĥ with Ĥ_n(φ) → H_∞(φ)}` — the approximant `Ĥ` is *efficiently computable*. Adding a modulus to this per-sentence definition gives: for each `φ`, an e.c. sequence converging to `H_∞(φ)` with a computable modulus — which is exactly "`H_∞(φ)` is a computable real" (the time-padding F4 itself invokes makes e.c. and computable interchangeable for a single real). Classically "any single real has a rational sequence with a constant modulus" is true and irrelevant: that sequence is not computable unless the real is. So the sentence inhabits a *computability* statement with a *classical* existence, the same error the round-3 auditor removed from the previous F4 ("every single real number has some approximation program").

**What is true, and what the package shows.** Four forms: (1) uniform, modulus-free — satisfied by the market's own quote table (`exists_computable_modulusFree_approximant`, proved); (2) uniform, with modulus — impossible (`limit_non_recoverable_of_inductor`, proved); (3) per-sentence, modulus-free — satisfied a fortiori by (1); (4) per-sentence, with modulus — "every `H_∞(φ)` is a computable real": **neither proved nor refuted** in this package, and not implied by (2): all limits could be computable reals individually without any single computable `f` approximating them all with one modulus (non-uniformity of the choice of program). This is the honest residue of the source's per-sentence `𝓡(H)` once the author adds the modulus the argument needs, and it is what the inventory's flag ("a question about the specific inductor, not obviously false") was pointing at before round 3 relabelled the whole per-sentence notion. Consequently "the modulus, not uniformity, is the load-bearing difference" is wrong as a summary: the impossibility needs *both* (the round-3 auditor's point was that uniformity *alone* does not carry it, which is (1); it did not say the modulus alone does, which would be (4)).

**Why blocking.** A false claim in the findings file (the brief's list), in the one finding the report sends to every dependent citing No-Forced-Trust; and the framing it supports lets a dependent read T6.2 as "some limit is not computable", which the Lean does not say. The theorems and their displayed statements are right (the uniform quantifier `∀ σ k` is in the statement); the labels and F4 are what mislead.

**Fix I'd accept.** (1) Replace F4's parenthetical with the four-form table above (or the two sentences: "per-sentence with a modulus is 'each limit is a computable real' — open here; the impossibility proved is the uniform with-modulus form, and uniform without a modulus is satisfied by the market itself"). (2) Change "the modulus, not uniformity, is the load-bearing difference" (F4 (iii), report §T6.2, the T6.2 docstrings' "the modulus is what carries the impossibility", the ledger row) to "the impossibility needs both uniformity and the modulus: without the modulus the market itself approximates every limit; without uniformity it is the open question whether each limit is a computable real". (3) In "Dependents", add the word *uniform* ("no computable *uniform* approximant with a computable modulus") and one clause that T6.2 does not show any single limit non-computable. (4) Optional, cheap and worth having as an OPEN of record: state form (4) in `Open.lean` — `∃ σ, ¬ ∃ f : ℕ → ℚ, Computable f ∧ ∀ k, |f k − P_∞(paperPrimeDecompose σ)| ≤ 1/(k+1)` over `liaHistory (paperDP 𝗜𝚺₁)` — with a reason line saying it is not implied by T6.2; or record in F4 that it is left unstated. Nothing in the Lean changes.

### B2. `convergence_rate_not_computable` is labelled `Fidelity: exact`; its theory class is strictly smaller than LI Proposition 5.5.1's

**Declaration.** `Church.lean`, `theorem convergence_rate_not_computable (T : ArithmeticTheory) [T.Δ₁] [𝗜𝚺₁ ⪯ T] [T.SoundOnHierarchy 𝚺 1] (P : History) [IsLogicalInductor P (paperDP T)] : ¬ ∃ f : ℕ → ℕ → ℕ, Computable (fun p => f p.1 p.2) ∧ ∀ σ k n, T ⊢ σ → f ⌜σ⌝ k < n → 1 − 1/(k+1) < P n (paperPrimeDecompose σ)`; docstring and ledger row: "Fidelity: exact (over `paperDP T` with Foundation's `Halting.lean` binders in place of 'represents computable functions'; codes in place of sentences; `ε = 1/(k+1)`)".

**The source** (`main.tex:2669–2673`, re-read): "Let `P̄` be a logical inductor over a theory `Γ` that represents computable functions … Then `f` must be uncomputable." Its proof (l. 2675–2677): "a task which is known to be impossible for a first-order theory that represents computations. (If we assumed further that `Γ` were sound as a theory of the natural numbers, this would allow us to solve the halting problem …)". So the paper's hypothesis is representability (with consistency), and Σ₁-soundness is the paper's own *additional* assumption for the halting-problem route. The run's inventory says the same (fixpoint-lit-095, Flags: "the paper's parenthetical … is the easier (Σ₁-sound) route; the stated version needs undecidability of provability for consistent theories representing computations, which is Church's theorem").

**What the Lean covers.** Every `T` with `[T.Δ₁] [𝗜𝚺₁ ⪯ T] [T.SoundOnHierarchy 𝚺 1]` — a strict subclass of the paper's: `𝗣𝗔 + ¬Con(𝗣𝗔)` represents computable functions and is consistent, so the paper's proposition applies to it and the Lean's does not. The other two clauses of the label are fine: codes for sentences is `Encodable`; and `ε = 1/(k+1)` is a *restriction of the rate function's domain*, hence not a weakening — my probe `FidelityRateQPlus.lean` derives the paper's `ℚ⁺` form (`f : ℕ → ℚ → ℕ`, `∀ ε > 0, …, 1 − ε < P n …`) from the package's theorem sorry-free, using FAF's `ratInv_prim`/`ratAdd_prim`/`ratNatCast_prim` for `k ↦ (k+1)⁻¹`.

**Why blocking.** The brief: a headline "weaker than its name/docstring/ledger says". The substitution is named in the label, which is the mitigating fact (and why this is a one-word repair), but the classification word a reader scans is `exact`, and STANDARDS §3 asks that when the Lean is weaker than the claim the docstring say so. The mandate prescribed these binders for the Church lemma ("build the Church lemma once"), so no target is missed — only the label is wrong.

**Fix I'd accept.** In the docstring, the ledger row and report §T7/extension: `Fidelity: weaker: Σ₁-sound T (Foundation's Halting.lean binders; the paper assumes only that Γ represents computable functions, and uses Σ₁-soundness as an extra assumption for the halting route) — stronger in the rate domain (ε = 1/(k+1) is a restriction; the ℚ⁺ form follows, audit r4 probe FidelityRateQPlus.lean); codes in place of sentences`. Optionally port `convergence_rate_not_computable_qplus` from the probe (kind L). The same clause ("over Σ₁-sound `T`") belongs, non-blockingly, on the T6.2 rows (`limit_non_recoverable*`): anson-029 says "any LI over `Γ ⊇ PA`", the mandate prescribed the `Halting.lean` binders, and the rows' `variant` clause should name the class along with the modulus.
