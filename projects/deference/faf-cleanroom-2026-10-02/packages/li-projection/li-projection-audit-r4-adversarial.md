## 2. Blocking issues

### B1. `convergence_rate_not_computable` — `Fidelity: exact` where the Lean is weaker than LI Proposition 5.5.1 (Σ₁-sound theories only); the same narrowing undisclosed on the T6.2 rows

**Declaration.** `Church.lean` l. 575–578, under the section binders `variable (T : ArithmeticTheory) [T.Δ₁] [𝗜𝚺₁ ⪯ T] [T.SoundOnHierarchy 𝚺 1]` (l. 394):

```
theorem convergence_rate_not_computable (P : History) [IsLogicalInductor P (paperDP T)] :
    ¬ ∃ f : ℕ → ℕ → ℕ, Computable (fun p : ℕ × ℕ => f p.1 p.2) ∧
      ∀ (σ : ArithmeticSentence) (k n : ℕ), T ⊢ σ → f (Encodable.encode σ) k < n →
        1 - 1 / ((k : ℝ) + 1) < P n (paperPrimeDecompose σ)
```

Docstring l. 573: `Fidelity: exact (over paperDP T with Foundation's Halting.lean binders in place of "represents computable functions"; codes in place of sentences; ε = 1/(k+1))`. Ledger row 57: the same words. Report §T7 and "Repair round 3": "Fidelity: exact over `paperDP T` with Foundation's `Halting.lean` binders in place of the paper's 'represents computable functions'".

**What's wrong.** The Source line quotes the paper: "Let `P̄` be a logical inductor over a theory `Γ` that represents computable functions … Then `f` must be uncomputable." The paper defines that phrase (`main.tex:600–605`): `Γ` satisfies the representability theorem for computable functions — for every total computable `f` a formula `γ_f` with `Γ ⊢ γ_f(n, f(n))` and uniqueness. Its proof (`main.tex:2673`) reduces to "computably determine whether `Γ ⊢ φ` … a task which is known to be impossible for a first-order theory that represents computable functions", and adds: "(If we assumed *further* that `Γ` were sound as a theory of the natural numbers, this would allow us to solve the halting problem by letting `φ` be a sentence of the form 'M halts'.)" The Lean takes exactly that further-assumption route: `church_haltCode` is Mathlib's `halting_problem` transported along Foundation's `re_complete`, whose converse direction (`T ⊢ haltSentence a → a halts`) is Σ₁-soundness — `re_complete` is stated as a biconditional under `[T.SoundOnHierarchy 𝚺 1]` (`Representation.lean:260`), and `SoundOnHierarchy T 𝚺 1` unfolds to `T.SoundOn (Hierarchy 𝚺 1)` (`Hierarchy.lean:476`). So the Lean proves the proposition for Σ₁-sound theories extending `𝗜𝚺₁`; the paper's statement covers every consistent theory representing computable functions. `PA + ¬Con(PA)` is consistent, represents computable functions, is not Σ₁-sound, and is outside the Lean's statement. That is `weaker`, not `exact`. The parenthetical in the label reads as if the binders were an equivalent rendering of "represents computable functions"; they are strictly stronger (Σ₁-soundness is not implied by representability; `𝗜𝚺₁ ⪯ T` is more than `𝗥₀ ⪯ T`, though that part is harmless).

The T6.2 rows have the same gap in a milder form: `limit_non_recoverable_of_inductor`'s Fidelity (row 49; docstring l. 434) says "stronger: every inductor…; variant: with a computable modulus" and never says the result is for Σ₁-sound `T`, while its source (chat 11 L4593) claims it for "any logical inductor `H` over a sufficiently rich `Γ` (e.g., `Γ` extending PA)". And `limit_non_recoverable`'s docstring (l. 447–448) says "Binders are those of Foundation's `Halting.lean` (Church's theorem needs Σ₁-soundness)" — Church's theorem, the undecidability of any consistent theory in which the computable functions are representable, does not need Σ₁-soundness; *this route to it* does.

**Why blocking.** The brief's list: a headline "weaker than its name/docstring/ledger says". The theorem is correct and valuable, and its binders are printed in its statement; what misleads is the word `exact` on the round's flagship addition and the false sentence about Church's theorem. A dependent citing "LI Prop 5.5.1 over `paperDP T`" from the ledger would cite it for `PA + ¬Con(PA)` and be wrong. (The mandate prescribed these binders for the extension; STANDARDS §3 governs the label.)

**Fix I'd accept.** (1) `convergence_rate_not_computable`: `Fidelity: weaker: for Σ₁-sound T extending 𝗜𝚺₁ (Foundation's Halting.lean binders — the halting-family route the paper's proof mentions as the further-assumption case); the paper's statement for every consistent theory that represents computable functions is not proved; codes in place of sentences; ε = 1/(k+1)` — docstring, ledger row 57, report §T7 and "Repair round 3", open list section (E). (2) Rows 49–51 and the docstrings of `limit_non_recoverable_of_inductor`, `limit_non_recoverable`, `no_computable_modulus`, `no_computable_modulus_market`: one clause "for Σ₁-sound `T`; the source's 'Γ extending PA' without soundness is not covered". (3) Replace "(Church's theorem needs Σ₁-soundness)" with "(the halting-family route through `re_complete` needs Σ₁-soundness; Church's theorem for merely consistent theories is not taken)". (4) Optional, one line in F11: the consistent-theory form as an open item of record — `¬ ComputablePred (fun a => T ⊢ haltSentence a)` under `[Consistent T]` in place of Σ₁-soundness would restore the paper's generality for both T6.2 and 5.5.1 (a Rosser-type or representability argument; I did not look for one in Foundation).

### B2. Findings F4, closing paragraph: the per-sentence form with a modulus is called "trivially inhabited … empty of content"; it is "`H_∞(φ)` is a computable real", and the source's per-sentence claim is unrecorded and open

**Text.** `li-projection-findings.md` F4, last paragraph: "either state `𝓡(H)` with a computable modulus (then the uniform, with-modulus statement is T6.2 — no computable `f` recovers all the limits with a modulus; a *per-sentence* form with a modulus is trivially inhabited, since any single real has a rational sequence with a constant modulus classically, so the per-sentence version is empty of content either way, and only the uniform form says anything), or drop the claim."

**What the source says** (chat 11 L4590–4596, re-read): "`𝓡(H) := {φ ∈ 𝓢 : ∃ e.c. Ĥ with Ĥ_n(φ) → H_∞(φ)}`. Then `𝓡(H) ⊊ 𝓢` … Proof sketch: `𝓡(H)` is contained in the set of `φ` where `H_∞(φ)` is computable. But logical inductors over PA have non-computable limit prices on some sentences — specifically, on Gödel-like sentences where the limit value depends on internal dynamics that aren't computable from observation." Two steps: (1) a containment; (2) the existence of a sentence with a non-computable limit price.

**What's wrong.** F4 is right about step (1): modulus-free, the containment is invalid and `𝓡(H) = 𝓢` by the market's own table (`exists_computable_modulusFree_approximant`; the e.c. gap by time-padding, disclosed as not machine-checked — I re-derived the padding argument, §4). But the closing paragraph then dismisses the per-sentence form *with* a modulus on the ground that "any single real has a rational sequence with a constant modulus classically". That drops the computability of the approximant, which the source's `Ĥ` carries ("e.c."). Keep it, as the source does, and the per-sentence form with a computable modulus is

`∃ f : ℕ → ℚ, Computable f ∧ ∀ k, |f k − H_∞(φ)| ≤ 1/(k+1)`

— "`H_∞(φ)` is a computable real", exactly the set step (1) names. It is not trivially inhabited (it is a real property of a real), and step (2) — some `H_∞(φ)` is *not* a computable real — is a well-posed, non-trivial per-sentence claim. The package does not address it: T6.2 (`limit_non_recoverable_of_inductor`) forbids a *uniform* computable recoverer with a modulus, and uniformity can fail with every single limit computable (non-uniformly), so T6.2 neither proves nor refutes step (2). F4 records neither step (2) nor its status; "empty of content either way" tells the note's author the question does not exist. Probe `PerSentenceOpen.lean` states the claim over FAF's objects —

```
theorem some_limit_not_computable_real (P : History) [IsLogicalInductor P (paperDP T)] :
    ∃ σ : ArithmeticSentence, ¬ ∃ f : ℕ → ℚ, Computable f ∧
      ∀ k : ℕ, |(f k : ℝ) - limitingBelief P (paperPrimeDecompose σ)| ≤ 1 / (k + 1)
```

— with `sorry` by design (it is the OPEN the package should carry, not a proof), and shows the per-sentence clause *is* inhabited on every theorem (`f ≡ 1`, limit `1` by `limitingBelief_paperPrime_of_provable`), so the content is entirely about the undecidable sentences, where non-dogmatism puts the limit in `(0,1)` and says nothing about its computability. I tried, on paper, to settle it either way with the package's tools and could not: an inductor with every limit a computable real is excluded neither by T6.2 (uniformity), nor by non-dogmatism, nor by the criterion as FAF states it; and no diagonal argument through the limits is available without a uniform modulus. "Neither proved nor refuted" is the honest status.

**Why blocking.** The brief lists "a false claim in the findings file" as blocking; F4 is the finding the report routes to every dependent citing No-Forced-Trust, and the false sentence ("empty of content either way") is the one that tells the note what to do. The round-3 fidelity audit caught F4's first false parenthetical; this one came in with the rewrite.

**Fix I'd accept.** (1) Replace the clause from "a *per-sentence* form with a modulus is trivially inhabited" to "only the uniform form says anything" with: "a per-sentence form with a computable modulus is '`H_∞(φ)` is a computable real' — the set the proof sketch names; the source's second step, that some limiting belief is not a computable real, is well-posed and is **neither proved nor refuted** here (T6.2's uniform impossibility does not imply it: uniformity can fail with every limit computable); stated OPEN as `some_limit_not_computable_real`". (2) Add that statement to `Open.lean` (section (D) of the open list; a ledger row with Source anson-029 L4596, Kind OPEN, Fidelity exact for the per-sentence computable-real reading — the probe's statement can be ported as is), or, if the repairer prefers no new OPEN, say the same in F4 and F11 without the Lean. (3) The "Dependents" note on T6.2 gains the clause "whether any single limit is a non-computable real is open".
