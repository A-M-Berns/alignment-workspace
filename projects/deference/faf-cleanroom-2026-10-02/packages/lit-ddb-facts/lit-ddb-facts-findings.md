## F1. Two inequivalent fixed-option Dutch books in DDB itself (Target 9)

**Severity: imprecision (the theorem survives at every grade).** Fn 20 (transcription l. 1143; PDF ll. 492–494) defines a fixed-option Dutch book by a *sure* strict loss, `O(w) + S_w(w) < 0` at every world. The glossary (l. 428; PDF ll. 1791–1794) defines it by an *almost-sure* loss, `π(O + S ≤ 0) = 1` and `π(O + S < 0) > 0`. Fn 22 argues against even an *expected* loss. These are three different objects (sure ⟹ almost-sure ⟹ expected, `AlmostSureLoss.of_sureLoss`, `ExpectedLoss.of_almostSureLoss` in `DutchBook.lean`; the converses fail in general). The paper's claim "Value ⟺ no fixed-option Dutch book" is nevertheless true at each grade, because fn 21's construction delivers the strongest (sure loss `−ε`) and fn 22's refutation needs only the weakest (expected loss): `not_value_iff_exists_book_sureLoss`, `not_value_iff_exists_book_almostSureLoss`, `not_value_iff_exists_book_expectedLoss`. What the paper should say: pick one definition (fn 20's is the one Theorem 5.1's second bullet presupposes) and note that the equivalence is grade-independent. Witness at the sure-loss grade: `Examples.fact21_book`.

## F2. Fn 57's converse (Fact 4.2, ⇐) uses a standard theorem without citing it (Target 10)

**Severity: imprecision (a true step asserted without argument); the Fact is true.** *Reworded in repair round 1 (round-1 audits NB4/N5): the first version called this "not a proof / local error" and described a naive proof rather than fn 57's.* Fn 57 (ll. 1219–1220) argues (⇐) by letting `B` be the extreme points of the hull of all rows, asserting every extreme row is immodest (correct: an extreme point is outside the hull of the other rows, hence outside the hull of `C_i⁻`, so by hypothesis immodest — `rows_mem_convexHull_immodest` in `Geometry.lean`), and then asserting "for `P_j ∈ A − B`, `P_j` is in the convex hull of the immodest `B`". That step is the Krein–Milman/Minkowski theorem for the compact convex hull of a finite set (the hull is the hull of its extreme points): true, but not invoked. The rest of fn 57 does carry the disjoint-support computation and does say "(the last two equalities come from the fact that each `P_i ∈ B` is immodest)", so the single gap is the uncited theorem. The proof of record: Krein–Milman (`closure_convexHull_extremePoints`, with the hull of a finite set closed) shows every row is a convex combination of immodest rows, and `reflects_of_mem_convexHull_immodest` then reads Reflection off the disjoint supports (a candidate `τ` of `ρ = ∑ λ_σ σ` must be in the combination, `ρ(P = τ) = λ_τ`, and on the cell `ρ w = λ_τ τ w`). Lean: `validates_reflects_iff` (kind P). Note also that fn 57's `P_j(q ∧ [P = P_k]) = ∑_i λ_i P_i(q ∧ [P = P_k]) = λ_k P_k(q ∧ [P = P_k])` needs exactly the disjoint-support argument, which is where `selfMass σ = 1` (not merely `σ ∈ B`) is used.

**Severity: imprecision (a truncated sentence in the PDF; the converse's presupposition is unstated).** *Corrected in repair round 1: the first version of this finding said the (⇐) "runs off the page" in PDF and transcription. Under the strong reading NR-str the Fact holds with the junk `P̂ = 0` of a null self-cell *included* in the image: any convex representation of `P_i` over `{P̂_j : P_j ∈ C_i}` gives weight `P_i(P = P_j)` to `P̂_j` because the informed vectors have pairwise disjoint supports, so a candidate with a null self-cell (whose `P̂` is `0`) would receive mass `0` from every `P̂`, contradicting candidacy. ## F4. Corollary 4.5 is false under the guarded modestly-informed predicate; fn 61 reads `λ_ii = 0` at a null self-cell (Target 12)

**Severity: local error in the statement as `lit-ddb-frames` froze the predicate; true under DDB's literal fn 54.** `lit-ddb-frames`' definition of record `Frame.ModestlyInformed` carries the guard `0 < ρ(P = ρ)` (needed so that `P̂_ρ` is DDB's object). With that predicate, Corollary 4.5 (⇒) — "the frame validates Total Trust only if each `P_i` is modestly informed" — is refuted by `Examples.nullSelf`: rows `(0, ½, ½)`, `(0, 1, 0)`, `(0, 0, 1)`. Every row totally trusts the frame (row 0 is the midpoint of the two immodest Diracs it leaves open; rows 1, 2 are their own only candidates), so the frame validates Total Trust, but `P_0(P = P_0) = P_0(w_0) = 0`, so `P_0` is not (guarded-)modestly informed: `nullSelf_validates_totalTrust_not_modestlyInformed` (N+). DDB's fn 61 silently handles this case by reading modest informedness at a null self-cell as `λ_ii = 0` (hull membership over `C_i⁻` alone). The package therefore defines the *unguarded* `ModestlyInformedU` (fn 54's literal hull membership, with the junk `P̂ = 0` forced to weight `0` by the sum-to-one constraint — `modestlyInformedU_iff_of_zero`) and proves Corollary 4.5 with it: `validates_totalTrust_iff` (kind C). Under `0 < ρ(P = ρ)` the two predicates agree (`modestlyInformedU_iff_of_pos`). This is exactly where the frames audits' warning (adversarial 3, fidelity NB1: "the guard is not redundant outside the hull condition") bites.

## F5. "Hyperplanes are the only divisions that divide probability space into two convex sets" (§2 l. 219, fn 34) is false as stated (Target 7)

**Severity: imprecision; the theorem it is used for needs only the separation.** *Corrected in repair round 1: the first version said the lexicographic set's "boundary is not a hyperplane"; that is false (both round-1 audits caught it) — its topological boundary is the hyperplane `{x₁ = 0}`, and fn 34's parenthetical that a biconvex `B` has a hyperplane boundary is correct for every nonempty proper biconvex subset of `ℝ^n`. What fails is the unstated step DDB's argument at ll. 223–225 needs, from "boundary a hyperplane" (true, fn 34) to "`B` is one side of the cut, `{E(X) ≥ t}` or `{E(X) > t}`" — which `lexHalf` refutes; the converse "boundary a hyperplane ⟹ biconvex" is also false (round 1's `{x₁ > 0} ∪ {x₁ = 0, x₂ ∈ ℚ}`) but neither DDB nor the package uses it (sentence corrected in round 2, fidelity NB8 / adversarial N5).* In `ℝ^W` the empty set and the whole space are biconvex (`biconvex_empty`, `biconvex_univ`; degenerately — they are not divisions into two sets), and so is the lexicographic half-space `L = {x : x₁ > 0} ∪ {x : x₁ = 0 ∧ x₂ ≥ c}`, which is neither `{ρ : E_ρ(X) ≥ t}` nor `{ρ : E_ρ(X) > t}` for any `X, t` (it is neither closed nor open), although its boundary is the hyperplane `{x₁ = 0}`. Lean: `Examples.lexHalf i j c` (any pivot coordinates, any threshold), `lexHalf_biconvex`, `lexHalf_ne_closedHalf`, `lexHalf_ne_openHalf`, collected in `lexHalf_not_halfSpace` (N+). Since DDB's sentence is about *probability space*, the threshold matters: with `c = 0` (the example as first written) `L` contains the whole simplex; with `c = ½` on three worlds `L` contains `(0, ½, ½)` and misses `(0, 0, 1)` (`lexHalf3_nontrivial`), and `L ∩ Δ` is not `{ρ ∈ Δ : E_ρ(X) ≥ t}` or `{ρ ∈ Δ : E_ρ(X) > t}` for any `X, t` either (`lexHalf3_ne_closedHalf_simplex`, `lexHalf3_ne_openHalf_simplex`, via the points `(ε, 0, 1 − ε) ∈ L` whose expectations tend to that of `(0, 0, 1) ∉ L`). So l. 221 is false as a claim about probability space, not only about `ℝ^W`. Fn 34's correct content — for *finite* sets of rows on either side of a biconvex `B` there is a strongly separating hyperplane — is `biconvex_strong_sep` (kind P). More importantly, fn 34 alone does **not** prove Total Trust ⟹ its convexity version: separating `{P_w ∈ B}` from `{P_w ∉ B}` puts the conditional `π* = π(· | P ∈ B)` on the half-space of the `B` rows, not in `B`. The proof of record separates the `B`-rows from the `Bᶜ`-rows *together with* `π*`, which is where convexity of `Bᶜ` is used: `totalTrust_iff_totalTrustBiconvex` (kind P, `Convexity.lean`).

## F14. Trust of the prior forces factive evidence, which fn 25's prior frames do not assume (Target 17(ii)/E1)

**Severity: imprecision (in fn 25's definition, relative to the theorem it cites); a structural fact about the OPEN rows.** Fn 25 defines a prior frame by a regular prior and an evidence map, with no factivity (`w ∈ E_w`) requirement, and `PriorFrame` follows it. But Simple Trust of the prior already forces factivity: at `q = E_w`, `t = 1` the event `A = [P(E_w) ≥ 1] = {v : E_v ⊆ E_w}` contains `w` (the row at `w` is certain of `E_w`, `PriorFrame.mass_row_Ev`), so Simple Trust gives `μ(A) ≤ μ(E_w ∩ A)`, and regularity forces `A ⊆ E_w` (`PriorFrame.probEvent_Ev_subset_of_simpleTrust`). Hence `E_v ⊆ E_w → v ∈ E_w` (`PriorFrame.mem_Ev_of_subset_of_simpleTrust`) and in particular `w ∈ E_w` (`mem_Ev_self_of_simpleTrust`, `mem_Ev_self_of_trust`; kinds P/C). So every reading of "Trust ⟹ Value on prior frames" only ever concerns factive, nested-factive evidence. *Last sentence amended in round 2 (fidelity NB4, adversarial N10):* the first version said the OPEN statement "may be stronger than the cited theorem"; since Trust forces factivity, the unrestricted statement is *equivalent* to its restriction to factive prior frames, and it is false either way (F15) — factivity is not the missing hypothesis, nestedness is. Round 2 adds a second structural consequence: Simple Trust of the prior forces the evidence to be **mass-monotone**, `v ∈ E_w → μ(E_v) ≤ μ(E_w)` (`PriorFrame.mass_Ev_le_of_mem_of_simpleTrust`), which on nested evidence is transitivity `v ∈ E_w → E_v ⊆ E_w` (`PriorFrame.Ev_subset_of_mem_of_nested`). For the separate-deferrer reading the same arguments give factivity and mass-monotonicity only on `supp π` (not formalized). Pointed out by the round-1 fidelity audit (NB5).

## F15. "On prior frames Trust ⟹ Value" (§2 l. 157, fn 25) is false as stated; true on nested prior frames (Target 17(ii)/E1)

**Severity: local error in the source's paraphrase of Dorst 2020a Theorem 7.4 (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED what Dorst's own hypotheses are: the corpus has only DDB's paraphrase); and a correction of this package's own rounds 0–1, which carried the paraphrase as two OPEN theorems.** DDB §2 l. 157 says "in a natural subclass of frames, Value also follows from Trust (Theorem 7.4)", and fn 25 identifies the subclass as prior frames `⟨W, E, μ⟩` with `μ` regular and `P_w = μ(· | E_w)`. The round-2 adversarial audit found, by an exact search over three-world prior frames, that this fails: `ExamplesPrior.cxQ`, prior `(1/10, 3/10, 3/5)`, evidence `E_0 = {0}`, `E_1 = {0, 1}`, `E_2 = {1, 2}`, has factive evidence, `Trust μ` (checked at every `q, p` and every real threshold: `cxQ_trust`) and `¬ Value μ` (`cxQ_not_value`; the Total Trust failure is `X = (−6, 3, 0)`, `s = 1`, where only world 2 has `E_w(X) ≥ 1` and `X = 0` there; equivalently `P_2 = (0, ⅓, ⅔)` is not modestly informed, since `P_1 = (¼, ¾, 0) ∈ C_2⁻` puts mass on world 0). Lean: `prior_trust_value_refuted` (deferrer-is-the-prior reading), `prior_trust_value_all_refuted` (separate deferrer), `cxQ_summary`; the two `sorry` declarations `prior_trust_value_open`/`prior_trust_value_all_open` are gone. The search (audit r2 B1: 343 evidence maps × 25 regular priors × 37 deferrers, exact arithmetic) found every failing evidence map non-nested.

**What is true — proved in round 2.** Call a prior frame *nested* when any two evidence sets that meet are comparable under inclusion (`PriorFrame.Nested`). On a nested prior frame, Simple Trust of the prior already forces Total Trust of the prior — `PriorFrame.totalTrust_of_nested_of_simpleTrust` (kind P) — hence Trust ⟹ Value (`prior_value_of_trust_of_nested`, C) and the whole ladder collapses for the prior: Simple Trust ⟺ Trust ⟺ Total Trust ⟺ Value (`PriorFrame.tfae_nested`, C). The proof: Simple Trust makes the evidence factive (F14) and mass-monotone (`v ∈ E_w → μ(E_v) ≤ μ(E_w)`), which with nestedness is transitivity (`v ∈ E_w → E_v ⊆ E_w`); then, for any `X, s`, an induction over evidence-closed sets `T` (peeling off an evidence set of maximal size, or the worlds sharing the row of `T`'s owner) shows `∑_{T ∩ [E_w(X) ≥ s]} μ_w(X_w − s) ≥ max(0, ∑_T μ_w(X_w − s))`, using that `∑_{E_u} μ_w(X_w − s) = μ(E_u)(E_{P_u}(X) − s)` has the sign of the indicator at `u` (`totalTrust_of_factive_of_trans_of_nested`). The counterexample is not nested (`cxQ_not_nested`), so the hypothesis is not an artifact. N+ witness with modest rows: `ExamplesPrior.chainQ` (uniform prior, evidence `{0} ⊂ {0,1} ⊂ W`, rows `δ_0, (½,½,0), (⅓,⅓,⅓)`, row 1 modest), whose prior simply trusts, totally trusts and values it (`chainQ_nested_trust_value`) — not an instance of the immodest collapse. This is the Geanakoplos-shaped condition the mandate and the round-0 report anticipated, now a theorem; whether Dorst's Theorem 7.4 carries nestedness or some other hypothesis is ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED.

**What remains open.** The separate-deferrer reading on nested prior frames, `∀ π ∈ Δ, Trust π → Value π` (`prior_trust_value_all_nested_open`, the package's one OPEN row). It genuinely needs full Trust: on `chainQ` the deferrer `π = (½, 1/5, 3/10)` simply trusts the frame but fails Trust at `q = {1}`, `p = {1, 2}`, `t = ½` and does not value it (`chainQ_simpleTrust_not_value`, N+) — so, unlike for the prior, Simple Trust of a separate deferrer is not enough. The audit's search found no three-world counterexample; a proof sketch is in the report (E1).
