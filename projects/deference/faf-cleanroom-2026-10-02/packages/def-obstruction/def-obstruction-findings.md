So `tracking_fails`, `tower_fails_diagonal` and `tower_fails_diagonal_sameDay` carry **no** relation between `e` and `F`, and the alternating witness with next-day publication (`altPair_tower_fails_sameDay`) proves the day-`n` tower instance fails although the day-`n` quote enters the process only at `n+1`; `latePair_tracking_fails` (`Witness.lean` § E) pushes this to publication 100 days *after* the deferral reads the credence, with the same certificates. **Register (audit r1 N2/N3):** what is established is that the certificates are *sufficient* and the schedule is irrelevant *given* them; whether they are *necessary* — whether 2a's boundary lies in them — is the OPEN `tracking_bound_fails_some_table` (F-Oracle), not a theorem. Against vq-wiki-045 precisely: its premise (ii) ("the day-`n` quote as decided data before the lookahead") is refuted for tables with e.c. sides; the certificate-free regime-2 question — the one the corpus's `𝒞_H ⊊ 𝒞_A` setting poses, where `A`'s quote is readable only through the ledger — is the OPEN. Why the schedule can never enter a criterion-derived theorem: FAF's traders see prices, not the deductive process, so publication timing can only affect the LIA's *actual* prices, about which no FAF theorem speaks.
