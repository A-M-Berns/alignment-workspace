## F-D — anson-2-022 Theorem 3's conclusion holds with no predictor hypothesis: the stated hypothesis is unnecessary (its own proof uses only a weaker, pointwise property), not equivalent to the conclusion and not shown always true (severity: local error in the source — chat 11 L3019 and L3057, = chat 01 L1558: the conclusion is presented as depending on a hypothesis its proof does not need; the inventory's rendering of the definition is also wrong; anson-2-022)

Pointer: chat 11 L2989–2996 (§6.1, the definition of `𝒫_F`, the prediction loss, and "asymptotically calibrated" — the section chat 01's excerpt omits); chat 11 L3010–3017 = chat 01 L1550 (Theorem 3: "Suppose there exists an asymptotically calibrated `Ĥ ∈ 𝒫_F` for every `φ ∈ 𝒮` such that `Ĥ_n(φ) → H_∞(φ)` … Then for every `A ∈ LI(H,F)` and every `φ`: (a) `A_∞(φ) := lim_n A_n(φ)` exists, (b) `A_∞(φ) = H_∞(φ)`, (c) `A_∞` extends to a coherent probability measure"); chat 11 L3019 = chat 01 L1558 ("The condition in Theorem 3 … is a non-trivial assumption about `H`'s convergence rate. It says the slow inductor's eventual beliefs are predictable from its observable history within polynomial time, with errors that vanish in the limit. Whether typical Garrabrant inductors satisfy this is an open question"); chat 11 L3057 ("Without that assumption, we don't have it"); Theorem 2's proof sketch, chat 01 L1541–1544 ("… the second is bounded by calibration"); chat 11 L2814 (the draft: "The natural definition is e.c. predictor whose error on `H_{F(n)}` has bounded total variation across `n` — but bounded against what, and in what norm?"); anson-2-inventory, entry anson-2-022 (renders the definition as "`Ĥ_n(φ) − H_{F(n)}(φ) → 0`").

*History of this finding.* Round 1 said the hypothesis was "essentially the conclusion", squeeze-shaped (copying the inventory's flag and the mandate's expectation). Repair round 1, after audit r1 fidelity B1, said the opposite error: that the hypothesis is *always true*, discharged by the trivial predictor — reading "asymptotically calibrated" as `Ĥ_n(φ) − H_{F(n)}(φ) → 0`. That reading is the **inventory's**, not the source's; audit r2 fidelity B1 found the source's definition (chat 11 §6.1, which chat 01's excerpt omits) and showed the two readings differ in shape (probe `audit-r2-probes/PointwiseNotCumulative.lean`). This is the third version.

**What the source defines.** `𝒫_F` is the class of e.c. predictors `Ĥ_n(φ)` depending only on `H_{≤n}`, polynomial-time in `n`. `Ĥ ∈ 𝒫_F` is **asymptotically calibrated** for `φ` if `Σ_{n=1}^∞ (H_{F(n)}(φ) − Ĥ_n(φ)) · sign(Ĥ_n(φ) − p_n)` remains bounded for every e.c. comparator sequence `p_n` — a *cumulative* condition ("cannot be systematically beaten by another e.c. predictor in cumulative loss against `H_{F(·)}`"). The draft at L2814 says what was intended: bounded total variation of the error across `n`. The inventory rendered this as the *pointwise* `Ĥ_n(φ) − H_{F(n)}(φ) → 0`; L1558's own gloss ("with errors that vanish in the limit") invites that rendering, but it is the gloss, not the definition.

**What the Lean proves.** `trivialPredictor_error_tendsto_zero` (`Column.lean`; named `trivialPredictor_calibrated` until repair round 2): for the trivial predictor `Ĥ_n(φ) := H_n(φ)`, `H_n(φ) − H_{F(n)}(φ) → 0` and `H_n(φ) → H_∞(φ)`, for every inductor over a process with satisfiable stages and every `F → ∞` — two applications of FAF's `thm:con`. That is the pointwise-vanishing *weakening* of the source's hypothesis. It does **not** give the cumulative form: a per-day error can vanish pointwise with unbounded partial sums (the harmonic error, the probe), and at the comparator `p_n := −1` (`sign ≡ +1` on a `[0,1]`-valued predictor) the source's condition for the trivial predictor reads "`Σ_n (H_{F(n)}(φ) − H_n(φ))` bounded" — which telescopes and holds at `F n = n + 1`, but at the corpus's `F(n) = 2^n` is `Σ_n (H_{2^n}(φ) − H_n(φ))`, whose terms vanish with no rate (FAF's `lic_limitingBelief_tendsto` is a bare convergence) and whose sum has no reason to stay bounded (an inductor converging on `φ` like `1/2 − 1/log n` gives `≈ Σ_n 1/log n`). So whether the trivial predictor is asymptotically calibrated in the source's sense at `F = 2^n` is **not established**, and plausibly fails for slowly converging `H`; "the hypothesis is always true" and "it holds for every inductor" (repair round 1's wording) are withdrawn. L1558's remark that the *stated* condition is "a non-trivial assumption about `H`'s convergence rate" is right about the stated condition: it is a rate-type condition.

**What is true, and what the source gets wrong.** The stated hypothesis is *stronger than the proof uses*. Theorem 2's proof (chat 01 L1541–1544) sells one share on each day with `A_n(φ) > Ĥ_n(φ) + ε`; the per-trade value is `A_n − H_{F(n)} = (A_n − Ĥ_n) + (Ĥ_n − H_{F(n)}) ≥ ε − |Ĥ_n − H_{F(n)}|`, which is `≥ ε/2` on all but finitely many trading days as soon as the error vanishes *pointwise* — the proof's "bounded by calibration" step is not needed (the sketch's cumulative use is a flaw of the sketch, not evidence for the stronger definition). With `Ĥ_n := H_n(φ)` the pointwise error vanishes for every inductor, so Theorem 3's conclusion holds with **no predictor hypothesis at all**: the hypothesis is an artefact of the proof route, and the source's error (L3019/L1558, repeated at L3057: "Without that assumption, we don't have it") is to present the conclusion as *dependent* on it — not the hypothesis's non-triviality. The corpus later said the right thing: `trust-between-inductors-summary-v2` §2.1, "holds unconditionally (the trivial predictor … already *suffices*)" — suffices for the conclusion, not "is asymptotically calibrated". The inventory's and the mandate's "essentially its conclusion" (squeeze-shaped) is also wrong: the hypothesis is neither equivalent to the conclusion, nor always true, nor needed.

Status: **Theorem 3 proved unconditionally per sentence, with the predictor hypothesis bypassed** — `thm3_unconditional` (`Column.lean`, kind L, grade (a)): (a) the pointwise-vanishing weakening at the trivial predictor (`trivialPredictor_error_tendsto_zero`), (b) the conclusion `A_∞(φ_j) = H_∞(φ_j)` as `deferred_column_tendsto` (T6 (ii)), whose proof uses no predictor, for **any** inductor over the deferred process, matching the source's "for every `A ∈ LI(H,F)`" quantifier. The Lean is *stronger* than the source (no predictor hypothesis; any reader), under the package's rendering (the reader's expectation of the ledger contract under FAF's criterion over the ledger process, T8 (i) — the real-payout argument under the source's own criterion `LI(H,F)` has no FAF object, F-J). The source's cumulative "asymptotically calibrated" is neither assumed nor discharged anywhere in the package. Part (c) of the source's statement ("`A_∞` is coherent", Garrabrant 4.1.2) is FAF's limit-coherence fact and is not re-proved here. The cluster's uniqueness quantifier is `pinning_approximants_agree` (kind L; the second clause of that corollary needs no inductor, only the two approximation hypotheses).
