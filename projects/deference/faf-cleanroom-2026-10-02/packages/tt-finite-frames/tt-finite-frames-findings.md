## F-T2 — the lab's chain gives the middle agent two priors (imprecision)

Source: trust-lab-063 (`TTTransitivity.lean` `PA = condExpert πH cA`, `PB = condExpert πA cB`, `link1 : TT πH PA`, `link2 : TT πA PB`). In the chain `H → A → B`, expert `A`'s *frame* `PA` is `H`'s own conditional expert on `A`'s partition (built from `πH`), while `A`'s *deferral* in Link 2 uses `πA`. So the two links are about different objects both called `A`: Link 1 is `H` trusting `H`'s own partition expert on `A`'s partition, and Link 2 is `A` trusting `A`'s own partition expert on `B`'s partition — each a self-instance of `TT_condExpert`. The lab did not hide this: its header says both short links hold "by lemma 1, hence with the genuine ∀X∀s quantifier" (lemma 1 being `TT_condExpert`, the self-instance) and calls the recovery "the failure is caused by the prior mismatch, not by the frame"; trust-lab-063's inventory line likewise writes `PB' = condExpert πH cB`. The recorded chain therefore has no link in which the deferrer's prior differs from the prior the frame is built from; its long-edge failure is real (`T2.long_edge_gap = −1/8`), and the long edge is the only place two different priors meet. Severity: imprecision (the source's conclusion "chain-transitivity of TT is false" survives, and the source is careful about scope); ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED as to whether the lab intended `A` to have one prior. Repair: T4 (`Chains.lean`, `T4.totalTrust_not_transitive`) with one prior per agent, both links `∀ X ∀ s` through T3 (`T4.agree1`, `T4.agree2`). **Qualifier (audit r1 fidelity N3):** at the frame level T4's links are self-instances too, necessarily — T3's (⇐) direction is the frame identity `Frame.ofPartition_P_eq_of_agree`, so `T4.FA_P_eq_own : FA.P = (ofPartition πH cA).P` and `T4.FB_P_eq_own : FB.P = (ofPartition πA cB).P`; cross-prior Total Trust in a partition expert *is* identity of that expert with the deferrer's own partition expert, so no partition-expert chain can have a link that is not a self-instance at the frame level. T4's repair of F-T2 is therefore in the bookkeeping (one prior per agent; the identity holds between *distinct* priors, as in `Sharper.recovery`), not in the trust relation. Lean: `T2.lab_chain` (as recorded), `T2.link1`/`T2.link2` are `totalTrust_ofPartition` instances.

## F-B6 — the targeted-suppression "iff" of miri I9.4 needs an upper bound and splits at `α = 1/2` (imprecision)

Source: `corrigibility/workflow-2026-09-13/positive/miri.md` I9.4: "the targeted point `(α, β')` is a garbling iff `β' ≥ α(1−β)/(1−α) = 0.0053`" (for `(α, β) = (1/20, 9/10)`); the mandate B6 repeats it for `0 ≤ α < β ≤ 1`. Two corrections. (i) The upper bound `β' ≤ β` is implicit (targeted *suppression* lowers `β`): every garbling `(α, β')` of `(α, β)` with `α ≤ β` has `β' ≤ β` (`targeted_necessary`, second clause). (ii) The lower end `α(1−β)/(1−α)` is attained at `p = 0`, where the channel entry `q = α/(1−α)` must be `≤ 1`, i.e. `α ≤ 1/2`; for `α ≥ 1/2` the constraint `q ≤ 1` binds and the admissible interval is `[1 − β(1−α)/α, β]` (e.g. `(α, β) = (0.9, 0.95)`: the printed formula gives `0.45`, the true lower end is `161/180 ≈ 0.894`). Lean: `blackwellLE_binarySensor_iff` (the parallelogram, exact), `targeted_iff` (`0 ≤ α ≤ 1/2`, `α < β ≤ 1`: garbling ⟺ `α(1−β)/(1−α) ≤ β' ≤ β`), `targeted_iff_half` (`1/2 ≤ α < 1`, `α < β ≤ 1`: garbling ⟺ `1 − β(1−α)/α ≤ β' ≤ β`; added in repair round 1 — the audit noted the `α > 1/2` formula had been asserted, not proved), `targeted_necessary` (the `α ≤ 1/2` lower bound is necessary for every `α < 1`). The two branches agree at `α = 1/2` (both ends equal `1 − β`). I9.4's instance has `α = 1/20 ≤ 1/2`, so its number `0.0053` and its conclusion stand. Severity: imprecision (a missing side condition; the instance is right).

## F-G3b — Geanakoplos's second result for a partitional coarser experiment needs neither transitivity nor nesting of `E₁`, and holds for every prior (imprecision: a stronger hypothesis than needed; proved in repair round 1)

Source: `Deference and Infinite Frames` §2 ll. 142–146 ("both of Blackwell's results continue to hold when `E₁` is reflexive, transitive, and nested, as long as `E₂` is partitional"); `fixpoint-lit-inventory` 026 ("both halves of 025"). The first result is `geanakoplos_partition_coarse` (RTN `E₁` refining partitional `E₂` never loses; audit r1 noted the second half was neither stated nor listed open). The second result — an `E₁` that does not refine a partitional `E₂` is beaten by some prior and menu — is `partition_coarse_converse`, and the proof uses **only reflexivity** of `E₁` (needed so that its cells have positive mass) and works for **every** full-support prior, with the strict inequality for **every** pair of recommended strategies: with `w` such that `E₁ w ⊄ E₂ w`, the menu `{0, O}`, `O = 𝟙_{E₂ w} − d·𝟙_{W∖E₂ w}` with `d = π(E₁ w ∩ E₂ w)/π(E₁ w ∖ E₂ w) + 1`, makes the `E₂`-agent pointwise optimal (it knows the cell `E₂ w`) while the `E₁`-agent declines `O` at `w` because `∑_{E₁ w} π O = −π(E₁ w ∖ E₂ w) < 0`. So in Weatherson's sentence the RTN hypothesis carries only the first result; the second is a statement about the partitional experiment alone (the informed agent on a partition is omniscient about its cells, and any correspondence that does not respect a cell can be made to pay for it). Existential form in the shape of `weatherson_ii`: `partition_coarse_converse_exists`. Inhabitant of the full package (repair round 2, audit r2 fidelity N5): `Lab042.converse_witness` — trust-lab-042's `K₂ = {{0},{1},W}` (RTN) does not refine the partitional anchor `Q = {{0,2},{1},{0,2}}`, so under the uniform prior some menu makes every `Q`-recommended strategy strictly beat every `K₂`-recommended one (on the menu the proof builds at `w = 2`, `{0, (1, −3, 1)}`, the `Q`-agent earns `2/3`, the `K₂`-agent `1/3`); with `Lab042.values` (the anchor ties with the finer `K₁` at `4/9` on the lab's own menu) this is G3 and its converse on one frame. Severity: presentation — Weatherson's sentence is true as stated (a stronger statement also holds and is proved here; that is a finding about the mathematics, not an error of the source); for the package, an item the audit asked to be stated open and which is now proved.

## F-I2 — v6 §2.2's "continuum-many, gapless values" overstates the finite theorem (imprecision)

Source: `deference-in-logical-induction-v6` §2.2 ("A reasoner at once modest and conditional-tower-coherent needs the expert's estimates to take continuum-many, gapless values — an infinite, self-referential process"); trust-lab-005 ("near-miss: as the gap → 0 (a continuum of values) the implication fails"). What is proved (`softCM_immodest`): on a *finite* frame, the soft identity for all widths below the (always positive) spectral gap forces immodesty on the support. What this excludes is *finitely many gapped values*; it says nothing about countably many values (which can be gapless, e.g. dense in an interval, on a countable frame), so "continuum-many" is not what the finite theorem forces — "not finitely many" is. There is no finite "gap → 0" near-miss to compile: every finite frame has a positive gap, so the flag in trust-lab-005 ("the gap→0 near-miss must compile or the result is an artifact of the gap hypothesis") asks for something that does not exist at finite `W`; the hypothesis is not an artifact, it is a theorem about finite frames (`Frame.gap_pos`). Severity: imprecision (the conclusion "clean modest deference lives only on infinite frames" survives; the cardinality clause overstates). See F-I2c for the sharper point that on a finite frame the gap is not what makes the reduction work.

## F-I2b — the soft hypothesis forms are all one proposition: the conditional martingale at every world (presentation)

`softCM_immodest_core` consumes, for each `X`, only *one* width below the gap (with any `0/1`-shaped indicator) at all thresholds; the corpus's "for all `δ` below the gap", the rational-width form, and a single fixed width `δ₀` for *all* `X` each yield it (`softCore_of_gap_form`, `exists_rat_btwn`, `softCore_of_fixed_width`). The relation between these forms is not "stronger"/"weaker" in either direction — that was the mandate's trap (ii), and the first two versions of this finding repeated it (audit r2 adversarial B1 / fidelity N2). By instantiation the forms are **incomparable** (the fixed-width form says nothing at other widths; the gap form says nothing at `δ₀` for an `X` whose gap is at most `δ₀`); through the collapse they are **equivalent**: each is equivalent to the hard identity `CondMartingaleAt π F w` at *every* world, for any ramp family, any `δ₀ > 0` and any prior with no sign condition (`softCore_iff_condMartingaleAt_all`, `softCM_iff_condMartingaleAt_all`, `softCM_fixed_width_iff_condMartingaleAt_all`, `softCM_hyp_iff_fixed_width`; the direction hard ⟹ soft, `soft_identity_of_condMartingaleAt_all`, holds for *every* weight function of the estimate, not only ramps, by regrouping the sum over cells). This is exactly the corpus's own intermediate sentence — "the hypothesis collapses to `E(X) = E_π(X ∣ 𝒫)`" (`deference-in-logical-induction-v1` §5.2's proof, v6 §2.2) — which the package's proof of record established en route and now exports as `condMartingaleAt_all_of_soft_core` (no positivity of `π w`: the guard `0 < π w` is used only to pass from the identity to immodesty via I1). Consequence for the witnesses: every inhabitant of I2's `hsoft` is a conditional martingale at every world, so for a full-support prior the inhabitants are exactly T1's partition experts and `T4.FA_witness` is the generic inhabitant. Severity: presentation. (Version history: the first version said the fixed-width statement "is not what is proved" — proved in repair round 1, `softCM_immodest_fixed_width`; the second called it a weaker hypothesis — corrected in repair round 2.)

## F-I2c — on a finite frame the spectral gap is a red herring: one fixed width suffices, by rescaling (imprecision in the sources' framing; proved in repair round 1)

Source: trust-lab-005 ("spectral gap `γ > 0`", "all `δ < γ`"); `deference-in-logical-induction-v6` §2.2; the mandate's I2 and trap (ii). `softCM_immodest_fixed_width`: the soft identity at a *single* width `δ₀ > 0`, for all `X` and all thresholds, forces immodesty on the support, with no mention of the gap. The reason is that the hypothesis is quantified over all `X` and hence closed under rescaling: at `λ = 2δ₀ / gap F X` the identity for `λX` at width `δ₀`, divided by `λ` (using `E_w(λX) = λ E_w X`), is the identity for `X` with the `0/1`-shaped indicator `J t x := ι δ₀ (λt) (λx)` of width `δ₀/λ = gap F X / 2` — exactly `softCM_immodest_core`'s hypothesis at `X` (`softCore_of_fixed_width`). So what makes the finite reduction work is the scale-freedom of the hypothesis, not the gap: the sources' hypothesis carries a redundant gap clause — the gap-form and fixed-width hypotheses are the same proposition (`softCM_hyp_iff_fixed_width`, F-I2b), so neither is stronger than the other, and the "spectral gap" motivation does not describe what the finite proof uses. On a finite frame every `X` is bounded and the gap-form hypothesis is affine-invariant in `X` (the identity at `(aX + b, at + b, aδ)` is `a` times the identity at `(X, t, δ)`, with the rescaled family still a ramp), so v6 §2.2's restriction to bounded `X` (l. 318) changes nothing here; the gap clause can only matter on an infinite frame where the scale of `X` is pinned (bounded payoffs, gapless estimate values), which is where v6 §2.2's discussion lives — this package makes no claim about that case. Severity: imprecision (the sources' framing does not describe what the finite proof uses); for consumers, cite either form — they are equivalent — and the fixed-width one is the simpler (audit r1 fidelity N2, r2 both lenses).

## F-S1 — Theorem 5.1's frame form collapses to T1 (presentation; the per-quote form is the content)

Source: `route-transitivity` §5.1 Theorem 5.1 ("nesting suffices"). In frame form, when `c` refines `d` the composite `D.comp C` *is* `D` (`comp_ofPartition_eq_of_refines`, the tower at the level of rows), so "`π` totally trusts `D.comp C`" is T1 for `d` and carries no cross-agent content. What the inhabitant shows about 5.1's content: when `a X` is both `c`- and `d`-measurable it is constant on the cells of the *meet* of the two partitions (the components of their overlap graph), where two tower steps give `a = E_π(X ∣ meet cell)`; so `thm51`'s inequality at `(X, t)` coincides with T1's inequality for the meet partition's expert at `(X, t)` — even when, as here, the frame `D.comp C` is not that expert. Formalized with the coarsening as a parameter (the join itself is not constructed): `quote_eq_condExp_of_common_coarsening` — if `c` and `d` both refine `e` and `a X` is constant on `e`-cells then `a X = E[X ∣ e]` — and `thm51_of_common_coarsening`, which derives `thm51`'s inequality at such an `X` from T1 for `e`'s expert alone (`Squares.lean`); on the `Fin 6` inhabitant the join is `{{0,1,2,3},{4,5}}` and `Thm51Inhabitant.quote_eq_join` checks it. The note's random sweep (895 failures of 4000 frames on `|W| = 4`, 0 among the 1982 frames where `a` is `𝔐`-measurable) is recorded here, not in Lean. Severity: presentation.
