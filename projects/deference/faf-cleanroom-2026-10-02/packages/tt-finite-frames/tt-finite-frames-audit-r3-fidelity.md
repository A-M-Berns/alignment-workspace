**N2 — F-I2c overstates what the rescaling shows, and grades the sources' correct framing an "imprecision".** F-I2c's title: "on a finite frame the spectral gap is a red herring"; body: "So what makes the finite reduction work is the scale-freedom of the hypothesis, not the gap … the 'spectral gap' motivation does not describe what the finite proof uses"; severity "imprecision (the sources' framing does not describe what the finite proof uses)". Two corrections. (i) The package's own proof of record uses the gap essentially: `hard_threshold_of_soft` hardens the ramp *because* every attained value below `c*` is at least `gap` below it (step 1), `condMartingaleAt_all_of_soft_core`'s `hsep` uses `Frame.gap_le` on the separating `X*` (step 3), and `softCore_of_fixed_width` rescales precisely *to* width `gap F X / 2` (l. 484–488) — the scale-freedom is what lets a fixed width reach below the gap; the gap is what then does the hardening. `Frame.gap_pos` is the reason finite frames collapse at all, which F-I2 itself says. So "not the gap" and "does not describe what the finite proof uses" are false about this proof; what is true — and what F-I2c's own later sentence says — is that the gap clause *in the hypothesis* is redundant because the hypothesis is scale-free. (ii) Severity. trust-lab-005 states the hypothesis as "all `δ < γ`" and v1 §5.2 (l. 225) proves the proposition "for `δ` below the minimal gap": a correct sufficient hypothesis with a correct proof. That an equivalent gap-free form also suffices is a finding about the mathematics, not an imprecision of the source — exactly the standard the package adopted in round 2 for F-G3b ("Weatherson's sentence is true as stated; a stronger statement also holds and is proved here": severity presentation). Fix I would accept: retitle "on a finite frame the gap clause in the hypothesis is redundant: one fixed width suffices, by rescaling"; replace the "not the gap … does not describe what the finite proof uses" sentence by "the scale-freedom of the hypothesis reaches below the gap from any fixed width; the gap itself remains what the proof uses to harden the ramp (`hard_threshold_of_soft`) and to separate cells (`Frame.gap_le` in step 3)"; severity "presentation (the sources' hypothesis is correct and their proof uses the gap as this one does; an equivalent gap-free hypothesis also holds)".
