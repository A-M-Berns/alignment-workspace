## F1 — "Two cells are settled" is false in DDB's own definition of local Value (blocking for the two-cell Value claim; local error for the sources that assert it)

**Claim in the sources.** ddb-mm-authors C5 (`workflow-2026-09-13/critique/ddb-mm-authors.md` ll. 93–95; the quoted sentence is l. 95): "for two-cell questions the local conjecture of DDB §5 is settled: … Value w.r.t. `Q` holds iff `π(q | P(q) ≥ t) ≥ t` and `π(q | P(q) ≤ t) ≤ t` for all `t` — Simple Trust w.r.t. `q`, which is also Total Trust w.r.t. `Q`". Repeated as [reported] by legitimacy.md R5.3 (l. 100: "for two-cell `Q` it is settled … so the binary shutdown question stands on proved ground") and legitimacy-general-final.md Open problem 4 (l. 205: "proved equivalent only for binary questions"); the mandate's T4(b) asks for it as a theorem. (Corrected in repair round 1: legitimacy-general-final Statement 1 l. 43 and Statement 4 were cited here before, wrongly — Statement 1's two-cell sentence, "(V) on a two-cell Φ is strictly stronger than local (TT) even for immodest candidates", is about the *value form of Reflection* (its S6 (V), `ReflectsWrt`), i.e. DDB fn 66, which the package proves as `fn66_witness`; Statement 4 is the compliance rule and asserts nothing about two-cell Value.) DDB itself (§5 l. 398) asserts only the Total-Trust half ("you totally trust a frame with respect to every 2-cell question … iff you simply trust it"); the Value half is C5's own sketch ("recommended strategies threshold on `P(q)`").

**What is true.** The Total-Trust half holds exactly: `totalTrustWrt_questionOf_iff` (`TotalTrustWrt (questionOf q) π F ↔ SimpleTrustOn q π F`) and `forall_totalTrustWrt_questionOf_iff_simpleTrust` (DDB l. 398). Local Value implies the cuts (`valuesWrt_questionOf_imp_simpleTrustOn`).

**What is false.** With Value with respect to `Q` as DDB fn 64 defines it — for *every* recommended strategy — the cuts do not imply local Value. Witness `tie4` (`WitnessesFn66.lean`): `W = Fin 4`, `q = {0, 2}`, `π = (3/5, 2/5, 0, 0)`, rows `(3/5, 2/5, 0, 0)`, `(0, 0, 3/5, 2/5)`, `δ₂`, `δ₃`. Both support rows give `q` probability `3/5 = π(q)`, so `π` reflects on `{q, ¬q}` and totally trusts on it (`tie4_totalTrustWrt`); but the rows differ, so the menu `{𝟙_q − 3/5, 3/5 − 𝟙_q}` — tied at both support worlds — admits the recommended strategy that takes the decreasing option at `0 ∈ q` and the increasing option at `1 ∉ q`, worth `−12/25 < 0 = E_π(3/5 − 𝟙_q)` (`tie4_not_valuesWrt`, `tie4_refutes_two_cell`). The failure is a tie phenomenon: with strict inequalities the same choices would violate Simple Trust. The sources' sketch ("recommended strategies threshold on `P(q)`") is right about the threshold and wrong about ties: a strategy is cellwise in the full row `P_w`, not in `P_w(q)`, so two worlds with equal `P_w(q)` may be split.

**Surviving neighbours (proved, `TwoCell.lean`).** (i) `valuesWrt_questionOf_of_simpleTrustOn`: when the rows on the support are determined by `P_w(q)` (`hdet`), every recommended strategy is tie-consistent and local Value follows — this covers fn 66's frame (`fn66_valuesWrt`) and gives the two-cell equivalence `localTotalTrust_iff_localValue_two_cell` on that class. (ii) `weakValuesWrt_questionOf_of_simpleTrustOn`: in the weak reading (some recommended strategy beats every option) the implication holds unconditionally. The core (`valuesWrt_questionOf_core`) is a real proof: induction over upward-closed sets of worlds in `P_w(q)`, subtracting the lowest cluster's line and using the above cut at its probability; mirror for the lower part. **Where the position statement stands:** the binary shutdown question is on proved ground under either reading (i) or (ii); under fn 64's literal reading it is not, and no source states the tie issue.

**Reading caveat (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED).** DDB l. 117 defines a strategy as a function of the full row (`S_w = S_v` whenever `P_w = P_v`) and fn 64 quantifies over *every* recommended strategy; `ValuesWrt` is exactly that, so `tie4` refutes fn 65 as written. Whether the authors *intended* strategies in the local setting to be functions of the expert's opinion about `Q` only (`Q_* P_w`) is a question about intent; under that reading `tie4`'s strategy is inadmissible and `valuesWrt_questionOf_core` with its tie-consistency hypothesis `hSdet` is precisely the two-cell theorem. The finding is therefore: C5's sketch is right under the `Q_* P_w`-reading of "strategy" and wrong under DDB's stated definition.

**Robustness.** The refutation needs neither `π`-null worlds nor Dirac rows: `tieF_witness` (`WitnessesTieFull.lean`, adopted from the round-1 adversarial probe) has the full-support deferrer `(2/5, 1/10, 1/5, 3/10)`, full-support rows `(2/5, 1/5, 1/5, 1/5)` at worlds `0, 1` and `(1/5, 1/5, 2/5, 1/5)` at `2, 3`, `A(q) = B(q) = π(q) = 3/5`, two-cell local Total Trust, and the tie-split strategy loses `1/5`.
