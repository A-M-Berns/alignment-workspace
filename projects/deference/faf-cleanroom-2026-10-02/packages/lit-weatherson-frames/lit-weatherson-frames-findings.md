## F-T2 — Zhang's constraint 4 is unsatisfiable at `a = b` as printed, and false under the "only when `a ≠ b`" reading (local error / imprecision)

(i) Source, l. 59: "For any `a, b`: `C(p | A(p) = a ∧ B(p) = b)` is strictly between `a` and `b`." At `a = b` no number is strictly between `a` and `a`. (ii) Readings formalized (`Pooling.lean`): **R-literal** (`BetweenLiteral`, strict betweenness on every positive-mass cell including `a = b`): forces every agreement cell to be null (`BetweenLiteral.agree_null`) and is contradictory with deference to both experts for any weight of positive total mass (`zhang_literal_degenerate`) — degenerate. **R-open** (`BetweenOpen`, constraint 4 only when `a ≠ b`): **refuted**, `R8.refutes_open`: the eight-world model `(a, b, p)` with `h = 4/5`, `l = 2/5` and masses `3/50, 2/50, 3/50, 2/50, 17/50, 3/50, 7/50, 13/50` defers to both experts (`C(p | A = h) = 20/25 = h`, `C(p | A = l) = 10/25 = l`, same for `B`), has posterior `3/5 ∈ (2/5, 4/5)` on both disagreement cells, `C(A = B) = 40/50 < 1`, and the experts disagree on a positive-mass world. On the agreement cells the novice is *more extreme* than the agreeing experts: `C(p | A = B = h) = 17/20 > 4/5` and `C(p | A = B = l) = 7/20 < 2/5` (`R8.extreme_high`, `R8.extreme_low`). (iii) Surviving neighbour: **R-closed** (`Between4`: posterior in the closed interval `[min a b, max a b]`, strictly inside when `a ≠ b` — equivalently R-open plus the agreement clause `C(p | A = a, B = a) = a`), proved as `zhang_finite`; it fails on `R8` exactly at the agreement cells (`R8.not_between4`), as it must. The theorem as Weatherson states it needs the agreement clause. ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED: whether Zhang's own theorem (forthcoming; paper absent from the corpus) carries the agreement clause is not checkable here; the refutation is of Weatherson's printed statement.

## F-T2a — the proof gloss of l. 88 is not a proof (presentation)

Source, l. 88: "If one tries to make a similar construction to this one with only finitely many possible values for the probabilities, there will be some value which only the more informed probability can take, and in that case `C`'s posterior probability will be equal to the probability of the more informed expert." This names the extremal value but not why deference at it forces agreement, nor how the agreeing worlds above the extremal disagreeing value are handled. The proof of record is `no_top_disagreement` / `zhang_finite_upper` (`Pooling.lean`): among the supported worlds where the experts disagree, take one maximizing `max (A w) (B w)`; deference of the expert attaining that maximum `M`, split into the other expert's cells, is a sum of non-positive cell contributions `(c − M) · C(cell)` with the cell of the chosen world strictly negative. No induction is needed (the mandate's strong induction is replaced by this single step; see the report's "Deviations" 2).

## F-T2b — only the upper half of constraint 4 is used (presentation, a strengthening)

`zhang_finite_upper` assumes only `c ≤ max a b`, strictly when `a ≠ b` (`BetweenUpper`), never the lower bound. The symmetric statement with only the lower half follows by `Y ↦ −Y` (not compiled). So the theorem is really: a novice who defers to two experts and never plans to be *above* the more confident of them (strictly below when they disagree) must expect them to agree. Pointer: `Pooling.lean`, `zhang_finite_upper`; the same holds on countable carriers with finite range (`zhang_countable_finiteRange`).

## F-T2c — finiteness enters exactly once, and countability is not enough: item 024 answered negatively (new result)

The finite proof uses finiteness only for the existence of a maximal expert value on the disagreement set (`Finset.exists_max_image`); with a finite *range* on any carrier the same step works (`zhang_countable_finiteRange`, S3). **Weatherson's finiteness constraint 5 cannot be weakened to countability**: `Chain.zhang_countable_fails` (`Chain.lean`) exhibits on `ℤ × Bool` a full-support probability `C`, an event `Y = 𝟙_p`, and experts `A, B` with deference to both, strict closed-reading betweenness on every cell (`Between4C`), `A ≠ B` at every world (so `C(A = B) = 0 < 1`), a countably infinite range accumulating at `0` and `1`, and no finite set of values covering the support. Values `v k = 2^k/3` (`k ≤ 0`), `1 − 2^{1−k}/3` (`k ≥ 0`); `(A, B) = (v k, v (k+1))` at even `k`, swapped at odd `k`; masses `q k = 4^{k−1}` (`k ≤ 0`), `2·4^{−k}` (`k ≥ 1`); posteriors `c k = (10/27) 2^k` (`k ≤ 0`), `1 − (10/27) 2^{−k}` (`k ≥ 1`). The deference identity `q (j−1)(c (j−1) − v j) + q j (c j − v j) = 0` holds at every `j` (`Chain.identity`, three cases from the closed forms). This settles the open question of §1's last paragraph / §4 and inventory item 024 in the negative; the paper's Gaussian example is not the only obstruction, and the discrete one is formalized.

## F-T1a — Gallow's `λ ∈ (0, 1)` is used only as `λ ∉ {0, 1}` (presentation)

Source, l. 50: "For some `λ ∈ (0,1)`". `gallow_finite` (`Pooling.lean`) needs only `λ ≠ 0` and `λ ≠ 1`: the two one-sided identities `∑ C A (A − B) = 0` and `∑ C B (B − A) = 0` divide by `1 − λ` and by `λ` respectively, and add to `∑ C (A − B)² = 0`. Also unused: the `[0,1]`-valuedness of `A, B`, that `Y` is an indicator, and that `C` sums to one (`C ≥ 0` suffices). The paper's remark (l. 52) that the result "does not make any finiteness assumptions" is confirmed by `gallow_condExp` (S1, F-S1); the corollary `gallow_finite_of_zhang` (through Zhang) does need `0 < λ < 1`, since only then is the mixture strictly between when `a ≠ b`.

## F-T1b — Gallow's package is inhabited non-degenerately (non-vacuity)

`G5.witness` (`PoolingWitnesses.lean`): weights `(1/4, 1/4, 1/4, 1/4, 0)`, `A = (1, 1/2, 1/2, 0, 1)`, `B = (1, 1/2, 1/2, 0, 0)`, `Y = 𝟙_{0,1,4}` satisfy deference to both experts, `Pools` for every `λ`, and the conclusion `AgreeAE`, while `A ≠ B` as functions (they differ at the null world `4`) and the value `1/2` is genuinely uncertain (a two-world level set with `Y = 1, 0`). The theorem is about the support, and its hypothesis package is not trivially unsatisfiable. (Any inhabitant has `A = B` on the support — that is the theorem — so "agreeing experts" is the most non-degenerate witness possible.)

## F-T4 — the abstract's "breaks down in both directions" is false in the Value ⟹ Total Trust direction (blocking for the abstract's claim, under the positive-mass convention)

(i) Source, abstract l. 29: "The equivalence Dorst et al. establish between Total Trust and Value breaks down in both directions on infinite frames … Value can hold without Total Trust (when the option set is finite but the state space is countably infinite)"; §3 l. 186: "it breaks down even when `W` is countably infinite". (ii) Reading formalized: Total Trust in the product form of record (`TotalTrustC`, `TotalTrustInt`; on a `π`-null threshold event every summand is `0` — DDB Lemma 7.1's convention and `lit-ddb-frames`' `totalTrust_iff_cond`), Value as `ValueBdd` / `ValueFinInt`. Under it, `value_imp_totalTrust` (`CFrame.lean`): **Value ⟹ Total Trust on every countable frame**, by DDB's two-option witness, which needs no finiteness. So no countable frame can be Valued without being totally trusted, and the paper's Bentham (F-T10) is no exception. (iii) Surviving neighbour: with Total Trust read pointwise on null worlds (F-T10), Bentham fails at one null world. The abstract's first direction survives, restricted as in F-T8.

## F-T10 — Bentham's Total Trust "failure" conditions on a null event (blocking for the abstract's claim under DDB's convention)

(i) Source, l. 198: "Let `Y` be a random variable such that `Y(F = ∞) = 0`, and for all finite `n`, `Y(F = n) = 1 − 2^{−n}`. `E(Y | {w : Exp(Y, P(w)) ≥ 2/3}, π) = 0 < 2/3`. The only world `w` where `Exp(Y, P(w)) ≥ 2/3` is `F = ∞`, and at `F = ∞`, `Y = 0`." (ii) Verified: `Bentham.Y_event` — the threshold event is exactly `{F = ∞}` (`Bentham.E_succ_Y : Eℕ (P (n+1)) Y = 2/3 − 2^{−(n+1)}/3 < 2/3`, `Bentham.E_zero_Y : Eℕ (P 0) Y = 2/3`). But `π(F = ∞) = 0`: the conditional expectation is `0/0`, and the paper evaluates it as `Y(∞) = 0`. Under the convention of record only positive-mass events constrain, the product-form sum at `(Y, 2/3)` is `π 0 · (0 − 2/3) = 0 ≥ 0` (`Bentham.Y_event_null`), and Bentham **is totally trusted**: `Bentham.totalTrustC` (through T4 from `Bentham.valueBdd`) and `Bentham.totalTrustInt_direct` (through the truncations and `totalTrust_ofCorr_of_rtn` directly). So the Value ⟹ Total Trust direction has no countable counterexample in the paper, and by F-T4 can have none. (iii) Surviving neighbour: with Total Trust read as "`E_π(X | E(X) ≥ t) ≥ t` at every world of the event, null worlds included, evaluated pointwise", Bentham fails at the single world `F = ∞` where `P(∞) = π` — compiled in `Bentham.Y_event_null` as `Y 0 < 2/3 ∧ 2/3 ≤ Eℕ (P 0) Y ∧ π 0 = 0`; root-deference-012 already paraphrases the failure this way ("fails only at a null world"). Severity: blocking for the abstract's claim under DDB's convention; the paper's sentence is true under a null-event-conditioning convention it does not state.

## F-T8 — Total Trust ⇏ Value on Coin needs *both* unbounded options and an infinite menu; the abstract names only unboundedness (imprecision of the abstract)

Source, abstract l. 29: "Total Trust can hold without Value (when utilities are unbounded)"; l. 194: the two objections, "the value function used to generate the counterexample was unbounded" and "I didn't just make `W` infinite, I made `O` infinite as well". The ray lemma (`Ray.lean`, for every full-support prior on `ℕ`, not only Coin's geometric one) shows each objection *alone* restores Value: `ray_valueFinInt` (any finite menu of integrable options — Coin's unbounded `O_i` included) and `ray_valueBdd` (any menu of uniformly bounded options); `Coin.status` collects the instances. Only the conjunction — an infinite menu of non-uniformly-bounded options — fails (`Coin.not_valueInt`). Two refinements: (a) "bounded" must mean *uniformly* bounded across the menu: each `O i` is bounded (`Coin.O_bdd`), the family is not (`Coin.O_not_bddFam`), so the mandate's per-option `Bdd` reading of `ValueBdd` is false on Coin (report, "Deviations" 1); (b) the paper's cutoff proof of Total Trust on Coin (l. 190–192) is not needed — Total Trust follows from the finite Geanakoplos theorem on the lumped frames and a limit, for every integrable variable (`ray_totalTrustInt`), and the paper's "for any random variable `X`" (l. 190) must be read with the integrability the conditional expectations need.

## F-T6 — "the strategy `s` … is recommended" is right but recommended strategies are far from unique (presentation)

Source, l. 188: "The strategy `s` such that `s(F = i) = O_i` is recommended, as can be easily checked. But `E(s) = 0`". Verified (`Coin.s_recommended`, `Coin.stratValue_s`); the estimate table is `Coin.estimate_of_le` / `Coin.estimate_of_lt`: at world `n` (`F = n + 1`), `E_{P_n}(O_j) = 2^n/2` for `j ≥ n` and `2^j` for `j < n`, so **every** `O_j` with `j + 1 ≥ n` is optimal at `n` (`Coin.estimate_le`). Item 032's alternative `s'` (`O_{n−1}` at `n ∈ {2, 3, 4}`) is recommended with return `3/4 ≥ 1/2` (`Coin.weakValue_menu`): Value fails while Weak Value holds on the menu — the two notions DDB prove equivalent on finite frames separate on Coin (the inventory's separation, compiled as the instance on the menu `O`, not as the full `WeakValueInt` predicate).

## F-T6b — item 032's `s'` with "`E = +∞`" is a remark, not a theorem (imprecision of the inventory)

Inventory 032 proposes `s'(F = i) = O_{i−1}` everywhere, return `2^{i−2}` at every `i ≥ 2`, "giving `E_π(s') = +∞`". Its return `π n · O (n−1) n = 2^{−(n+1)} · 2^{n−1} = 1/4` at every `n ≥ 1` is not summable, so `stratValueC` would be Lean's junk `0`; no theorem is stated about it. `ValueInt` is guarded by summability of the strategy's return for exactly this reason (report, "Deviations" 5); the Weak Value witness uses the finitely supported `s'` of the mandate instead.

## F-T6c — the factor 2 in `O_i` (local error, the paper's; transcription note 3 confirmed)

## F-S2 — the cutoff argument's last step needs countable additivity and a possibly infinite last cell (presentation; not formalized)

Source, l. 190–192: "Since `Exp(X | {w : Exp(X, P(w)) ≥ t}, π)` is a weighted average of the values of `Exp(X | F ∈ I, π)` where `I` is one or other of the positive cells, it follows that …". The positive cells may be infinitely many and the last one infinite (`{c, c+1, …}`), so the "weighted average" is a countable convex combination, which needs the conditional expectations to exist (integrable `X`) and countable additivity. Not formalized (S2 not attempted); the lumping route proves the same conclusion for every integrable `X` and every full-support prior without cells (`ray_totalTrustInt`).

## F-S4 — the Gaussian counterexample (recorded; not formalized)

Source, §1 ll. 63–102: `X, Y, Z ~ N(0,1)` independent, `p = {X + Y + Z > 0}`, a fair coin deciding which expert knows `X` and which knows `X + Y`; the claim is constraints 1–4 with `C(p | A = a, B = b) = λ(a,b) b + (1 − λ(a,b)) a`, `λ ∈ (0,1)` the displayed ratio of Gaussian densities. Not attempted (XL; regular conditional distributions of Gaussians, conditioning on null events `A = a`). The structural half (constraints 1–2 from the tower property once `μ[𝟙_p | σ(H, A)] = A`) would need the two Gaussian conditional probabilities `μ[𝟙_{X+Y+Z>0} | σ(X)] = Φ(X/√2)` and `μ[… | σ(X+Y)] = Φ(X+Y)` as (b) hypotheses; constraint 4 needs the joint densities. E1 gives the discrete counterexample that the inventory (023, flag) asked for instead.

## F-S1 — Gallow's theorem without finiteness (confirmed, formalized)

Source, l. 52: Gallow's result "does not make any finiteness assumptions". Confirmed: `gallow_condExp` (`GallowCondExp.lean`) proves it over any probability space for bounded measurable experts and any target, with `λ ∉ {0,1}` (the proof is `E[B | A] = A`, `E[A | B] = B`, `E[(A − B)²] = 0`, as in the finite case).
