## F-4. "Traders don't touch large sentences" is claimed, never argued; and the program's proof sketch has a gap (local error in `bli-program` §2.1; the target stands) — Known issue 4, confirmed, with the gap made precise

**Pointer.** bli-slides-002/030, bli-paper-031 footnote; `bli-program` §2.1: "token count and every numeral length are $O(n^d)$".

**What is true (where the gap sits, sharpened in repair round 1 per audit r1 fidelity §3.7).** FAF's `parseRpn` accepts three spellings of a sentence slot: the canonical run, the Gödel escape `1 :: c`, and the structured paper-prime escape `1 :: 0 :: …`, which names the tag-5 atom `Formula.atom (Nat.pair 5 (Nat.pair polarity formulaCode))` by a *symbol-count* payload whose `formulaCode` is built by nested `Nat.pair`s — each of which roughly squares — so the atom's *index* has $2^{\Theta(L)}$ base-4 digits for a payload of $L$ tokens. The program's sentence is right about the **stream**: the numerals a polynomial-time trader writes *are* $O(n^d)$ digits long. What fails is the inference from that to the size of the **parsed** sentence under D3's definition of record — the *canonical* `tokenSize` (the digit-metered canonical RPN run, chosen for decidability): a polynomial-length machine word can name, through the structured escape, an atom of canonical `tokenSize` $2^{\Theta(n^d)}$. Under a "shortest name" size measure there would be no gap. So the gap is real for D3's `tokenSize` and the doubly exponential `sizeBound n = 2^{2^n}` is what makes the target stand ($2^{2^n}$ dominates $2^{c\,n^d}$ eventually). `Bridge.bridge_lemma` is the first proof of the claim, and it is proved in full from FAF's `def:ec` metering — the stream lemma `exists_token_of_mentionedBy` (the decoder produces sentences only by decoding a token), the contraction lemma `mem_unRpn` (every token of the contracted stream is copied, a failure marker, or the Gödel code of a parsed block), the digit bounds on `undigitize`, the Gödel-escape bound `tokenSize_le_of_decode` (`tokenSize φ ≤ c` for `decode c = some φ`), the parse-size lemma `tokenSize_le_of_parseRpn` and the growth lemma `eventually_poly_le_two_pow` — and the structured-escape bound, F-4b, which was the package's one OPEN statement until continuation 1 proved it.

**F-4b (resolved by continuation 1; was the one OPEN of the package).** `tokenSize_le_of_structured`: `∃ C, parseStructuredPaperPrime ts = some (φ, rest) → tokenSize φ ≤ 2^(C·(ts.length − rest.length)) + C`, now proved in `Structured.lean` with `C = 10`. The first pass's diagnosis stands as a record of what does *not* work: every numeric invariant for `negFormulaCode` (`neg x + 1 ≤ (x+1)^K`, `d(neg x) ≤ K·d(x) + K'`, `d(neg x) ≤ K·d(x)²`) fails at the margin because additive or multiplicative slop compounds across nesting levels, and the relative-error argument only bounds the growth up to a polynomial with no fixed constant. **What works is to stop measuring numbers and measure shape.** `FTree k D n` ("`n` is a formula-code tree of depth `≤ D` whose leaves `Nat.pair a c + 1`, `a ≤ 3`, are `< 4^k`") is preserved by `negFormulaCode` at the *same* depth with the leaf bound raised by two digits (`FTree.neg`: the involution swaps `0↔1`, `2↔3` at the leaves — costing at most `+2`, or the constants `13`/`7` — and `4↔5`, `6↔7` at the nodes, recursing into the same subtrees), and any `FTree k D` is `< 4^(4^D·k)` (`FTree.lt_pow`). The parsers' outputs are trees by construction (`parseStructured_bounds`: fuel `f` gives `FTree (32·64^f) (2f)`; the `⟺` case at token `22` is the only one that adds two levels, hence `2f`). So the number of nested negations never enters the bound. The first pass's conditional ("if `negFormulaCode`'s digit growth were quadratic the bridge lemma would be false at `sizeBound = 2^{2^n}`") is settled: on the codes the parser can produce, `negFormulaCode` changes the digit count by at most a factor `4^D·(k+2)/(4^D·k)`, i.e. it is not even linear-with-a-constant but *bounded by the same tree bound*. A refinement worth recording for FAF: `negFormulaCode` is only ever applied by the parser to codes of the `FTree` shape, and `FTree` is exactly the image of Foundation's `Semiformula` `toNat` on negation-normal-form formulas — a Semiformula-level proof (the first pass's route (3)) would have worked too but needs Foundation's first-order `toNat`; the shape predicate avoids it.
