### 3.1 L3.0's witness inhabits the `E1x` clause degenerately (ledger wording)

Probe `E1xDayZero.lean`, P1: **any** market equal to `Q` on days `≥ 1` and at `(0, ⊥)` is `E1x`-related to `Q` (`e1x_of_agree_succ_and_falsum`), and in particular the market that replaces day `0` off `⊥` by an *arbitrary* valuation `w` is (`e1x_dayZero_arbitrary`). The docstrings carry the scope clause ("day-`0` edit; `smallSet 0 = {⊥}`") and the mandate designed the target this way, so this is not an undisclosed weakness — but the ledger's Witness column grades the row **N+** without saying that the agreement clause is met degenerately. Fix: in the L3.0 row's Witness column, add "`E1x` inhabited at the degenerate day (`smallSet 0 = {⊥}`); the non-degenerate agreement witness is L3.4's, over OPEN T7"; keep N+ for the exploitation content.

### 3.2 L3.3 has no closed inhabitant in the run, and its non-vacuity *is* T7 (ledger wording)

Probe `E1xDayZero.lean`, P2, makes the dependence sharper than the report states: FAF's `IsLogicalInductor` is a class with fields `marketComputable` and `processComputable`, so **L3.3's hypothesis package forces `ComputableDeductiveProcess DP`** (`package_forces_computable_process`), and since `hdec` + `hworld` decide every member at a computable stage, any inhabitant of the package carries a computable pseudorandom stream. Consequently (i) the two open statements `leakQ_isLogicalInductor` and `leakDP_computable` are *equivalent* (`LIA_is_logical_inductor` one way, `.processComputable` the other), and (ii) the question "is L3.3 vacuous?" is exactly li-pseudorandom's T7, not merely the instance's certificate. The mandate accepted this dependency and the status vocabulary is followed; I ask only that the L3.3 row's Witness column say "conditional on OPEN T7 — no closed inhabitant of the full package exists in the run; the package forces a computable process (probe P2), so T7 is its non-vacuity", and that the report's "grade (a)" for L3.3 be read as the implication's grade, which the report's wording ("over any inductor with a pseudorandom late-decided e.c. family") already allows.

### 3.3 E1's docstrings read as a trader-uniform delay; the package's own trader reads the leak on the edit day (presentation, with a probe)

`no_early_read_large : ∃ A E, ∀ n φ k, MentionedBy (Tr.strat n) φ → ¬ SmallOn k φ → 2 ^ k < A * (n + 1) ^ E` is honest as stated: `A`, `E` are chosen *per trader*, before `n` and `k`. But the docstring's "a leak planted on day `k` is unreadable before a day of order `(2 ^ k / A) ^ (1 / E)`", the root module's "unreadable before a day of order `2 ^ (k / E)`" (which drops `A`), and the ledger's "singly exponential in `k`" invite a uniform reading — that *no* e.c. trader reads a day-`k` leak before a day exponential in `k`. That reading is false, and the package's own trader shows it: probe `ReadsOnEditDay.lean`, P3 (`record_trader_reads_large_on_edit_day`): `leakTrader (leakAtom (4 ^ sizeBound N₀)) (fun _ ↦ N₀) memberAtom` (= `leakTraderRecord N₀`) is `EfficientlyComputable` **and** on day `N₀` mentions, in a `price` leaf, `leakAtom (4 ^ sizeBound N₀) N₀`, which is not `SmallOn N₀`. A trader with the pad hardcoded (a constant costs nothing in `n`) reads a day-`N₀`-large sentence on day `N₀` itself; its `A` exceeds `2 ^ N₀`. The correct reading of E1, which the theorem supports, is: for a *fixed* trader, as `k` varies, the first day it can name a day-`k`-large sentence grows like `2 ^ (k / E) / A ^ (1 / E)` — which is what refutes X8's "one trader reads every day's leak on the next day" (findings K2), and why L3.4 needs no waiting time. Fix: say "for a fixed trader, as `k` varies (its constants `A`, `E` may exceed `2 ^ k` for any particular `k`)" in the two docstrings and the ledger row; cite P3 as the reason the leak trader of record can read on its edit day.

### 3.5 `leakCoordinates_infinite` is an N+ row for a fact about indices, not values

The row proves that the *set of edited cells* `{(N₀, leakAtom (recordPad N₀) n)}` is infinite — immediate from `leakAtom_injective`, and true of any injective family regardless of what the market does. It does not show the leak market is not a `FiniteSupportPerturbation` of the base (FAF's notion is about cells where the *values* differ: `∃ S finite, ∀ d φ, (d, φ) ∉ S → P d φ = P' d φ`), which is the fact that would make `lic_iff_of_finiteSupportPerturbation` inapplicable. The report discloses this honestly (§ L3.5 "Why `¬ FiniteSupportPerturbation` is not stated outright") and notes that the value-level fact follows from exploitation under the instance hypothesis. I would grade the row N− with the report's reason, or fold it into `leakMarket_ne_leakQ`'s row rather than list it as a separate N+ witness.

### 3.6 Findings F-6 (ii) overstates the patience obstacle

F-6 (ii) says the market-reading weighting `W i := price (χ i) i` "is not `f`-patient" because its window sums grow like `f n − n`. That is right for growing deferrals but wrong for bounded-window ones: FAF's `DeferralPatient f W P := ∃ C, ∀ n, ∑_{i ∈ Icc n (f n)} (W i).denote P ≤ C`, and at `f = succDeferral` (the deferral the package itself uses in L3.5) every window has two terms in `[0, 1]`, so `W` is patient with `C = 2`. The obstacle is therefore specific to the universal-`f` form of E2 as the mandate stated it; the bounded-window case is blocked only by F-6 (i) (no FAF hook giving `Q n (χ n) ≈ₙ t n` for a mixed-truth family) and by (iii) (the claim's doubtfulness over an arbitrary inductor with a non-poly-time truth). Fix: qualify (ii) to unbounded windows and say that for `succDeferral` the weighting is patient and (i)/(iii) are what remain. The severity of F-6 as a whole (imprecision in the mandate; E2 open and to be re-scoped) is unaffected.
