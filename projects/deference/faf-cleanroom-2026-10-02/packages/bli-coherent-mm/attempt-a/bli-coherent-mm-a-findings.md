## F1 — Soto's Theorem 2: claimed with a stalled proof; where each of his two maps fails; clipping closes it (Known issue 1)

**Confirmed, with the gap located.** PDF 05 p. 3 states Theorem 2 ("given `T_n`, there is a PC-valuation `P_n` over `W_n` such that, for any `P_{n+1}`, `T_n` doesn't make a profit") and the sketch ends with "each has failed somewhere". The attempt proves the theorem (`coherent_fixed_point_abstract`, `coherent_fixed_point`) with Nash's map `f p u := (p u + max (G p u) 0) / (1 + ∑ max (G p, 0))`.

Where the two maps stall, in this attempt's reading (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED as a diagnosis of Soto's attempts; the algebra is this run's):

- **First map** `fix(P)(W) := P(W) + B(W)·P(W)·δ(P)`. The increment is proportional to `P(W)`, so a zero-mass world is never moved: a fixed point on the boundary with `B(W) > 0` and `P(W) = 0` is a fixed point of the map and says nothing about `B(W)`. That is exactly the case Soto marks "this breaks". Nash's map adds `max(B(W), 0)` *regardless* of `P(W)`, so a zero-mass world with positive benefit is pushed up; at a fixed point `p u · ∑ max(G p, 0) = max (G p u) 0` for every `u` (`nashMap_fixed_le`), so if any benefit is positive the clip mass `M` is positive, every world with positive mass has positive benefit, and `∑ p u · G p u > 0` contradicts the identity.
- **Second map** `fix(P)(W) := (P(W) + (B(W) − min B)) / ∑_{W'} (P(W') + (B(W') − min B))`. At a fixed point with `Z := ∑ (B − m) > 0` (`m := min B`), `P(W)·Z = B(W) − m`, so a minimiser has `P(W) = 0` (Soto's observation), and the identity `∑ P·B = 0` gives `Z·∑ P² = −m`. This is **not a contradiction**: with three worlds, `B = (√2 − 1, −2, −1)` and `P = (B − m)/Z = ((1+√2)/(2+√2), 0, 1/(2+√2)) ≈ (0.707, 0, 0.293)` satisfy both the fixed-point equation and `∑ P·B = 0` while `B₁ > 0`. So subtracting the minimum instead of clipping at `0` loses the inequality the argument needs; whether an actual strategy's `B_P` realises such a configuration at its fixed point is not decided here (it would need an explicit strategy), but the algebraic route Soto followed cannot conclude `B ≤ 0` from the identity alone — consistent with "I haven't been able to prove a contradiction by assuming `B(W') > 0`". The inventory's diagnosis is confirmed at this level.

Severity: local error in the source's proof (the theorem is true; proved here). Pointer: Soto PDF 05 p. 3 (Thm 2, the two displayed maps); `FixedPoint.lean` (`nashMap_fixed_le`).

## F10 — The enumeration order is classical; the acceptance test is decidable (new; honesty note on D4)

## F13 — T1 needs no atom bound in its finite-world form, and the `PCWorld` form needs it only on `T.support ∪ D` (new)

The mandate's T1 lists `hB : ∀ φ ∈ mentionedSet T ∪ D, atomBound φ ≤ B` as a hypothesis of the headline. The finite form `coherent_fixed_point` (over `WD D B`) needs **no** atom bound: the simplex is over `FiniteWorld B` and the strategy is evaluated in those worlds directly. The atom bound enters only when a `PCWorld` is restricted to `B` atoms (`coherent_fixed_point_pcWorld`, `dayValue_le_of_coherentAccepts`), and there only for the **traded** sentences (`value_eq_of_world_eqOn_support`) and the stage — `T.support ∪ D`, not `mentionedSet T ∪ D`. The mentioned set matters for the *history* cells (`S ⊇ mentionedSet T`), not for the world. The recursion's `B n` is taken over `S n ∪ D n ⊇ T.support ∪ D n`, as the mandate says. Severity: presentation (mandate T1).

## F14 — The mandate's justification of T0 names the wrong world (new; local)

Mandate T0: "the record's lemma quantifies over all `PCWorld`s, which the coherent maker cannot supply (it prices `φ ∧ ¬φ` at `0` and a world where both hold pays the trader)". A `PCWorld` is a Boolean valuation (`PCWorld.holds_neg : v.Holds (∼φ) ↔ ¬ v.Holds φ`), so no `PCWorld` holds both `φ` and `∼φ`; on `T_pair` the coherent maker's value is `1 − (π φ + π ∼φ) = 0` in **every** `PCWorld`. The world "where both hold" is FAF's `supportBitWorld T b` (an arbitrary Boolean table on the support, not a valuation) — that is F5, the all-Boolean-tables test. The reason the stage-relative T0 is needed is different and correct: a `PCWorld` **inconsistent with `D`** can pay a coherent maker (with `D = {φ}` it prices `φ` at `1`; a strategy selling `φ` gains `1` in a world refuting `φ`), and the coherent bound (`CoherentAccepts`, `dayValue_le_of_coherentAccepts`) holds only on `D`-consistent worlds, which is exactly what `Exploits` ranges over. Severity: local error in the mandate's justification; the conclusion (T0 is needed and sufficient) stands.
