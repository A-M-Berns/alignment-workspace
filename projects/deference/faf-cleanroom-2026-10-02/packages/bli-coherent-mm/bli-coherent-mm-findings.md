## About the sources

### F1 — Soto's Theorem 2 is true and now proved; both of his maps stall, and two different clips close the gap (Known issue 1; both attempts)

PDF 05 p. 3 states Theorem 2 and the sketch ends with "each has failed somewhere". Both attempts prove it (`coherent_fixed_point_abstract`, `coherent_fixed_point`), by different maps: attempt A by Nash's map `f p u := (p u + max (G p u) 0) / (1 + ∑ max (G p, 0))`, attempt B by the clip-and-normalise map `g p u := max 0 (p u + G p u) / ∑ max 0 (p + G p)`. Where Soto's two maps stall, in this run's reading (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED as a diagnosis of his attempts; the algebra is this run's, found independently by both attempts and agreeing): his **first map** `fix(P)(W) := P(W) + B(W)·P(W)·δ(P)` multiplies the step by `P(W)`, so a zero-mass world with positive benefit is a fixed point that says nothing about `B(W)` (the case Soto marks "this breaks"); his **second map** subtracts `min B` and normalises, and at a fixed point the minimiser has `P(W) = 0` while the identity gives only `Z·∑ P² = −min B`, which is **not a contradiction** — attempt A exhibits three worlds with `B = (√2 − 1, −2, −1)` satisfying the fixed-point equation and `∑ P·B = 0` with `B₁ > 0`. The common mechanism of the two repairs: the map must move a zero-mass world with positive benefit, and it must not subtract a global constant that lets a positive benefit survive at a positive-mass world. The inventory's diagnosis ("clipping at `0`") is confirmed twice over. Severity: local error in the source's proof (the theorem is true). Pointer: Soto PDF 05 p. 3; `AttemptA/FixedPoint.lean` (`nashMap_fixed_le`), `AttemptB/FixedPoint.lean` (`clipMap_fixed_le`); record `FixedPoint.lean`.

### F7 — PDF 04's intermediate-payoff scheme assigns probability `1/2` to every undecided prime and fails exactly there (Known issue 7; both attempts; T8)

`Payoffs.payoff_eq_uniform`: the running total a share of `W` has received is `1/|completions s|` if `W` completes the decision state and `0` otherwise — the uniform distribution on assignments conditioned on the decisions; Soto's three increment rules are its differences and telescope along every refinement path (`sum_increments`). Once every prime is decided, worlds and bundles pay their truth (`payoff_full`, `bundle_full`) — the only case in which "the expected reward works out", as the source says. On a never-decided prime the scheme silently prices each side at `1/2` independently, and `half_forcing` shows the per-step consequence: splitting an unrefuted world on a fresh prime and buying the cheaper child at any `θ < 1/2` pays `2^{−(m+1)}` at once against a cost `θ·P(W) < 2^{−(m+1)}` when `P(W) ≤ 2^{−m}`. The source concedes it ("we need all prime formulas to be eventually decided"). ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED: the uniform-average reading (2-003 (i)) and the `½`-forcing (2-003 (iii)) are this run's observations. Severity: local error / hidden assumption in PDF 04, conceded.

## About the mandate and the program

### F10 — `IsWorldMarginal (coherentMarketMaker …).quote S D B` is unsatisfiable as written; coherence of a quote is `CoherentQuoteOn` (both attempts, independently: A's F7, B's F-B2)

`bli-finite`'s `IsWorldMarginal V A D B` requires `V φ = π w φ` for every `φ ∈ GenBy A` — the whole algebra, including `⊤` (marginal `1`) and compounds outside `S` — while a `RationalBeliefState` quotes `0` off its entries. So no finite-support state is `IsWorldMarginal` on `GenBy S`. Both attempts, independently, restated T2(b) and T6's coherence as agreement with a world marginal on `S` (A: `CoherentQuoteOn`; B: `IsWorldMarginal (piTable w) S D B ∧ ∀ φ ∈ S, quote φ = piTable w φ`, plus `CoherentOn`), which is what "exactly coherent on `S`" means. The record uses `CoherentQuoteOn`, with the `IsWorldMarginal` form for the full marginal function (`coherentMarketMaker_isWorldMarginal`) and the `CoherentOn` packaging (`coherentOn_actualTable_of_marginal`). Dependents (`bli-measure`) should cite `pcOverlay_coherent_mentioned` / `pcOverlay_coherentQuoteOn`, never an `IsWorldMarginal` of a quote. Severity: imprecision in the mandate (T2(b), D5 `coh`, T6).

### F11 — T6's coherence clause and `PCInductor.coh` cannot be unconditional: FAF's `DeductiveProcess` admits stages with no consistent world (both attempts, independently: A's F8, B's F-B9)

`DeductiveProcess` has fields `D` and `mono` only; `D n = {⊥}` is legal, and on such a day no world measure over `DP.D n` exists. Both recursions take a fallback on such a day (then no `PCWorld` is consistent with the stage, so FAF's `Exploits` imposes nothing and `pcOverlay_no_ec_trader_exploits` is unconditional), state coherence under `hcons n : ∃ v, v.ConsistentWith (DP.D n)` — FAF's own `hworld`, discharged at every stage of `paperDP 𝗜𝚺₁` — and inhabit `PCInductor DP` under `∀ n, hcons n`. The mandate's unconditional `∀ n, IsWorldMarginal …` is true only for processes with a consistent world at every stage. Severity: imprecision in the mandate and `bli-program` §2.7.

### F12 — T1 needs no atom bound in its finite-world form; the `PCWorld` form needs it only on `T.support ∪ D`; T2(a)/T3(a) need none (attempt A's F13, confirmed by the comparison)

The mandate lists `hB` on `mentionedSet T ∪ D` as a hypothesis of T1's headline. Attempt A's `coherent_fixed_point` (the record's) has **no** atom bound and any prior `History`; the bound enters only on restricting a `PCWorld` to `B` atoms, and only for the traded sentences and the stage. Attempt B's headline carries the mandate's `hB` throughout (and so do its T2(a)/T3(a)), which is why its transported existence theorems (`exists_coherentAccepts_viaB`, `…_fullSupport_viaB`) carry `hB` where the record's do not. The mentioned set matters for the *history* cells (`S ⊇ mentionedSet T`), not for the world. Severity: presentation (mandate T1).

### F13 — The mandate's angle-B normaliser bound is false; positivity is what the argument needs (attempt B's F-B1)

Mandate §Attempt angles (B): "the denominator is `≥ ∑ (p + G p) = 1` by the identity". The identity is `∑ p u · G p u = 0`, not `∑ G p u = 0`; attempt B's two-world example (`p = (0.9, 0.1)`, `G p = (0.05, −0.45)`) has `Z = 0.95 < 1`. What is needed is `Z(p) > 0` on the simplex, which holds since `∑ p u (p u + G p u) = ∑ p u² > 0` (`clipNorm_pos`). Severity: local error in the mandate's sketch; no effect on the result.

### F14 — The mandate's justification of T0 names the wrong world (attempt A's F14)

Mandate T0: "the record's lemma quantifies over all `PCWorld`s, which the coherent maker cannot supply (it prices `φ ∧ ¬φ` at `0` and a world where both hold pays the trader)". No `PCWorld` holds both `φ` and `∼φ` (`PCWorld.holds_neg`); on `T_pair` the coherent maker's value is `0` in **every** `PCWorld` (`pair_value_eq_zero`). The world "where both hold" is FAF's `supportBitWorld T b` — that is F5/F6. The correct reason T0 is needed: a `PCWorld` **inconsistent with `D`** can pay a coherent maker (with `D = {φ}` it prices `φ` at `1`; a strategy selling `φ` gains `1` in a world refuting `φ`), and the coherent bound holds only on `D`-consistent worlds, which is exactly what `Exploits` ranges over. The conclusion stands. Severity: local error in the mandate's justification.

### F15 — The identity for T1 needs only `∑ p = 1` (attempt A's F15; attempt B's `sum_worldValue_eq_zero` says the same)

Both attempts' identity lemmas take only total mass one, negative weights allowed; the support condition enters only to make the conclusion a statement about `D`-consistent worlds. Severity: presentation.

### F16 — The enumeration order of attempt A's maker is classical; attempt B's maker is a rounding of a `Classical.choose` (attempt A's F10; reconciler's comparison)

Attempt A's `decodeWeights` reads a decoded `List ℚ` through `Fintype.equivFin (FiniteWorld B)` (Mathlib chooses it through `Trunc`), so the enumeration — hence `coherentWeights`, `interiorWeights` — is `noncomputable` for a second reason besides the `Nat.find`; the **acceptance test** is a genuine `Decidable` instance. A computable enumeration (reading coordinates through the bits of the world) would remove the `Trunc`; not done. Severity: presentation.

## The reconciler's findings (R1–R8)

### R1 — The two attempts' D3 bundle different side conditions; the predicates are equivalent where it matters (definition of record chosen)

Attempt A's `CoherentAccepts` is `IsWorldMeasure w D ∧ (bound in every `D`-consistent world)`; attempt B's is `mentionedSet T ⊆ S ∧ (the same bound on `candidateRationalHistory past n (ofWeights S w)`)`. The mandate's D3 is the bound alone "together with `S ⊇ mentionedSet T` (as a hypothesis or a field)" and filters by `IsWorldMeasure` in D4 — so both attempts deviate, each bundling one side condition. `coherentAccepts_iff_attemptB` proves the two equivalent for a world measure and `S ⊇ mentionedSet T` (the only regime in which either is used), cell for cell. Both attempts' makers pass the record's predicate (`coherentWeights_accepts`, `gridCoherentWeights_accepts`, `gridInteriorWeights_accepts`) although they choose different candidates — the mandate's cross-check. Severity: n/a (a reconciliation decision, recorded).

### R8 — T7 stated OPEN by the reconciler; the finite-support reduction proved; the import the attempts feared does not clash

Neither attempt stated the omni-trader fixed point, both because the `Mathlib.MeasureTheory.Measure.ProbabilityMeasure` import beside FAF's vendored PFR was never tested. The reconciler tested it (a probe importing FAF's `BooleanWorlds` and Mathlib's `ProbabilityMeasure` elaborates; every needed instance — Borel σ-algebra, `MeasurableSingletonClass`, compactness of the Cantor space and its closed subsets, the weak topology on `ProbabilityMeasure` — is found) and wrote `Omni.lean`: `ConsistentWorlds D` (closed by FAF's `isClosed_consistentWith` at a constant process, compact), `omniPrice`, `omniBenefit` (a `tsum` over all sentences), and **`omni_fixed_point`**, the package's one `sorry`, listed in `bli-coherent-mm-open.txt`. The **proved companion** `omni_fixed_point_of_finiteSupport`: a demand vanishing off a finite `S` (within the atom bound) and factoring through the marginal of `μ` on the first `B` atoms has a fixed point — the finite benefit `finBenefit` satisfies the zero-expected-value identity from `∑ q = 1` (`sum_mul_finBenefit`) and is continuous in the simplex coordinates, so T1's abstract lemma gives `q*` on the simplex over `WD D B`; the Dirac mixture `∑ x, ofReal (q* x) • dirac (embedWorld x)` is a probability measure on the consistent worlds (`diracMixtureP`) whose marginal is `q*` (`omniMarginal_diracMixtureP`) and whose prices are `q*`'s marginals (`omniPrice_diracMixtureP`), and the benefit of the factored demand at a consistent world `v` is the finite benefit at `restrict v B` (`tsum_eq_sum` plus FAF's `eval_toBoolPCWorld_restrict`). So the open part of T7 is **exactly** the infinitely-supported case, as the mandate conjectured. Two remarks for a continuation: (i) the OPEN statement takes weak continuity of `G` and a uniform ℓ¹ bound as hypotheses, this run's reading of "an infinite strategy with ℓ¹ demands" (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED as a reading of Soto; he states no hypotheses); (ii) the reduction does not need continuity of the marginal map `μ ↦ omniMarginal D B μ` in the weak topology, because the fixed point is found on the finite simplex and realised by a Dirac mixture — a proof that the infinitely-supported case reduces to a limit of finite cases would need that continuity (it holds: the sets `{w | restrict w B = u}` are clopen), which is the natural next step. Severity: n/a (status).
