Findings about the research ([STANDARDS](../../STANDARDS.md) §5), each with a severity (blocking the note's conclusion / local error / imprecision / presentation) and a pointer. ## F1 — The all-`n` strong reading of the learning desideratum is self-inconsistent on the model (blocking for any statement assuming it)

**Source.** bli-soto-a-073 (`soto-email-thread-2023.md` ll. 231–262): "Strong interpretation: *For every `n`, there is a time `m`, beyond which all actions can be interpreted as being chosen by `Q_n`.* (This strong interpretation only allows later actions to be even more updateful than `Q_n` if `Q_n` itself endorses the further updatefulness.)" The email says "I was assuming the strong interpretation rather than the weak one."

**Finding.** `strongLearns_inconsistent` (Kind P): on the model with `gain > 0`, `0 < N`, whenever a round `k < K` lies in both tails (`f 0 ≤ k`, `f N ≤ k`), no policy strongly learns at every `n` — at `k` it must pay (as `Q_0 = P`) and refuse (as `Q_N`). The parenthetical is exactly why: `Q_0` does not endorse `Q_N`'s refusals. A theorem whose hypothesis is "strong `f`-learning for all `n`" is therefore vacuous on every tree where two states disagree on a late round. This is why Theorem A (`theoremA`) is stated with `StrongLearnsAt N`, strong learning at the one decided state — the weakest hypothesis with content — and why the mandate's design decision §3.3 (ii) is a theorem, not a convention. ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED: that the email's parenthetical *intends* the all-`n` reading to be a constraint on the prior (rather than a description of when it can hold) is the formalizer's reading.

The single-coin instance is proved: `refrozen_weak_not_strong_not_tiles` — the re-frozen policy weakly learns, does not strongly learn at `Q_0`, is strictly beaten under `P` by the one-point precommitment "pay at `Ask_{t 1}`" (`udt-bli-tiling`'s `refreeze_not_noStrictPrecommitAt`) — **this is the overthrow**, the thread's scenario with `n = 0`: `Q_0` does not endorse the schedule's update and strictly prefers the self-modification that undoes it — and the re-frozen prior itself strictly prefers the re-frozen policy to `payAll` by `c · ∑_{k ≥ t 1} γ_k` (`refrozen_gap`), the decided state's *endorsement* of the re-freeze, the other side of the dilemma (repair round 1: the round-0 docstring glossed the endorsement as the overthrow; both audits caught it). Severity: imprecision (the source concedes it).

## F4 — The weak/strong distinction needs three pairwise-disagreeing states; the single-coin model has two (imprecision, model)

`weakLearns_iff`: on the model, weak learning for all `n` is strong learning at every decided state; with a monotone rate it *is* strong learning at `N` (`weakLearns_iff_strongLearnsAt`). Every policy weakly learns at every early `n` (the witness is `n` where it pays, `N` where it refuses — **per round**: `WeakLearns` is `∀ n k, f n ≤ k → ∃ x ≥ n, ActsAs x k`, the state index may depend on the round, which is the reading under which bli-soto-a-2-017 (i) is "by inspection"; one `x` for the whole tail would be strong learning at `x`). Concretely, on this model `WeakLearns f π` says "`π` refuses at every round `k ≥ f n` for every `n ≥ N`", so `refuseAll` weakly learns with *every* rate (`refuseAll_weakLearns_any_rate`, N−) and `refrozen_weakLearns` is that characterization at one policy, with the schedule's `t 2, t 3, …` idle. So the single-coin model cannot separate the two readings beyond "strong at `N` versus strong at `0`" — the separation (`refrozen_weakLearns` with `refrozen_not_strongLearnsAt_zero`) is between the frozen and the decided state. A model exhibiting weak-not-strong at a *decided* state needs three states disagreeing pairwise on a late round (the mandate's two-coin stretch, not built in this pass). Pointer: mandate §3.3 (i), T3 stretch.

## F5 — In the two-realization shadow the uniform average is the `q = ½` prior; PDF 14's "won't correspond to any *simplicity* prior" is about the limit over trees and is not engaged here (presentation; the source item has no error)

**Source.** bli-soto-b-012 (PDF 14 p. 3): "if we take the average uniformly as above, it won't correspond to any simplicity prior, so we punish all agents"; "We're baking in 'the right prior' when defining 'average'." PIBBSS §5.3: "Our computation of the average implicitly assumes all decision trees have the same importance (a uniform prior). But this uniform prior doesn't exist as a probability distribution (all priors are eventually simplicity biased)."

**Finding.** `avgValue_eq_half` (Kind L, `ring` on the closed form): in the finite single-coin shadow the uniform average over the coin's two realizations *is* the ex-ante value under the prior with `q = ½`. This is consistent with everything the source says — the source itself calls its finite average "a uniform prior" and places its claim in the limit over *all decision trees of depth `n`*, where a uniform law is no *simplicity* prior and under-weights what any agent's prior weights. The shadow has one tree and no simplicity prior, so that claim is neither confirmed nor refuted here. **Correction (repair round 1).** The round-0 version of this finding quoted the source as "uniform averaging is no prior" (deleting "simplicity" by ellipsis) and graded it "local error in the finite case" — a case the source never addressed; both audits (adversarial B3, fidelity B2) caught it. Severity now: presentation (the identity is useful as the bridge that makes `not_conj2_shadow_calibrated` a one-liner; see F7); the source item carries no error.

## F6 — PDF 14's Theorem 1 ("doesn't learn ⟹ a learner does better on average") is false in the finite shadow for every stake (refuted, finite shadow)

**Source.** bli-soto-b-008 (PDF 14 Thm. 1 / PIBBSS Conj. 1): "Suppose `A` doesn't learn. Then, another `A'` that does learn does better on average." Def. 2: "`A` doesn't learn if, for any decision tree, when `A` faces (at two different times) two identical situations in different parts of that one tree, it takes the same actions."

**Finding.** `not_conj1_shadow` (Kind P, refutation): with "identical situations" = the rounds `Ask_k` of the one coin and "average" = the uniform average over the coin's realizations, a constant policy is average-optimal for every stake (`payAll` if `c ≤ V`, `refuseAll` if `V ≤ c`, `exists_constant_avgOptimal`), so no learner does strictly better. The mandate expected the refutation for `V ≠ c`; it holds for `V = c` too (every policy ties). **Reading (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED).** PDF 14 restricts "identical" to subtrees "isolated from the rest of the tree, … no inter-dependencies (explicit cross-entanglements) with the rest of the tree"; the rounds here share the coin, so the author may hold that his clause excludes this tree — under which reading the conjecture is unstatable here rather than false. The author's objection (journal 2023-09-11 ll. 940–946): "even if UDT sees that a subtree is identical to one it faced previously, … UDT faces a different observed market state in the two subtrees" — the finite shadow sharpens it: even with *identical* states, not learning is optimal when the stakes favour it. Surviving neighbours: Theorem A (`theoremA`), the well-posed incompatibility; `trunc_oneStep_iff` (F9).

## F7 — PDF 14's Theorem 2 (freeze versus forced update) is false in the finite shadow for `c ≤ V` with the frozen prior's own policy as `A`; it holds iff `V < c` (under `gain p > 0`) — iff the frozen prior pays while the fair-coin average refuses — which forces `q < ½`, so miscalibration against the average is necessary, not sufficient; with the frozen prior calibrated to the average it is false for every stake, which is the source's own caveat formalized (refuted, finite shadow)

**Source.** bli-soto-b-009 (PDF 14 Thm. 2 / PIBBSS Conj. 2): "There is `f` such that `A_f` does better than `A` on average"; p. 4: `A` "just runs `Q` for `n` steps, and then freezes that prior and uses it forever", `A_f` "does the same, but hard-codes into the prior 'after `f(n)` steps, become updateful'". The author (journal l. 985): "But LI can produce something that's already in agreement with the surgery? So this seems implausible."

**Finding.** `A` is *the frozen prior's own policy*, so the shadow must quantify it over the frozen prior's optimal policies rather than fix it. Two forms, both proved.
- **Calibrated frozen prior** (`q = ½`, the averaging prior itself): `not_conj2_shadow_calibrated` — **false for every stake, with no hypothesis**. The uniform average *is* the ex-ante value under `q = ½` (`avgValue_eq_half`), so a policy optimal for that prior is beaten on average by nothing, re-freezes included. The refutation is structural: "does better on average than the frozen agent" is impossible as soon as the average is the prior the frozen agent optimizes. **This is the source's own caveat formalized, not a refutation of the conjecture as posed** (repair round 2, fidelity N1 / adversarial N3): PDF 14 p. 3 says "if we average differently, for example with a simplicity prior, then indeed the agent with that prior will perform best. It seems like we're just saying something tautological here: We're baking in 'the right prior' when defining 'average'", and poses Theorem 2 for an average that is *no* agent's prior (its "won't correspond to any simplicity prior", F5). Kind L (two lines over `avgValue_eq_half`); the refutation with content is the frozen-prior form below.
- **The model's frozen prior** (`scPrior p`, any `q`; `A` any `IsPriorOptimal` policy of it; `refrozen t` is its re-freeze exactly when it pays, `gain p > 0`): `conj2_shadow_frozen_iff` — the sentence holds iff `V < c`. False exactly when `c ≤ V` (the case the thread is about); and `gain p > 0 ∧ V < c` forces `q < V/(V + c) < ½` (`q_lt_half_of_gain_pos_of_lt`): the "true" half is a frozen prior that pays because it under-weights the coin's true side relative to the fair-coin average that judges it — miscalibration against the average, not a vindication of Theorem 2. **Necessary, not sufficient** (repair round 2, fidelity B1: the round-1 headline compressed this into an "iff"): `q < ½` does not make the sentence true — at `(c, V) = (10, 100)`, `q = ¼`, `gain = 72.5 > 0` and `q ≠ ½`, yet `c ≤ V`, so the sentence is false. What the Lean proves is "holds ⟺ `V < c`" and "holds ⟹ `q < ½`". The computation behind both is `avg_refrozen_gap`: `avgValue (refrozen t) − avgValue payAll = −((V − c)/2) · ∑_{k ≥ t} γ_k`, for every re-freeze day including the author's guess `n/2`.

**Correction (repair round 1).** The round-0 version fixed `A := payAll` for every stake (`conj2_shadow`, kept as the two-policy computation it is) and reported "true when the stakes are adverse" as a fact about the source; for `V < c` a calibrated frozen prior refuses, so `payAll` was not the source's `A` there (adversarial B2, fidelity N3; the adversarial probe `T8cHonestA` supplied the `V < c` half of the calibrated refutation). The mandate's T8 (c) prescribed the round-0 reading ("true when `V < c`"); STANDARDS §3 wins and the conflict is recorded in the report. Severity: the source's conjecture is refuted in the shadow (every stake, calibrated; `c ≤ V`, the model's frozen prior); the shadow reading is ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED as in F6. Surviving neighbour: Theorem B (`refrozen_gap`), which says what freeze-versus-re-freeze really contrasts: *which prior judges*, not *which does better on average*.

## F8 — "Learning UDT" in the prefix reading is uninhabitable wherever two states in a prefix disagree about the optimal policy; the quantifier order inside it carries no content (blocking for the prefix reading, source-level)

**Source.** bli-paper-2-010 (b.300–302): "'Learning UDT': for some definition of 'updateful' which implies a sequence of updated belief states `P₀, P₁, P₂, …`, the agent is eventually UDT-optimal for any particular `P_n`. Note that this requires UDT-optimality for all earlier `P_{<n}`. We could also consider a version which does not require this, but, that's less interesting."

**Finding.** Two things. (i) `learningUdtR1_iff_eventually`: the prefix reading R1 (`∀ n, ∃ M, ∀ m ≥ M, ∀ n' ≤ n, optimal for Q_{n'}`) is *equivalent* to per-state eventual optimality (`∀ n', ∃ M, …`) because the prefix is finite — the "requires optimality for all earlier `P_{<n}`" clause adds nothing to the quantifier order; the content is R1 versus R2 (optimality for the *current* state). (ii) `not_learningUdtR1` (Kind C): on the model no policy sequence is R1 (`Q_0` wants pay, `Q_N` wants refuse, at one time; the proof reads the contradiction off round `0` — on this model the states disagree at every round, so nothing hangs on "late"); `learningUdtR2_switch`: the switch policy is R2 at every time (`M = 0`), and the *constant* `refuseAll` is R2 too from `M = N` (`learningUdtR2_const_refuseAll`, N−) — R2 does not need the policy to change. So the reading the note calls interesting is empty on exactly the tree the note lists as an example ("iterated mugging with a single coin", b.283); the "less interesting" reading is the inhabited one. ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED on the identification of "eventually UDT-optimal for `P_n`" with `IsPriorOptimalOnSupport` of the finite state. The readings ask whole-policy optimality at each time, with no tail restriction (r2 fidelity N3): under a tail-only reading the finite horizon makes R1 vacuously inhabited by any `M ≥ K`, so the whole-policy reading is the one with content here, and (ii) is unchanged under a tail reading with a nonempty tail because the two states disagree at every round.

## F9 — "`U_{>n}` at each node reintroduces dynamic instability" has no instance on the single-coin model, whose payoffs are round-local and whose points are independent, and *does* have one with an additive cross-round payoff (`hitchhiker_instability`): the claim is confirmed for additive utility, with the mechanism named (confirmation, with the precision the source lacks)

**Source.** bli-soto-a-060 (PDF 07 "Utility", pp. 5–6).

**Finding.** `trunc_oneStep_iff` (Kind L): on the single-coin model, dropping the rounds up to `n` changes no one-step choice at any round `k > n` (the verdict at `Ask_k` is the sign of `γ_k · gain`, `isOneStepChoice_askT_iff`, and `γ_k` is unchanged); at the dropped rounds every action ties (`trunc_tied`). The averaged (`1/K`) and discounted (`δ^k`) conventions give the same verdict as any positive weights (`oneStep_sign_gamma_free`). **What this does and does not show.** For any `FiniteBLIPrior` with `U = U_{≤n} + U_{>n}`, the node-`n` one-step value differs from the node-`0` value by `𝔼[U_{≤n} | pp·T = a]`, so the two verdicts agree iff the dropped past payoff is uncorrelated with the policy point at `T`. The single-coin model has that for two structural reasons that are *not* "additive utility": every round's payoff reads only that round's own `Ask` point (`scU`), and the points are independent (`structure_facts`). An additive tree with a Parfit's-hitchhiker shape — a reward at round `j < k` paid iff the predicted action at `Ask_k` is `pay`, a cost at round `k` for paying — has `𝔼[U_{≤n} | A(Ask_k) = pay] > 𝔼[U_{≤n} | A(Ask_k) = refuse]` for `j ≤ n < k`, so the node-`n` agent evaluating with `U_{>n}` refuses at `Ask_k` while the node-0 agent pays. **Built (repair round 1): `hitchhiker_instability` (Kind N+).** On the single-coin base, tables and product-uniform policy law with the utility `hhU = γ_j R [π Ask_k] + γ_k (−c) [π Ask_k]` (`hhPrior`), for `γ_k c < γ_j R`, `c > 0`, `γ_k > 0` and `j ≤ n < k`: the node-`0` agent's unique one-step choice at `Ask_k` is `pay` (`EU Ask_k a = (γ_j R − γ_k c)[a]`, `hhPrior_EU`), and the node-`n` agent with `U_{>n}` — the same `truncParams` convention as `trunc_oneStep_iff` — uniquely refuses. So PDF 07's remark is **confirmed** for additive utility, and the mechanism is named: the instability needs a past payoff *correlated with a later policy point*; round-local payoffs with independent points (the single-coin model) cannot produce it, and non-additive utility (the log-wealth agent of `LogWealth.lean`) is a second route. **Correction (repair round 1).** The round-0 version said the claim "has no instance with additive utility" and that "the surviving reading is non-additive utility" (fidelity B1); both overran the Lean, and the first is now refuted by `hitchhiker_instability`. Severity now: confirmation with added precision; the source item is not in error.

## F12 — b.333 is confirmed in M2 (and on the threshold in one-shot M1), with the parenthetical's mechanism made explicit as `bobMove`; the mandate's third variant coincides with M2 (confirmation; ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED)

**Source.** bli-paper-2-011 (b.333): "If Alice *would not swerve just because crazy-bob is a possibility* (the probability of crazy bob seems too low), but *would overall choose to swerve now, due to sane-bob imitating crazy-bob*, then in *that* case, it seems like Alice should not swerve? The inventory flags the claim as unargued and model-dependent.

**Finding.** In M2 (Bob's type sees Alice's policy, `eaomu_straight_of_lt`) EA-OMU swerves iff `μ₁(unconditional) > thr = (win − lose)/(win − crash)`; below the threshold it goes straight, although the posterior on crazy Bob given "Bob straight" is `1` for every `u > 0` (`posterior_straight`) and an *updateful* Alice swerves for every `u` (`updateful_swerves`: against a straight Bob, `lose > crash`). So the case b.333 describes is the updateful agent's, and "already the case for EAOMU" holds. The note's parenthetical names the mechanism exactly (repair round 2, fidelity N5; the round-1 text said "for a reason the note does not state"): "the way Alice thinks rational-bob chooses a policy after observing her own choice" is `bobMove`'s dependence on the whole policy `σ` — the best responder answers the swerving policy by going straight — which is *why* `swerveOnStraight` is valued at `lose` ex ante and EA-OMU's ex-ante evaluation comes out "straight" below the threshold. So the two coherent models are M2 (Bob-first with prediction) and the one-shot M1 (Alice-first, no growth point), both built, and the inventory's "model-dependent" flag is answered: b.333 holds in the model where its scenario is posed (M2) and on the threshold in the other. ## F14 — The asymptotic clause of the ε-correlation story holds as *value* and fails as *choice*: "eventually optimal" is true for every tolerance and false exactly, on one family (refuted in the choice reading; the approximate statement is the source's defensible content)

**Source.** bli-soto-b-046 (Notion ll. 366–368): "for each environment, there is some epsilon such that if the agent's belief in acausal correlations is below epsilon, and the environment doesn't systematically introduce such correlations, then the performance of UDT will eventually be optimal in that environment."

**Finding.** The finite form is a corollary of core's `eps_updateful` (`eps_corr_implies_eps_updateful`, Kind C; not a squeeze: `corrBounded_zero_not_necessary`). Over a belief-state sequence with `ε_n → 0` the honest conclusion is **approximate** — for every `η > 0`, eventually every one-step choice is `η`-updateful-optimal (`eventually_eps_updateful`) — and exact only under a uniform home-value gap (`eventually_updateful_of_gap`). **The exact clause without a gap condition is false** (repair round 2; until then the package's one OPEN statement, `eventually_updateful_exact`, "stated, not believed"): it is now the proposition `ExactAsymptoticClause` and `EpsCorrWitness.not_exactAsymptoticClause` proves its negation on core's two-table carrier (`K = 2`). The family `xPrior n` has `T1`'s own branch prefer `refuse` by `g_n = 1/(2(n+1))` and `T2`'s branch pay `b_n = 1/(n+1)` when `T1`'s point is `pay`; so `CorrBounded _ T b_n` at both tables with `ε_n = b_n → 0`, `|U| ≤ 1`, `ρ = ½`, positive points (`x_package`) — every hypothesis of the clause — while `EU T1 pay = b_n/2 > g_n/2 = EU T1 refuse` and `homeEU T1 refuse = g_n > 0 = homeEU T1 pay`: `pay` is the one-step choice and not an updateful one at *every* `n` (`x_pay_oneStep_not_updateful`). Exactly the diagnosis recorded in round 0 — `δ(ε_n) → 0` bounds the *loss* (`g_n → 0`), not the choice — now a construction rather than a belief. On the same family the approximate conclusion holds (`x_value_eventually_optimal_choice_never`): for every `η > 0` the one-step choice is eventually `η`-updateful-optimal. So "performance … eventually optimal" is **true as value** and **false as choice**, and the two readings separate on one family; the source's defensible content is the approximate statement (or the exact one under a uniform home-value gap). Two further readings (repair round 1, adversarial N1): the source's "optimal *in that environment*" is rendered as optimal by `Q_n`'s own home values (by the agent's lights, not by an external criterion), and the source's quantifier is a *threshold* ("for each environment there is some epsilon such that if the belief … is below epsilon"), which the finite form carries (`δ(ε) ≤ η` for small `ε`) and the `ε_n → 0` form reshapes. **Witnesses for the asymptotic headlines** (repair round 2, adversarial N2's probes adopted; the ledger used to say `flagged: no witness`): they live on core's two-table carrier, not the single-coin model, because on the latter `CorrBounded _ Ask_j 0` fails at *both* states for `K ≥ 2` — not only at `Rec_j` (`condEU_rec_diff`), but at every cross branch `Ask_k'`, `k' ≠ j`, whose value moves by `−c γ_j` with the action at `Ask_j` because the utility sums all rounds — so a constant sequence of either state needs `ε ≥ c γ_j`, incompatible with `ε_n → 0`.
