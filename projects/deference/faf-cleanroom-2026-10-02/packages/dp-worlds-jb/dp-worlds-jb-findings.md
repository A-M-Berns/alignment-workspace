## 2. Annihilation as printed presumes the chain is built from the world's own choices — imprecision

Appendix A line 327: "atomlessness yields a decreasing chain of halves with measures `2⁻ⁿ`; a world following the chain holds every element". The chain is not fixed in advance: at each step the world holds exactly one of the two halves (finite witnessing, Remark 0.1), and the chain must be built *inside* the world by recursion on that choice. `no_world_of_halving` (`Annihilation.lean`) does exactly this (`choose next hnext using step; I n := next^[n] ⟨⊤, top_mem⟩`). The theorem needs only a finitely additive probability that is strictly positive with exact halving — no σ-additivity and no quotient algebra — so it is strictly stronger than the appendix's instance (`Fidelity: stronger`); the N+ witness is the dyadic algebra on `[0,1)` (`Dyadic.lean`), a countable atomless Boolean subalgebra of `Set [0,1)` with Lebesgue measure, because Mathlib has no measure algebra (Borel mod null). The appendix's instance itself (the Lebesgue measure algebra) is not formalized: it needs the quotient Boolean algebra, which is the `stretch` the mandate names and was not attempted.

## 3. v2 Definition 2's non-null domain convention breaks slide 30's vector-form equivalence; the slide's own convention does not — imprecision (two domain conventions in the corpus that are not interchangeable)

*Rewritten 2026-09-30 after audit r1 (B1 in both lenses). The round-0 version of this finding, following the mandate (§3 T4, §6 item 3), charged the slide with a "local error … at the axiom written beside it"; that was wrong, and the inference it rested on — "also the slide's since `V(A) + V(B)` must be defined" — presupposed the very convention the slide does not state. Recorded here rather than silently replaced.*

`AISC 2025 - 1 Understanding Trust.pdf` p. 30: `V(A ∨ B) = (P(A)V(A) + P(B)V(B)) / (P(A) + P(B))` if `A ∧ B = ⊥`, "`V(⊥)` is not defined", and "equivalently, `𝒥` is a finitely-additive vector-valued measure with vectors `𝒥(E) = [P(E), V(E)·P(E)]`, `𝒥(A ∨ B) = 𝒥(A) + 𝒥(B)` when `A ∧ B = ⊥`". The slide's stated domain convention is that only `V(⊥)` is undefined: `V` has a value at every non-`⊥` event, null or not, and the averaging formula is imposed on every disjoint pair where its denominator is positive. `decision-problems-v2` §1 Definition 2 (line 32) uses a different convention — `V` "assigns a real number to each `P_s`-non-null event", averaging "for disjoint non-null `X, Y`" — and Remark 1.2 (line 42) relies on it ("desirability being undefined on `P_s`-null events"). The two are not interchangeable:

- **Under v2's convention the equivalence fails.** `slide_vector_form_fails_at_v2_axiom` (`JB.lean`): on the three-atom algebra `Finset (Fin 3)` with `P = δ_0`, the v2 pair `slidePair` (`V {0} = 0`, `V {0,1} = V {0,2} = 1`, `V ⊤ = 5`; v2's averaging axiom is vacuous since every non-null event contains `0`) admits no additive `𝒥` with `𝒥.1 = P` and `𝒥.2 = V·P` on non-null events — additivity would force `𝒥(⊤).2 = 0 + 1 + 1 = 2 ≠ 5`. Verified in Lean.
- **Under the slide's own convention the sentence holds, and that pair is not a JB structure.** The slide's formula at a null `N ≠ ⊥` and a non-null `A` reads `V(N ∨ A) = (0·V(N) + P(A)V(A))/P(A) = V(A)` — exactly the package's null-tolerant axiom (`JBPairNT.avgNT_of_null_left` is that instance). At `A = {1}`, `B = {0}` it forces `V {0,1} = V {0}`, which `slideV` violates whatever `V {1}` is (`slide_formula_fails_on_slidePair`); no null-tolerant pair restricts to `slidePair` (`slidePair_not_null_tolerant`). And the equivalence is then `jbVec_equiv`: vector measures with `𝒥(N).2 = 0` on null `N` ≃ null-tolerant pairs. Two things the slide leaves unstated remain: the side condition `𝒥(N).2 = 0` on null `N` (the slide's own `𝒥(E) = [P(E), V(E)·P(E)]` gives it once `V(N)` is finite), and the fact that `V`'s values on null events are invisible to `𝒥` (the vector form forgets them). Both are imprecisions of a slide, not a false sentence. In the Jeffrey–Bolker tradition the algebra is quotiented by the null ideal, so non-null = non-`⊥` and even those vanish (`jbVec_equiv_of_pos`).

So the real finding is about **v2 versus the slide**, and it is the same root as finding 8: v2's Definition 2 leaves `V(N ∨ A)` free of `V(A)` on non-quotiented algebras, which is why the vector form, atom-determination (`slidePair_not_atom_determined`) and the Bolker transformation (`Bolker.lean` header) all fail for v2 pairs with a null atom while holding for the slide's. Severity: imprecision (in v2's Definition 2, or in reading the slide with it). Harmless for BLI proper (the paper dropped JB).

## 4. The expressivity lemma has no theorem-shaped statement in the chat — imprecision

Drafting chat line 2389 states the lemma "working finite and atomic" as prose: "probability-only `cf` plus rigidity is exactly as expressive as pair-valued `cf` when the algebra is rich enough that atoms settle every value-relevant fact". "Value-relevant fact" has no meaning inside a bare JB pair; a theorem needs an external fine space. **Reading A** (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED): a finite fine type `W` with utility `u`, a coarsening `π : W → Ω`, an actual belief `μ` and a supposed belief `μᵃ`; the rigid `V^a_rig X := E_{P^a}[V_s{·} | X]` versus the true `V^a_true X := E_{μᵃ}[u | π⁻¹ X]`. Under it the lemma is a theorem: `rigid_eq_true_iff` (pointwise: faithful on every charged event iff no within-atom revaluation on any `P^a`-charged atom) and `rigid_faithful_iff_fibres_settle` (the extension: for fixed `(W, u, π)`, faithful for all pairs `(μ, μᵃ)` with the chat's `≪` side condition iff `u` is constant on every fibre of `π`). The chat's *conclusion* (pair-valued `cf` is the right type) survives under Reading A, and its counterexample is verified with its numbers (`coarse_smoking_lesion`: `V_s(smoke) = −36/5`, `V^a_true(smoke) = −4`, rigidity returns `−36/5`; `P^a = δ_smoke` forced). **Reading B** (the purely internal statement: the rigidity map is not onto pair-valued `cf` on any algebra with a charged atom) is true and trivial (`rigidity_not_surjective`, a `T` row) and is not what the chat means. One more precision the chat elides: the `≪` condition ("every charged atom is `P_s`-positive") is a *meaning* condition, not a proof condition — `rigid_eq_true_iff` holds without it because Lean's conditional expectation is total (`0/0 = 0`) and both sides then read the same junk value at a `μ`-null, `μᵃ`-charged atom, where "rigid = true" degenerates to `u = 0` there (`rigid_eq_true_junk_off_ac`, audit r1 N1); the extension quantifies over pairs satisfying `≪` so that no junk value is compared. v2's Remark 1.2 (line 42) states the same limitation in prose ("it has nothing to read off exactly where a supposition moves mass onto the subjectively impossible") and is the vetted document closest to this lemma; the chat is where the lemma is stated.

## 8. The mandate's T2 lemma "V is determined by its atom values" is false for the v2 pair — imprecision in the mandate (and in the natural reading of Definition 2 on finite algebras)

The mandate (§3 T2) asks for "on the finite atomic carrier, `V` is determined by its atom values, `P X · V X = Σ_{ω ∈ X, P{ω} > 0} P{ω} · V{ω}` (by induction on the finset, from `avg`)". Under v2's Definition 2 (averaging on *non-null* disjoint pairs only) this is false on any finite algebra with a null atom: the same three-atom example of finding 3 has `P ⊤ · V ⊤ = 5` while the only charged atom contributes `P{0} · V{0} = 0` (`slidePair_not_atom_determined`, `JB.lean`). The induction step `insert ω X = {ω} ⊔ X` needs the axiom at the pair `({ω}, X)` even when `{ω}` is null, which v2's axiom does not supply. The lemma holds for the null-tolerant pair (`JBPairNT.pv_eq_sum_atoms`) and, by `jbVec_equiv_of_pos`, for v2 pairs with every atom charged — which is every worked example in v2. So v2's text is not wrong, but its Definition 2 leaves desirability on unions with null events free data even on finite algebras, and a reader who assumes atom-determination is assuming the null-tolerant axiom — which is what slide 30's own formula imposes (finding 3, rewritten): this is the divergence between v2's convention and the slide's, seen on finite algebras. Severity: imprecision; same root as finding 3.

## 11. On a countable algebra, annihilation needs no measure: `Jω`-worlds are exactly the atoms — presentation (sharpens Appendix A)

Appendix A's annihilation argument (line 327) runs on the Lebesgue measure algebra with a strictly positive halving measure, and `no_world_of_halving` abstracts exactly that. The round-1 adversarial audit (N2) observed that the package's N+ witness `Dy` is *countable*, and on a countable Boolean algebra a diagonal argument with no measure at all shows every `Jω`-world holds an atom: enumerate `E`, for each non-`⊥` `eₙ` pick a member `Yₙ` of the world not above `eₙ` (its complement if `eₙ ∉ ω`, a proper part if `eₙ ∈ ω` is not an atom), and `{Yₙ}` is a countable designated family of members with infimum `⊥`. Now in `Countable.lean`: `exists_atom_mem_of_countable`, `worldJω_equiv_atoms : World (Jω E) ≃ {b // IsAtom b}` for countable `E` (through the atom world `atomWorld J b := {X | b ≤ X}`, a world for every designation on any Boolean algebra — the general form of `principalWorld`, `principalWorld_eq_atomWorld`), `no_world_of_countable_atomless`, `Dy.countable` and `dyadic_no_world_of_countable`. Consequences for the source and the package: (i) the appendix's phenomenon has a measure-free form — "designating countable meets kills every world of a countable atomless algebra" — which is the cleaner statement of what designation costs; (ii) the halving hypothesis of `no_world_of_halving` earns its keep only on *uncountable* algebras, i.e. on the appendix's own instance, which is not built here (Mathlib has no measure algebra); an uncountable N+ witness for the halving theorem — e.g. the interval algebra on `[0,1)` with Lebesgue measure, or the clopen algebra of `2^ℝ` with the fair-coin product measure — is an open agenda item, recorded in the report, not in the open list (no Lean statement). Severity: presentation.

## 12. `imaging` is a supposition only under a charged-cells hypothesis — imprecision in the secondary report's rendering (T10)

The CDT-foundations chat (lines 264–280) describes `U₃` as taken over a supposition `P^A` with `P^A(A) = 1` and presents `K`-imaging as the supposition that makes `U₂ = U₃`. As defined from that description (`imaging P A k ω := P(K_{k ω}) · P ω / P(A ⊓ K_{k ω})` on `ω ∈ A`), imaging sums to `∑_{c : P(A ⊓ K_c) > 0} P(K_c)`, which is `1` exactly when every charged dependency hypothesis meets `A` with positive probability (`imaging_sum_eq_one`, `mass_imaging_eq_one`, `Gallow.lean`); otherwise it is a sub-probability and `U₂` silently drops the uncharged cells under the `0/0 = 0` convention (`imaging_sum_lt_one_example`: `Ω = κ = Bool`, `k = id`, `A = {true}`, `P` uniform, imaging mass `1/2`). The identity `gallow_U2_eq_U3_of_imaging` holds regardless — it is a regrouping that holds by construction — but the *interpretation* "`U₂` is `U₃` of a supposition" needs the hypothesis. Whether Gallow 2024 states it is unknown (the paper is not in the repo; ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED). Severity: imprecision (in the secondary report, or in the package's rendering of it).
