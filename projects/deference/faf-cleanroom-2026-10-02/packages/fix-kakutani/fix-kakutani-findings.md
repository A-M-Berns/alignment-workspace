## F1 — The surveys did not mention that Mathlib has hemicontinuity (presentation; an addendum, not a correction)

- Pointer: FAF `LogicalInduction/Construction/Brouwer.lean` module docstring ("Mathlib has neither a Brouwer nor a Kakutani fixed-point theorem"); `fixpoint-lit-inventory` 017 and 094 ("none in FAF/Mathlib"); `uea-2-inventory` 017 ("Mathlib has neither Kakutani nor a general Brouwer").
- What is true in the pinned Mathlib (checked 2026-09-29 by grep of `.lake/packages/mathlib/`): no Kakutani, no Brouwer, no Sperner — so the surveys' literal sentences, which are about the theorems, are correct; the phrase "none of the Kakutani machinery" was the mandate's paraphrase, not theirs (round-1 audits, N1 / issue 7). What the surveys did not mention, and what shortened target 3: `Mathlib/Topology/Semicontinuity/Hemicontinuity.lean` (Jireh Loreaux, 2025) defines `UpperHemicontinuousOn`/`At`/`WithinAt` and proves the two sequential characterizations `UpperHemicontinuousAt.of_sequences` and `UpperHemicontinuousAt.mem_of_tendsto`. This package uses both: `HasClosedGraphOn.upperHemicontinuousOn` and `HasClosedGraphOn.of_upperHemicontinuousOn` (ClosedGraph.lean) are the two halves of "closed graph on a compact ⟺ upper hemicontinuous with closed values", and the ε–δ upper hemicontinuity that the limit step of the Kakutani proof needs is read off Mathlib's predicate rather than reproved by a compactness contradiction.
- Severity: presentation. Nothing in the surveys' conclusions changes; the inventories' "Existing Lean: none" lines for 017/094 would be more useful as "no Kakutani/Brouwer; hemicontinuity exists (`Mathlib.Topology.Semicontinuity.Hemicontinuity`)".

## F2 — Wyeth–Hutter–Leike–Taylor Thm 33 is Kakutani–Ky Fan; the package proves the finite-dimensional special case (imprecision)

- Pointer: `uea-2-inventory` 017 (Thm 33: `L` locally convex TVS, `K ⊆ L` compact convex, `f : K → {closed convex nonempty subsets of K}` upper semicontinuous ⟹ fixed point).
- The finite-dimensional forms `kakutani_pi_stdSimplex` and `kakutani_pi_Icc` are ledgered `variant: finite-dimensional, closed graph`, not `exact`. Two differences from Thm 33: (i) the ambient space is finite-dimensional (which is all the `uea-*` and reflective-oracle uses need: a finite product of simplices or a finite cube — the countable-query reflective oracle of `fixpoint-lit-inventory` 018 is *not* covered; that would need the infinite-dimensional Ky Fan form, or a limit argument, and is out of this package's scope); (ii) the hypothesis is a closed graph over the domain rather than upper semicontinuity with closed values. In finite dimension with values in the compact domain the two are equivalent, and the package proves this equivalence (`HasClosedGraphOn.upperHemicontinuousOn`, `HasClosedGraphOn.of_upperHemicontinuousOn`), so a consumer that has an upper-semicontinuous closed-valued map can also discharge Thm 33 by the converse bridge plus `kakutani_pi_stdSimplex`.
- Severity: imprecision (in how the audit cites Thm 33 for finite games). Consumers citing "Thm 33" for a finite product of simplices should cite `kakutani_pi_stdSimplex` and say "finite-dimensional case".

## F3 — The plan's target-6 extension ("a fixed point in the relative interior of a rational face is algebraic of bounded degree") is ill-posed as worded (imprecision; the plan, not a source)

- Pointer: `plan` `### fix-kakutani`, `extension`; mandate target 6; rigor critique 12.
- As stated for *arbitrary* correspondences the claim is false: values can be arbitrary closed convex sets, so on `Icc 0 1` the correspondence `F x := {c}` for any real `c ∈ Icc 0 1` has rational-vertex domain and the unique fixed point `c`, which can be transcendental. So no theorem about the fixed points of Kakutani correspondences with rational inputs bounds their algebraic degree; "rational inputs, real fixed points" is exactly what `kakutani_findim` already gives (a `Finset`-simplex with `ℚ` vertices is a compact convex subset of `Fin n → ℝ`).
- The precise version, for the `uea-*` packages if they ever need it: for the best-response correspondence of a finite game with rational payoffs and a fixed support profile, every equilibrium with that support solves the indifference-and-normalization system of that support (each player is indifferent between the pure strategies in her support and weakly prefers them to those outside it; each mixture sums to 1). For **two** players this system is *linear* in each player's mixture with rational coefficients, so the equilibria of a given support form a rational polyhedron and every *isolated* (in particular every unique) equilibrium is rational (bounded degree 1) — the classical bimatrix fact behind Lemke–Howson. An isolated irrational equilibrium therefore cannot come from a two-player bimatrix best-response map with rational payoffs; it needs either three or more independently mixing players (the system becomes multilinear, degree grows with the number of players) or a payoff that is itself nonlinear in the mixture (as in a self-referential game where an agent's payoff depends on the fixed point). The corpus does contain such an equilibrium: the exact mixed fixed point of the §6 instance of [updateless-self-game](../../sources/unbounded-embedded/unbounded-embedded-lab/models/updateless-self-game.md), with mixing weight `q = (86 + √8801)/180` at `s₀` (`uea-inventory` 029 / R2-A; `updateless_existence.py` "closed form", verified there in `ℚ(√8801)`). On the inventory's description that instance has both features — three situations mixing independently, and conditional expectations that are rational functions of `σ` under the continuous extension — so a quadratic irrational is unsurprising (reading of the inventory entry; the polynomial system was not re-derived here). The "bounded degree" statement should be made about *that* system, with the degree bound read off its polynomial structure, and is not a Kakutani statement. Not formalized here (no dependent needs it; the mandate allows recording it instead).
- Correction (repair round 1, after the round-1 fidelity audit's B1): the plan (`### fix-kakutani`, `extension`), `critique-rigor.md` critique 12, the mandate's target 6 and "Known issues", and the first version of this finding all cited `uea-self-game`'s `(15 − √97)/16` as the irrational *fixed point*. That is a misreading of [updateless-self-game](../../sources/unbounded-embedded/unbounded-embedded-lab/models/updateless-self-game.md) §5.1 / `uea-inventory` 028: `(15 − √97)/16` is the root of `8δ² − 15δ + 4` at which the pure fixed point `HH` of the Stag Hunt self-game stops violating the trust bound, a threshold on the discount parameter `δ`; the fixed points of that instance are the pure profiles `SS`, `HH`, `SH`, `HS` for every `δ`, and nothing irrational is a fixed point anywhere in that section. The number the corpus does have is the `(86 + √8801)/180` above (uea-029). The claim that a "rational Kakutani" with rational fixed points is false rests on the constant-correspondence example in the first bullet, not on either number. The plan, the critique and the mandate are outside this package's write scope; the orchestrator should correct them (see [fix-kakutani-report](fix-kakutani-report.md), "Repair round 1").
- Severity: imprecision in the plan; no source conclusion depends on it.
