## F-1 — The second limit inequality is claimed unconditionally with a false reason (refuted)

- **Severity**: local error — blocking for the Gaifman *identity* as stated (the one property the extrapolation was claimed to have), not for the construction.
- **Pointer**: PDF 07 p. 2, last paragraph; PIBBSS §4.3 p. 18; `bli-soto-a-inventory` 052 (v); `bli-soto-b-inventory` 033 (iii) and its flag; `bli-soto-a-2-inventory` 006.
- **(i) The source's sentence, quoted** (PIBBSS §4.3 p. 18): "We also get the other inequality²², because `lim_m P(¬∀mφ(m) ∧ ⋀_m φ(i)) = 0`. Indeed, the latter could only fail if a finite and `Ax`-consistent `C ∧ ¬∀mφ(m)` propositionally entailed infinitely many `φ(i)`. This isn't possible with our `Ax`, because the only way to have this is through quantification. As a consequence, the Agnostic clause will be triggered infinite times, and the infinite multiplication by 1/2 drives the limit to 0." PDF 07 p. 2 has the same argument up to "through quantification" but lacks the "As a consequence…" sentence and ends instead with "In some sense, the reason why this happens is that we don't propagate equality of sub-terms or equivalence of sub-formulas (more on this below)" — the two texts are not identical (audit r1 N6). That extra sentence is the finding's sharpest evidence: the stated reason (no propagation of sub-formula equivalence) is exactly what the 2-006 witness bypasses, since `¬¬φ(i) ⊢_Prop φ(i)` needs no propagation schema.
- **(ii) The precise reading formalized**: "for every quantifier structure `S` (design decision 1), enumeration `e`, `Ax`-consistent base `q` (`BaseAxConsistent`, `0 ≤ q`, `∑ q = 1`) and universal `u`, `μ (univEvent S u) = μ (⋂ i, instEvent S u i)` where `μ = extrapolate S e q`" — i.e. the identity, with the limit read as the measure of the intersection (design decision 7; `tendsto_conj_instances` makes it the limit). ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED: Soto may have intended a language in which `∀m¬¬φ(m)` is not a prime sentence separate from `∀mφ(m)`; nothing in PDF 07 or the report says so, and PDF 07's own "Propagation" bullet (057 (ii)) shows the same failure for a propagation schema without noticing it applies to plain `Ax`.
- **The refutation**: `gaifman_lt_of_hiddenUniversal` (`Gaifman.lean`; kind P) — any level-`k` conjunction of positive chained mass refuting `U` and `Ax`-entailing every instance forces `μ U < μ (⋂ I_i)` strictly — instantiated by `refute_second_inequality` (`Refutations.lean`; kind N+): the 2-006 structure (universals `U = ∀mφ(m)`, `U' = ∀m¬¬φ(m)` with instances `a_i`, `∼∼a_i`; exactly PDF 07's schema 1, empty schema 2), enumeration `U, U', …`, base uniform on `{U, U'}` (`B = 2`, every conjunction `¼`, `BaseAxConsistent` proved); the conjunction `¬U ∧ U'` has mass `¼`, refutes `U`, and `Ax`-entails every `a_i` through schema 1 on `U'`. Values: `μ U = ½`, `μ (¬U ∧ ⋂ a_i) ≥ ¼`, `μ U < μ (⋂ a_i)`. The stated reason is false: the entailment is "through quantification" in the only sense `Ax` knows (schema 1 on a *different* universal), so no propagation schema is needed. A second instance, `refute_propagation` (`U'' = ∀m(φ(m) ↔ φ(m+1))`, `C = ¬U ∧ a_0 ∧ U''`, mass `⅛`), is PDF 07's own 057 (ii) counterexample, which the source files against a propagation schema.
- **(iii) The surviving neighbour**: `gaifman_eq_of_halving` (`Gaifman.lean`; kind P): the identity holds under `HalvingCondition S e B u` — infinitely many instances of `u` are fresh atoms at strictly increasing levels `≥ B` that no `Ax`-consistent `¬U`-conjunction below them decides — which is Soto's argument made true by naming the condition it needs; `halving_of_fresh_instances` gives the syntactic sufficient condition (`FreshInstances`: the instance atoms appear in no other axiom), and `halving_witness` inhabits it non-degenerately (`μ U = ½ = μ (⋂ I_i)`, `μ (I_0) = ¾`). The unconditional `≤` (`gaifman_le`) survives as stated.

## F-2 — Decidability of `Ax ∪ {C} ⊢_Prop φ` is argued with a wrong reason

- **Severity**: local error in the argument; the claim is plausibly true (not decided here).
- **Pointer**: PDF 07 p. 2 ("deciding whether `Ax ∪ {C} ⊢_Prop φ` for finite `C` is decidable, since from a set of prime formulas `Ax` can only derive smaller or equal length prime formulas … we can syntactically cap the length of a possible proof"); PIBBSS §4.3 p. 18 ("note that deciding the predicate … is decidable", no argument); `bli-soto-a-inventory` 055 flag; `bli-soto-a-2-inventory` 008.
- **What is wrong**: schema 1 instantiates `∀mφ(m) → φ(S^i 0)` with numerals of unbounded length, so `Ax` derives *longer* prime formulas; the length cap does not exist as stated. The repair is 2-008's Lemmas A (numeral interchange) and B (conservative extension of a valuation of the finitely many "reachable" primes).
- **Here**: decidability is stated in its honest form — relative to a locality structure `UnivStructure.Local` (a finite `reach` and a finite `axOn` with an exactness field `spec`), from which `axEntails_decidable` is a `Decidable` instance (`Syntax.lean`; kind L: a one-step transfer, `decidable_of_iff` on `spec` — the round-1 audits rightly refused the label C for it). The content is in *inhabiting* `Local`, which the repair round did for the fresh-atomic template — `mkAtomic_spec`/`mkAtomicLocal` (`Witnesses.lean`, kind P: the atomic case of 2-008's Lemma B, a valuation of the atoms of `Γ, φ` satisfying the finitely many local axioms extends to an `Ax`-world by reading every instance atom off the set as true), giving `halvingS_local` and `enumS_local` and a computable decision procedure `halvingS_decidable`. ## F-4 — PDF 06 eq. (1) is the ½-rule in disguise; the display mixes `φ(0)` and `φ(1)`

- **Severity**: presentation (both parts).
- **Pointer**: PDF 06 p. 2 eq. (1) and the convergence display; `bli-soto-a-inventory` 052 (ii); `bli-soto-a-2-inventory` "Verification" (confirms the display and notes the index mix).

## F-14 — FAF's `ValuesAt` leaves the threshold at the value undetermined: the dyadic cell of a dyadic-rational value is a free verdict

- **Severity**: imprecision (against the source's half-open convention as a *definition* over FAF's worlds); presentation (the partition-of-unity remark of bli-soto-a-051).
- **Pointer**: PDF 07 p. 3 ("LUVs"); PIBBSS §4.2.2; `bli-soto-a-inventory` 056, 051; FAF `PCWorld.ValuesAt` (`Framework/Expectations.lean`).
- **What is wrong / what survives**: `ValuesAt v X x` fixes `X.gt r` for `r < x` and `r > x` only. So `dyadicSentence X n k` (`X.gt (k/2^n) ⋏ ∼X.gt ((k+1)/2^n)`) is held by a world valuing `X` strictly inside the cell (`holds_dyadic_of_mem_Ioo`), forces the closed cell (`mem_Icc_of_holds_dyadic`), and is an exact iff only off the level-`n` grid (`holds_dyadic_iff_of_notMem_grid`); off the grid it is the *only* cell held (`floor_eq_of_holds_dyadic`) and Soto's `D_X(m)` reads the `m`-th binary digit exactly (`digitSentence_holds_iff`: `D_X(m)` holds iff `⌊x·2^m⌋₊` is even, for `x < 1` off the grid; proved in the repair round). At `x = k/2^n` the world holds exactly one of the two adjacent cells according to its verdict on `X.gt x`, consistently across levels (the same rational is the same threshold sentence), and the two cells have opposite parity — so on the grid the digit is the world's free verdict and no iff holds. At `x = 1` a world affirming `X.gt 1` holds the out-of-range cell `k = 2^n` and no cell of `D_X(n)`, at every level. The source's "half-open intervals make expansions unique" is therefore a convention the *world* chooses, not one the encoding imposes; Soto's rewrite of `X = Y` is nevertheless sound — `values_eq_of_digits` (`Dyadic.lean`, kind P, proved in the repair round through the held-cell sequence `cellIdx`: existence via the first denied threshold, uniqueness, nesting `cellIdx (m+1) / 2 = cellIdx m`, parity-driven induction, a `2^{−m}` squeeze). The proof uses `ValuesAt`'s bounds `0 ≤ x ≤ 1`, without which the statement is false (audit r1 adversarial §3.10): the rewrite is sound for `[0,1]`-LUVs valued in `[0,1]`, which is all FAF's `ValuesAt` admits. **The converse is false** (repair round 2, audit r2 fidelity N3; `not_digits_of_values_eq`, `Dyadic.lean`): one world values two LUVs at the same `x = ½` and holds `D_X(1)` but not `D_Y(1)` — it denies `X.gt ½` (holding the cell `[0, ½]`) and affirms `Y.gt ½` (holding `(½, 1]`), both verdicts being free under `ValuesAt`, and the two cells adjacent to `½` have opposite parity. So over threshold worlds Soto's rewrite `∀m (D_X(m) ↔ D_Y(m))` is *strictly stronger* than `X = Y`: it also asserts agreement of the free verdicts on every dyadic threshold, and a chained measure can give the rewrite a value strictly below what "`X = Y`" deserves. This is the sharp form of "the convention is the world's, not the encoding's". Separately, the cells of one level are **not propositionally exclusive**: FAF's `X.gt r` are independent atoms, so a `GaifmanCoherent` valuation need not give `∑_k P(dyadicSentence X n k) = 1`; 051's "propositional coherence over digit primes makes the induced law on dyadic values a probability" needs the valuation supported on threshold-monotone worlds (an `Ax`-like a.e. statement, e.g. the axioms `X.gt r' 🡒 X.gt r` for `r < r'` in the process), which is not stated here.

## F-16 — Constraint 2's price events are exclusive only under the measure; their exclusivity is itself a base constraint

- **Severity**: imprecision (against PDF 06 p. 1 and Appendix B's reading of constraint 2 as a family of identities over a price grid); a modelling lesson from audit r1's B1.
- **Pointer**: PDF 06 p. 1 (`P_n("P_{n+1}(φ) = q" ∧ φ) := q · P_n("P_{n+1}(φ) = q")`, one display per `q`); `bli-soto-a-2-inventory` 005 (i)/(iv); bli-soto-a-006 (constraint 4 ⟺ the partition axiom).
- **What is wrong**: the price events `⌜P_{n+1}(φ) = q⌝` for the different `q` of a grid are distinct prime sentences. Over propositionally consistent worlds (FAF's `PCWorld`, whose atoms are independent) they are **not** exclusive: a world can hold several of them, and no propositional exclusivity hypothesis on more than one such event is satisfiable (the round-1 statement of 5b carried exactly that hypothesis and was uninhabitable for two prices; `audit-r1-probes/Constraint2Exclusivity.lean`). Their exclusivity, and the partition axiom `∑_q P(⌜P_{n+1}(φ) = q⌝) = 1`, are properties of the *measure* — in the chaining framework, properties of the base rule at the levels of the price events, i.e. further linear conditions on `q` in the sense of F-7 — not consequences of the events' form. `constraint2_definitional` now states exclusivity in that form (`chainVal base (E r ⋏ E r') = 0` for `r ≠ r'`), `partition_gives_balance` likewise, and `constraint2_witness` shows the point on a two-price grid: two atoms with a world holding both, made exclusive and exhaustive by the base (`a₁` has probability `0` given `a₀`, `1` given `¬a₀`), cell masses `⅓`, `⅔`, constraint 2 on both cells, balance `7/12`.
- **Consequence for Appendix B**: "constraint 2 holds by definition for large `φ`" (F-7) is right, but only *given* a base whose price events are a.e. exclusive and exhaustive; that part of the constraint sits in the finite base with the rest, which strengthens F-7's cross-slice point rather than weakening it.
- **`InUnit` is a second such constraint** (audit r2 adversarial §3.3, repair round 2): the sum-form `constraint2Rule` reads the sum of the prices on a world holding several cells; under exclusivity that is only a.e. such worlds are null but still in the domain of `InUnit`, so for a grid such as `{½, ¾}` on the package's own `twoCellRule` the rule leaves `[0,1]` and no `chainMeasure` of it exists, although `constraint2_definitional` applies (the auditor's probe). `constraint2Rule_inUnit_of_exclusive` gives `InUnit` under propositional exclusivity and `R ⊆ [0,1]`; for a.e.-exclusive events it must be checked per grid (as `twoCellC2Rule_inUnit` does: `¼ + ¾ ≤ 1`). In BLI terms: whether the constraint-2 *measure* exists depends on the price grid and the base together, not on the identity alone.
