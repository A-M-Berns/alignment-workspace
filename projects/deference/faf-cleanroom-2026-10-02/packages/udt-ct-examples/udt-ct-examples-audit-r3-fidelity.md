## 3. Non-blocking issues

**N1. F-15 should say which clause fails and why the note's reading differs from the formal one.** The paper's clause (2) is `E ⊥ D_{I,B} ∣ Π̈`, a symmetric conditional independence; the paper's gloss of it is directional — "the external policy is the only thing intrinsic to the agent that *impacts* the external environment" (line 390) — as is line 177 ("a fair environment is, roughly, allowed to depend on external behaviors but not internal behaviors"). On `MemI` (and `MemH`) the independence fails because the *light* (in `E`) is correlated with the *pill content* (in `D_I`) inside a `Π̈`-atom — information flowing from the environment into the agent's internal dynamics through the agent's own internal action, the direction the gloss permits, not the environment reading the agent's internals, the direction it forbids. [examples-revisited](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/examples-revisited.md) line 52 reads decision-determination by the gloss ("the environment's reward depends only on the external report and the actual light color"), and by the gloss it is right; by the formal clause it is wrong. So the finding is correct against the definition of record and should say so in those words, and it should record the sharper observation it has in hand as a finding about the *definition*: in this structure a memory channel between instances must live in `D_I` (`Ǒ ⊑ Ȯ ⊑ I` with `Ȧ` trivial at the reading instance, as F-1 shows), so whenever the use of such a channel is itself an option in the support (pill or no pill — which it must be for the UDT comparison to be well-posed), any environment information it carries that does not surface in `Π̈` breaks clause (2). That is why `Mem` escapes only through the `Π̈(light)` leak, and it is a stronger and more useful statement than "the note erred". Fix I'd accept: one paragraph in F-15 (severity may stay, qualified "relative to the formal clause (2); by the paper's directional gloss the note's reading is defensible") and a sentence in report §T2(e). Prose only.
