## F-1 · The abstract structure cannot make a pre-instance's *internal* action determine the rooms' side channel — the pill choice is an external action here (imprecision in the paper; a design constraint for every example) — `aI_indep_of_dI`, report §0.1

`I_fac` (`I` factors as `(Ȧ, D_I)`) makes `Ȧ` support-independent of every subvariable of `D_I` (`CountExt.aI_indep_of_dI`, two lines). The rooms' side channel `Ǒ ⊑ Ȯ ⊑ (Ȧ, D_I)` lies in `D_I` whenever `Ȧ` is constant at the rooms; putting it in `Ȧ` at the rooms instead runs into `IB_det` + `B_fac` + `IB_common` (report §0.1 lists the three attempts). So the mandate's "`AI` carrying the choice at `pre`, trivial at the rooms" with `m(pill) = 1` is unrepresentable. Two faithful options remain: (i) the paper's own picture — "there is merely some probabilistic relationship between internal actions and (potentially self-modifying) internal observations" (C&T LaTeX draft, Modification) — a free internal action correlated with the channel state in law (`CBm`, 48 worlds: the pill is modifying with probability `3/4`, and even "nothing" would be with positive probability); (ii) the choice as the pre-instance's *external* action (`CB`, `Mem`, `TB`: deterministic, `m(pill) = 1`, `hlink` holds everywhere). **Consequence of (ii)** (`TB.not_isCA_pill_msg`, graded weaker and flagged): `Π̈(pre)` reveals the choice, so no two distinct pre-actions can be communicative alternatives at the pre input — the CA identity compares the laws of the whole `Π̈`. That is why T1(d) needs `CBm`. Neither the translation nor the LaTeX says which representation the examples use; both are `(c)` here, disclosed on every witness. Severity: imprecision (the examples are not instances of the formal structure until a representation is chosen; the natural one for the pill is not available at the support level).

## F-2 · Copies, and the pill's effect on them, are beliefs (imprecision; ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED) — `CB` module docstring, report §0.2

Inherited from `udt-comm-trust` T8′ and made concrete: the other room's action is a free `D_E` coordinate whose law the prior ties to `Π̈` at the other room. Two things follow that the corpus's prose does not have. (a) The pill's effect on the copy is whatever the prior says: `W5`/`W10` put `7 : 1` on the copy pressing `$10` under the pill; with the choice as `Ä(pre)`, `Π̈(pre)` distinguishes "pill" from "`$10` policy without pill", so the prior *may* treat them differently under decision-determination — and `W5` *does* (copy `7 : 1` towards `$10` under the pill, `5 : 3` towards `$5` under the `$10` policy without it), so `CB.decisionDetermined5` holds only through the `Π̈(pre)` leak: with the choice hidden the atom `(pre ↦ $10, rooms ↦ $10, $10)` mixes the two and clause (2) fails — in Lean since repair round 2, `CBh.not_decisionDetermined5` (`CoordButtonsHidden.lean`, `3 · 48 ≠ 10 · 24`); `W10` is robust (`wa10 0 1 = wa10 1 1`, `CBh.decisionDetermined10`). With the choice internal (`CBm`) the prior may not treat them differently, and that sameness is what decision-determination *means*. (b) In the Memory Problem the pill *content* `d ∈ D_I` is support-independent of the light (`IB_fac`), so "the pill matching the light" is a `7 : 1` correlation in law (`Mem`) — and on `Mem` the agent does not choose the colour at all (`AE = Fin 2`: the light instance's action is pill / none; the colour is the dynamics coordinate `d`), so `Mem.score_light`'s "the pill is strictly preferred" is a preference for a `7 : 1` gamble on the colour, not the source's sure pill (a third report value at the asked instance would restore the choice, at 36 worlds). "Functional identity" and "the pill I took matches what I saw" are both beliefs inside the paper's structure.

## F-3 · On `CB`'s symmetric two-policy family the `$5` fixed point needs an asymmetric copy-belief (design note about this package's family; **not** a claim about the source — rescoped in repair round 1 after audit r1 fidelity B3) — `CB.score5_room`, `CB.udtRuleAt5`, `CB.W5`

On `CB`, whose chosen policies press the same `k` at *both* rooms, a symmetric coupling (the copy follows the agent's own room action `3 : 1` whatever it is) makes `$10` win at both room inputs under *every* prior over `k`: `E[U ∣ $10] = 3/4 > 3/8 = E[U ∣ $5]`. So on this family the `$5` fixed point needs a prior on the *copy's* action that leans `$5` even when the agent's own policy is `$10` (`wa5`: `7 : 1` towards `$5` under a `$5` policy, `5 : 3` towards `$5` under a `$10` policy), and then `$5` is a UDT fixed point at both room inputs — by a margin of `1/48`, because the pill worlds (which press `$10` and pay `7/8`) enter the conditioning event `{Π*(ȯ, red) = $5} = {k = $5}` as well. Two consequences for the package's own rows: `W5` is not "copies in law" at `k = $10` (the copy is believed anti-correlated with the agent's own policy there), and `CB W5`'s decision-determination holds only through the `Π̈(pre)` leak (F-2(a)).

**Why this is not a fact about the source.** The first version of this finding said the paper's "sufficiently high prior expectation that you'll press the `$5` button" *must* be a belief about the copy that the agent's own policy does not move. That is an artifact of `CB`'s family: conditioning on `Π*(ȯ, red) = $5` fixes the agent's green action too, so a functional copy is pinned. With room-dependent chosen policies `(k_red, k_green)` — legitimate for Coordinated Buttons, where the rooms are distinct observations (only the Third Button forbids *instructing* room-dependently) — and a symmetric `7 : 1` coupling of the copy to the agent's *own policy at the other room*, with `p = P(k_green = $5)` independent of `k_red`: `E[U ∣ Π*(red) = $5] = 7p/16 + (1 − p)/16` and `E[U ∣ Π*(red) = $10] = p/8 + 7(1 − p)/8`, so `$5` is the strict UDT argmax at red iff `p > 13/18` (with a `3 : 1` coupling, iff `p > 5/6`) — pill-free arithmetic: when `P(k_red = $5) ≠ 1/2` the two conditioning events have different masses and the pill worlds dilute them differently, so the threshold holds as stated only with the prior on `k_red` symmetric or the pill independent of `k` (audit r2 fidelity N4; the first version said the pill worlds "cannot change the sign"). That is exactly the corrected [examples-revisited](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/examples-revisited.md)'s mechanism ("a prior concentrated on `$5` makes `$5` self-confirming … stable *within* fully functional-identity-respecting reasoning"), and it respects functional identity. Arithmetic by hand (the audit's); not machine-checked — the 48-world carrier is in the open list. Neither package resolves the selection (mandate §6.13): `W5` and `W10` are both consistent. Severity: none against the source (a design note); the first version's "imprecision in udt-rep-024(b)" is withdrawn.

## F-4 · The pill's strict preference at `pre` on a full-support prior is not universal: it needs the pill to be believed to reach the copy at least as well as the agent's own `$10` policy does, and under that condition "be indifferent about taking the pill" needs certainty (imprecision; **rescoped in repair round 2** — the first version said "under *every* prior", refuted by audit r2 fidelity B1) — `CB.score10_pre`, `CB.nothing_beats_pillN`

Under `W10` the pill beats "nothing" at the pre input (`CB.score10_pre`, `15/32 < 7/12`): "nothing" averages over the agent's own room policy, which has positive weight on `$5` on the support, while the pill forces `$10` *and is believed to reach the copy as well as the `$10` policy does* (`wa10`: `7 : 1` under both). That second clause is the side condition the first version omitted. Room-conditionally, with `q = P(copy = $10 ∣ pill)`, `q₁ = P(copy = $10 ∣ $10 policy, no pill)`, `q₀ = P(copy = $5 ∣ $5 policy, no pill)` and `p = P(k = $10)`: `E[U ∣ pill] = q` and `E[U ∣ nothing] = p · q₁ + (1 − p) · q₀ / 2`, so the pill wins for every `p < 1` iff `q ≥ q₁` and `q > q₀ / 2`, and with `q = q₁` indifference needs `p = 1` — that is the point about the translation's line 152 ("be indifferent about taking the pill, because you'll do the same thing whether you take the pill or not"): it holds only with probability one on one's own policy, which the support convention excludes. The boundary case is in Lean: `CB.WN` (room policies `3 : 1` towards `$10`, the copy a fair coin under the pill and `7 : 1` towards the agent's chosen policy otherwise; decision-determined, `CB.decisionDeterminedN`) has "nothing" strictly beating the pill, `1/3 < 49/96` (`CB.nothing_beats_pillN`; room-conditional `1/2 < 49/64`, the `pre` worlds diluting both sides equally under this prior). The source's narrative (the pill is taken before copying) supports the side condition; `CB`'s family does not enforce it. Local to the example's intuition; the theorems are unaffected.

## F-5 · The Third Button's two claims need incompatible beliefs on this family; under one prior the message is either stable or better than the pill (imprecision: the section's two sentences need two priors, unless the author confirms a single-prior reading; ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED on "trust"; severity lowered from "local error" in repair round 1, audit r1 fidelity N4) — `TB.stableR_fi`, `TB.pill_beats_msg_fi`, `TB.not_stableR_nt`, `TB.msg_beats_pill_nt`

The source (lines 173–174): "Depending on its prior, UDT may still need to use the pill … each copy would trust that the other copy will follow this instruction; however, if that were true, this would lead both copies to choose `$20`, resulting in a payoff of `$0`." Read through the paper's structure with the pre-action silent about the room policy and decision-determination, let `p₁ = P(copy = $10 ∣ the agent exploits the message)`, `p₀ = P(copy = $10 ∣ the agent follows)`, `τ` the prior weight of the exploiting policy, `q > 1/2` the probability that the choice takes effect. At the message-observed room input, exploiting beats following given the other room was told `$10` iff `p₁ > 1 − p₀/2`; at the pre input, the pill beats the message iff `(2q − 1) τ [(1 − p₀/2) − p₁] > 0`, i.e. iff `p₁ < 1 − p₀/2`. **Same quantity, opposite signs.** On the witness: under `Wnt` (the copy is believed to follow `7 : 1` while the agent exploits) `¬ StableR`, `¬ Stable`, Advice-Following's conclusion fails at the message input — and the message beats the pill at `pre`; under `Wfi` (the copy runs the agent's policy) the pill beats the message at `pre` — and the recommendation is stable in the told form (`5/8 > 1/4`: exploiting is self-defeating, which is what UDT prices in). The `$0` outcome the source describes is what happens to an agent whose belief `p₁` is *wrong*, not a preference UDT has: on this family "communication fails" and "UDT flees to the pill" are not both available in one prior. The corrected [examples-revisited](../../sources/udt-representation-theorem/examples-revisited.md) already notes that the symmetric-reasoning reply is "again a fixed-point-selection argument"; this makes it exact. Severity: imprecision. The source says "*Depending on its prior*, UDT may still need to use the pill" and describes the `$0` outcome of symmetric naive trust; it does not assert instability and flight-to-the-pill under one prior, so the finding is that the section's two sentences need two priors (the mandate's expected conjunction is false *on this family*), not that the example is wrong. Also worth saying: under `Wfi` the pill beats the message only because the prior puts `3 : 1` on the exploiting policy, which is not a UDT fixed point under `Wfi` (`stableR_fi`: following is strictly better given told). Caveat: the reduction to `{$10, $20}` and the two-policy family are this package's; the sign identity is prose, and the Lean is two priors on a two-policy family — a richer family could change the numbers but not the sign identity, which uses only decision-determination and the silence of `Ä(pre)`.

## F-9 · The write-up's per-instance forcing rule contradicts its global validity definition (local error, write-up only; bli-paper-2-018(b)) — `HF`

`HF` (four worlds) has forcing with mass `4/5` and `Π† = Π*` everywhere: modification in the LaTeX's sense with mass `4/5`, behavioural modification with mass `0`. Read through the write-up's lines 336–348 (modification as `Π† ≠ Π*`, per instance) the same worlds are unmodified, and through line 390 (validity as a global property) the forcing counts. The action whose event is forced with probability `7/8` is minimally modifying behaviourally and not under forcing (`HF.minModBeh_not_minMod`): the Self-Trust theorem's subject changes with the definition. The LaTeX's forcing definition is the one `udt-comm-trust` formalized; the write-up's is a different theorem. Two facts about the package's own witnesses (audit r1 N2/N3, probe `BehModOnWitnesses`, not lifted into the library): (i) under the package's representation of the pre-choice the behavioural notion flags *every* world of Coordinated Buttons and the Third Button — `Π†(ȯ, pre) = ȯ` (the pre action *is* the channel state) while `Π*(ȯ, pre) = s`, so the full policies differ at the unrealized input `(1 − s, pre)` whether or not anything is forced; `modProbBeh = 1` on every non-empty event and `GlobalLink` fails there — a second reason the write-up's definition and the LaTeX's come apart; (ii) on `CBm` and `TB` the pill is *harmless forcing*: the chosen policy reads the channel and already outputs `$10` at the pill-observed room input, so the side channel forces the action the policy takes anyway (`Π† = Π*` at every world of `CBm`), and `m(pill) = 3/4` on `CBm`, `m(pill) = 1` on `TB` count forcing only — the write-up's "drawback" (line 390) on the package's own examples. **Not on `CB`** (the first version of this item said so; refuted by both audit-r2 auditors): `CB`'s chosen policy presses `k` at a room whatever it observes, so at the `(pill, $5)` worlds the pill overrides a `$5` press at the realized input — `Π†(pill, red) = $10 ≠ $5 = Π*(pill, red)`, `CB.pill_overrides_five`, `CB.modBeh_pill_five` — and is harmless only at the `(pill, $10)` worlds (`CB.pill_harmless_ten`); `m(pill) = 1` on `CB` counts forcing that is behavioural with probability `P(k = $5)`. That is the source's own mechanism for the pill (line 153) and makes `CB` the package's witness of forcing *with* behavioural modification at a realized input, the counterpart of `HF`: at the `$5`-policy worlds the write-up's notion and the LaTeX's agree, at the `$10`-policy worlds they come apart.
