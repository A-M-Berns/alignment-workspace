## A. The mandate's twelve known issues, verified

1. **Self-Trust's display is a tautology** (translation lines 472–478; original lines 480–486). *Local error in the statement of the main theorem.* Proved as the `T` row `ConcreteDS.selfTrust_printed`: the conclusion `E(U ∣ Π* = a) ≤ max_{a'} E(U ∣ Π* = a')` holds for every attained `a` by `Finset.le_sup'` at `a' = a`; the theorem's three conditions and the min-clause are unused binders. The intended claim is the repaired `selfTrust_repaired`: the UDT argmax contains a minimally modifying action. The original and the translation state the same display (2-008(b)).
2. **Communicative Expectation: statement and proof condition on different events** (translation line 458 vs 466; original 470 vs 474). *Local error.* `commExp_proof` proves the proof's version (right side conditioned on `Π̈ = R`); `commExp_printed` proves the printed version under the extra hypothesis `P(Π̈ = R) = 1`, which the paper lists only for the theorem. The original's Self-Trust proof writes `≤` where the lemma gives `=` (line ~484).
   - **2′ (new).** The proof's decomposition of `E(U ∣ Π* = ca(a), Π̈ = R)` over `Π̈` "by decision-determination" needs the conditioning event to be `D_{I,B}`-measurable. `{Π* = c}` is (given issue 3), but `{Π̈ = R}` is only if the recommendation is a function of the agent's dynamics: `R ⊑ Ȯ ⊑ (Ö, D_{I,B})` in general lets `R` depend on the focal observation `Ö`. `commExp_proof` therefore carries the explicit hypothesis `IsSubvariable S.DIB S.R`. *Imprecision* (a hypothesis the proof needs and the paper does not state; true in every model where `Ȯ ⊑ D_I`, e.g. `W2`).
3. **The decomposition over external policies needs `Π* ⊑ D_{I,B}`** (translation lines 463–466). *Blocking for the printed proof, repaired by the paper's own assumption* ("I assume that a subvariable representing the chosen policy exists", original line ~345). `AbstractDS.decomposition` takes `IsSubvariable S.DIB S.polS` explicitly. Witness of necessity: designed (four worlds; display gives `0 ≠ 1/2`), Lean status in the report.
4. **`Π*` has no definition** (original line ~343–345). *Ill-posed, left as a field; a construction is available under preconditions (repair round 1, `Construct.lean`).* `Π*_c(ω)(ȯ,ö) := Π†(ω)(ȯ⟨Ǒ := ǒ₀⟩, ö)` — the effective policy at the observation with the side channel set to a neutral value — is definable when `Ȯ` is determined by `(Ô, Ǒ)` (`SemChanSplit`; the original's line ~333 allows this to fail) and the neutral value combines with every realized semantic observation (`NeutralCompatible`, weaker than the mandate's `FactorsAs Ô Ǒ`: `W2m` has the former and not the latter, `W2m.not_factorsAs_oH_oC`). Then (i) `Π*_c ⊑ D_B` by construction (`polSC_sub`), which discharges the silent hypothesis of item 3 for the constructed policy (`polSC_sub_DIB`). **Finding 4′ (imprecision, about the mandate's (ii)):** "`Π† = Π*_c` at the realized observation on the unforced event" is *not* a theorem of the abstract structure. It holds by construction at worlds whose side channel is *at* the neutral value (`polD_eq_polSC_of_oC`); on the rest of the unforced event it is exactly the assumption that the boundary dynamics respond to the side channel only through forcing (`SideChannelInert`), and `polD_eq_polSC_iff_inert` records the equivalence. That assumption is the content of the paper's prose "what would run if unmodified", and it is `hlink` of "`Π̈ = R` in fact" for the constructed policy (`withPolSC_hlink`). (iii) follows from inertness and the junk-`0` reading of `m` (`polD_eq_polSC_of_modProb_zero`). On `W2` the side channel is constant, so `Π*_c = Π*` (`W2.polSC_eq_polS`, N−).
5. **The "condensation variable" definition is the translator's** (translation lines 320–329; the original's dynamics section 318–361 has no such definition). *Attribution.* Every `CTCondensation` docstring says "the translation's definition"; the prefix `CT` keeps it apart from Eisenstat's condensation. See §T16 (a).
6. **Condensation variables claimed unique up to bijection** (`topics/dynamics-and-condensation` line 76). *Local error in the corpus note.* Refuted by `ct_not_unique` (XOR on four uniform points: `snd` and `xor` are both condensation variables for `(xor, fst)` and `xor` is not a function of `snd`). Surviving neighbour `CTCondensation.unique_given`: functions of each other *given `Y`*.
7. **Conditional probabilities on null events have no stated convention; `−1` makes "stable" convention-dependent, and junk `0` makes "internally driven" an attainment constraint** (udt-rep-018/022). *Imprecision, quantified — and, for T13(c), decisive (audit r1, B1).* This package fixes junk `−1` for expectations (the paper's convention, translation line 269) and junk `0` for probabilities. Consequences proved: `isCA_nonempty` (a communicative alternative of an attained action is attained together with `Π̈ = R`: else `1 = 0`), `internallyDrivenJunk_nonempty` (under the *unguarded* identity the events `{Π* = (ȧ, ä)}` are all empty or all non-empty as `ä` varies), `stable_nonempty`/`stableR_nonempty` (an empty `(ȧ, r ö)`-event would make `−1 > x` false, so stability forces those events non-empty for *every* `ȧ` — but an empty *deviation* event makes the inequality hold vacuously, `x > −1`: this is where "stable" depends on the convention; on the T15 design and on the `Det` witness that vacuous case is exactly what makes stability hold). **The r0 claim that the guarded reading "leaves T10, T11 and T13 unchanged" was false for T13(c)**: with the unguarded `InternallyDrivenJunk`, the pointwise UDT rule and told-form stability are jointly contradictory on every structure with two external actions and a non-silent recommendation (`udtRule_internallyDrivenJunk_stableR_inconsistent`: internal drive makes every `(ȧ, ä)` attained, the rule makes all attained actions tie, stability makes the recommended one strictly better), so the r0 `polE_follows_R` was true and empty. The definition of record is now the guarded `InternallyDriven` (the identity where both conditional probabilities are defined); `adviceFollowing_R` and `polE_follows_R` take it, and the deterministic `Det` satisfies the whole package. T10 and T11 are unaffected (their conditioning events are required or proved non-empty). Not re-run mechanically under a different junk value for `−1`.
8. **Advice-Following's proof interchanges `max`/`argmax` and replaces "told `r`" by "follows `r`"** (original lines 534–541). *Local error in the proof; the theorem survives* in the told form (`adviceFollowing_R`, for each `ȧ` separately, no interchange) and in the Π-form under `Faithful` (`adviceFollowing_Pi`), which holds under `P(Π̈ = R) = 1` when no realized recommendation is silent anywhere (`faithful_of_nonsilent`).
   - **8′ (new).** The proof also needs the *combined* recommendation ("`ä₀` at `ö`, the others' realized recommendations elsewhere") to be realized, so that "stable with probability one" applies to it. That is the instance factorization of `Ȯ` (the paper's Instance Structure definition), used silently; `adviceFollowing_R` takes `FactorsAsFam S.R`. With one shared message copied to both instances (the first `W2` design) it fails, and stability of unrealized recommendations would be needed. *Imprecision.*
9. **"`Π̈ = R` in fact" conflates belief with fact** (original line 545; bli-paper-089). *Imprecision; the fact needs the rule at the realized world.* `polE_follows_R` is the printed clause with five named hypotheses (pointwise UDT rule; no simultaneous recommendation and modification; the prose meaning of `Π*`; `R ⊑ D_{I,B}`; AF-R's hypotheses, internal drive guarded) and no `P(Π̈ = R) = 1`; its full package is inhabited only degenerately (`Det`: a constant following `Π*`, N−), because on a nondegenerate prior the *pointwise* rule fails wherever advice-following has bite (`W2.not_udtRule`: scores `1/2` vs `7/16`; structurally, under unguarded internal drive the rule forces universal ties, `tie_of_udtRule_internallyDrivenJunk`). The version a nondegenerate prior can satisfy is **per world**: `aE_follows_R_of_udtRuleAt` assumes the rule only at `ω`'s own realized input and concludes for `ω`'s own action; `W2` at its following world is an N+ witness (`W2.aE_follows_at_follow_world`, with a deviating world of positive mass at the same input, `W2.deviating_world`). So the paper's sentence is right about the worlds whose chosen policy is a fixed point at their input, and bli-paper-089's "its existence is the real question" is the question whether the prior puts mass on non-fixed-point policies. (Repair round 1 corrects the r0 text here, which said a deterministic witness was available for the r0 package; under the unguarded internal drive none was, item 7.)

The Gács–Körner point (the common variable can carry far less than `I(X;Y)`) concerns entropy, not existence; no Lean.

## B. New findings

- **T8′ (blocking for the mandate's witness design; imprecision in the paper).** On the support, `B ∨ E = (Ö, Ä)` forbids any environment coordinate that the boundary determines. In a two-instance structure the other instance's action is determined by the boundary (`Π† ⊑ D_B` and the messages `⊑ Ȯ`), so it cannot be a *function-of-dynamics* coordinate of `E`: the mandate's "`U = 1` iff both instances match the recommendation" with `U ⊑ E` is impossible in any `AbstractDS`. The other instance must enter `E` as a free coordinate, correlated with the agent only in law — and, for decision-determination, only through `Π̈` (a copy of the *recommendation* would correlate `E` with `D_I`). `W2` is built this way (the other instance copies the agent's external policy at the other observation with probability `3/4`). Consequence for the paper: "functional identity" of instances cannot be a support-level identity inside the paper's own structure; it is a belief. ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED that the paper intends otherwise.
- **T8″ (imprecision).** The Instance Structure definition's "`Ä` factors as `(Ä_ö)_ö`" is incompatible with the abstract structure's `Ä = Π̈(Ö)` (one action at the realized instance, not a tuple); the same for `B, Ȧ, A, Ȯ`. Only the policy families (`Π*`, `Π†`, `Π̈`) and the observation-indexed semantics are representable; `ConcreteDS.InstanceFactored` records the two policy families.
- **T14 (finding, proved in r1).** The pointwise UDT rule is always satisfiable: a constant chosen policy satisfies it on every structure (`AbstractDS.udtRule_of_const`: the chosen action's event is `Ω`, every other action's event is empty and scores `−1`). This is only because `Π*` is a free field; the self-referential difficulty is confined to nondegenerate priors, where a fixed point can fail (`W2.not_udtRule`) and, under unguarded internal drive with stability, cannot exist (item 7), and to `Π*` tied to the dynamics (`hlink`, item 4′).
- **T15 (finding; first half proved in r1, second half an unverified design).** With a *constant* recommendation `r`, `HasCA` alone implies `P(Π̈ = R) = 1` — proved as `followsR_of_hasCA_const` (`Fairness.lean`), and more: communicative alternatives at a *single* input suffice, and DD, stability and the UDT rule are not used (`followsR_of_fairness_const` states the conjunction form with those as unused binders). The argument is the one recorded here: for `π̈` not following `r`, `P(Π̈ = π̈ ∣ Π* = ca(a), Π̈ = R) = 0`, so `P(Π̈ = π̈ ∣ Π* = a) = 0` for every attained `a`, and the events `{Π* = a}` cover `Ω`. With a two-valued recommendation the implication is expected to fail (unverified): eight worlds `(m, k, ö)` with `Π̈ = (k, k)`, `R = (m, m)`, `U = ö` have DD, `HasCA` (each action is its own communicative alternative because `{Π̈ = R} = {m = k}` has positive mass on each policy), `Stable r` for both realized `r` (the deviating event is empty, so strictness is the junk `−1`), `UdtRule` (all attained scores equal `1/2`) and `P(Π̈ = R) = 1/2`. So the fairness conjunction does not yield the self-esteem condition; the honest positive is `polE_follows_R`.
- **T17 (finding).** Under DD no strict version of Self-Trust can come from a modification cost invisible to `E` (`no_modification_cost`); the only strict version is under a unique argmax (`minMod_of_isStrictArgmax`), and `W2m` has a tie between a modifying and a non-modifying action under all of the repaired theorem's hypotheses (N+ for T17). For `selfTrust_repaired`'s *conclusion* `W2m` is N− (audit r1, B2): with two actions the CA identity forces the tie, every action is an argmax, and `∃ a, MinMod ∧ IsArgmax` follows from `minMod_follow` alone. A witness that exercises the `IsArgmax` conjunct needs at least three attained external actions with two score levels (open file, T11(c)+).

## C. Junk conventions and the guarded reading — verdict (rewritten in repair round 1)

The r0 verdict ("every theorem holds under the positive-mass-only reading as well") was not re-run and was false for T13(c) (item 7). Present state: the junk values enter the proofs only through `junk_lt_of_nonempty`, `score_lt_of_empty`, `evS_nonempty_of_isArgmax`, `isCA_nonempty`, `stable_nonempty`, `stableR_nonempty` and — for the finding only — `internallyDrivenJunk_nonempty`. T10, T11 and T13(a),(b) require or derive the non-emptiness of every conditioning event in their statements, so a positive-mass-only reading of the *probabilities* changes nothing there. T13(c) now takes the guarded `InternallyDriven` explicitly, so it *is* the positive-mass-only reading for internal drive; its r0 form was vacuous under junk `0`. Stability (`Stable`/`StableR`) is the one definition the `−1` convention decides: an empty deviation event satisfies the strict inequality vacuously, which is what makes the deterministic `Det` witness and the T15 design "stable". A guarded stability (inequality only where both events are non-empty) would *not* support "`Π̈ = R` in fact": at an input where only `(0, a₀)` and `(1, ä)`, `ä ≠ a₀`, are attained, guarded stability says nothing at `ȧ = 1`, and a world choosing `(1, ä)` violates the conclusion while the rule can hold with a tie. So the paper's corollary genuinely needs the strong reading of stability (every internal action combined with the recommended external action attained, or the junk convention). Not re-run mechanically with another junk value for `−1`.
