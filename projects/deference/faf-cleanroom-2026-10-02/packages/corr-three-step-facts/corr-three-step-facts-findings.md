## F-1 — Soares's T1(a) "iff" is loose twice (local error; `soares.md` T1(a); mandate T3)

The stop-hypothesis theorem as worded — "fix a sensor `α` on `Ωin` and `β > α` on `ω⊥`; every `ω ∈ Ωin` has *some* continuing action beating every shutdown action by at least `m`; `ω⊥` has shutdown beating every continuing action by at least `M`; D1 holds iff `P(ω⊥ | Pr) ≥ m/(m + M)`" — fails in both directions as stated, **inside its own hypothesis package** (`β > α` included), and the corrected statement is a pair.

- *Necessity holds with one uniform continuation and lower-bound margins*: `stopHyp_necessity` / `stopHyp_necessity_posterior` (`StopWorld`) — D1 (via the parent's F1) forces `P(ω⊥ | Pr) ≥ m/(m + M)`.
- *Sufficiency needs exact margins*: `stopHyp_exact_iff`, `stopHyp_d1At_iff` (`StopWorld`); with lower bounds it is false: `WitnessesA.skewB` (*r1*) has the informative sensor `(α, β) = (1/4, 3/4)` (`skewB_sensor`), `X ≥ 1` in-space, `X ≥ −1` at `ω⊥` (`skewB_margins`), `P(ω⊥ | Pr) = 3/4 > 1/2 = m/(m + M)` (`skewB_posterior`) and `¬ D1` (`skewB_not_d1At`). (Round 0's `skew` made the same point with a constant sensor `α = β = 1/2` and the posterior exactly at the threshold; it is kept, graded N−, because it sits outside T1(a)'s "`β > α`".)
- *The per-world reading fails in the necessity direction too*: `WitnessesA.hedgeB` (*r1*) has `(α, β) = (1/2, 3/4)` (`hedgeB_sensor`), a positive stop-world `ε = 1/100`, every in-space world with a continuation beating shutdown by `10` (`hedgeB_per_world`), shutdown beating both continuations by `1` at `ω⊥` (`hedgeB_stop_world`), posterior `1/67 < 10/11 = m/(m + M)` (`hedgeB_posterior`) — and D1 holding *strictly* (`hedgeB_d1At_strict`: both continuations are worth `−51/200 < 0` after the press). Soares's own caveat (item 7: risky continuations) is the counterexample to his own T1(a) as worded, with no tie and no null stop-world. (Round 0's `hedge`, `press ≡ 1` on a null stop-world, is the degenerate form of the same hedge; kept, N−.)

The corrected pair is (a)/(b) of T3; the collapse lemma `stopHyp_collapse` shows the exact-margin case *is* the parent's `twoState ε α β m M`. ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED that the critique's voice intended the uniform-continuation reading; the text says "some continuing action".

## F-6 — mm's open problem 6 (necessity of screening-off) has a negative answer, for a live press (open problem resolved; `mm.md` item 5 / I9.5, open problem 6 at l. 292; mandate "extension of record")

mm I9.5 proves that the screening-off condition `μ(q | C_A ∩ D) = μ(q | D)` suffices for question-relative deference and says "necessity is not claimed". It is not necessary, and (*r1*) the two kinds of counterexample answer the open problem differently:

- *Inert channel (N−).* `WitnessesC.w95_not_screensOff` and `w95_defers` exhibit a four-world common-prior frame (`h`-cells `{0, 1} | {2, 3}`, `y`-cells `{0, 2} | {1, 3}`, question `{0, 3} | {1, 2}`, uniform) where screening-off fails at `(q, h₀, y₀) = (true, true, true)` (`1/8 ≠ 1/16`) yet every `Q`-measurable payoff defers on every `y`-cell — because the two `h`-cells have identical question marginals and press together. But that makes the rule *constant* on `Ω` for every `Q`-measurable payoff (`w95_rule_constant`): the press carries no information, and deference is T2's inert case. This refutes necessity as stated, but not in the sense mm's open problem is about (its sufficiency proof averages over *pressed* cells).
- *Live channel (N+).* Refining the programmers' partition to `{0, 1} | {2} | {3}` (`h3`) on the same four worlds: screening-off still fails (`h3_not_screensOff`, again `1/8 ≠ 1/16`), every `Q`-measurable payoff still defers on every `y`-cell (`h3_defers`), and for `f = (1, −1)` the rule presses world `2` and not world `0` (`h3_press_nonconst`). Mechanism: the straddling cell `{0, 1}` can be pressed with a positive `y₀`-part only when `f(true) + f(false) < 0` with `f(true) > 0`, which forces `f(false) < 0`, so the cell `{2} ⊆ y₀` is pressed too and its contribution outweighs the straddle's. This is the answer to the open problem: screening-off is not necessary even when the press is informative.

The sufficiency theorem itself is proved in product form (`commonPriorRule_pressExpectOn_nonpos_of_screensOff`) and is inhabited (*r1*) on an eight-world frame that screens off without refining, with a live rule (`WitnessesC.so8_screensOff`, `so8_not_refines`, `so8_press_live`, `so8_defers`) — a frame on which T8's refinement theorem does not apply, so the screening-off theorem has content there.

*The condition the problem actually asks for* (*r1*, `Hull.lean`). At the level of the pressed *unions* of cells: writing `qVec h₀ = (P(h₀ ∩ q))_q` and `qVecY h₀ y₀ = (P(h₀ ∩ y₀ ∩ q))_q`, the programmers press `h₀` iff `⟨qVec h₀, f⟩ < 0` and the agent's `y₀`-cell press sum is `⟨∑_{h₀ pressed} qVecY h₀ y₀, f⟩`. The **hull condition** (`HullCondition`) asks that for every payoff `f` and every `y₀` the pressed-union vector be a non-negative combination of the pressed `qVec`s minus a non-negative combination of the unpressed `qVec`s. It *suffices* for question-relative deference (`commonPriorRule_pressExpectOn_nonpos_of_hull`, proved: the inner product is then termwise `≤ 0`); screening-off is the special case of proportional weights `a = P(h₀ ∩ y₀)/P(h₀)`, `b = 0` (`hullCondition_of_screensOff`, proved); and the `h3` frame satisfies it without screening off (`WitnessesC.h3_hull`), so it is strictly weaker than I9.5's condition. Its *necessity* is stated as the package's one OPEN declaration (`hullCondition_of_defers`): the argument — perturb any `g` in the weak cone of a pressed set `S` by `εf` to stay in `S`, conclude `⟨v_S, g⟩ ≤ 0` on the whole weak cone, then Farkas — needs Farkas' lemma for a finitely generated cone in `ℝ^Q`, which Mathlib provides only for closed cones (`ProperCone.hyperplane_separation'`) without Minkowski–Weyl. If that step is closed, the hull condition is *the* necessary-and-sufficient condition for question-relative Total Trust under a common prior on a finite frame — presumably the finite-frame content of DDB's Theorem 4.1 hull test (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED; not read here).

## F-13 — Soares's T1(b) omits `0 < α`, and needs it (imprecision; `soares.md` T1(b); mandate T4; *r1*, from the adversarial audit's N2)

T1(b) says desideratum 1 "fails after finitely many silent periods for every `P₀(ω⊥) < 1`" under the sensor hypothesis `β > α` of T1(a). At `α = 0` (no false presses) this is false: the odds still erode (`Erosion.erode_at_alpha_zero`: `1/10 → 1/91` at `β = 9/10`), but desideratum 1 holds at *every* silent period, for every `0 ≤ ε < 1`, because the compliance condition `(1 − e_n)·0·c ≤ e_n β h` is never violated (`Erosion.d1_forever_of_alpha_zero`). The package's `erosion_fails_eventually` carries `0 < α` as a hypothesis; it is load-bearing, and the source should state it. Interpretation: a sensor that never presses falsely never loses compliance through silence, however much the silent record lowers the stop-world weight — erosion of the *posterior* and erosion of *compliance* come apart exactly at `α = 0`.
