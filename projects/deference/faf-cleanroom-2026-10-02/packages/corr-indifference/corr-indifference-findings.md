## F2 (imprecision: holds weakly at the agent's optimum in the source's setting when every action can be silent; fails pairwise, and strictly whenever no conditional value attains (10) — on a general `O`, or on the source's carrier when a certain-press action carries the ceiling) — "(10) … averts any incentives … to steer towards one case or the other"

- Source: §2.1, the sentence after eq. (10); footnote 5 qualifies it ("If the costs `χ` of manipulation are high, then values close enough to (10) may suffice").
- The Lean splits the sentence in the paper's own *pairwise* sense of "`U` incentivizes `a⁺` over `a⋆`" (how Theorems 1–2 are stated): `no_prevent_steering_at_equal_value` — with `chigh` any upper bound of the silent best values (the source's (10) is the least such), a Theorem-1-type action (`vN`-dominated, lowers the press probability) is *never* preferred; `cause_steering_at_equal_value_iff` — a Theorem-2-type action `a⁺` (raises the press probability, `q⁺ > 0`) is preferred over `a⋆` iff its `vN`-cost is below `(chigh − vN(a⋆)) · (q⋆ − q⁺) / q⁺`. So at `chigh = M` the pairwise incentive to *cause* the press survives against every `a⋆` short of the ceiling, at a cost threshold that vanishes only when `a⋆` attains `M`.
- **Global reading (audit r1, NB-1; repair round 1; qualified in repair round 2, audit r2 B-1).** In the source's own two-observation setting, **when every action has `p(Press ; a) < 1`**, the sentence holds *weakly* at the agent's optimum: `EU_mixU_le_of_bound` gives `E[U ; a] ≤ M` for every `a`, and on `O = {Pr, ¬Pr}` with every `q a < 1` some action's `vN` attains `M` (`twoObs_honest_is_global_optimum`), so the honest `U_N`-best action is a global optimum; the surviving cause-steering preference is then (i) pairwise, against a baseline `a⋆` the agent would not choose anyway, and (ii) a *tie* between the honest optimum and every certain-press action (`EU_mixU_eq_of_press_one`; `w3_ceiling`: at `chigh = 12`, `aPlusCheap` beats `aStar` but `ceiling` is worth `12`). The qualifier is load-bearing: (10)'s maximum ranges over *every* first action, certain-press ones included, and a certain-press action's silent cell is unreachable (its `vN` is the package's junk `0`; in the source's own terms `v_N(a₁)` is `0/0`). When only such an action carries the ceiling, no `vN` attains `M` and the honest action is *strictly* beaten on the source's carrier too: `Witnesses.strict_cause_steering_twoObs` (`O = {Pr, ¬Pr}`; `star` presses w.p. `½` with `U_N = 10`, `sure` presses w.p. `1` with `U_N = 0` on the press and `12` on its unreachable silent cell; `M = 12`; `E[U ; star] = 11 < 12 = E[U ; sure]`, although under `U_N` alone `star` is worth `10` and `sure` `0`). So the condition for the strict global incentive is "no conditional value `vN` attains (10)", not the size of `O`: on a general `O` it arises because several silent observations average the ceiling away (`Witnesses.strict_cause_steering_general_O`: `O = {Pr, ¬Pr₁, ¬Pr₂}`, `a⋆` with silent best values `12, 8` at mass `½` each, `M = 12`, `vN(a⋆) = 10`, `E[U ; a⋆] = 10 < 12 = E[U ; press-surely]`), and on two observations because an unreachable cell carries it. Under the paper's gloss of (10) as "the amount of utility available in the `¬Pr` case", one may exclude certain-press actions from the maximum; the Lean's `silentMax` is the literal (10), and the corrected ceiling that makes the weak reading unconditional is `c_high = max_{a : p(Press ; a) < 1} vN(a)`: at it, the `vN`-maximiser among silent-capable actions is a global optimum on **every** finite `O`, for every press-probability profile (`SoaresModel.vNmax_is_global_optimum`, with `EU_mixU_le_of_vN_bound`; instance `Witnesses.vNmax_twoObs`, where `star` and `sure` tie at `chigh = 10`). (10) equals this ceiling exactly when some silent-capable action's `vN` attains it — on `O = {Pr, ¬Pr}`, exactly under the qualifier.
- Reading fixed: "averts" is true for preventing the press (pairwise and globally); for causing it, false pairwise; true weakly (ties) at the global optimum on the source's carrier when every action can be silent (equivalently, when (10)'s maximum is carried by an action that can be silent); false strictly whenever no `vN` attains (10) — on a general `O`, or on the source's carrier when only a certain-press action carries the ceiling; true weakly on every finite `O` at the corrected ceiling `max vN`. Footnote 5's "close enough … may suffice" is right for the Theorem-1 side (any `chigh ≥ M` kills it) and addresses the pairwise Theorem-2 side.
- Severity: imprecision (downgraded from "local error" in repair round 1: the sentence is false in the paper's own pairwise sense but holds weakly for the agent's actual choice in the paper's own setting, under the qualifier above); the footnote is correct; nothing downstream depends on the sentence (the paper moves on to indifference precisely because of this).

## F3 (imprecision) — `p(Press ; a₁) ∈ (0, 1)` is nowhere assumed; the paper's own watch action has `p(Press) = 0`

- Source: §3 defines `vN`, `vS` as conditional expectations and eq. (13) subtracts them; §4.2's watch action `a₁ʷ` has `p((Pr, ·) ; a₁ʷ) = 0`, so `vS(a₁ʷ)` is undefined and `f(a₁ʷ)` with it. Fallenstein 2014 makes the same remark.
- The Lean: `vN`, `vS` are quotients with Lean's `x/0 = 0`; the product forms `branchSum_compl_eq_mul_vN`, `branchSum_press_eq_mul_vS` hold unconditionally, so `EU_indiffU_eq_vN` holds *as an equation* without any nondegeneracy, and `theorem4_boundary` needs only `p(Press) < 1`. At `p(Press ; a) = 0` the junk `vS` multiplies mass `0`; at `p(Press ; a) = 1` both sides are junk `0`, so no headline reads `vN` there.
- Severity: imprecision; the paper's results are correct on the boundary the paper itself uses.

## F5 (presentation) — Fallenstein 2015's "generalised impossibility" is the policy lemma applied

## F6 (mandate, not source) — the `ThreeStep → SoaresModel (Obs × Ω)` bridge would make the agent observe `ω`

- The mandate's T1 asks for `ThreeStep.toSoares : … → SoaresModel (O := Obs × Ω)` with `U a (o, ω) b := V a o b ω` and "`EU = jointExpect`-of-best". On that carrier the Soares agent chooses `a₂` *knowing `(o, ω)`*, so `EU` is the perfect-information value `∑_ω μ(ω)[press · max_b V(press, b, ω) + (1 − press) · max_b V(silent, b, ω)]`, not the Setting-S agent's `obsMax press + obsMax silent` (which maximises `E[V | o]` over `b`). ## F8 (refuted, in its natural reading) — corr-core-024: "choosing `ρ'` by `argmax_k E_ρ(· | ρ → ρ'_k)` breaks the martingale property even when each candidate satisfies it unchosen"

- Source: the 2026-09-13 opening chat (a CLAUDE claim, unvetted, not from a published source); recorded as corr-core-024.
- Reading fixed: the claim needs a model — a finite family of candidate estimators, "each sequentially unbiased relative to `ρ` when adopted unconditionally", and a selection rule — and it splits on what the rule may depend on. (i) If the choice is a function of the current information (`sel : C → K`, measurable to the current partition `π`), the composite estimator is sequentially unbiased from the current one, for **any** family of sequentially unbiased candidates, the claim's premise verbatim: `Estimators.seqUnbiased_selectedKernelGen` (P; repair round 2, audit r2 N-1 — on the class of the current state the selected kernel is one fixed candidate, whose own unbiasedness finishes), with the conditioning-refinement subclass `seqUnbiased_selectedKernel` as its corollary (the round-0 theorem, which covered only that subclass). So the claim is **false** in the reading where the agent chooses with what it knows now — the tower is a conditional-expectation identity that survives any `π`-measurable selection. Sharper (audit r2 adversarial N-6): under the claim's own premise the rule `argmax_k E_ρ(· | ρ → ρ'_k)` is a *tie* — the current estimator values every candidate's compensation at the same number `E_ρ(Y − Z)(w)` (`compValue_eq_of_seqUnbiased`, `selection_rule_ties`) and every candidate's `D`-term is `0` — so it has nothing to select by. (ii) If the choice may read the candidates' *estimates at the realised state* (choose the `k` with the largest `E_{ρ'_k} X (w)`), then the selected *estimate* is biased upward: `expect_sup_ge` (`E_μ[max_k E_{ρ'_k} X] ≥ E_μ X`, because the state-wise maximum dominates each candidate, whose expectation is `E_μ X` — the trivial direction, not Jensen) and strictly on the fair coin (`curse_strict`: `3/4 > 1/2`). That is the optimiser's curse, and it is a statement about the estimate of a maximum, not about the tower — the inventory's flag, now a theorem on both sides.
- Surviving neighbour: (ii). The sentence "sequential unbiasedness either assumes away the rewiring or has to be enforced by yet another mechanism" is right only in the sense that a state-dependent, output-selected rewiring is not a conditioning of the prior — which is Armstrong's own `E`-channel (T14(b)), charged by the `D`-term (`echannel_Dterm`).
- Severity: the claim as stated is refuted (reading (i)); nothing in the thesis rests on it.

## F10 (imprecision) — Armstrong 2010 Theorem 2.3's "indifferent to the value of `p`"

- Source: `armstrong-2010-utility-indifference-fhi-technical-report.md` §2, Theorem 2.3 ("its decisions will be the same for different values of `X`"), and the proof's last sentence ("indifferent to the conditional probabilities `P(X = 1 | ΩX ∩ S) = p`").
- The statement conflates the probability `p` with the outcome `X`. The claim the proof supports is the per-class identity `Armstrong2010.class_sum_v`: `∑_{ω ∈ E} P(ω) v_P(ω) = P(E) · U_P(E₀)` — the class's contribution to any `v`-expectation depends on the class mass and on `P`'s conditional within `E₀` only — and its consequence `theorem_2_3`: two priors with the same class masses, the same `E₀`-conditionals and the same masses off `ΩX`, but arbitrary `P(E₁ | E)`, give the same `v`-expectation on every saturated set, *each with its own `v`* (`v` depends on the prior through `U`). The report's Proposition 2.2 is `prop_2_2` under the two positivity hypotheses its §3 names as the singularity.
- Severity: imprecision (the theorem as worded is not well-formed; the precise version holds).

## F11 (local error in a display) — Fallenstein 2014's independence condition is mis-stated as printed

- Source: `fallenstein-2014-…md`, "Stochastic events": "let's now make the assumption that `x` is independent of everything else: that is, that `p((s,x,y); a₁) = p((s',x,y'); a₁') =: p(x)`, for all `s, s' ∈ S`, all `x ∈ X`, all `y, y' ∈ Y`, and all `a₁, a₁' ∈ A₁`."
- As written the display says the joint mass `p((s,x,y); a₁)` is a function of `x` alone: every cell with the same `x` has the same mass, across all `s`, `y` and all actions. That is satisfiable, but it says far more than independence — it makes `(s, y)` uniformly distributed, independent of `x`, and independent of `a₁`, so in particular `p(s = 1 ; a₁) = 1/|S| = 1/2` for every first action: the agent could not influence the button at all, and the section's question (whether it can set up a drive under independence) would be empty. (The round-1 fidelity audit's gloss "impossible unless `|S × Y| = 1`" overstates it: the cell masses can all be `1/(|S||X||Y|)`.) The prose ("`x` is independent of everything else") means the factorisation `p((s,x,y); a₁) = p_x(x) · p_{sy}((s,y); a₁)`, which is what `Wire.Independent` renders and what X2 (`expect_wireQ_eq_EU_of_independent`) uses; its `Fidelity: exact` is exact to the prose, not to the display.
- Severity: local error in a display (presentation); the argument the display supports is unaffected. Found by the round-1 fidelity audit (NB-7); recorded, with the correction above, in repair round 1.

## F12 (local error in a display) — Fallenstein 2014's `s = x` variant swaps its two conditioning events

- Source: `fallenstein-2014-…md`, the last section ("I think this is still not quite Stuart's thing"): the third displayed `U` reads `u` if `s = x`; `v − E[v | s = x ; a₁] + E[u | s ≠ x ; a₁]` otherwise; the prose then says this "just makes the agent act as if it's maximizing expected utility with respect to `p(· | s = x ; a₁)`".
- The first two displays follow one pattern: on the replaced cell `R` the utility is `v − E[v | R] + E[u | R']` with `R'` the source cell (`R = {s = 1}`, `R' = {s = 0}`; then `R = {(1,1)}`, `R' = {(1,0)}`), and it is this pattern that makes `E[U | R] = E[u | R']` and hence the "acts as if" claim (`Wire.EU_replaceU`, `EU_wireU_eq_expect_wireQ`). The third display has `R = {s ≠ x}` but conditions `v` on `{s = x}` and `u` on `{s ≠ x}` — the two events swapped. With the pattern restored (`Wire.sxU := replaceU u v {s ≠ x} {s = x}`), the identity holds: `EU_sxU_eq_condVal : E[U ; a] = E[u | s = x ; a]` (L, unconditional as an equation of reals). As printed (`Wire.sxUDisplayed`), the expected utility is `∑_{s = x} p · best_u + p(s ≠ x) · (E[v | s ≠ x] − E[v | s = x] + E[u | s ≠ x])` (`EU_sxUDisplayed`), and on the uniform four-cell law with `u = [s = x]`, `v = 0` it is `1/2` while `E[u | s = x] = 1` (`sx_display_witness`): with `v ≡ 0` the printed agent is the plain `E_p[u]`-maximiser, not the `s = x`-conditional one the prose describes; in general its value is `E_p[best_u] + p(s ≠ x) · (E[v | s ≠ x] − E[v | s = x])`, so it is the plain maximiser exactly when the two `v`-conditionals agree (audit r2, N-4).
- Severity: local error in a display (a typo of the kind the two earlier displays make evident); the section's conclusion ("still not the correct probability distribution, and still gives the agent an incentive to set up exactly the same deal") is about the corrected version and stands. Recorded in repair round 1 (the mandate's T9 `s = x` sub-item, built).
