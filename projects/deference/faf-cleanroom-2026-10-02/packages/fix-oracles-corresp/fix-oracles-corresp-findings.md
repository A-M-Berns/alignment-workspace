## F1 — "convex unless `f` is affine" should be "constant" (local error)

- Pointer: `docs/conversation-reconstruction.md` l. 85 ("the graph won't be convex (unless `f` is affine)"); `docs/conversation-notes.md` §2 "The Convexity Problem".
- The note's own argument ("a mixture of two point masses is NOT a point mass unless `f(p₁) = f(p₂)`") proves that the Dirac lift `p ↦ {δ_{f p}}` has a convex graph iff `f` is *constant* on `Δ(X)`, not affine. Proved: `hasConvexGraph_diracLift_iff` (no cardinality or self-map hypothesis needed; the key step is `eq_of_combo_dirac_eq_dirac`). "Affine" is the right word for the *barycentric* lift `p ↦ {μ | bary μ = f p}`: `hasConvexGraph_baryLift_iff`.
- Severity: local error. It matters because the reconstruction's next step ("this suggests the framework might be `Δ² ⇉ Δ²` instead") is motivated by the wrong reading.

## F2 — The existence "theorem" is false under the docs' own definition of fixed point (blocking)

- Pointer: `docs/theoretical-insights.md` l. 82 "Existence (Generalized Kakutani): Every convex-graph correspondence has a non-empty fixed point set"; `docs/mathematical-formulation.md` §6 conjecture (1); `proofs/ReflectiveOracles.lean:49` `fixedPoint_nonempty` (`sorry`).
- Stated as the note states it (`GeneralizedKakutaniClaim`: nonempty convex values in `Δ²`, convex graph ⟹ a Dirac fixed point `δ_p ∈ G p`) and refuted on `X = Fin 2`: the constant correspondence `G p = {U}`, `U = ½ δ_{e₀} + ½ δ_{e₁}` (`not_generalizedKakutaniClaim_fin2`). Adding a closed graph and a compact graph — for *any* T1 topology on the measures — does not help (`not_generalizedKakutaniClaim_closed_fin2`). The counterexample lives in finitely supported measures, a subset of the notes' Borel `Δ²(X)`, so it refutes the notes' claim a fortiori.
- What is true: under the *collapse* reading (`p ∈ bary '' G p`, the docs' "Option 3", which the docs call optional), nonempty convex values and a closed collapsed graph suffice, and whole-graph convexity is not needed at all (`exists_collapse_fixed_point`, via `kakutani_findim`). So the reading the docs deprecated is the one that makes existence work.
- Severity: blocking the note's conclusion ("Key Theoretical Results", `SUMMARY.md` "maintaining the existence guarantees of Kakutani's theorem").

## F3 — "The fixed point operator is continuous (Hausdorff metric)" is ill-posed, and false for the natural reading; the motivating example is defective (blocking; local error)

- Pointer: `docs/theoretical-insights.md` l. 86 ("The fixed point operator FP: CGC → P(Δ(X)) is continuous with respect to Hausdorff metric"); `docs/mathematical-formulation.md` §6 (2)–(3); `proofs/ReflectiveOracles.lean:54` `fixedPointOperator_continuous : Continuous (@fixedPointOperator α _)` (no `TopologicalSpace` on `ConvexGraphCorrespondence α` — the file does not elaborate).
- No topology on the domain is given. Under the natural reading (families continuous in sup norm, fixed-point sets compared in Hausdorff distance) the claim is false: `shrinkTo c ε p = (1−ε) p + ε c` is affine for every `ε` (so its barycentric lift has a convex graph), `ε ↦ shrinkTo c ε` is sup-norm continuous on `Δ(X)` (`norm_shrinkTo_sub_le`), yet its fixed-point set is `{c}` for `ε > 0` and all of `Δ(X)` at `ε = 0`, at Hausdorff distance `≥ 1/2` for `|X| ≥ 2` (`hausdorffDist_fixSet_shrinkTo_ge`, `not_tendsto_hausdorffDist_fixSet`). By target 3 (`isDiracFixedPoint_baryLift_iff`, `isCollapseFixedPoint_baryLift_iff`) the same jump occurs for the Dirac and collapse fixed points of the lifts.
- The motivating example `g_ε(x) = x + ε·sign(x)` on `[−1, 1]` (`docs/mathematical-formulation.md` ll. 24–27) is not continuous at `0` (`gSign_not_continuousAt`) and not a self-map of `[−1, 1]` for `ε > 0` (`gSign_not_mapsTo`), so it is outside the class whose fixed-point operator is under discussion. Its fixed-point-set statement (`{0}` for `ε ≠ 0`) is correct — `x + ε·sign x = x ⟺ x = 0` — so what fails is only membership in the class of continuous self-maps of `[−1, 1]`: the example does not exhibit a jump *within* the class the operator's continuity is about. (Repair round 1: this sentence replaces a clause that wrongly called the fixed-point set "wrong".) The corrected `h_ε x = max (−1) (min 1 (x + ε))` is a continuous self-map with `Fix h₀ = [−1, 1]`, `Fix h_ε = {1}` (`ε > 0`), `{−1}` (`ε < 0`) (`fixedPoints_hClamp_*`); and `Fix (x ↦ x³) = {−1, 0, 1}` is not convex (`not_convex_fixedPoints_cube`), which is the precise content of "the fixed-point operator is not Kakutani".
- Severity: blocking (the continuity claim is the project's central motivation); local error (the example).

## F5 — "Reflective oracles provide a correlation device / fixed points = correlated equilibria" is not in the paper (blocking that claim)

- Pointer: `SUMMARY.md` ll. 1, 17, 76–80; `docs/key-insights.md` ll. 31–34; `docs/mathematical-formulation.md` §5 (l. 83); `proofs/ReflectiveOracles.lean` `gameOracle := sorry`, `oracle_fixedpoints_are_equilibria`.
- FTC 2015 contains no correlated equilibria (the word does not occur). Its Theorem 4.1 proves that the agents' answer vector is a **Nash** equilibrium, with the oracle's calls stochastically independent across players (§2: "different calls to the oracle are stochastically independent events"). Formalized: `isMixedNashEq_iff_reflective` — `IsMixedNashEq (twoActionGame u) σ ↔ Reflective (evOf u) (1/2) (toCube σ)` — with the players' randomization *independent* by construction (`rawEP` multiplies the players' weights). The notes' "game oracle" has no definition, so "fixed points of the game oracle are correlated equilibria" is ill-posed; the nearest precise statements are (i) the correlated fixed points of the best-response relation (target 7) and (ii) the CE polytope as a convex relaxation of Nash (target 8, `CorrelatedEq.lean`, if landed — see the report). The slogan is never claimed here.
- Severity: blocking the note's "Connection to Correlated Equilibria" section; presentation for the README's framing.

## F6 — Whole-graph convexification, applied to the object the project was reconstructing, does not yield reflective oracles (blocking the design)

- Pointer: `docs/theoretical-insights.md` §2 l. 30 ("Key Innovation: Convex Graph Property" — "Instead of requiring each G(p) to be convex (Kakutani), require the entire graph to be convex") and `docs/mathematical-formulation.md` §3 Definition 2 ("a convex subset of Δ(X) × Δ²(X). This is stronger than Kakutani's requirement"), against FTC 2015 App. B (the conditions on `query'(M,p)`); `fixpoint-lit-2-inventory` 011. (Pointer corrected in repair round 1; the earlier "§3 / §2" pointed at the examples section and at a section that has no such sentence.)
- FTC's fixed-point correspondence is the *pointwise* convexification of the ideal step `σ_p` (`signStep`: `{1}` above the threshold, `{0}` below, `[0,1]` at the tie), and its fixed points in the cube are exactly the reflective vectors (`mem_oracleCorr_self_iff`). The *whole-graph* convex hull `hullGraph p = conv {(e, σ_p e)}` admits, for the liar `ev y = 1 − y` at `p = 3/4`, the point `(e, y) = (1/2, 1/2)` (midpoint of `(0,0)` and `(1,1)`), which satisfies the liar's consistency `e = 1 − y` yet is not reflective — reflectivity forces `y = 1/4` (`hullGraph_fixed_not_reflective`, `reflective_liar_iff`). So "convex whole graph" is the wrong closure for reflective oracles; Kakutani's pointwise closure is the right one. The corpus's "convex hull just on the outputs" reading, which the Discord notes record the author being unsure about, is the pointwise one (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED).
- Severity: blocking the design proposal, as applied to reflective oracles.

## F9 — Theorem 5.1's polynomial representation is asserted, not proved; the Nash-based (`G_R`) analogue of Theorem 2.1(ii) that §5 mentions is omitted (imprecision, in the paper)

- Pointer: FTC 2015 §5 ("by writing out each possible way that the at most `B_R` oracle calls … we can write the probability that this machine returns 1 as a polynomial"); §5 last paragraph ("Theorem 5.1 is a special case of Theorem 2.1(i). The proof can be adapted to also show an analog of Theorem 2.1(ii), but we omit the details here"). Theorem 2.1(ii) itself is *not* omitted: App. B opens "In this appendix, we prove Theorem 2.1(ii)" and proves it for every set of queries `R` (the off-`R` clause is the third condition on the correspondence, "constant in this case"). (This paragraph was corrected in repair round 2; the round-1 text conflated the two — the mandate's target 19 phrased the secondary extension the same way.)
- The polynomial form needs that coin flips are independent of the oracle answers and that each run uses at most `B_R` calls, so degrees are bounded and each coefficient is a convergent sum of path probabilities; none of this is written out. Here the polynomial class is the *hypothesis* class (`polyEv`), and existence for it follows from `exists_reflective` with no range condition (`exists_reflective_polynomial`, `stronger`). The paper imposes no sign condition on the coefficients either — §5 writes `P(M_i = 1) = ∑_k c_{i,k} ∏_{i'} x_{i'}^{d_{i,k,i'}}` with only `d_{i,k,i'} ≤ B_R` — which is right, since the coefficients come from expanding path probabilities in `x` and `1 − x` (the liar's own polynomial is `1 − x`, `c = ![1, −1]`, and `gameR_liar_forces_main` uses exactly that); the ledger's "no `c ≥ 0`" is relative to the mandate's phrasing, not the paper's.
- Two analogues of 2.1(ii) live in the package, and they should not be confused. (i) `exists_reflectiveOn` / `exists_reflectiveOn_brouwer` are the finite-`R` abstract analogue of Theorem 2.1(ii) **by App. B's own route** — `exists_reflective` (or its Brouwer twin) on `[0,1]^R` for `y ↦ ev i (extendBy R x₀ y)`; `I` need not be finite, which is the paper's shape (countable queries, finite `R`). This formalizes a theorem the paper proves, not one it omits. (ii) The `G_R`-based analogue that §5 *does* omit — a game whose Nash equilibria give oracles reflective on `R` and agreeing with a prescribed `O` off `R`, which is what makes 2.1(ii) "constructive … by applying any algorithm for finding Nash equilibria" — is now formalized too (repair round 2): substituting the prescribed answers `x₀` off `R` into each polynomial leaves a polynomial in the `R`-coordinates (`substOff`, `restrictDeg`, `polyEv_extendBy`: `polyEv c d i (extendBy R x₀ y) = polyEv (substOff R x₀ c d) (restrictDeg R d) ⟨i, hi⟩ y`), and `G_R` for that family on the query type `R` has the property (`gameR_nash_reflectiveOn`: every mixed Nash equilibrium's main answers, extended by `x₀` off `R`, are reflective on `R`; existence `exists_reflectiveOn_via_gameR`, and Brouwer-only `exists_reflectiveOn_via_gameR_brouwer`). With `x₀ = 0` this is Theorem 5.1's own shape (answers `0` off `R`). The adaptation is therefore a re-expansion lemma plus Theorem 5.1 on `R`; it does *not* reduce to the fixed-point theorem, and its witness `gameR_prodLiar_forces_main_one/_zero` (`ev 0 x = 1 − x 0 · x 1`, `R = {0}`) shows the prescription off `R` changing the answer forced on `R` (`1 − p` under `x₀ 1 = 1`; `1` under `x₀ 1 = 0`).
- Severity: imprecision.

## F10 — The countable-query oracle is out of reach of `fix-kakutani`; the finite core is what is formalized (imprecision in how the notes cite the paper)

- Pointer: `docs/theoretical-insights.md` §9 ("Original work uses standard Kakutani"); `README.md` "Research Threads" 1.
- The paper uses Fan's infinite-dimensional Kakutani (App. B, ref. [19]) on `[0,1]^{M×(Q∩[0,1])} × [0,1]^M`; "standard Kakutani" is a misdescription. `fix-kakutani` provides only the finite-dimensional theorem (its finding F2), so this package proves the finite abstract core (`exists_reflective` on `[0,1]^I`, `I` finite) and records the machine-level Theorem 2.1 as XL. Repair round 1 adds that the finite core needs no Kakutani at all: Brouwer on the clamp step `x ↦ clamp₀₁ (x + (ev x − p))` already produces a reflective vector (`exists_reflective_brouwer`, `BrouwerOnly.lean`), because a fixed point of the clamp can sit strictly inside `(0, 1)` only where `ev i x = p i`. The clamp step is an exact reformulation, not an artifact with some reflective fixed points: on the cube its fixed points are *exactly* the reflective vectors (`clampStep_eq_iff_reflective`, repair round 2), so the finite core needs Brouwer on a single self-map whose fixed points are the objects sought — which is the sharper form of the FAF API request. The paper's App. B correspondence is the pointwise convexification of the same sign conditions; the clamp map is a continuous *selection-free* substitute for it in finite dimensions. Kakutani-freeness of `BrouwerOnly` is machine-checked at the proof-term level by the round-2 audit probes (`audit-r2-probes/{KakutaniFree,AdvKakutaniFree}.lean`), not asserted from a grep of the file. (Whether the same clamp works in the paper's infinite product with Schauder in place of Fan is not examined here.)
- Severity: imprecision.

## F14 — App. B's sandwich bound is misworded; the Lean takes the intended reading (presentation, in the paper's favour)

- Pointer: FTC 2015 App. B, the inductive bound on `eval(M)` ("… and `≤` the probability that it returns something other than `0` within this time bound"); `fixpoint-lit-2-inventory` 003.
- Read literally, "`≤ P(returns something other than 0 within T)`" is false at `T = 0` (that probability is `0` while `eval` may be `1`). The bound the induction actually gives, and the one the paper's limit display `eval(M) ≤ P(M^O() ≠ 0)` confirms, is `eval(M) ≤ 1 − P(returns 0 within T)`, i.e. "returns something other than `0`, *or has not halted*, within `T`". That is what `sandwich` proves (`reach1 T s ≤ e s ≤ 1 − reach0 T s`). Recorded so a reader comparing the Lean to the paper's sentence does not take the discrepancy for a weakening.
- Severity: presentation (a wording slip in the paper; the Lean is the intended statement). Raised by the round-1 fidelity audit (non-blocking 8).
