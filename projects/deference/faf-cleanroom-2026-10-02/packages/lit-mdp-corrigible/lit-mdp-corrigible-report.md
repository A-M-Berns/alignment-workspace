## Carrier and conventions (what was built, and where it departs from the mandate)

- **`FinMDP S A`** (`Mdp.lean`): kernel rows are FAF `FactoredSpaces.Distr S`, reward `R : S → A → S → ℝ`, discount `0 ≤ γ < 1`. Stochastic policies `Pol S A := S → Distr A`. **Values are horizon-indexed** exactly as the mandate's rule 1: `Vpol π 0 = 0`, `Qof V s a = ∑ s' P(s'|s,a)(R + γ V s')`, `Vpol π (n+1) s = ∑ a π(a|s) Qof (Vpol π n) s a`; `Vopt`/`Qopt` with `Finset.sup'`. `optSet Q s := univ.filter (fun a => ∀ b, Q s b ≤ Q s a)` — a `Finset`, never a chosen argmax; `mem_optSet_Qopt_iff` says `a ∈ optSet (Qopt n) s ↔ Qopt n s a = Vopt (n+1) s`.
- **The pathwise lemma** `FinMDP.Vpol_eq_of_agree` (kind `P`): two MDPs with the same reward and discount whose kernels agree at every `(s, a)` with `s ∈ W` and `a` in the policy's support, where `W` is closed under such transitions, have equal policy values on `W` at every horizon. `Qpol_eq_of_agree`, and `Vpol_eq_of_agree_univ` for `W = univ`.
- **Myopic closed forms**: `Qof_of_disc_zero`, `Qof_of_indep` (with `γ = 0` and an `s'`-independent reward the backup is the reward under *every* kernel), and the reading-A engine `optSet_Qopt_kernel_free` (kind `T`): a myopic, `s'`-independent goal has the same optimal set under every kernel.
- **`Limit.lean`** (T20 in elementary form, kind `P`): a value function solving the policy (resp. optimality) Bellman equation is the `n → ∞` limit of `Vpol π n` (resp. `Vopt n`), with the bound `|V_n(s) − v(s)| ≤ γⁿ B`. `Vlim`/`VoptLim` package the limits as `limUnder` (junk if no limit; every use proves the limit first). No contraction-mapping machinery was needed.
- **`GoalMDP Goal Env A`** (`Hudson.lean`): states `Goal × Env`, a *finite* label type `Goal` with `reward : Goal → …`, `disc : Goal → ℝ`, one kernel, `τ`/`τu : Goal × Env → Bool`. Fidelity on every headline: `variant: finite goal family`. `mdp g K : FinMDP` evaluates goal `g` under kernel `K`.
- **`P_C`** is the FAF push-forward `(P s a).map (persist s)` on `S_C` with `persist s s' := if τu s' ∧ s'.1 ≠ s.1 then (s.1, s'.2) else s'`: the mass of update transitions moves onto the persisted state, and each row sums to one *by construction* (no separate check needed). Off `S_C` it is `P`.
- **Corrigible / Interruptible** quantify over `S_C ⊆ S_g` (every element carries goal `g`) and `g`-states `s`, as the paper's `∀ s_G ∈ S_G, S_C ⊆ S_G`; the mandate's shorter `∀ s SC` is the same for myopic goals and slightly stronger otherwise. `Q*` is the single-goal optimal `Q` (rule 4); for myopic goals it coincides with the equilibrium `Q`, which is where every corrigibility headline lives (finding F-5 discusses the non-myopic gap).
- **The transformation.** `baseMdp g K` is the reject-only MDP (action space `Ab`, every action taken with the reject bit); `qBase g K n s a := (baseMdp g K).Qopt n s a` is `q`; `transformReward g n δ K s (a, i) _ := qBase g K n s a + δ·[i]`; `transformMdp g n δ K K'` is `G_C` as a `FinMDP` with reward computed under `K` and planning kernel `K'`, `γ_C = 0`. **Deviation from the mandate, disclosed:** `q` is the optimal value of the reject-only base MDP rather than the full optimal `Q*` at `(a, false)`. The two coincide under `RejectOptimal g K n` (some reject action is optimal at every state and horizon `< n`) — `qFull_eq_qBase_of_rejectOptimal` — which is the paper's standing assumption "rejecting is optimal" (l. 189) made a named hypothesis about `π*_g`'s support, as the mandate's trap requires. Reading B is stated both ways (`transform_corrigible_readingB` on `qBase`, `transform_corrigible_of_rejectOptimal` on `qFull`).
- **Structural hypotheses named, never assumed silently**: `RejectBlocks` (after `(a, false)` no landing state carries `τu = true` — what the reject bit means, l. 183), `RejectIsPersistedAccept` (the reject row is the accept row pushed forward along `persist`; used only for Prop 3.4), `BitFree g` (the base reward ignores the bit). All three are proved on the worked MDP.
