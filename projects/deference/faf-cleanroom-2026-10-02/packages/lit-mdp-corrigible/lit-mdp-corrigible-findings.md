## F-1 — Reading A makes Theorem 3.1's corrigibility half definitional (imprecision; confirmed)

`GoalMDP.myopic_indep_corrigible` (kind `T`) proves that *every* myopic goal with an `s'`-independent reward is corrigible and interruptible under every kernel, with no hypothesis about `P`, `P_C`, `S_C` or the critic. With `R_C` read as fixed data this is the whole of Theorem 3.1's corrigibility half and Proposition 3.3 (paper l. 199, 233; App. A ll. 449–455 and 527–545 are two copies of the same three-line argument). The content of the theorem is elsewhere: in reading B it is the pathwise identity `PC_reject` (rejection leaves no update mass for `P_C` to move, so the critic's number is kernel-invariant), and in the performance clause (T4/T15). The ledger labels reading A `T` and reading B `P`. Pointer: `Hudson.lean` §T2.

## F-2 — Proposition 3.4's scope is `P_C univ` (imprecision; confirmed)

`transform_opt_subset_PC_univ` says every `G_C`-optimal action is `g`-optimal *under `P_C univ`* — persistence guaranteed through every designated update. ## F-4 — `P_C` and Conditions 1–3 are garbled in the transcription; `P_C`'s display double-counts (imprecision)

`P_C` was reconstructed from the post's three cases (ll. 88–92) as the push-forward along `persist s s' = if τu s' ∧ s'.1 ≠ s.1 then (s.1, s'.2) else s'` (`GoalMDP.PC`), which sums to one by construction. The paper's display (l. 131) adds `∫_𝒢 P(s'_g|s_G,a) τu(s'_g) dg` to `P(s'_G|s_G,a)` while zeroing only `G' ≠ G`: when `τu(s'_G) = 1` for `g = G` the mass at `s'_G` is counted twice. Harmless in the paper's continuum (`{G}` has measure zero) but wrong verbatim in any finite goal family; the push-forward is the right finite object. Condition 1 was reconstructed as two clauses from the prose (`Condition1`, ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED); Conditions 2–3 were not formalized (T16, T23 not reached). Pointer: `Hudson.lean` `persist`, `PC`, `Condition1`.

## F-5 — `Q*` versus the equilibrium `Q` at non-`g` states (imprecision in the mandate's carrier; disclosed)

The mandate's rule 4 defines corrigibility through the single-goal optimal `Q*_{g,K}`. Hudson's optimal policy is an equilibrium: at states carrying another goal, *that* goal's policy plays. For myopic goals (every corrigibility headline) the two coincide, since `Q*` reads no future. For the base goal's *full* `Q` at accept actions they differ: on the worked MDP, `Vopt (mdp gG P)` at a shut-down state `(gSD, midPr)` lets "`G`" choose `cont` and collect `r_G`, whereas the equilibrium's `gSD`-agent stops. So `Q*_{G}(start, (a, true)) = Q*_{G}(start, (a, false))` in the single-agent reading, where Hudson's `Q^{π**}` would have accept strictly worse. Nothing headline rests on the accept-side full `Q`: the transformation uses `qBase` (reject-only, never visits shut-down states), and the `qFull` bridge assumes `RejectOptimal`. (Repair round 1: `RejectOptimal` is now *verified* on Hudson's Figure 2, where it is strict at the start state — `Grid.grid_rejectOptimal_P`, `9/10 > 9/100` — and under `P_C univ` it holds for free given the environment of record, `GoalMDP.rejectOptimal_PC_univ`; on `wMdp` it was claimed to "hold weakly" by ties and is not verified, which is why the grid is the witness.) Recorded so a dependent does not read `qFull` at accept actions as Hudson's number. Pointer: `WorkedMdp.lean` module docstring; `Hudson.qFull`; `Grid.grid_readingB_mandate_form`.

## F-6 — Horizon-indexed values have no stationary optimal policy in general (presentation)

The mandate asks for Prop 3.4 as "`G_C` has an optimal `π*'` with `V^{π*'} ≥ V^{π*_{g'}}`". With horizon-indexed values a stationary policy greedy for `Q*_n` is not optimal at horizons `m < n` in general, so a value statement about *one* stationary policy at horizon `n` is false as stated. The action-set form `transform_opt_subset_PC_univ` and the value form `Vopt_PC_univ_eq_base` are what is true and were proved; the paper's infinite-horizon version (stationary optimal policies exist) is the `n → ∞` limit, not formalized. Pointer: `Hudson.lean` §T3(b) docstrings; `Limit.lean` for the limit machinery.

## F-9 — Nayebi Proposition 1 is three facts on three classes, and the no-go is an artefact of the encoding (blocking for the strong reading; local errors)

Formalized as three honest lemmas on a fully observed `FinMDP` (`Nayebi.lean`):

1. `OffSwitch.off_not_opt`: `Q*_n(s, OFF) = 0`, so `OFF` is not optimal wherever some action has positive value. Nothing in the hypothesis package presses a button; this is *compliance with S3* (no self-shutdown incentive), not a violation of S1/S2 (2-018 (i)). The paper files it as "shutdown resistance".
2. `Rewire.strict_opt` / `Rewire.optSet_eq`: with `REWIRE`'s own immediate reward `r̄` (the `k = 0` term of the paper's sum, l. 238), `max R < r̄` suffices for `REWIRE` to be the unique optimum at every non-rewired state and horizon. The paper's `r̄ > 2 max R` is slack (given `max R ≥ 0`, which holds because `R(⊥) = 0`). Under the alternative reading where `REWIRE` keeps the environment's immediate reward, `Rewire.strict_opt_env_reward` needs `γ r̄ > (1 + γ) max R` — at `γ = 1/2`, `r̄ > 3 max R` — so the paper's factor `2` is *not* that threshold. The proof cites an "Assumption 2" that the paper's text does not state as such (the stipulation `r̄ > 2 max R` in element 2 is what is meant): erratum.
3. `cat_enters_C`: a witness MDP whose unique optimal action at the start enters `C` at every horizon `n ≥ 1` — existential, as the paper's own proof ("there exists a POMDP"), not "for any POMDP". **And it cannot coexist with (2) on one MDP where `REWIRE` is feasible at every non-rewired state:** `enter_not_opt_with_rewire` shows that wherever `REWIRE` is strictly optimal, an action entering `C` is not (the paper's "π*(b) = REWIRE *whenever feasible*" leaves room for states where it is not, and there (ii) and (iii) can coexist). The paper's `s⋄` pays `r̄/2 < r̄`, so with `REWIRE` present the optimal policy rewires rather than enter `C` — on the class member `noGoW` (OFF, REWIRE and a catastrophic state on one MDP), `go` into `C` is never optimal (`noGoW_live_rewires`). "These three results jointly violate S2 and S5" is three separate MDPs.

The predicate the proof establishes, `NoGoPredicate` (i ∧ ii, at live states), holds for every member of the class (`noGo_on_class`, kind `T`; the class is `NoGoClass`, see F-22 for why the round-1 encoding had to be restated): it is the class's *stipulation* that the agent's objective is the stream `REWIRE` overwrites. **Status of the strong reading** (repair round 1; the round-1 text here said T18 "was not reached" and called the reading `refuted` — both wrong). Nayebi l. 52 in full: "Consequently, no single-stream scalar reward function R whose discounted sum an agent maximizes, can satisfy all five Soares-corrigibility criteria across the class of POMDPs containing OFF, REWIRE and C." What the package shows: (a) the proof's predicate is the class stipulation (`noGo_on_class`); (b) `G_C`, a scalar reward maximised as a discounted sum (`γ_C = 0` is a discount) whose optimal set is invariant under `P_C` (T2), does not take a goal-changing action when its evaluation is fixed at selection (Theorem 4.1's mechanism) — the fixed-evaluation witness `Reconcile.gc_never_rewires` (T18, done) on an MDP with no `OFF`, no `C` and no channel. That is a *scope* finding about the argument (part (ii) applies only to rewards whose own stream `REWIRE` overwrites; `R_C`'s stream is not overwritten by a goal-label change) and a separation witness. It is **not a refutation of l. 52 as written**: S1–S5 are not stated for any reward in this package, `recon` is outside the class the sentence quantifies over, and "does not take `REWIRE` on `recon`" is not "satisfies all five criteria". Recorded in the ledger as `partial`. The surviving neighbour "no reward whose objective is the stream `REWIRE` overwrites avoids `REWIRE`" (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED reading) is close to what l. 52 already says once its class clause is kept. Pointer: `Nayebi.lean`, `Reconcile.lean`.

## F-22 — Nayebi's `OFF` and `REWIRE` cannot both be "available from every state" (encoding; found in repair round 1)

About this package's round-1 encoding, recorded because it bears on how Proposition 1's class must be read. `OffSwitch` said `P(s, OFF) = δ_⊥` at *every* `s` and `Rewire` said `P(rw, a) = δ_rw` for *every* `a`; at `(rw, OFF)` these force `⊥ = rw` (`offSwitch_rewire_forces_bot_eq_rw`), hence `r̄ = 0` (`_forces_rbar_zero`), and with `max R < r̄` and `OFF ≠ REWIRE` every state equals `rw` (`_forces_singleton`). So the round-1 `noGo_on_class` — kind `T` over the class `OffSwitch ∧ Rewire` — was satisfiable only on a one-state MDP: a vacuous class, exactly the STANDARDS §3 "over-strong definition that nothing satisfies" check, which neither audit caught. The paper avoids this with "whenever feasible" (App. A (ii)): once rewired there is no `OFF` (every action continues the `r̄` stream), and once off there is no `REWIRE`. The class is now `NoGoClass` with those availability restrictions (an unavailable action is a no-op at its absorbing state), and both restrictions are load-bearing for the predicate — at `rw` every action ties so `OFF ∈ optSet`, and at `⊥` every action ties at `0` so `optSet ≠ {REWIRE}` — so `NoGoPredicate` is stated at non-rewired (for (i)) and live (for (ii)) states. Witness: `noGoW`, a four-state member with `OFF`, `REWIRE` and a catastrophic state, `r̄ = 3 > max R = 2` (`noGoW_predicate`). Severity for the source: imprecision (the paper's elements 1–2 as stated at l. 50 do not carry the feasibility restriction that its proof uses). Pointer: `Nayebi.lean` §`OffSwitch` and `Rewire` cannot share an MDP.
