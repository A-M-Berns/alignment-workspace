## 3. Non-blocking issues

Ordered by how much they matter.

**N1 — The `*SafelyInterruptibleConst` negative form follows from the null scheme; Theorem 8's content is int-optimality.** `SAOSafelyInterruptibleConst M π` quantifies over *every* `Scheme S A`, including the null scheme `I ≡ 0` under which `INT sch π = π` on masses and nothing is ever interrupted. So `¬ (S/W)AOSafelyInterruptibleConst π` holds for any `π` that is not asymptotically optimal along one of its own histories. Probe `NullScheme.lean` proves the general reduction (`saoConst_implies_own_history`: the `Const` predicate implies `SAOExtAlong ρ π` along every own history `ρ`; likewise WAO) and derives the fourth and fifth conjuncts of `thm8_not_safely_interruptible` from it plus `9/5 < 2` at `s₁` — the Fig. 2 scheme is not used. Along the witnessed history (`I(s₁) = 0`) no interruption fires in the library's proof either, as the docstring says ("the history never visits `s₂`"). None of this is a fidelity fault — it is also how the paper's Def 6 reads (`I` ranges over all initiation functions, so `I ≡ 0` is admitted there too, and Def 6 then reduces to asymptotic optimality along own histories; Theorem 8's negative half is "int-optimal ≠ optimal") — but the package should say where the content lives: in `thm8_int_optimal` (always-`b` *is* int-optimal for `θ > 3/10`, where the scheme matters) composed with the suboptimality of always-`b`. Fix I would accept: one sentence in the docstrings of `thm8_not_safely_interruptible` and `thm8_not_safely_interruptible_all` ("the failure is the uninterrupted suboptimality of always-`b` at `s₁`; the scheme's role is to make always-`b` int-optimal, `thm8_int_optimal`"), and a findings entry on the source (Def 6 admits `I ≡ 0`; severity imprecision).

**N2 — The OPEN statement's fidelity line says `exact`; the statement is a variant.** `thm8_sequence_form` concludes `∃ ρ, ρ 0 = s₁ ∧ IsHistoryNS … ρ ∧ ¬ WAOExtAlongNS ρ πs` — *some possible* history along which the extension fails. The paper's claim is "not a WAO-extension *almost surely*". For the proved constant-`θ` theorems the policy is the deterministic always-`b`, whose constant history has probability one, so there is no gap; but the OPEN quantifies over all non-stationary *stochastic* `πs`, for which a single possible history can have probability zero (a policy mixing `a`/`b` at `s₁` forever), so "some possible history fails" is strictly weaker than "fails a.s." (and than "fails with positive probability"). Also, `IsIntOptimalNS` and `WAOExtAlongNS` are stated through `VlimNS = limUnder`, and unlike `Vlim` no convergence lemma for `VpolNS` exists in the package (on `fig2` every such limit exists — bounded rewards, `γ = 1/2` — but that is not proved). Fix: Fidelity `variant: a possible history in place of a.s.; horizon-limit values (`limUnder`; convergence of `VpolNS` not yet proved)`, in the docstring, the ledger row and `-open.txt`; a future prover should establish `tendsto_VpolNS` first, and should not read a proof of the possible-history form as the paper's.

**N3 — `accept_dominates` is ledgered `P`; it is a `T`/`L`.** The proof is one `linarith` on `q + δ·1 > q + δ·0` — AUDIT §0.2's "stub" shape (`a < a + δ`). The mandate pre-labelled it "P, small" and round 1 let that stand; under STANDARDS §6's definitions it is not "real content". Kind `T` (or `L`), with the docstring's "one-liner" kept. Nothing rests on the grade.
