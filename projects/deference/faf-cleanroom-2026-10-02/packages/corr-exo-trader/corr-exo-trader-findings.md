## F2 — The generalised LIC as displayed is false for unbudgeted traders (severity: local error in the conjecture's statement; the sketch's content survives in two weaker forms)

Line 95: "for every e.c. trader `T`, `Value_n(T) ≤ c_T + Loss_n(H)` in plausible-value terms … The original LIC is the case `H = 0` … I haven't checked this against the budgeter details." **Refuted at `H = 0`** by `genLic_coefficientOne_refuted` (`Undecided.lean`, kind P): over any inductor `P` over `DP` with a fresh atom `u` and a consistent world at every stage, the e.c. trader `buyDaily u` (one share of `u` daily) has plausible assessments unbounded above — in the `u`-worlds, plausible at every stage, its net worth is `∑_{i ≤ n} (1 − P_i(u))`, divergent because `P_∞(u) < 1` (non-dogmatism). So no constant `c_T` bounds its plausible value, although `Loss_n(0) = 0`. The criterion forbids *exploitation* — plausible assessments bounded below *and* unbounded above — not unbounded upside alone; a trader unbounded in both directions is permitted, and the display quantifies over its upside. What the sketch ("if `T_k` exploited, the budgeted supertrader eventually would too; since `TF_n + H_n` has no plausible upside each day, `H` must carry the corresponding loss") actually supports is: (i) the firm's bound with `c = 1` — T2.1 `exo_finiteHorizon_bound`; (ii) the *gated, budgeted* component's bound with a weight-dependent multiplicative constant — T2.2 `budgeted_value_le_loss`: `≤ (3 + Loss_n(H)) / w_{j,r+1}`; (iii) the exploitation form — T2.3 `noEcExploit_of_boundedLoss`. The corrected conjecture should read: *if `H`'s plausible loss is bounded, no e.c. trader exploits the exo-market* (proved), or, in plausible-value terms, *for every e.c. `T` and budget `b` there are `c, C` with `Value_n(Budgeter(T, b)) ≤ c + C · Loss_n(H)`* (proved with `c = 3/w`, `C = 1/w`). The counterexample is e.c. (`genLic_coefficientOne_refuted_ec` conjoins li-projection's `buyDaily_ec`), so the display *as quantified* is refuted.

**The charitable reading is open, not refuted** (repair round 1, audit r1 N5). The display's natural reading — *for `T` whose plausible values are bounded below*, `Value_n(T) ≤ c_T + Loss_n(H)` pointwise in `(n, v)` — is at `H = 0` exactly the LIC (bounded below ⟹ bounded above), is not touched by `buyDaily u` (which is unbounded below), and is quantitative where T2.3 is qualitative. It is stated as `genLic_boundedBelow_pointwise` (OPEN, `Open.lean`). It may be false: the budgeted route gives `c + Loss/w` with the enumeration weight `w < 1`, not coefficient one, and nothing in the sketch controls the coefficient.

**Scope of the exploitation form** (repair round 1, audit r1 N2/N4). T2.3's hypothesis — `H`'s plausible loss bounded uniformly over days and plausible worlds — fails for exactly the demand the thesis cares about: a persistent push that lands has unbounded plausible loss (`persistent_push_unbounded_loss`: `constBuyer φ c` with its own market price of `φ` at `≥ p + δ` from day `N` on loses `≥ c(p+δ)` a day in the `¬φ`-worlds forever). So T2.3 and T3.2 cover finitely-supported or self-financing demands (the N+ `pushOnce`) and are silent on T6/T10's persistent push; the source's upshot "`H` is constrained not at all … the humans push for free" is precisely the unbounded-loss regime, where what holds is T2.1 (`≤ 1 + Loss` with `Loss` growing) and T6.1.

## F4 — "Low prior mass makes the agent try harder" is ambiguous; false on the literal reading (severity: imprecision / local error on one reading)

Line 97: "low prior mass on a suspicious `Q'` just makes the agent try harder to bring it about." Two readings (audit r1 N5, adversarial). **Literal** — a *lower* level of `P_n(Q')` gives a *stronger* incentive — is false: in the identity a next-day state enters the incentive only through the *action-dependence* of its weight — `incentive_eq_weight_difference` gives `valueAlpha a₁ − valueAlpha a₂ = ∑_q (P_n(Q'_q ⋏ a₁) − P_n(Q'_q ⋏ a₂)) · Q'_q[u]`, and `no_incentive_of_action_independent` shows a state whose weight is the same under both actions contributes nothing, whatever its level; lowering a state's level uniformly shrinks its contribution. **Charitable** — low prior mass does *not remove* the incentive, and the agent acts to raise `P_n(Q' ∧ a)` — is true and is exactly what the identity supports: raising `P_n(Q' ⋏ a)` is what an action does, and that action-dependence is the incentive. "Doubt beforehand plus full acceptance afterwards" (line 97) is the right diagnosis of the mechanism; the sentence quoted is ambiguous between a claim about the level (false) and a claim about the mechanism surviving low prior mass (true).

## F5 — The exo-market is not a `ComputableMarket`; FAF's §4 theorems do not apply to it as typed (severity: imprecision in scope; the bridge is OPEN)

Line 95: "preservation should follow whenever `H_n` is a continuous, per-day-bounded strategy so the fixed point exists." Two things are being conflated. The fixed point's *existence* is true and immediate (T1.3: FAF's `fixed_point_lemma` quantifies over an arbitrary day strategy, and a `Strategy.join` is one; continuity and per-day boundedness are built into `EF`). *Inductor-hood* — `IsLogicalInductor (exoHistory DP H) DP` — is a different matter: the class bundles `ComputableMarket`, which an arbitrary `H` destroys and which even an e.c. `H` only restores through a compiler run (FAF's `LIACompiler` does it for the firm alone). Hence decision 3: every market-level statement here is over `NoEcExploit` (`IsLogicalInductor.noExploit` and nothing more), and FAF's `lic_*` property theorems — convergence, coherence, non-dogmatism, self-trust — **do not apply to the exo-market as typed**. The bridge `exoHistory_computableMarket_of_ec` is OPEN (T2.5), with `exo_isLogicalInductor_of_boundedLoss` and `exo_u_price_converges` (T7.2) resting on it. "Convergence still applies to `⌜u > 0.7⌝`" (line 115) is therefore available over an inductor (`u_price_converges_of_inductor`) but over the exo-market only modulo T2.5.

## F9 — Deviations from the mandate's signatures (severity: imprecision in the mandate; [STANDARDS](../../STANDARDS.md) over the mandate)
