## 1. Verdict

**blocking** — on one item. Repair round 3's refutation `LiaPackage.not_lia_small_coherent_mixture_exists` is kernel-clean and its three vacuity corollaries are true of the record definitions; what is wrong is how the package *characterizes* it. The ledger (Table 1d, Witness cell), FR-11, the ledger header and the report present it as "a counting fact about FAF's own `MarketMaker` and `TradingFirm`, not about an encoding of this package: nothing here is a stand-in", and as "the LIA lists polynomially many sentences a day". Neither holds. (a) The refutation turns on the record's **syntactic** small set (bli-found D3, `tokenSize ≤ 2^(2^n)`) standing in for the BLI paper's own definition of "small" at an LIA-constructed inductor — "the set of sentences bought or sold by any trader", i.e. the LIA's support — a substitution `bli-program` §2.1 made deliberately and recorded as the *rejected* reading B of bli-soto-a-002. The contradiction is exactly the LIA's default quote `0` on a small tautology it never listed, copied onto the superbelief by `E1x` and set against coherence's `1` (probe, claims 1–3, every `DP`). Under the paper's definition every small sentence is listed by definition, the counting gives nothing, and the package's satisfiability at the LIA returns to open — to the paper's own stated precondition ("Eisenstat requires propositional coherence … a constraint on which Logical Inductors can be converted"). So the impossibility is an artifact of the encoding in precisely [STANDARDS](../../STANDARDS.md) §3's sense, and the row's artifact check asserts the opposite. (b) The support bound of record, `∑_{j ≤ n} (j (n+1)^j + j + 1)`, is at least `n (n+1)^n` (probe, claim 5): superpolynomial, not polynomial; "polynomial against doubly exponential" misdescribes the inequality that does the work (`n (n+1)^n` against `2^(2^n − 2)`). Everything else I checked is what it says: the repair-round-3 plumbing is FAF's objects link by link, the local form `no_degenerate_linked_bli_lit` is strictly stronger than the conclusion of record and — this is the constructive half of B1 — is the one K3 instance at the LIA whose package means the same thing under both notions of small.

**What the source says "small" means.** Appendix B, `main.tex:427`: "For a given `𝕼_n`, we can distinguish 'small' propositions which the market assigns real prices to (if the logical inductor was constructed by the Logical Induction Algorithm, this is the set of sentences bought or sold by any trader). We can call the rest of the sentences 'large'. The only real constraint on the way we define small and large is that if we assign new prices to large sentences … in any way we like, it does not jeopardize the Logical Induction Criterion." Constraint 1 (`:435`) is then "`ℙ_n(φ) := D_n(𝕼_n(φ))` for `φ` which are small on day `n`". At FAF's LIA the market "assigns real prices" exactly to the listed keys of `liaStates DP n` — FAF's `RationalBeliefState` docstring (`MarketMaker.lean:670`): "`support` is … the set of listed keys", and `quote_eq_zero_of_not_mem` makes every other sentence's `0` a default, not a price. At the constructed inductor — which is where every K3 instance of this package lives — it is not circular: the support is a finite set FAF defines. bli-found's `sizeBound` docstring says the same thing from the other side: "any computable superpolynomial bound that **dominates the trading firm's budget** would do" (program §2.1 F2: `support (firm.strat n) ⊆ smallSet n` eventually). The record's small set was *designed* to be a strict superset of what the LIA prices.

**What the refutation actually uses** (probe `audit-r4-probes/SupportVsSmall.lean`, every `DP`, sorry-free):
