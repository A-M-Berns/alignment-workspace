## F1 — Thorstad's Monotonicity restatement of Theorem 1 is false (local error, Thorstad 2026)

Thorstad §4.1 l. 142 replaces Better Chances by the weak one-directional Monotonicity (`X ≽ Y → p > q → pX+(1−p)Y ≽ qX+(1−q)Y`) and at l. 146 asserts Theorem 1 unchanged. Refuted in Lean: `SIS.thorstad_monotonicity_restatement_refuted` (`SISWitnesses.lean`) exhibits a complete, transitive relation satisfying IABM, IBIL and Monotonicity, with `U a ≻ P a` for every `a`, in which Prevent and Leave are indifferent. The counter-model ranks lotteries by whether their unpressed mass is positive; both `act Prevent` (unpressed mass `1 − f`) and `act Leave` (`1 − g`) have positive unpressed mass because `g < h ≤ 1`. Mandate-writer claims checked: the refutation holds; `f = 0` does not rescue it (`thorstad_refuted_f_zero`); the mandate's three-level class function was not needed (two levels suffice for every admissible `(f, g, h)`). Surviving neighbour: Thornley's theorem under Strict Monotonicity (`SIS.first_theorem_averse`), which Better Chances implies (`Weak.strictMonotone_of_betterChances`) and Monotonicity does not. Thorstad's §4.2 objection (conditional vs unconditional preference) does not use the restatement, so the error is local.

## F2 — DReST Theorem 5.1's neutrality clause and Lemma D.2 are false as stated (blocking for the theorem, not for the experiments)

DReST Thm D.1 (l. 356) / Thm 5.1 (l. 106): "if π maximizes expected return in E according to our DReST reward function, then π is maximally useful and maximally neutral", for meta-episodes of more than one mini-episode. Lemma D.2 (l. 386): "Equalizing probabilities increases expected return." In the `(p, ρ)` model with the expected return computed by the closed form `F n lam mu p ρ = ∑_{i<n} mu^i ∑_l p l ρ l (1 − (1−lam) p l)^i` (i.i.d. lengths, `mu = lam^{−1/k}`), at `lam = 1/4`, `k = 2`, `mu = 2`, `ρ ≡ 1` and `n = 8`: `F(1/4, 3/4) − F(1/2, 1/2) = 13191189/4194304 > 0` (`Drest.lemma_D2_false`, exact `norm_num`; at `n = 7` the gap is `36351/262144`, `lemma_D2_false_seven`), so Lemma D.2 fails with `(π, π') = ((1/4, 3/4), (1/2, 1/2))`, and no maximiser of `F 8` on the compact simplex is uniform (`Drest.theorem_5_1_neutrality_false`, which also proves a maximiser exists). The mechanism: the per-round rescaling `lam^{−(i−1)/k}` grows geometrically, so late mini-episodes dominate and a skewed distribution that keeps one length "fresh" wins. The usefulness clause stands (`Drest.usefulness_eq_one_of_isMaxOn`). The paper's eq. (1) treats `N_{e_i}(l)` as a number; the derivation needed is the expectation over `N_{e_i}(l) ~ Bin(i−1, p l)`, which is what `F` encodes — **the derivation from the enumerated meta-episode is now in Lean** (repair round 1: `DrestDerivation.F_eq_metaReturn`, for every `n` and every `p` on the simplex), so the refutation holds for DReST's own Def D.2 enumerated over all length sequences (`Drest.lemma_D2_false_enumerated`, `theorem_5_1_neutrality_false_enumerated`), and the joint-`(p, ρ)` quantifier of the theorem is matched by `theorem_5_1_neutrality_false_joint`. The remaining `(c)` is the `(p, ρ)` model itself. The experiments (clipped rewards at 5, `|E| = 64`, `lam = 0.9`) are not touched by this. Surviving neighbour: with the rescaling dropped, uniform is optimal for every `n` (`Drest.repaired_return_le_uniform`, Jensen on a concave `φ`; strictness for `n ≥ 2` not proved).

## F3 — Neutrality is ILPACS specialised; ILPACS is strictly stronger than the 2024 TD Principle (imprecision, 2025 §7 and item 2-010)

`neutrality_of_posl_ilpacs` (`Post.lean`) is ILPACS applied to the length decomposition `X = ∑_{l ∈ lengths X} mass_l • condLen X l` (`Lottery.p_eq_sum_condLen`), with the single-length case separate — a specialisation, not a squeeze. Conversely the 2024 Timestep Dominance relation, taken as the agent's strict preference, satisfies POSL and Neutrality (`posl_TD`, `neutrality_TD`) but **violates ILPACS** (`not_ilpacs_TD`: `X₁ = ½[1]+½[0,0]`, `X₂ = ½[0]+½[1,1]`, `Y₂ = ½[0]+½[1,½]`, `p = (0.9, 0.1)`, `q = (0.1, 0.9)`; `X` and `Y` have conditional sums `(0.9, 0.2)` versus `(0.1, 1.35)`). So the 2025 supersession did not merely "ordinalise" the 2024 principle: ILPACS quantifies over *all* decompositions into pairwise-incomparable parts, not only the length decomposition, and rules out the 2024 agent. "Nothing was retracted" (2-010) is true; "the proposal was ordinalised" undersells. **Added in repair round 1**: POSL ∧ ILPACS is not merely stronger than POSL ∧ Neutrality — it is *inconsistent* with the 2024 agent's own within-length preferences (any asymmetric relation ranking single-length lotteries by expected sum-total), see F7; so ILPACS is not a live alternative to TD for such agents, and the antecedent of `neutrality_of_posl_ilpacs` is empty for them.

## F7 — POSL ∧ ILPACS is inconsistent with expected-sum-total within-length preferences (blocking for Thornley 2025 §7's chain as applied to any such agent; the theorem is true, its antecedent is empty)

Found by the round-1 adversarial auditor (probe promoted to `PostConsistency.lean`). `PostConsistency.posl_ilpacs_two_cycle`: if `lt` satisfies POSL (2025 §4) and ILPACS (2025 §6 l. 175, as displayed), is irreflexive, and ranks single-length-`1` lotteries by expected sum-total (2024 fn `t08gkcadpjj`: "the agent prefers same-length sublotteries in line with their expected sum-total utilities"; the money-making POST-agent of 2025 App. 1 at the trajectory level), then for `D = ½[0] + ½[1,1]`, `X = 0.1[10] + 0.9D`, `Y = 0.9[9] + 0.1D` both `X ≻ Y` and `Y ≻ X` hold; with asymmetry, `False` (`posl_ilpacs_inconsistent`). Both ILPACS instances are the paper's own moves: the decomposition into `[10]` and the part-shared `D` (POSL makes them lack a preference, `[10] ≻ [9]`) gives `X ≻ Y`; the *length* decompositions `Y = (19/20)Y₁ + (1/20)[1,1]`, `X = (11/20)X₁ + (9/20)[1,1]` — the decomposition 2025 §7 uses to derive Neutrality — give `Y ≻ X` because the reweighting flips the length-1 conditionals (`Y₁ = (18/19)[9] + (1/19)[0]` has expected sum-total `162/19`, `X₁ = (2/11)[10] + (9/11)[0]` has `20/11`). Mechanism: POSL forces part-shared lotteries to lack a preference; ILPACS then licenses *arbitrary* reweighting between such components; the reweighting moves mass at a shared length in opposite directions. Consequence: `neutrality_of_posl_ilpacs` (POSL ∧ ILPACS ⇒ Neutrality) is correct but says nothing about any agent with EU-like within-length preferences — the 2025 chain POSL → ILPACS → Neutrality → never-resist has an empty antecedent for the very agents the papers describe. Not an encoding artifact: `ILPACS` and `POSL` are the displays verbatim, and the only exhibited model of POSL ∧ ILPACS is the empty relation (`posl_ilpacs_trivial_model`). **Open** (`posl_ilpacs_nontrivial_model_open`, listed): whether *any* asymmetric relation with a strict preference satisfies POSL ∧ ILPACS; what is known is in the report (§ Repair round 1) — a model, if one exists, must be ordinal within lengths (every cardinal potential tried is defeated by a three-part decomposition with decoupled weights). A related imprecision in the display (`ilpacs_lt_mix_of_lt`): since a lottery lacks a preference with itself, ILPACS admits the repeated decomposition `A = ½A + ½A` and yields `A ≻ qB + (1−q)A` for all `q ∈ (0,1)` whenever `A ≻ B` — a betweenness property the text does not state; benign, but the display quantifies over more decompositions than "sublotteries" suggests.

## F4 — NRATDR/ReSIC are environment claims; the never-resist theorems are kind S; the content is the cost model (imprecision, 2024 §11, 2025 §8)

`never_resist_TD` and `not_maximal_of_resists` are one-line: the hypothesis (NRATDR, ReSIC) already contains the dominance the conclusion needs. Thornley's own justification of NRATDR (2024 l. 351: resisting costs resources and only shifts mass between shutdown times) is made a theorem in `CostModel.lean`: for options `(c, w)` (cost `c ≥ 0` at timestep 1, positive reweighting `w` of the length classes), the `c = 0` option timestep-dominates every `c > 0` option whatever the reweightings (`TD_cost`), so NRATDR holds in every such situation with a free option (`nratdr_costModel`) and the chain is grade (a) inside the model (`never_resist_costModel`); the model itself is the `(c)`. Byproduct caveat (fn `776pi0t3l93`): `byproduct_witness` — a free option that shifts mass toward longer lengths still dominates; nothing says the button stays pressable (presentation).

## F5 — IBIL is a schema over sublotteries; `IBILsub` is the reading of record (imprecision, 2023 §6; ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED)

Thornley 2023 §6 states IBIL as "indifferent between lotteries that differ only insofar as probability mass is shifted between indifferent sublotteries", illustrated by `0.1X+0.4Y+0.5Z ~ 0.3X+0.2Y+0.5Z`. The definition of record is the substitution form `Weak.IBILsub`: `indiff X Y → indiff (pX+(1−p)Z) (pY+(1−p)Z)`. From it (with Transitivity) the paper's example (`Weak.indiff_mix_mix_of_indiff`) and the pointwise lift Lemma 1 needs (`Weak.indiff_map_of_pointwise`) are derived — the lift needs no injectivity of the relabelling. That `IBILsub` is the intended reading is ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED (item corr-refs-048). The 2025 §12 IBIL is a different predicate (an iff, restricted to lotteries sharing a length distribution); it is not yet defined (Target 16 not done).
