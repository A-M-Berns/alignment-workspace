## 3. Non-blocking issues

**N1. F14 and the two MM 3.4 refutation rows omit the reading under which the printed theorem is true — and it is the one the name "stochastic choice" suggests.** The refutations are correct under the literal reading (a deterministic `B_ω` that may vary within a type cell; App. D's stochastic-choice postulate presupposes exactly that, `no_typeMeasurable_stochasticChoice_G2`), and the surviving neighbour the package proves (`typeMeasurable_valuesB_iff_strictAgree`) drops stochastic choice because deterministic type-measurable behaviours cannot satisfy it on any frame with an agent tie. But there is a second neighbour that keeps stochastic choice meaningful: read `B` as a **type-measurable lottery** over the agent-optimal acts (for each type and menu a distribution `β_τ(𝒜)` on `argmax`), stochastic choice as "every tied act gets positive weight", and "values" as the expectation over the lottery. Under that reading Theorem 3.4 holds **as printed, in both directions**, by MM's own splice argument (my derivation, not in Lean): (⟹) if strict agreement fails at `(a, b, ω)` then on `{a, b⋆}` with `b⋆ := splice [ω] b a` the cell's lottery puts weight `p > 0` on `b⋆` (all of it if the agent strictly prefers `b`, some of it by stochastic choice if it ties), off the cell every choice agrees with `a` pointwise, so delegation is worth `E_π u(a) − p·(cellEU(a) − cellEU(b)) < E_π u(a)`; (⟸) on each cell every act in the support is agent-optimal, hence cell-`π`-optimal by the contrapositive of strict agreement, and the delegated value is a mixture of cell-optimal values. The refutations work precisely because the printed postulate realises the randomisation *through the state* (`ω'` of the same type), which lets the tie-break correlate with the payoff-relevant world; on `G2` with `π2` a world-independent fair coin on `{tf, ft}` is worth `½ < ⅔`, consistent with the printed theorem. Fix I'd accept: a paragraph in F14 and a clause in the "surviving neighbour" cells of the `mm34_forward_refuted`/`mm34_backward_refuted` rows naming this reading (ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED) as the one on which the printed statement survives with stochastic choice intact; stating the two directions as OPEN (or proving them — the proofs are the existing ones with a weighted sum over the argmax) would give `corr-legit-general` the strongest true form. The severity line of F14 ("blocking for the theorem as stated") stays accurate for the printed formal statement.
