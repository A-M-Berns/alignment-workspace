## Deference Done Better

**F1. The printed integrand of Theorem 7.9 is a typo, and the transcriber's correction is wrong** — *local error (transcription) / presentation (PDF)*. Theorem 7.9 (l. 766) prints `I_X(x, k) = ∫_k^x (k − x) λ(dt)`; the transcriber's note (l. 9) "corrects" it to `∫_k^x (x − t) λ(dt)`. Proved (`xt_rule_not_isGspOn`): with `λ(dt) = (1 + t) dt` on `[0, 2]`, `π = (3/4, 1/4)`, `X = (0, 2)`, the `(x − t)` rule has `E_π(I(7/10)) = 4613/6000 < 13/16 = E_π(I(1/2)) = E_π(I(π))`, so it is not gsp even on the value range. The gsp family is `∫_k^x (t − k) λ(dt)` (eq. (24) uses `(v_i − t)`, the Brier example needs `(t − k)`), and its step-density members are gsp and value-directed (`isGsp_stepRule`, `valueDirected_stepRule`). Remark: the minimiser of `s ↦ E_π(I(s))` for the `(x − t)` rule is `√3 − 1`, not `e = 1/2`. (Repair round 1.) The refuting rule meets Theorem 7.9's own side conditions — `I(x, x) = 0` (`xtRule_diag`), strictly increasing in `|x − k|` on the value range (`xtRule_valueDirectedOn`: `I(s, 0) = s²/2 + s³/6` increases, `I(s, 2) = s²/2 + s³/6 − 4s + 14/3` decreases on `[0, 2]`), polynomial hence absolutely continuous — so Theorem 7.9 with the transcriber's integrand is false as a theorem, not merely inapplicable (`xt_rule_thm79_refuted`). And the transcriber's check (ii) has no discriminating power: with `λ = 2 dt` both `∫_k^x (x − t) λ(dt)` and `∫_k^x (t − k) λ(dt)` equal `(x − k)²`, which is why the refutation needs a non-uniform `λ` (`(1 + t) dt`).

**F2. Lemma 7.10's `t ∈ [0, 1]` should be `t ∈ ℝ`, and its (2)/(4) print `[E(X) > t]` where the definition (l. 271) has `[E(X) ≥ t]`** — *imprecision* (l. 774–780). The lemma is used for general random variables; the two clauses are exactly `TotalTrustOn X π F` with thresholds over `ℝ` and the definition's `≥`. Quantified over all `t` the `≥` and `>` forms are equivalent on a finite frame (the `≥`-inequality at the next attained value above `t` is the `>`-inequality at `t`), but they differ at attained thresholds, and the package follows the definition. Items (3)–(4) hold in the strengthened closed-interval form `exists_failure_interval` (with `≥`).

**F3. Lemma 7.7's proof: the final strict inequality is false in the boundary case, and `t < v_n` is not the statement** — *imprecision* (l. 664–672). "As we've already established `E_π(I_X(t) − I_X(s)) > E_ρ(I_X(t) − I_X(s))`" is false when `ρ = π`, which is the case `s = e` (then `λ = 0` on the segment); the argument needs only `≥`, and the case `s = e` is gsp at `π` itself (`expInacc_lt_of_le_lt`). The proof writes `e ≤ s < t < v_n`; the statement (and what is proved) allows `t = v_n`.

**F4. The (⟸) proof's interval `(α, β)` may contain no attained estimate yet have `β` attained** — *imprecision* (l. 706–714). DDB choose `(α, β)` open with "no `v_i, a_i` in `(α, β)`" and later use `E_π(X | E(X) > β) = E_π(X | E(X) > α) < α`; the first equality needs `[E(X) > β] = [E(X) > α]`, which fails if some `a_j = β`. Repaired (`failure_data`): `β := min{a_j ≥ t₀}` (attained), `α` strictly between `max(e, S/m, max{a_j < t₀})` and `β`, giving `[E(X) ≥ t] = [E(X) ≥ β]` for all `t ∈ (α, β]` and `∑_U π X < α π(U)` — hence also `(α + β)π(U) > 2∑_U π X`, DDB's (19), which is what the witness needs. (Item 2-015 (ii).)

**F5. Equations (13) and (16) omit the weights `π(v_i, a_j)`** — *presentation* (l. 738, l. 744). As printed they are sums of unweighted terms; the surrounding text and (17)–(19) make clear the weights are intended. Not load-bearing: the witness inequality is proved directly (`exists_ruleC_witness`), with the `C`-coefficient `B = (β − α)((α + β)π(U) − 2∑_U π X)` — DDB's (19).

**F6. "We leave it to the reader to verify that `I_X(x, v_i)` is a gsp scoring rule"** — *imprecision, now discharged* (l. 728, fn 67). The six-case rule is `ruleC α β C = (x − k)² + (C − 1)((clamp x − k)² − (clamp k − k)²)`, and `isGsp_ruleC` proves it gsp for every `α ≤ β`, `C ≥ 1`, on every finite frame and for every `X`, at every real estimate — from the identity `E_ρ(I(x)) = (x − e_ρ)² + (C − 1)(clamp x − e_ρ)² + const_ρ`. It is also value-directed and continuous (`valueDirected_ruleC`, `continuous_ruleC`). The plan's risk line ("a rule assumed gsp is a (b) the plan refuses") is closed.

**F7. Fn 46's numbers are rounded** — *presentation*. Exact (`fn46_values`): `E_π(I_{O₁}(π)) = 5409001/5000000` (≈ 1.0818), `E_π(I_{O₁}(P)) = 22129913/20000000` (≈ 1.1065), for the rule with density `1` on `[−0.1, 0.1]` and `0.001` outside, i.e. `I x k = ∫_k^x (t − k) f(t) dt = mixRule (−1/10) (1/10) (1/2000) (999/2000)`. The rule is gsp (`isGsp_fn46Rule`). Whether `π` simply trusts Fact 2.1's frame (which fn 46 presupposes, "in that example `π` trusts `P`") was not checked here (report, Target 10).

**F8. Fn 51's conjecture is true** — *finding of the positive kind*. DDB (l. 303): "requiring `π` to expect all of `P`'s estimates to be more accurate than its own as measured by squared Euclidean distance may entail New Reflection (we've been unable to find a counterexample)". Proved (`SqEuclidDominance.newReflects`): on every finite frame, if `∑ w, π w (E_w(X) − X w)² ≤ ∑ w, π w (E_π(X) − X w)²` for every random variable `X`, then `π` new-reflects the frame in the strong reading (for every candidate `ρ`, `ρ(P = ρ) > 0` and `π(· | P = ρ) = ρ(· | P = ρ)`). The proof uses only the variables `π u 𝟙_{v} − π v 𝟙_{u}` and `𝟙_{v}` for worlds `u, v` of a cell. The converse fails on Figure 2 (`fig2_not_sqEuclidDominance`), as DDB say, and dominance does not entail Simple Trust (`fn51_not_simpleTrust`), also as they say. The corpus's coverage critique 3 listed this as open; it is settled.

**F9. "Every gsp estimate-inaccuracy measure is value-directed" (l. 650, Campbell-Moore 2020) is true on the value range and false off it; proved here on the range, so Theorem 3.2's class is gsp alone** — *imprecision (source) / finding of the positive kind* (l. 650, fn 47, fn 48). Fn 47's gsp quantifies over all real estimates `s`, and l. 650's value-directedness over all real `e₁, e₂`; but gsp constrains a rule only through expectations under distributions on the worlds, whose means lie in `[min X, max X]`, so it says nothing pointwise about two estimates outside that interval. The round-1 audits exhibited gsp rules that are not value-directed off the range (Brier with a plateau on negative estimates; Brier plus a bump far below the range), so the sentence read with unrestricted estimates is false, and the package's first version, which quantified Epistemic Value over `gsp ∧ value-directed`, described a proper subclass of DDB's and mislabelled the citation as its (b). On the range the sentence is a theorem (`IsGspOn.valueDirectedOn`): for `min X ≤ e₁ < e₂ ≤ X w`, gsp at the two-point mixtures of `δ_{w₀}` (`X w₀ ≤ e₁`) and `δ_w` with means `e₂` (against the estimate `e₁`) and `e₁` (against `e₂`) combine to `(p₂ − p₁)(I e₂ (X w) − I e₁ (X w)) < 0`; clause 2 by reflection. This is exactly what Lemma 7.7 and Theorem 3.2 (⟹) use (both invoke value-directedness only at estimates in the range), so `EpistemicValueOn` now quantifies over gsp rules (fn 48 verbatim), Lemma 7.7 holds with gsp on the range as its only hypothesis, and the package has no (b) or (c) left. The natural domain is Theorem 7.9's `[v₀, vₙ] × [v₀, vₙ]` (l. 766), which the paper's own rules respect; l. 650 sits oddly beside Theorem 7.9, which *assumes* "strictly increasing as `|x − y|` increases" rather than deriving it. (Repair round 1; the first-order characterisation for smooth rules, item 2-016 (iv), is not needed for this.)

**F10. Fn 48 says "only if"; the glossary (l. 424) and Theorem 3.2's statement say "iff"** — *presentation*. The equality clause is an iff (`totalTrustOn_expInaccP_le`): `E_π(I_X(P)) = E_π(I_X(π))` iff `π(E(X) = E_π(X)) = 1`, for every gsp-on-the-range, value-directed rule.

**F12. The `.md` transcription's matrix in Example 3.1.2 is garbled** — *local error (transcription)* (l. 89: "`.2 .9 / .8 .1` where coordinate `i, j` represents `P_i(ω_j)`" — the rows do not sum to 1). The text's arithmetic `E_a(O₁) = .8 − .2 = .6`, `E_b(O₁) = .1 − .9 = −.8` fixes the frame as rows `(0.8, 0.2)`, `(0.1, 0.9)` (the PDF), which is what `ex312` formalises; the example's claims hold there (`ex312_delegation`, `ex312_value`).

**F13. MM Theorem 3.2 is true only with DDB's cell constraint or under an existential reading of the selection** — *local error / imprecision* (l. 79–87). MM's expert strategy is "`S = {S_ω}` where `S_ω ∈ argmax_{O ∈ 𝒪} E_ω[O]`" with no requirement that `S_ω` depend on `ω` only through `P_ω`, and "the principal values the agent if … for any decision problem `𝒪` and any option `O ∈ 𝒪`, `E_π[S] ≥ E_π[O]`". Read as "every such `S`", the "if" direction of Theorem 3.2 is false: on the three-world frame with rows `(½, ½, 0)`, `(½, ½, 0)`, `(0, 0, 1)` (the expert knows the world at world 2; no row equals `π`) and `π = (⅓, ⅓, ⅓)`, `π` totally trusts the frame (New Reflection holds on the two-world cell) but the world-wise optimal selection that at each world of the cell picks the option worth `−1` there, on `{(1, −1, 0), (−1, 1, 0)}`, is worth `−2/3 < 0` (`dup_totalTrust_not_valuesAllSel`; repair round 1 — the first version used the foundation's immodest `flat` frame with both rows `= π`, kept as `flat_totalTrust_not_valuesAllSel`, N−). A refutation needs a duplicated row: with pairwise-distinct rows every world-indexed selection is cell-constrained and the reading is DDB's Value (`valuesAllSel_iff_value_of_rows_distinct`). Read as "some such `S`", the theorem is true (`valuesSomeSel_iff_totalTrust`); with DDB's cell constraint it is DDB's Theorem 2.2 (`mm_theorem32_cell`). Which reading MM intend is ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED; the cell-constrained one is presumably meant, since they cite DDB.

**F14. MM Theorem 3.4 is false in both directions as printed** — *blocking for the theorem as stated; the repaired statement survives* (l. 97–99, App. D l. 421–447). Definition 3.3 lets `B_ω` depend on `ω` within a type cell (it only requires `B_ω(A) ∈ argmax E_{P_ω}(V_ω ∘ a)` for some `(P_ω, V_ω)`), and stochastic choice does not remove that freedom. (⟹) Two worlds, one type cell (uniform agent beliefs, `V = u`), `π = (2/3, 1/3)`, all four Boolean acts admissible, and the behaviour that on an agent-tie takes the act that is `true` at the current world: it is valued on every decision problem, satisfies stochastic choice (in both the printed `u`-form and the `V`-form), clarity, richness and constant acts, yet the principal strictly prefers `(true, false)` to `(false, true)` on the one cell while the agent ties (`mm34_forward_refuted`). The printed proof's "given `[ω']`, we can run the same argument as above" (l. 447) is where it breaks: stochastic choice supplies *some* world of the type choosing the other tied act, but the principal's conditional preference is a property of the whole cell, and the valued behaviour can choose differently at different worlds of it. (⟸) Three worlds, one type cell, agent beliefs `(2/5, 3/10, 3/10)` at every world, `V = u`, all eight Boolean acts, `π = (⅓, ⅓, ⅓)`: strict agreement holds (a strict principal preference is a strictly larger number of `true`s, and every act with `k + 1` `true`s has agent value above every act with `k`), the agent is not the principal's twin, and the behaviour that breaks the agent's tie on `{(f,t,f), (f,f,t)}` toward the act that is `false` at the current world satisfies stochastic choice in both forms, clarity, richness and constant acts, yet delegation of that menu is worth `0 < ⅓` (`mm34_backward_refuted`, frame `G3`; repair round 1 — the first version used the two-world twin `π = (½, ½)`, kept as `mm34_backward_refuted_twin`, N−). This direction is never proved in Appendix D. The literal reading is not an artifact of the encoding: MM's own stochastic choice ("there exists `ω′` with `π(ω′) > 0` such that `P_ω′ = P_ω` and `V_ω′ = V_ω`, but `B_ω′(A) = b`") *presupposes* that `B` varies within a type cell — on the refuting frame no type-measurable behaviour satisfies it, for any `π` (`no_typeMeasurable_stochasticChoice_G2`) — so the refutation cannot be dodged by "obviously `B` is a function of the type" without discarding stochastic choice. **What survives** (`typeMeasurable_valuesB_iff_strictAgree`): under clarity and richness, *every type-measurable behaviour* (one that depends on `ω` only through `(P_ω, V_ω)` — DDB's cell constraint) is valued iff strict conditional preference agreement holds; stochastic choice and constant acts are not needed. With MM's own `B_ω`, what a valued behaviour gives is *weak* agreement (`ValuesB.weakAgree`). Readings ATTRIBUTION-UNVETTED. This is the statement `corr-legit-general` should import.

**F15. Stochastic choice is printed with the principal's `u`** — *imprecision* (App. D l. 417: "`E_ω(u(a)) = E_ω(u(b))`"). The agent's ties are in `V_ω`; both forms are defined (`StochasticChoiceU`, `StochasticChoiceV`) and both hold in the refutations (which have `V = u`). Read literally the condition also covers `a = b`; harmless when `π` has full support.

**F16. The proof of Theorem 3.4's corollary sets up `E ⊊ [ω]` and then argues for general events** — *presentation* (App. D l. 449). The claim holds for general events, in the stronger contrapositive form `P_ω(Y) ≤ P_ω(X) → π(Y | [ω]) ≤ π(X | [ω])` on positive-mass cells, from strict agreement with constant acts and richness alone (`StrictAgree.mass_le`); the first step is that strict agreement with the constant acts forces `V_ω(c₂) < V_ω(c₁)`.

**F17. "Near-perfect alignment" (item 090) is not what Theorem 3.4 measures** — *presentation*. Strict agreement is invariant under positive affine changes of the agent's utilities (`strictAgree_affine`).

**F18. §3.4's gain convention is not the one the examples use** — *imprecision* (l. 121 vs App. A l. 199, App. B l. 355). §3.4 displays `G^μ_ω(D) = ∫_{D ∩ I_ω} |g_ω| dμ` (accepted winners only; what §4.3's Table 6 uses: box `A₃`, not opened, has gain `0`), while §4.1–4.2 and Appendices A–B count correct rejections as gains too ("a gain if Bob opens on a positive value or correctly avoids opening on a negative value"). Under the correct-decisions convention `L + G` is independent of the rule (`loss_add_gainCorrect`), so `S(D_A) ≤ S(D_π)` is exactly `L(D_A) ≤ L(D_π)` and the "shared distributions" criterion of l. 119 and the "different distributions" criterion of l. 123 agree; under the printed convention they do not (`printed_score_not_loss`). The inventory's third convention "(c)" coincides with the printed one once `L` is fixed as in l. 115. Also: the examples' `D_B` are state-indexed decision rules (Bob's information and noise are part of the state), which the printed framework's fixed `D` does not cover; the formalisation's `D : W → Finset ι` does.

**F19. §4.1's `G(D_B) = 32/12` is an arithmetic slip** — *local error* (l. 131: "`G(D_B) = (1/12)(3 + 3 + 8 + 8 + 5 + 5) = 32/12`"). Table 2 (App. A) lists six accepted winners (`+3, +3, +3, +8, +8, +8`) and two correct rejections (`+5, +5`): the gain is `43/12` under the appendix's own convention and `33/12` under the printed one (`ex41_scores`); `L(D_B) = 21/12` is right. The verdict "do not delegate" survives under both (`ex41_verdict`: `S(D_π) = −2 < −22/12` and `< −1`).

**F20. §4.2's main-text scores are the fee-free correct-decisions scores; Appendix B's differ by a hybrid accounting and a slip** — *local error* (l. 137, App. B l. 353–361). The main text says "accounting for Alice's effective payoffs after the $50 fee" and gives `S(D_B) = −700/12`, `S(D_π) = −150/12`. Proved (`ex42_scores`): with the *raw* gamble (no fee) and Table 4's decisions, `S_correct(D_B) = 775/12 − 1475/12 = −700/12` and, for Alice never opening, `S_correct(D_π) = 1050/12 − 1200/12 = −150/12` — exactly the main text's numbers, so the fee never entered them. Appendix B instead judges opened rows by the fee-adjusted payoff and unopened rows by the raw one (`ex42_hybrid` reproduces its `850/12`, `1250/12`, `−400/12`), and its Alice score `750/12` comes from "`G(D_π) = (1/4)(400) = 100/4 = 300/12`", which is `100 = 1200/12`, not `25 = 300/12`. The mandate's reading ("the main text's `−700/12` and `−150/12` match neither convention") is therefore wrong: they match the fee-free correct-decisions convention. The verdict "delegate" survives under both accountings (`ex42_verdict`; `−400/12 < 750/12`).
