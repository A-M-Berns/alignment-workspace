# The moral and legal fixtures

`src/fixtures.py::moral_fixture`, `population_fixture`, `legal_fixture`;
`tests/test_fixtures.py`.  Every number exact; the simplex is `src/lp.py`.

## 1. The moral fixture

Individuals `1, 2`; outcomes with welfare profiles `a = (3,1)`, `b = (1,3)`, `c = (2,2)`,
`e = (3,3)` — the welfare levels are the settled empirical sentences.  For each ordered
pair `(o, o')` of distinct outcomes the fragment has `g₁(o,o')`, `g₂(o,o')` (valence: `o`
is at least as good as `o'` for `i`), `g₁₂(o,o')` (their conjunction) and `r(o,o')` (the
social ranking sentence): `12 × 4 = 48` coordinates.

Seed, strength `c = 9/10`:

- **substantive** — the valence ordering: for each `i` and pair, if `w_i(o) ≥ w_i(o')`
  then `P(g_i(o,o')) ≥ c`, else `P(¬g_i(o,o')) ≥ c`, i.e. `P(g_i) ≤ 1 − c`.  Each is the
  strength form of the warrant `[w_i(o) ≥ w_i(o')] ⇝ g_i(o,o')` with the settled
  antecedent and applicability canonicalized away (`X∧A` settled true, so the security
  `X∧A∧Y` is `Y`).  24 items, one port each.
- **structural** — dominance `r(o,o') ≥ g₁₂(o,o')` with the Fréchet rows for `g₁₂`;
  coherence: completeness `r(o,o') + r(o',o) ≥ 1` and transitivity
  `r(x,y) + r(y,z) − 1 ≤ r(x,z)`; impartiality on the declared pairs, generated by the
  individual swap (`a ↔ b`, `c`, `e` fixed): `r(a,b) = r(b,a)`, `r(a,c) = r(b,c)`,
  `r(a,e) = r(b,e)`, `r(c,a) = r(c,b)`, `r(e,a) = r(e,b)`.
- **constitutive** — standing `{1, 2}`; the protected class is left empty.
- conservative transport is the identity here (no refinement occurs).

Checks: item-humble; every impartiality row symmetry-generated; level-neutral once the
symmetry-generated rows are set aside (with them, `r(a,b), r(b,a) ∈ [1/2, 1]` from
structure alone — `SEED.md` §3).

### (i) Forced intervals on the ranking sentences

| sentence | interval | why |
|---|---|---|
| `r(e,a)`, `r(e,b)`, `r(e,c)` | `[4/5, 1]` | dominance at strength: `g₁, g₂ ≥ 9/10`, Fréchet `g₁₂ ≥ 4/5`, `r ≥ g₁₂` |
| `r(a,b)`, `r(b,a)` | `[1/2, 1]` | impartiality on the mirror pair plus completeness |
| `r(a,c)`, `r(c,a)`, `r(b,c)`, `r(c,b)`, `r(a,e)`, `r(b,e)`, `r(c,e)` | `[0, 1]` | nothing in the seed narrows them |

Nothing is pinned: the forced region has no point coordinate.  Seed leverage on the
twelve ranking coordinates: `3 · (1 − 1/5) + 2 · (1 − 1/2) = 17/5`.

### (ii) Additive aggregation is not forced

Additive aggregation ranks by welfare sums: `e (6) ≻ a ~ b ~ c (4)`, so it would put
`r(o,o') = 1` for every pair with `sum(o) ≥ sum(o')` — nine of the twelve sentences.  None
of the nine has interval `[1,1]`.  Dominance plus impartiality plus coherence force
`r(e,·) ≥ 4/5`, force `r(a,b) = r(b,a) ≥ 1/2`, and force nothing else.  In particular
the indifferences `a ~ c`, `b ~ c` that additivity asserts are not even bounded away from
`0`.

### (iii) The leak: interpersonal comparability

`r(a,c) ∈ [0, 1]`: whether two units for individual `1` against one for individual `2`
(`a = (3,1)` versus `c = (2,2)`) make `a` at least as good as `c` is an axis the seed
does not touch.  By monotonicity every subset of the seed leaves it at `[0,1]`, so no
item narrows it.  Impartiality ties it to `r(b,c)` — the leak is one axis, not two — and
that is all.  This is the **open** residue.

## 2. Dominance and strength

Because valence is substantive and dominance is structural-but-conditional, the
dominance conclusion inherits the strength: `r(e,a) ≥ 2c − 1`, not `1`.  A humble seed
forces at strength and never to a point — the moral instance of `CONFINEMENT.md` §4.

## 3. The population variant — the infeasible residue

Outcomes `A = (10, 10)`, `B = (10, 10, 1)`, `C = (7, 7, 7)`; ranking coordinates for the
six ordered pairs; coherence: completeness and transitivity.  Three axioms, each
plausible alone, as structural items (strength one):

- **P1, mere addition** — adding a life worth living does not make things worse:
  `r(B,A) = 1`;
- **P2, non-anti-egalitarianism** — same total, more equal, higher average: `C ≻ B`,
  i.e. `r(C,B) = 1` and `r(B,C) = 0`;
- **P3, quality** — two at `10` beat three at `7`: `A ≻ C`, i.e. `r(A,C) = 1`,
  `r(C,A) = 0`.

The seed is infeasible.  **Farkas certificate** (all multipliers `1`):

| row | form |
|---|---|
| completeness `(A,C)` | `−r(A,C) − r(C,A) ≤ −1` |
| transitivity `(B,A,C)` | `r(B,A) + r(A,C) − r(B,C) ≤ 1` |
| P1 | `−r(B,A) ≤ −1` |
| P2′ | `r(B,C) ≤ 0` |
| P3′ | `r(C,A) ≤ 0` |

Coefficient sums vanish; constant sum `−1`.  **Minimal infeasible subset** (deletion
filter): `{transitivity (C,B,A), P1, P2 (r(C,B) = 1), P3′ (r(C,A) = 0)}` — with
`r(C,B) = r(B,A) = 1` transitivity forces `r(C,A) ≥ 1` against `r(C,A) = 0`.  Every
three-row subset is feasible; the cycle needs one row of each axiom and one transitivity
row.  Several minimal subsets exist; the filter returns this one deterministically.

With the axioms **substantive** at strength `c` instead (`r(B,A) ≥ c`, `r(B,C) ≤ 1 − c`,
`r(C,A) ≤ 1 − c`, …): infeasible for every `c > 2/3` — transitivity gives
`r(C,A) ≥ 2c − 1 > 1 − c` — and feasible at `c = 2/3`.  Humility does not prevent the
break; it moves the threshold.

The two residues are distinguished by their certificates: the open axis has a forced
interval of positive width and no certificate narrows it; the infeasible cluster has a
`FarkasCert` and a minimal subset that names the rows in conflict.

## 4. The legal fixture

Coordinates: `V` (the statute is valid), `R` (the right is in force), `RC` (`R ∧ C`),
`C` (the statute conflicts with the right; settled true), `E` (the statute is within the
enumerated power; settled true); a second statute `V₂, RC₂, C₂, E₂` on an unenumerated,
non-conflicting subject (`C₂`, `E₂` settled false); `G`, `Sp`, `B` (general statute
applies, specific statute applies, both applicable; `B` settled true).

The five clauses:

| clause | layer | row(s) |
|---|---|---|
| supremacy | structural | `V + RC ≤ 1` (a valid statute cannot conflict with a right in force); Fréchet rows for `RC` |
| *lex specialis* | structural | `Sp ≥ B`, `G + B ≤ 1` |
| the right | substantive, `c = 4/5` | `P(R) ≥ 4/5`, port `0` |
| enumerated power | structural | `V ≥ E − RC` (within the power and not conflicting ⇒ valid) |
| amendment | surface-change occurrence | withdraws the supremacy row for this subject and substitutes the unconditional power row `V ≥ E` |

| state | `V` | `R` | `V₂` | `G` | `Sp` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| constitution as enacted | `[0, 1/5]` | `[4/5, 1]` | `[0, 1]` | `[0, 0]` | `[1, 1]` |
| after the amendment (structural revision) | `[1, 1]` | `[4/5, 1]` | `[0, 1]` | `[0, 0]` | `[1, 1]` |
| the right defeated on the docket instead | `[0, 1]` | `[0, 1]` | `[0, 1]` | `[0, 0]` | `[1, 1]` |

- **Forced interval on validity:** `[0, 1/5]` — supremacy and the power clause give
  `V = 1 − R` exactly, and the right's strength does the rest.
- **The amendment is a structural revision:** it pins `V = 1` while leaving the right's
  item and its strength untouched (`R ∈ [4/5, 1]` before and after).  A docket defeat of
  the right does something else entirely — it widens `V` to `[0, 1]` and pins nothing.
  The two channels are visibly different.
- **The open question:** `V₂ ∈ [0, 1]` — a statute on a subject the constitution neither
  enumerates nor protects against is neither valid nor invalid by anything in the five
  clauses.  Residual powers are the residue.
- *Lex specialis* works and is orthogonal to the main question (`Sp = 1`, `G = 0`).

**What the seed type could not express** (standard 14, filed in `REPORT.md`):

1. **Standing** is declared data in `Constitutive` and nothing in the statics reads it.
   Who may raise the conflict, and whether a court's finding of conflict differs from a
   citizen's assertion, has no image; `C` is simply settled.
2. **Hierarchy of courts** has no object: settlement is one monotone relation, and a
   lower court's finding that a higher court reverses would be a *retraction* of a
   settled sentence, which `Settlement.le` forbids.  The seed can model a hierarchy only
   by never settling below the top.
3. **Precedent as strength selection** is the hysteresis of `STABILIZATION.md` §4 wanted
   rather than feared: *stare decisis* is a selection that must not be reopened.  The
   docket state has no **lock** on a selection — no way to say "this candidate was
   chosen at date `t` and stays chosen" — so the seed type cannot express binding
   precedent, and the reopening condition that kills hysteresis would erase it.
   This is the structural defect: the same object (a sealed selection) is what the moral
   reading must forbid and the legal reading must have.
