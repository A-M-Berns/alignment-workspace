# The moral and legal fixtures, two reasoners each

`src/fixtures.py::moral_two`, `population_private`, `legal_two`; `tests/test_fixtures.py`.
Every number exact; intervals from the seed round's simplex through a warm-started
driver (`multi_interval`, cross-checked against `lp.interval` in `tests/test_measure.py`).
`Φ` and the uniform weights are declared in the fixture and were not adjusted.

## 1. Moral

Four outcomes `a = (3,1)`, `b = (1,3)`, `c = (2,2)`, `e = (3,3)`; for each ordered pair
the coordinates `g₁, g₂, g₁₂, r` as in the seed round: `48` coordinates.  Shared
closed layer: dominance with Fréchet rows, completeness, transitivity, impartiality
generated by the individual swap.  Valence warrants at strength `9/10`; their
antecedents (welfare comparisons) are settled along the chain **nothing → individual 1
→ both**, and a valence warrant is live exactly when its antecedent is settled true
(the applicability row `P(g) ≥ c·P(ant)` is vacuous while `ant` is unsettled, since
`ant` occurs in no other row, and specializes to `P(g) ≥ c` once settled true; the
fixture keeps the specialized rows).  Declared `Φ`: the twelve ranking sentences,
uniform weights.

Reasoner 1 adds the **prioritarian** item on `(a, c)`: the worse-off is better off in
`c`, so `P(¬r(a,c)) ≥ 4/5` and `P(r(c,a)) ≥ 4/5`.  Reasoner 2 adds the **sum** item:
equal totals, so `P(r(a,c)) ≥ 4/5` and `P(r(c,a)) ≥ 4/5`.

| chain step | `(Hull, Ovl, Disc)` | `Δ` |
|---|---|---|
| nothing settled | `(47/60, 3/5, 1/6)` | `{r(a,c), r(b,c)}` |
| individual 1 settled | `(47/60, 3/5, 1/6)` | same |
| both settled | `(7/12, 3/10, 1/6)` | same |
| reference, empty seeds, both settled | `(43/60, 43/60, 0)` | `∅` |

Hulls at the last step: `r(e,a), r(e,b), r(e,c) = [4/5, 1]` (dominance at strength,
both reasoners); `r(a,b) = r(b,a) = [1/2, 1]` (impartiality with completeness);
`r(c,a) = r(c,b) = [4/5, 1]` (both items agree that `c ≽ a`, impartiality carries it to
`b`); `r(a,c) = r(b,c) = [0, 1]` — reasoner 1 has `[0, 1/5]`, reasoner 2 `[4/5, 1]`.

**The aggregation disagreement ends as discord**, not width: on `r(a,c)` the gap is
`3/5`, and the certificate names exactly `{prioritarian: ¬r(a,c), sum: r(a,c)}` — the
two items, nothing structural.  Impartiality transmits the discord to `r(b,c)`, whose
own items are none.  Against the reference the two seeds bought `43/60 − 35/60 = 2/15` of
hull for `1/6` of discord.

**Sharing.**  Sharing reasoner 2's sum item into reasoner 1 refutes reasoner 1 (its
bundle holds `r(a,c) ≤ 1/5` and `r(a,c) ≥ 4/5`).  Sharing a compatible warrant
`P(r(a,b)) ≥ 7/10` narrows both: profile `(11/20, 17/60, 1/6)`, `r(a,b)`'s hull
`[7/10, 1]`, `Δ` unchanged — shared narrowing lowers `Hull` and touches neither discord.

## 2. Population, private learning

Population coordinates `r(B,A), r(A,C), r(C,B), r(B,C), r(C,A), r(A,B)` with
completeness and transitivity; strength `4/5`.  Reasoner 2 holds the quality axiom P3
(`r(A,C) ≥ 4/5`, `r(C,A) ≤ 1/5`); reasoner 1 holds nothing, then privately learns P1
(`r(B,A) ≥ 4/5`) and P2 (`r(C,B) ≥ 4/5`, `r(B,C) ≤ 1/5`).

| | `(Hull, Ovl, Disc)` | `Δ` |
|---|---|---|
| before | `(1, 11/15, 0)` | `∅` |
| after reasoner 1's private learning | `(1, 4/15, 1/3)` | `{r(A,C), r(C,A)}` |

Transitivity turns P1 and P2 into `r(C,A) ≥ 3/5` for reasoner 1, against reasoner 2's
`r(C,A) ≤ 1/5`: gap `2/5`, certificate items `{P1, P2′, P3′}`.  Sharing P1 and P2 into
reasoner 2 refutes it (the seed round's population cluster at `4/5 > 2/3`).  Discord
created by one-sided learning; removed here only by reasoner 2 giving up P3 or reasoner 1
giving up P1 or P2 — which is what the certificate lists.

## 3. Legal, two courts

The seed round's constitution (supremacy, power with the supremacy exception, Fréchet
rows for `R∧C`; the second statute's coordinates for the enumerated-power question).
`Φ = {V, V₂}`: validity of the conflicting statute, and validity of a statute on an
unenumerated non-conflicting subject — the scope of the enumerated power.  Uniform
weights.  Chain: second statute's `C₂, E₂` settled false throughout; then `C` settled
true; then `E` settled true.

Court 1 carries a **locked** selection of the right's strength, `P(R) ≥ 9/10`, as a
hypothesis (item 93's object).  Court 2 selects the strongest feasible candidate among
`{6/10, 7/10, 95/100}` and holds a presumption of validity `P(V) ≥ 3/10`.

| step | court 2 selects | `(Hull, Ovl, Disc)` | `V` hull | `V₂` hull |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `C, E` unsettled | `19/20` | `(1, 17/20, 0)` | `[0, 1]` | `[0, 1]` |
| `C` settled | `7/10` | `(13/20, 1/2, 1/2)` | `[0, 3/10]` | `[0, 1]` |
| `C, E` settled | `7/10` | `(13/20, 1/2, 1/2)` | `[0, 3/10]` | `[0, 1]` |

The hull is eventually constant.  Where the lock sits: court 1's `V ∈ [0, 1/10]`
against court 2's `V = 3/10` is **discord** with certificate items
`{locked right 9/10, presumption P(V) ≥ 3/10}` (and the supremacy and Fréchet rows as the
structural carriers).  The lock does not stop convergence; it fixes which side of the
discord court 1 is on, and the certificate names it.  `V₂` stays `[0, 1]` for both — the
scope question is open and contributes half the hull mass; it is not discord.

In the small fixture `lock` the same lock against a *shared* weak presumption is width,
not discord: profile `(3/20, 1/20, 0)` at both chain steps, court 1's `R ∈ [9/10, 19/20]`
inside the hull `[4/5, 19/20]`.  A lock is discord only against an opposing item.

Standing and hierarchy are not attempted, as instructed.
