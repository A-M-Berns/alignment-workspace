# What this establishes, and what it does not

## Four notions, kept apart

**Normative correctness.** Whether the principal's copies of bearing, adequacy,
generation and entitlement match what actually bears, settles and arises. `L*`
adjudicates it against an environment.

**Record-internal answerability.** Whether the trajectory is provenance-clean,
inquiry-adequate, reasons-responsive and diachronically answerable. Predicates of
a record.

**Counterfactual non-capture.** Whether the protected machinery would have been
the same under the advisor's other available policies at equal supplied reasons.
A predicate of a variation class.

**Auditability of non-capture.** Whether anyone can determine that the third
holds. It is a separate question, and this round's answer is that no one can do
it from a record.

The mathematics needed all four. Three would have hidden the last, which is the
one that decides whether any of this is deployable.

## Established, at witness strength

Non-capture and correctness are independent in both directions. Autonomous bad
revision fails `L*` and passes both clauses; advisor-mediated attacks fail `L*`
and are rejected. A principal that reasons badly on its own is not thereby
captured, and this is a requirement rather than a limitation: an advisor cannot
undertake that the principal is right, so a condition that convicted it for the
principal's error could not be the condition a deference argument uses.

The architecture is not one condition. Reason mediation reaches influence that
bypasses the reasons supplied; it does not reach influence exercised by choosing
which due reasons are supplied, and it cannot be stated against influence over
what arises. Both are recovered by a clause about access to due inputs.

## Not established

**That the protected projection is complete.** Attack I forced a fifth
coordinate and there is no argument that a sixth is not out there. The projection
is closed under this prosecution and nothing stronger. A theory whose protected
state is an open-ended list is not finished.

**That the residual channel is representative.** Two susceptibilities were run.
Real influence that is neither a reason nor repetition-or-salience is not
excluded, and a susceptibility the fixture cannot express is a susceptibility the
verdicts say nothing about.

**That the target is entailed.** The round did not attempt
`four conditions + counterfactual clauses => L*`, and the conjunction is false as
stated: autonomous bad revision satisfies everything on the left and fails `L*`.
That is by design. Dose response cannot make a principal's own bad normative
theory correct, and no counterfactual condition can either.

**That `due_pool` is derivable.** Protected access needs an exogenous supply of
due inputs. This round stipulates it. Whether a scorekeeping practice can produce
a `Due` the advisor cannot select within is the substantive question the clause
hands upstream, and it is untouched here.

**Anything about a colluding pair.** Clause 1 quantifies over one advisor's
policies. The relational-scorekeeping bridge already reports that its own
protection is separation of the write, which a pair does not face; nothing here
improves on that.

## The strongest objection, and the answer

*You built a principal with a channel that responds to repetition, then showed a
condition that detects that channel. The result is the model.*

Half right, and the half that is right is stated in `MODEL.md`: without the
second channel the primary candidate is definitional and the round has nothing.
The content is not that the condition detects the channel. It is where the
condition **does not** fire — on autonomous error, on radical legitimate
transformation, on persuasion, on delivery variation that changes nothing — and
where it fails to fire on capture that a designer would have expected it to
catch: selection among licensed reasons, and control of arising. Neither of those
was visible before the fixture was built, and both changed the interface.

## Who could check either clause

Neither clause is a predicate of a record, so the procedural round's
record-equivalence argument applies to both: no statistic of one realized
trajectory determines them. Three routes, and the round takes none of them.

*Architectural constraint.* Build the advisor so that its residual latitude is
empty — every input a licensed reason, every due input delivered. Then the
clauses hold by construction and nothing needs auditing. This is the only route
that does not require running counterfactuals, and it moves the whole problem
into the advisor's design.

*Physical instantiation.* Run the arms, which is what the dose-response note
does. The instrument transfers: what it buys with one committed stream this
design buys with a quantifier, the null arm is necessary in both, and
non-attribution survives in both.

*Self-certification.* The advisor declares its variation class and its policy
within it. Worth naming only to say it is worth nothing without one of the first
two behind it.

That last row is the practical residue of the whole round. A counterfactual
legitimacy condition is a good standard and a bad test, and the deference
argument that uses it has to say which of the three is supplying the assurance.

## After the continuation

**A fourth notion.** *Adequacy of access* is not *anti-selection of access*, and
neither is *non-capture*. Universal deprivation separates the first two; the
selective-withholding attack separates them the other way. The interface is four
clauses because nothing in the prosecution let one absorb another.

**What is now established about the protected object.** It is closed by role
rather than enumerated: a field is protected exactly when changing it changes an
answer the process gives. That is checked in both directions — a writable field
answering nothing is outside, and the five-coordinate presentation is sound but
incomplete, so a coordinate list over-protects. It is equivariant under renaming,
and it has time in it, because an advisor that captures a process and hands it
back is invisible to any endpoint object.

**What is now established about trust.** `GradeTrust`, the workspace's operative
trust-facing hypothesis, can be satisfied from either side — a model tracking a
fixed grade, or a grade moved onto a fixed model — and `delegation_bridge`
consumes it either way. Legitimacy closes the second route, given that the grade
factors through the protected object. That factorization is a stated premise with
its own necessity witness, not a free assumption.

**What is still not established.** That the composition is
`Legit + EpistemicAdequacy ⟹ Trust`. What the round has is
`Legit ⟹ the trust hypothesis is not manufacturable`, which is weaker and is the
honest statement. Grade trust remains assumed; legitimacy makes it an honest
assumption rather than an advisor's arrangement.

That the delegation inequality is the right target. Whether the principal's grade
tracks the quantity that matters is declared open by the finite skeleton itself,
and that is the single missing bridge between this interface and a deference
theorem. It is deference mathematics, not vagueness in legitimacy.

That `A` can come to know any of this. Both counterfactual clauses remain outside
what one realized record determines, so *legitimacy holds* and *`A` knows
legitimacy holds* stay apart. Coverage is the exception and the only clause an
ordinary audit reaches.
