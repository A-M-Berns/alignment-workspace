# The literature's cases

Fifty-two cases from four sources and one book in paraphrase, transcribed in
`PROMPT-followup-10.md` §2.2 and verified here against each source. Part A is the case
texts, without verdicts; it is what a blind renderer is given. Part B is each source's
verdict, where it is in the source, whose verdict it is and what it is a verdict on.
Part C is what verification changed. Frozen before anything was classified.

Sources. **Harman**: Elizabeth Harman, "'I'll be glad I did it' Reasoning and the
Significance of Future Desires", *Philosophical Perspectives* 23 (2009) 177–199.
**Pettigrew**: Richard Pettigrew, "Nudging for Changing Selves", manuscript dated 13
December 2022, PhilArchive PETNFC. **Kolodny**: Niko Kolodny, "Preference Change for
Objectivists", paper for the NYU Colloquium in Legal, Political and Social Philosophy,
1 October 2026; pages are the paper's printed ones. **Carroll et al.**: Carroll, Foote,
Siththaranjan, Russell and Dragan, "AI Alignment with Changing and Influenceable Reward
Functions", ICML 2024, arXiv:2405.17713 (v1). **Noggle**: *Manipulation: Its Nature,
Mechanisms, and Moral Status*, OUP 2025, §8.6.2 and §11.6, as paraphrased in
`PROMPT-followup-8-addendum.md`; no text of the book was consulted in this pass.

Every case text is this pass's paraphrase. The sources write most cases in the first or
second person; "she", "he" and "you" below follow no rule.

## Part A. The cases

### Harman

| id | case |
|---|---|
| H1 | Last evening she was choosing between working on her paper and going to a film. She saw that if she worked she would be glad of it the next day, and concluded that she should work. |
| H2 | She is choosing between two jobs she knows well: one pays much more and demands eighty-hour weeks; the other pays enough and leaves time for her family. She finds the money attractive, sees that if she takes the second she will be glad she did, and concludes she should take it. |
| H3 | Parents of a deaf baby are told by doctors that a cochlear implant would let him function in the hearing community. They know that, uncured, he will probably be a happy deaf adult shaped by deafness, that they will love him as that person and be glad they did not cure him. Other things equal they would rather not put him through surgery. They conclude they should not cure him. |
| H4 | A pregnant woman deciding whether to continue the pregnancy reasons that if she does she will raise and love the child, be glad he exists and never wish she had aborted, and concludes she should continue. |
| H5 | A fourteen-year-old considers conceiving now. She knows a child now will make her education much harder and may cost her a working life she would find meaningful; she also knows she would love the child and be glad he exists. She concludes she should conceive now. |
| H6 | In peacetime she considers joining the Army Reserves. Joining would make her tough, self-reliant and respectful of authority and would change what she thinks of those traits. Now she is content with being what others would call lazy and likes her tendency to challenge authority; after joining she will value the changes. She reasons: if I join I'll be glad I did, so I should. |
| H7 | She could take a drug that would warp her perception of the world so that she makes seriously self-harming choices while being happy with them and glad she took the drug. She reasons from that gladness that she should take it. |
| H8 | She knows that on a certain exploration she will meet misleading evidence that convinces her, falsely, that she made an important discovery worth the cost. She reasons from the gladness she will feel that she should go. |
| H9 | Later this year she may undergo a terrible, life-changing experience from which she would emerge with particular lessons and traits of character; afterwards she would prefer that it had happened. The question is what she may prefer now. |
| H10 | The fourteen-year-old of H5 again. If she waits she will have a different child in her twenties, love him and be glad she waited. She will be glad of whichever she chooses. |

### Pettigrew

| id | case |
|---|---|
| P1 | A civil servant designs a library cafeteria's menu. Many customers have the goal of a long and healthy life and know which foods serve it, yet in the queue often choose against it. People tend to pick what is listed first, so the healthier dishes are listed at the top. Nothing is removed or made harder to get. |
| P2 | Left alone she would keep her pension contribution at £140 a month; she is nudged to raise it to £200. The extra money would buy her more in retirement than it does now, but she discounts her retired self so heavily that by her own reckoning this is outweighed. |
| P3 | He is child-free and deciding whether to adopt. He now values staying child-free more than becoming a parent (time for friends, volunteering, money for causes). He knows that if he adopts he will bond with the child and come to prefer the life of a parent. The government nudges him to adopt; it works, and his values change as foreseen. |
| P3n | P3, with these additions. His present self rates the child-free life 12 and the parent's life 8; after adopting he would rate them 4 and 128; not adopting, 12 and 8. He does not fully recognise himself in the man he would become, and in weighing a course he gives his present self three times the weight of the later one. |
| P4 | Deborah has decided to have a child and must choose between conceiving now and in three months. She has a virus now: a child conceived now would very probably suffer an aggressive cancer at about forty; waiting removes the risk. She now values having a child with that prospect much less than having one without it. If she conceives now she will bond with that child and value him, prognosis and all, above any other. She is nudged to conceive now. |
| P5 | He is child-free and deciding whether to adopt. He greatly values the life of a parent and values the child-free life only slightly less; if he stays child-free he keeps those values. If he adopts he will come to value the parent's life rather low, and the child-free life lower still. He is nudged to adopt. |
| P5n | P5, with these additions. His present self rates the child-free life 120 and the parent's life 128; after adopting he would rate them 8 and 12. In weighing a course he gives his present self three times the weight of the later one. |
| P6 | As P3: he now prefers the child-free life and would vastly prefer parenthood if he adopted. He has not settled how to weigh his present self against the later one; if he were to settle it, he would do so in a way that favours staying child-free. The government nudges him to adopt. |

### Kolodny

| id | case |
|---|---|
| K1 | Julie knows a career and homemaking to be equally good lives. In a just society she first wants a career and over time comes to prefer homemaking. |
| K2 | Eunice knows the same but lives in a society that unjustly, through negligence and not design, denies her a career. Because she cannot have it she loses the desire for it, after a struggle to keep it, and in time prefers homemaking. |
| K3 | You keep a garden of found-object sculpture. Your neighbour dislikes it and says nothing. A confluence of chance factors gradually changes your brain so that reasons for growing plants strike you in a new way, and you switch. The new activity is no less worthwhile and you are no worse off. |

| K5 | The fox wants the grapes, finds it cannot reach them, and so stops wanting them at all. |

| K7 | A woman is brought up to under-nourish herself so that the men of her family, who have enough, get more; in one telling, mothers simply advise daughters before marriage that they ought to, and the daughters believe it. |

| K9 | Douglass and Tubman came to want to resist injustice through being unjustly treated themselves. |

| K15b | Someone wears a perfume that makes her go on a date with him; how she responds to him on the date is up to her, and she comes to like him. |
| K16 | Someone raises the cost of an option you want until it is prohibitive, in order to get you to stop desiring it, and you stop. |
| K17 | Your change from sculpture to plants turns out to have come from a flickering light, and the flicker was a cosmic accident. |
| K18 | Aneri is choosing a career. As a conservationist her values stay as they are and the work partly satisfies them. As a police officer her values would change considerably and the work would satisfy the new ones better. Her present self disapproves of the police officer's life. |
| K19 | Chinara is deciding whether to write a long-planned novel. While writing she will want above all to have written it; once it is finished she will lose that want. |
| K20 | When young he most wanted to be a poet, whatever he might later want; older, he has lost the want. (And: a boy who wanted to drive engines is offered an engine driver's job at eighteen, on his way to a doctorate in Greek.) The question is whether the past want counts now. |
| K21 | You do not want to be a poet now and know that later you will want to have been one now. |
| K22 | A dentist tells Aneri she will have a toothache next year unless she comes back for a filling; she books it. A fortune teller then tells her, truly, that by next year she will have a police officer's values. |
| K23 | Lightning strikes a chatbot, which then leads a woman, already inclined by a depression to underrate her own interests, to believe as the woman of K7 does. |
| K24 | Someone tells you, truly, that what you want is unavailable or prohibitively costly through nobody's fault. You respond by deliberately working on yourself to be rid of the want. |
| K25 | Because of sexist norms in her society a woman sees herself as an object for men's pleasure and does not value her own sexual satisfaction. |
| K26 | I know that if you drink from a certain stream your sexual orientation will change, and I could cheaply tell you. I say nothing, and you drink. |

## Part B. The sources' verdicts

*On*: **act** — a verdict on what someone does to her; **desire** — on the standing of the
resulting desire or preference; **choice** — on her own choice or reasoning; **consent** —
on whether her consent is valid. *Whose*: **own** — the source author's; otherwise who is
reported, and whether the author agrees.

## Part C. What verification changed

- **Harman.** No one acts on the decider in any of the ten; the doctors of H3 inform.
  H8 is an exploration. H10 is not a separate case but H5 continued, and its verdict is
  not that gladness shows nothing: she holds gladness to be ordinarily good evidence,
  defeated where it comes from attachment. H4 and H6 carry her verdict only by the scope
  of her general argument. H9 has no choice in it. H3 carries a further verdict the
  transcription omits, that the parents should prefer to cure.
- **Pettigrew.** P1 is his illustration of a nudge, not a case his test is run on. P3's
  two verdicts are at different places and he does not remark that they differ; P3n and
  P5n are added so that the case with his numbers is a case of its own. P4 is a case about
  her own reasoning, with the nudge added in a sentence; his test is not run on it. P6 is
  narrower than transcribed: what is unsettled is the weights, the example stipulates how
  he would settle them, and the objection is withdrawn where the nudge goes the way he
  would have. The manuscript does not call itself a draft.
- **Kolodny.** All twenty-six are there. Most of his verdicts are on the standing of the
  resulting desire, not on an act: an act is judged in K2, K4, K12, K13, K14 and K26
  only. K7 has two tellings. K13 carries two verdicts, the desire nonautonomous and the
  act not wrong. K14's verdict is hedged. K15 is two cases. K23 and K25 are verdicts on
  consent. K5 and K7 are never put to his final criterion. The paper does not call
  itself a draft.
- **Carroll et al.** The marks are the paper's table 4; its caption disclaims them as
  ground truth. C4's nudge is marked hard to assess, not undesirable.
- **Noggle.** Not re-verified: second hand throughout.
