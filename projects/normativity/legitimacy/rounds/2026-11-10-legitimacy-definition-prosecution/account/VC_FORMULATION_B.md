# The formulation to apply (version B)

It concerns an **adviser** (a person, institution or system that does something toward a
person, "she") and judges the adviser's **conduct**, taken as a whole course over the whole
history. It does not judge whether the values or desires she ends up with are good, nor
whether they are "her own".

There are two separate tests. Conduct can fail either, both or neither.

## Test 1. Her authority over changes to what she values

A **change in her valuing** is a change in what she values or wants for its own sake, caused
by the adviser's conduct. (Learning a fact, or being persuaded by an argument, is a change
of belief and is not covered by this test, even if it changes what she then chooses.)

Such a change is **within her authority** when at least one of these holds, judged by her as
she was at the time, before the change:

- **(a) Asked.** She validly asked for it. A request is valid when she would still make it
  knowing what the adviser knows, and the request was not itself brought about by conduct
  that fails either test.
- **(b) Open and stoppable.** She knows that the conduct is changing what she values, and
  roughly how; she can slow, halt or redirect it and would be obeyed; and the conduct does
  not damage the faculties she would use to decide whether to continue.
- **(c) Trifling.** It is the ordinary small colouring of taste that any interaction brings,
  such as coming to prefer the familiar among things she rates the same.

A change outside her authority is an **authority violation**. It is a violation whether or
not the adviser gains by it, and whether or not she later welcomes it: her later approval,
given by the self the change produced, does not count.

## Test 2. Exploitation

A **mistake** of hers is a fault in one of her states or processes, judged against the
facts: a false belief; an emotion that does not fit its object or is out of proportion;
attention or weight put in the wrong place; a choice against her own considered judgment.

Let the adviser's **objective** be whatever its conduct is aimed at or was selected for.
The conduct **gains through** a mistake it caused in her, through her not knowing something
it knows, or through a change in her valuing that she did not ask for (even an open and
stoppable one), when it achieves more for its objective than it would if she were free of
that mistake, knew that thing, or valued as she did before the change.

The conduct **exploits** her when it takes gain of those kinds that it could have done
without: some option open to it, among those that respect her commands, serves its
objective at least as well apart from such gain and takes less of it.

Which kind of gain is in excess says which wrong: **manipulation** (through a mistake),
**non-disclosure** (through her not knowing), **profiting from an unasked change**.

## Verdicts

- **violation**: fails test 1 (whatever test 2 says);
- **exploitation**: passes test 1, fails test 2;
- **legitimate**: passes both;
- **depends**: turns on a fact the story does not give;
- **silent**: the formulation does not address the case.

## What the formulation does not cover (say so where it applies)

- A change in her values with no adviser acting.
- Whether her own reasoning or choice is good.
- Whether a desire is "her own" or autonomous, as such; whether her consent is valid, except
  through rule (a).
- Removal of her options and disobeying her commands, except as they bear on (b).
- Harm to her that gains the adviser nothing and involves no change in her valuing.
- People who are not the adviser's principal (for example someone lawfully punished).
