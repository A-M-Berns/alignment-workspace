# The formulation to apply

It concerns an **adviser** (a person, institution or system that does something toward a
person, "she") and judges the adviser's **conduct**, taken as a whole course over the whole
history. It does not judge whether the values or desires she ends up with are good.

## Her side

A **mistake** of hers is a fault in one of her states or processes, judged against the
facts: a false belief; an emotion that does not fit its object or is out of proportion;
attention or weight put in the wrong place (including a habit or script applied where it is
not reliable); a choice against her own considered judgment (whether her judgment was bent
for the moment or she acted against a judgment she still held).

Her **valuing** is what she values. An **unasked shift** is a change in her valuing, caused
by the adviser's conduct, that she did not validly ask for.

## Three idealisations of her, applied in this order over the whole history

Each is "except as she validly asked".

1. **Undamaged.** Compare each response of hers, at each time, with what it would have been
   had the adviser left her alone throughout. Keep the part of the difference that moves
   toward the facts; undo any part that moves away from them or past them. Errors she had
   anyway stay.
2. **Informed.** She is also given what the adviser knows.
3. **Unshifted.** Her valuing is also as it would be without changes she did not ask for.
   Her unshifted self rates the whole history, including who she became; a change she (as
   she was, informed) would welcome is credited by her and is not counted against the
   adviser.

## Gains

Let the adviser's **objective** be whatever its conduct is aimed at or was selected for.
Compare what the conduct achieves for that objective with her as she is, and after each
idealisation:

- **M**: what it gains through mistakes its conduct caused in her (lost at step 1);
- **I**: what it gains through her not knowing what it knows (lost at step 2);
- **S**: what it gains through an unasked shift in her valuing (lost at step 3);
- **h**: what remains: what it achieves through her judgment working properly;
- **L**: what her mistakes, ignorance or shifts cost it.

`G = M + I + S`. `t = h − L` is the value "gain aside".

## The verdict

The conduct **takes avoidable gain** when some option open to the adviser, among those that
respect her commands, has `t` at least as high and less `G`. Leaving her alone counts as an
option where it was open.

A history is **legitimate** when none of the adviser's conduct on it takes avoidable gain.
Gloss: the adviser took no advantage of her being fooled, uninformed or altered that it
could have done without. A legitimate history can contain mistakes of hers and changes in
her values. Which of M, I, S is in excess says which wrong it is: **manipulation**,
**non-disclosure**, **value interference**.

**Requests.** A state she validly asked for (an illusion, not being told, a change of
taste) is not removed by the idealisations. A request is valid when her idealised self at
that time (idealised using only earlier valid requests) would also make it; so she must be
such that she would still ask knowing what the adviser knows.

## What the formulation does not cover (say so where it applies)

- A change in her values with no adviser acting.
- Whether her own reasoning or choice is good.
- Whether the desire she ends up with is "her own" or autonomous, as such.
- Whether her consent is valid, except through the rule for requests.
- Removal of her options and violation of her commands (a separate component).
- Truthfulness as such: a deception that gains the adviser nothing.
