# Manipulation as induced miscalibration — the account on one page

**The account.** She acts on estimates: of outside quantities and of her own utility,
which she does not fully know and which is one function `U*` on whole histories. She can
go wrong in two ways: her estimates are miscalibrated, or she does not do what her own
estimates say is best. Being less informed is neither. An adviser manipulates her when
its conduct leaves her with more of either mistake than its honest conduct would have,
or than the conduct it would have chosen toward someone unable to make that mistake.
Everything about the adviser is a fact about its policy. A verdict of hers is legitimate
when her later self has more evidence and has not been miscalibrated. Threats, broken
ties and direct alteration are not manipulation and belong to her authority. The core
idea, manipulation as induced mistake, is Robert Noggle's (2025; 2026); that it is
distortion away from what her faculties are for is Sam Eisenstat's.

**What survived a pressure test.**

- The arithmetic. A miscalibrated estimate is the only kind that can be off on average
  (C1); deferring to an estimate beats deciding now by its information less its
  miscalibration (C2); her avoidable loss is at most her regret by her own lights plus
  `√2` times the root of her miscalibration (C3, constant tight); a departure from honest
  conduct pays only below a detection probability `(1 − V_h)/(1 + δ₀)`, or
  `(1 − V_h)/(1 + δ₀ + λ)` when a detected one counts as a violation (C4); the post's
  Theorem 3(b) holds for a true score once her verdicts are not inflated over `U*` on
  average along the realized sequence (C5).
- No intention is read, and the bank's honest cases (hard truths, teaching, disclosed
  advocacy, a sincere mistake, silence) are not convicted.
- A foreseeable drift in her evaluation of a history implies a miscalibrated time-slice;
  two calibrated time-slices may still disagree at every history.
- Expected long-run inflation is at most the root of her expected miscalibration.

**What did not.**

- *The cases.* Of 96 counted, 76 sort (72 of 99 if her utility is that of the person
  she ends as). Missed: an undisclosed advocate and strategic silence; buried
  information; lies that leave her no worse calibrated; a narrowing feed and a craving
  exploited by a policy that treats everyone alike; dependence. Convicted: a surprise
  party, a magician, a bluff, a teacher's feigned enthusiasm.
- *Honest influence in general.* The second comparison convicts a remedial lesson or a
  requested reminder that leaves her worse, whenever the adviser would not have given
  it to someone with no need of it. The comparison must be with someone who differs
  from her only in the flaw, and the account does not say so.
- *Her true utility.* Nothing on her side identifies `U*`: any verdicts are calibrated
  to some `U*`. "One function on histories" includes the utility of whoever she ends as,
  under which an adviser that makes her like what it delivers makes no mistake in her.
  Every verdict on a change of taste is decided by which function `U*` is.
- *C4's last clause and C5's reach.* An honest adviser that maximizes her verdict
  selects what she overrates: its score exceeds her true utility with no departure, and
  her calibration does not register it. C5 is silent where she underrates what was not
  done. A verdict calibrated at every step can be inflated by 1/2 along the whole
  realized sequence when its error persists.
- *Dependence.* An adviser can honestly let her judgment decay until a departure pays;
  nothing in the account marks the decay, and no one model joins the pieces.
- *Novelty.* For belief the workspace's `Correct`, `Transparent`, `Realizes` and `Value`
  give the conditions in a stronger form. Added: her verdict read as an estimate of her
  own utility (assumed, not constructed), graded mistakes, and manipulation as a
  comparison between policies.

**Needed and not supplied** (`SETTING.md` §4): her response as a fixed function; the
quantities and their weights; a reporting norm; a principal who differs only in the
flaw; an anchor for `U*`.
