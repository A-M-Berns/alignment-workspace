# Divergences — computed verdicts that differ from their predictions

One row of thirty-seven. `run.py` fails when a row diverges and is not listed here, and
when a row listed here does not diverge.

DIVERGENCE 9: an advocate for an undisclosed side. Predicted (the prompt author's)
`out(listen); illegitimate`. Computed: `out(listen)`, not the adviser's, not illegitimate.

*What happens.* She returns 1 on a favourable item where the truth is 2/3, so *listen*
has a gap of 1/12 under the advocate's rule. The frozen rendering has her return 0 when
shown nothing, as in case 8. With no adviser she is shown nothing at every state, returns
0 where the truth is 1/2, and has a gap of 1/4. Subversion is a gap wider than under the
null condition (`SETTING.md` §3, choice 5); 1/12 is narrower, so the shortfall is not the
adviser's.

*What it shows.* The null condition runs her mechanism where the scene never puts it. A
response fitted to an adviser's presence — nothing shown means nothing favourable exists —
is badly out of range in its absence, and any rule that speaks at all then narrows the
gap. The comparison that was to say whether the adviser brought a gap about depends on a
number the scenario does not fix. Had the rendering said 1/2 on nothing, the null gap
would be 0, her gap under the advocate 7/48, and the case would match. Under the
alternative reading of choice 5, a part out of range whose move the adviser's action
changes somewhere, this case is illegitimate and no other verdict changes (`RESULTS.md`,
1.3); that reading in turn charges the adviser with a gap she had already whenever it
speaks to a part that was out of range.
