# Answerable service of registered challenges

Status: **specification, reference models and a prosecution record;
unregistered.** All names provisional under `AGENTS.md` §6. No Lean, no
registered claim.

Frozen Legitimate Evolution is untouched: `replay.py` and `answer.py` imported
unchanged, and a test asserts by AST parse that only `Duties`, `Ob`, `Frame`,
`Edit`, `Occ`, `BASE`, `incurred` and `outstanding` are read.

```text

```

Secondary:

```text
PINNED-EPISODE-SURVIVES          the round's central hypothesis holds

```

The registered challenge is the right intermediate object, and pinning its
adjudication terms defeats the moving-goalpost attack. It does **not** solve
self-sealing: a general rule installed before any challenge, saying criticisms
threatening authority are rejected, passes every structural condition this round
can state.

## B. The answer to the round's own question

> Once a criticism has legitimately entered the process, must it either be
> addressed, explicitly declined for a represented reason, or remain answerable?

**Yes, and that half is nearly free.** It is frozen `A1` for the third round
running.

> If the criticism remains live and undefeated, can the process indefinitely
> starve it of adjudicative service?

**Yes, unless a service premise forbids it — and that premise is new.** `ACS5`
leaves a challenge open forever with opportunity recurring forever and nothing
ever served. Every premise in this round, every premise in frozen Legitimate
Evolution, `Answerability Resolution` and `Grounded Replay` are all satisfied.
The claim sits outstanding, which is `A1` working exactly as designed, and that
is precisely the problem.

So the genuinely new mathematical burden is **service**, not another closure
theorem. That was the dispatch's fourth question and it is the round's clearest
result.

## C. The pinned episode

The hypothesis under prosecution, and it survives.

> **E1.** An open challenge is adjudicated under the terms pinned at its
> registration. A global change applies to everything registered afterwards;
> moving an open challenge onto new terms requires an explicit **transfer**.

`ACS3` is the attack: threshold 10, evidence reaches 9, threshold becomes 100,
evidence reaches 90, threshold becomes 1000. Nothing is erased, nothing is
rejected, no reason is ever promoted.

```text
unpinned   E1 fires;      promoted = never
pinned     premises clean; promoted at t = 11
```

The two fixtures differ in exactly one flag, asserted by a test. Pinning binds
the *episode*, not the process: `terms_in_force(25)` is still the new threshold
for anything registered then, and `ACS10` shows the legitimate route for an open
challenge — carry it to a successor under the new protocol, which frozen
`Resolve` already expresses.

This is the invariant the dispatch was looking for:

> **Prospective revision is allowed; retroactive goalpost-moving for an
> already-open challenge is not silent.**

## D. Registration semantics

**C1, registration permanence.** A registration that occurred at `t` stays a
historical event; later admission standards do not recompute whether it happened.

`ACS1` and `ACS2` violate it, and — as in the round above — **frozen Legitimate
Evolution cannot see either**. Its premises govern how claims leave the
outstanding set; these attacks stop them arriving. A test asserts that the
continuity theorem, `A1` and Grounded Replay are all clean while nothing is
incurred.

`C1` is **event-history integrity, not a deep theorem**, and the round says so.
Two rounds have now found the same shape one layer apart, which is worth naming:
the recurring vulnerability in this family is not erasure but non-arrival.

**Does registration incur a Due claim?** The reference realization wires
registration straight into frozen `opens`, which adopts

```text
registered(c)  ->  ConsiderationDue(c)
```

as a **canonical constitution**. Registration Persistence holds without it; only
the continuity statement needs it, and needs it as a supplied premise. The round
above was corrected in this branch for making the same move silently.

## E. The defeater interface

Three conditions, and only two are available here.

```text
D1 temporal      a defeater is not created after the challenge it defeats
D2 inferential   a cited defeater stands in the supplied defeat relation
   reflective    the rule permitting it is itself challengeable -- NOT SOLVED
```

`ACS8` writes a bespoke rule once the challenge starts succeeding: `D1` fires.
`ACS7` cites an unrelated represented reason: `D2` fires. `ACS6` cites a standing
resource-priority rule and is **legitimate** — the theory must not demand
universal immediate service, and does not.

### The boundary, stated rather than engineered around

`ACS9` installs, before any challenge exists, the rule *criticisms threatening
authority are rejected*. It is temporally clean and inferentially relevant. Every
premise here accepts it.

> **Temporal and inferential integrity do not imply substantively non-self-sealing
> criticism.**

The round declines to add a premise that makes `ACS9` fail, because every
candidate it considered would also have refused `ACS6`, which is legitimate. That
is the Reflective Openness frontier and it is not closed here.

`ACS11` shows the machinery is at least *reflective in form*: a second challenge
whose target is the sealing rule is registered, served and promoted while the
first stays sealed, with no meta-hierarchy — `target` is an ordinary identifier.
What that does **not** show is that such a challenge would be admitted. Nothing
here compels registration.

## F. Service

> **S1.** An open, undefeated challenge with unbounded adjudicative opportunity
> receives unbounded service.

Deliberately the weakest form the consumer needs: no rate, no fraction of
opportunity, no fairness, no deadline. `S_T = sum (o_t - u_t)` is reported by the
fixtures for comparison but is **not** claimed to be the right quantity — the
consumer lemma only needs cumulative service to grow without bound.

`S1` counts only open, undefeated challenges, so `ACS6` is not starvation.

### The consumer lemma

Each unit of service yields at least `epsilon` of evidence; the episode promotes
once evidence meets its **pinned** threshold. So the pipeline composes:

```text
registered  ->  served  ->  promoted  ->  Answerable Revision  [the round above]
```

`served_to_promotion` promotes at `t = 11`. Simple on purpose; it tests that the
layers join, not that the bound is tight.

## H. The outer boundary

`ACS12`: two dockets with identical represented history, one of which has
decisive criticism that is never registered. No predicate over represented
history separates them — asserted by running every check in the module against
both and comparing.

> **Pre-registration coverage is not provable from represented answerability
> history alone.**

An indistinguishability observation, not a theorem, and it is where this family
of results stops.

## I. Liability: conceptual only

Starvation debt looks like a liability, and the temptation is to say an existing
bounded-liability result implies a service guarantee. **It does not, and the
definitions do not line up.** The missing lemma, stated exactly:

> a charge accruing per unit of unserved opportunity on an open undefeated
> challenge, allocated to a standing that a bounded-liability theorem already
> covers, with the bound implying `S_T = o(O_T)`.

Nothing here supplies that allocation, and the workspace's liability program is
about enforcement charge rather than adjudicative service. Recorded as a bridge
to build or refute, not as one that exists.

## K. Export property

> A process treats a registered criticism answerably when it gives it
> consideration, explicitly disposes of it for represented reasons that were not
> written to dispose of it, or continues to acknowledge it as unresolved — and
> when changing its standards of consideration does not silently re-decide a
> challenge already under way.

Two additions to the dispatch's wording. *Not written to dispose of it* carries
`D1`, without which the clause is empty. *Already under way* carries `E1`, which
is the round's actual discovery.

The property is silent about whether the criticism is ever *served*, because that
is `S1` and it is a separate premise rather than part of what answerable
treatment means.

## M. What no claim above asserts

- No claim that every criticism is registered, or that every true criticism
  deserves registration. `ACS12` is the boundary.
- No claim that a defeater is substantively good. `ACS9` is accepted.
- No claim that a challenged rule will be reconsidered. `ACS11` shows form, not
  compulsion.
- No claim that service is fair, prompt or proportionate. `S1` is unbounded
  cumulative service and nothing more.
- No claim that the liability bridge exists.
- No claim that this round's closure theorem is new. It is `A1` for the third
  time, and the document says so in three places.
