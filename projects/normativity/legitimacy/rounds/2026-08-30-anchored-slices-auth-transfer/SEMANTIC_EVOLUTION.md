# Semantic evolution

## Operations

Let \(o\) be inherited content and \(n\) the proposed successor content.
General semilattices need not have subtraction or canonical complements, so a
revision certificate supplies explicit witnesses:

\[
o=r\vee d,\qquad n=r\vee i.
\]

Here \(r\) is retained content, \(d\) is authorized disposition, and \(i\) is a
freshly admitted increment.

- Exact translation has \(d=i=0\) and authenticates the representation change.
- Weakening has \(d\ne0\); it is valid only with authorized disposition evidence.
- Strengthening has \(i\ne0\); the increment receives a fresh slice and birth.
- A substantive incomparable revision can have both \(d\ne0\) and \(i\ne0\).

This witness form works without lattice complements or atomic obligations.

## Four kinds of change

| Change | Semantic treatment |
| --- | --- |
| representational ontology change | same slice if an authenticated commuting transport exists |
| evaluator revision | representation change requiring the same kind of bridge; not factual obsolescence |
| substantive normative revision | disposition of removed content and admission of new content |
| external factual change | may justify disposition under the slice's anchored applicability rule |

External change does not rewrite the old denotation. It supplies evidence for a
terminal account evaluated under the anchored rule. “The new evaluator no
longer cares” supplies neither satisfaction nor disposition.

## Seed-quality boundary

This round exposes, but does not solve, four seed assumptions:

1. authenticated admission rules for new slices;
2. sound domain checkers for translations and dispositions;
3. bridges broad enough to recognize future ontology changes;
4. protected rules preventing a checker from authenticating its own arbitrary
   weakening.

If liability underwriters or payoff-profile ontologies are revised, their
meanings may need the same sort of authenticated bridge. That is a future
interface problem, not a unification of answerability and underwriting.
