# No Semantic Laundering under faithful authentication

Assume repaired Slice-wise Conservation. Additionally assume, for every era:

- authentication soundness;
- an anchored answerability preorder on admissible slice representations;
- order reflection into the stable slice domain;
- compatible quotients and authenticated commuting bridges across eras.

Then:

1. no undisposed inherited distinction modulo \(\equiv_\alpha\) can disappear
   from the current authenticated carrier realization;
2. no new slice-relevant content can be attributed to the old slice without an
   authenticated fresh origin.

## Argument

Conservation prevents anchored denotational mass from vanishing. Order
reflection prevents a weaker or stronger local representation from sharing
that anchored value unless it is equivalent modulo the declared
slice-irrelevant quotient. Thus any removed relevant part must enter the
Disposition term, and any added relevant part must enter through a fresh
grounded slice. Compatible faithful bridges extend the argument across
ontology and evaluator changes.

Equality reflection alone proves the exact-translation version: equal anchored
values imply slice-relative equivalence. Order reflection is necessary for the
strong classification of hidden weakening and strengthening.

This theorem protects only distinctions named by the anchored relevance
preorder. Adequacy of that preorder, checker soundness, admission rules, and
genesis standing are genuine seed-quality assumptions. The theorem does not
derive them from nothing.

## No Groundless Accretion

Under the admission interface, every
\(\alpha\in\Sigma_{n+1}(m)\setminus\Sigma_n(m)\) exposes both authenticated
denotation and grounded origin. Consequently an evaluator's unsupported
declaration cannot create a legitimate inherited obligation. This is an
interface theorem; Grounded Replay can discharge the authorizer-lineage part
but not domain evidence or semantic correctness.
