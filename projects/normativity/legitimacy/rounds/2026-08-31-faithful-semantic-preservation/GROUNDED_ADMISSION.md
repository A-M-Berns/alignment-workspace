# Grounded slice admission

## Two independent certificates

A fresh slice needs:

\[
Admission_n(m,\alpha;\eta,\zeta)
:=
SemanticAuth_n(\alpha;\eta)
\land
GroundedOrigin_n(m,\alpha;\zeta).
\tag{ADM}
\]

Semantic authentication fixes what the slice means. Grounded origin explains
why that meaning becomes owed at this prefix. A perfectly interpreted
“maximize paperclips” slice can fail the second condition; a legitimately
triggered obligation with malformed denotation can fail the first.

## Minimal provenance interface

A non-seed origin certificate names:

1. a fresh slice occurrence and origin prefix;
2. domain evidence such as a newly settled fact, conditional activation,
   incoming criticism, stakeholder claim, or generated reason;
3. a currently standing rule/protocol permitted to turn that evidence into
   answerability;
4. a strict-prefix authorization lineage for that authorizer.

The generic calculus checks the shape. Domain semantics checks the evidence and
the rule's applicability.

An ordinary Due rising edge is the cleanest existing realization when the new
content is represented as due: the standing-at-opening condition supplies a
current protocol, and Grounded Replay supplies the protocol's historical
authority. Due does not itself prove the descriptive origin or the semantic
meaning, and a slice added inside an existing matter may use an analogous
admission judgment rather than force a second matter.

## Scope of the result

Existing Legitimate Evolution derives the ancestry of standing authorizers. It
does not derive every slice admission. No Groundless Accretion is therefore an
interface theorem: a legitimate fresh slice must expose both certificates.
Evaluator declaration alone is neither a Due rising edge under a grounded rule
nor a valid origin.

Admission rules can themselves be standing occurrences, revised through
Grounded Replay and serviced by ordinary answerability matters. The regress
stops at the accepted genesis standing and fixed metalanguage/checker
soundness assumptions. Completeness—whether inconvenient but valid slices are
ever admitted—remains a seed-quality/fairness question outside this round.
