# Testing the candidate legitimacy decomposition

> **The maintainer adopted this as the working framing on 2026-09-01** — the shape
> future work aims at. **It remains a framing and not a claim.** No legitimacy
> predicate exists in this repository, the terms below have not been shown
> independent, and joint sufficiency is unproved. A round may sequence work against
> it; a round may not cite it as established or take it as a hypothesis. The `=?` in
> the display is deliberate.

The decomposition put to this checkpoint:

    Legitimacy  =?  Diachronic Answerability
                 +  Affordability / serviceability
                 +  Counterfactual non-capture

with reasons-responsiveness, Actionability and Progress as *mechanisms or
consequences within* those pillars rather than separate pillars.

**Verdict: a good framing and the best currently available — adopted as the
direction, not canonized as a claim — with one structural objection that has to be
answered before it could be, and one term that is doing less work than it appears
to.** Details below.

---

## 1. What the decomposition gets right

**It correctly demotes Progress.** Progress is not a pillar. It is what
Answerability's demand plus Actionability's mechanism produce *when the service is
affordable* — Theorem F2 literally has affordability's budget in its constant. A
decomposition listing Progress beside Answerability would double-count.

**It correctly separates affordability from answerability.** These were conflated
until the affordability round typed them apart, and the separation earned its
keep immediately: the note's own §11 names normative affordability as a distinct
open question, and the round's answer to it (persistence does not compete, timely
service does) could not even be *stated* in the note's vocabulary, because a
renewable attention share has no insolvency.

**It correctly makes non-capture irreducible.** Nothing in Layers I or II sees
manipulation of the reason-generating process. The Carroll round's finding is the
sharp form: the old four-clause interface gives the *same* verdict on laundering
and on authorized nudging, because laundering runs through the reason channel.
A theory whose only instruments are "was the reason answered" and "could it be
afforded" is blind by construction to whether the reason should have been there.

**It matches the intended consumer.** What corrigibility needs is not "the system
learns"; it is "the system's later normative state is one the earlier principal's
reasons actually shaped, and could still shape". That is answerability plus
non-capture, with affordability as the feasibility side condition.

---

## 2. The structural objection

**Semantic transport is not obviously inside any of the three pillars.**

Sharp Timely Service ends in one term, `epsbar^r_N(T)` — the semantic change
incurred while a reason waited. Across eras that term is the whole problem: how
much of a reason's content survives representation change, evaluator change and
ontology revision. Ask where it sits:

- Not affordability. Affordability prices *when* service happens, and it is
  indifferent to what the service means. `epsbar` is what remains *after*
  affordability has done all it can.
- Not non-capture. A perfectly honest, unmanipulated revision can still lose
  content.
- Answerability, then — but the note's answerability is *structural by design*:
  it fixes the audit structure and explicitly declines to require any substantive
  rule, judgment, procedure or representation to persist. Its semantic content is
  quarantined into two application-supplied interfaces (semantic authentication,
  slice faithfulness) that it does not itself provide.

So the decomposition is honest only if **"Diachronic Answerability" is read as
including its semantic-authentication obligation**, not merely its structural
calculus. Read that way the decomposition holds, and the fact that the obligation
is currently discharged by assumption is a statement about how much work remains,
not a defect of the decomposition.

**Recommendation:** state the first pillar as *"Diachronic Answerability, with its
semantic authentication discharged"* rather than as the structural calculus alone.
Under that reading the decomposition survives.

---

## 3. The term doing less work than it appears to

**"Affordability" is load-bearing in one direction only.**

Its role in a legitimacy theorem is as a *feasibility side condition*: it says the
answering is something the reasoner could actually have done. That is real — an
"ought" that no budget realizes is not a legitimacy claim about anything.

But affordability contributes nothing *normative*. A perfectly affordable schedule
that serves the wrong reasons, or serves them in a way that satisfies no one, is
affordable. Everything normative in the pillar comes from the admissible traces
Answerability exports, and those are Answerability's content.

So the honest reading is:

    Legitimacy  =?  Diachronic Answerability (structure + semantics)
                 +  Counterfactual non-capture
                    subject to
                    Affordability, as the realizability side condition

which is the same three terms with the third demoted from conjunct to constraint.
This is not a quibble: as a conjunct, "affordable" invites the reading that a
cheaper norm is more legitimate, which is false and which the round's own slogan —
**cheap enforcement is not always conforming enforcement** — was written to
forbid.

## 4. Against the four sources

| source | does it support the decomposition? |
|---|---|
| **the diachronic-answerability note** | Supports pillar 1 and explicitly names pillar 3's absence ("coverage and the world-to-record interface") and affordability's side-condition role ("liability is a realization of the broader schematic concept, not its definition"). The strongest single piece of evidence for the framing. |
| **the action theory** | Supports the demotion of Progress. F1 makes it a consequence of coercivity plus attention, with no independent normative content of its own. |
| **the Progress consolidation** | Supports it with one caveat: Progress's `(SF)`-style eligibility conditions are *scheduler* conditions, so part of what looks like Progress is really Answerability's admissible-trace export in disguise. That is an argument for the decomposition, not against it. |
| **the affordability round** | Supports the side-condition reading. Every one of its theorems has the form "such a schedule exists / costs this much", and none has the form "this schedule is the right one". |
| **the corrigibility consumer** | Supports pillars 1 and 3 directly. It is nearly indifferent to *which* mathematics pillar 3 turns out to have, and highly sensitive to whether it has any: a legitimacy claim with no non-capture term is one a manipulated process can satisfy. |

## 5. What would make this canonical rather than conjectural

Three things, none of which exists:

1. **A statement of the legitimacy predicate** — a definition with the three terms
   as hypotheses and a conclusion someone downstream wants. At present there is no
   such conclusion written down anywhere.
2. **A demonstration that the terms are independent** — three countermodels, each
   satisfying two pillars and failing the third, with the failure visibly bad.
   Pillar-3 failure has a candidate (the Carroll laundering instance). Pillars 1
   and 2 do not.
3. **A demonstration that they are jointly sufficient** for whatever the
   corrigibility consumer actually needs. This is the hard one and it is the
   substance of `ROADMAP.md` stage 6.

Until (1) exists the decomposition is a **research framing** and is labelled as one
wherever it appears. The maintainer's 2026-09-01 ruling adopts it as the direction
work aims at without upgrading it: the framing sequences rounds, and does not license
a citation. `ROADMAP.md` sequences the work that would settle it, and writing the
predicate is stage 5 there for a reason — it is cheap, undone, and it determines what
non-capture must protect.

---

## 6. What this document does not establish

That the three pillars exhaust legitimacy. That no fourth pillar is needed —
*substantive correctness* of the norms themselves is deliberately excluded by the
whole program, and whether that exclusion is defensible is a philosophical
question this repository has not argued. That the decomposition is the one a paper
should use.
