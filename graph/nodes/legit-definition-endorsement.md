## Statement
The operative definition of legitimacy for this programme is that a
belief-formation process is legitimate when its results merit endorsement,
where endorsement of another's beliefs is a property of the endorser's own
beliefs and legitimacy is a matter of uncertainty.

## Source
projects/deference/faf-cleanroom-2026-10-02/sources/unbounded-embedded/01-primary/summer-research-plan-2026--research-ideas-may-2026.md,
section "Modeling Legitimacy & Corrigibility": "If Alice endorses Bob's
beliefs, this is not really a property of Bob's beliefs, but rather Alice's."
"My word for this phenomenon is **legitimacy**. A belief-formation process is
legitimate if we should endorse its results. (I say 'should' because legitimacy
is something we can be uncertain about; we can fail to endorse someone, not
realizing that their reasoning is legitimate.)"

The earlier form, in the maintainer's 2023 post Meaning & Agency (LessWrong,
2023-12-19): "a mode of reasoning is legitimate if it has a reliable tendency
toward the truth", with "Legitimacy is to endorsement as goodness is to
utility".

## Notes
A cases node because the maintainer's two texts give two definitions three
years apart, and which one is operative decides what every formal candidate in
this frame is a candidate for. None of the candidates (the gated trust

2026 sentence; the task task-measure-candidates-against-definition is the
comparison. The Statement avoids the maintainer's "should" (the lint refuses
it) and renders it as "merit endorsement"; the Source carries his wording.
