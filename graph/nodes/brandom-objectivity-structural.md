## Statement
Brandom locates the distinction between claims that are objectively correct and
claims merely taken to be correct as a structural feature of each scorekeeping
perspective, rejecting objectivity as intersubjective agreement.

## Source
projects/normativity/legitimacy/rounds/2026-08-13-relational-scorekeeping-bridge/BRANDOM_MAP.md,
section 7, "Objectivity is a structural feature of every perspective, not a
privileged one" (Making It Explicit, chapter 8, section VI.3, pages 593 to 597,
quoted there): objectivity as intersubjectivity is rejected, and the
distinction between correct claims and claims taken to be correct "is a
structural feature of each scorekeeping" perspective.

## Notes
Cited source content, verified by page in the public source map. Its bearing,
the filing session's reading: it is an anti-subjectivist account of
correctness, which a legitimacy notion that distinguishes being right from
being taken to be right needs.
