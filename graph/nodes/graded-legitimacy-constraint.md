## Statement
Scoring illegitimate outcomes below legitimate ones, either by a cardinal
penalty or lexically (every illegitimate world below every legitimate one),
gives an agent no incentive to compromise legitimacy and no distortion of its
other choices, without a uniform floor on illegitimate outcomes.

## Source
The rule is stated from its content. The lexical variant is the public kernel's
ordering hypothesis (wiki/Corrigibility.md, section 5, Box 3;
projects/deference/CLAIMS.md, entry kernel.box3-no-incentive-to-compromise; see
kernel-lexical-order-hypothesis). Both variants are formalized in
projects/deference/faf-cleanroom-2026-10-02/packages/legit-neg-static/legit-neg-static-report.md
(P4a lexical, P4b cardinal gap).

## Notes
Filed as a plan: the rule is the course of action and meeting the two conditions
is its success. A cardinal penalty fails the first condition whenever the best
illegitimate outcome exceeds the best legitimate one by more than the penalty
(arithmetic). Whether the lexical variant distorts other choices is open here;
legit-neg-static records that its choice reads illegitimate-terminal data
(not_NegLBlind_P4a). Its edge from the ordering crux: the lexical variant is
faithful only if every illegitimate world is in fact worse.
