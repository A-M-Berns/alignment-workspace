## Statement
Semantic legitimacy uses a correspondence notion of truth and syntactic
legitimacy is a set of local rules, and legitimacy in general cashes out as
local rules in the way truth-preservation does.

## Source
projects/deference/note-dump-2026-08-11/related-chats/2026-07-05__theory-of-legitimacy__f57f5f2d.md,
turn 5, the maintainer: "semantic legitimacy uses a classical theory of truth,
based on map-territory correspondence, while syntactic legitimacy is a set of
local rules. Truth-preservation is a special case. More generally, we care
about whether it tracks the truth. Just as truth-preservation cashes out as
local rules, we want legitimacy in general to do so (and this is a drive which
humans are actually reasonably good at acting on; a major component across many
human cultures)."
