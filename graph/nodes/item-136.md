## Item
The statement of `account/BOARD.md` of the prosecution round measures manipulation in three quantities:
the miscalibration of her estimators of facts and of her own utilities, her regret by her own valuations,
and, for an adviser whose policy changes her way of valuing from how it would develop with no adviser
actions, how much better the best policy that leaves it alone would have been, by her valuing at the moment
the adviser acts. The tenth follow-up tested it on 52 cases from Harman, Pettigrew, Kolodny, Carroll et al.
and Noggle (`account/LIT_CASES.md`), rendered twice. Where a case says how she now rates the course that
changes her, the two renderers give the third line the same answer (ten of ten) and it is the source's
verdict on the act; in most cases the text does not say, and one renderer left the line unfixed in 21 of 33
(`account/LIT_RENDERINGS.md`). In 9 to 13 of 33 the line's answer depends on whether a want formed by living
through something is booked as a change of valuing or as a discovery. Seven constructed cases of plain
subversion pass all three lines: in six nothing she values is changed (blackmail, destroyed options,
destroyed evidence, erased memory, an induced inconsistency), and in one her valuing is swapped between
circumstances with its law unchanged (`prompts/2026-11-10-legitimacy-definition-prosecution/REVIEW-astra-followup-10.md`,
stage two, (a)). A variant of the third line compares the best way of making the same change with the best
way of leaving her alone; what "the same change" is has a recommendation and two open points
(`account/BOARD_CHECKS.md`, items 15 and 16). What standing her later verdicts have when the adviser is
scored is open: on a model the workspace's segment gate scores a later clean run by her changed verdicts
(`account/BRIDGE.md` §5), and a position reported for the maintainer gates her later evaluation completely. The account of manipulation as induced mistake is Noggle's (Robert Noggle, *Manipulation: Its Nature, Mechanisms, and Moral Status* (Oxford University Press, 2025, doi:10.1093/9780198924920.001.0001), and "The Mistake Account of Manipulation", *Journal of Practical Ethics* 14(1), 2026, doi:10.3998/jpe.9951).

Wanted first: the 33 cases in which an adviser acts, each given as a finite scene with the null policy, her
later valuing under each policy and her present rating of each course written out before anything is
computed, by two renderers independently, so that the three lines and the variant are computed and not read;
for each case, whether its text fixes the verdict, by a proof or by two consistent scenes that differ. Then
the seven cases as scenes, with candidate further measures computed on them (her unused information; the
best she can do under the policy against under the role's conduct). Then "the same change" and f-neutrality
by one relation, the law of the development of her valuing given the circumstances no policy affects, with
the instances of items 15 and 16 and a scene in which the means of a change matter. Then a model of several
moments scored by her later evaluation under a complete gate, with each of four choices made explicit: the
tolerance that counts as illegitimacy, whether illegitimacy from another source gates, how a gated history
is repaired, and what moves the adviser once the gate has closed
