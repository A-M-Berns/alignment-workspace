## Statement
A computationally uncertain UDT exists over a logical-induction-like belief
process, specified so that its policy evaluation uses the process's later
beliefs, and it satisfies a tiling theorem.

## Source
projects/deference/faf-cleanroom-2026-10-02/sources/references/bli/understanding-trust/overleaf-final-source/main.tex,
section Computational Uncertainty: "the framework does not support a suitable
version of UDT. In joint work with Martín Soto \cite{soto2023logically}, we
spelled out some additional rationality conditions to allow a version of UDT to
be specified. Unfortunately, the version of UDT achieved there is not a very
good one, and a tiling theorem did not result from that work."

projects/deference/faf-cleanroom-2026-10-02/sources/references/bli/notion-notes-for-soto-and-diff.md,
section The Research Direction: "For better or worse, the apparent issues with
combining UDT and logical uncertainty don’t add up to an impossibility proof
(at least not yet). What happens if we try harder to get something to work? Can
we succeed, or in failing, provide a clear impossibility proof?"
