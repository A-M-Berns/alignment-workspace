## Statement
Representing beliefs about beliefs as random variables, and restricting the
language so that distributions stay computable, obscures that realistic agents
are probabilistically inconsistent because they do not know all the
consequences of their beliefs.

## Source
projects/deference/faf-cleanroom-2026-10-02/sources/unbounded-embedded/03-journals/2026-03-26.md:
"Generally speaking, I do feel strongly that representing beliefs as a random
variable (avoiding explicit beliefs-about-beliefs embedded into the sigma
algebra itself) misses something important about the structure of
beliefs-about-beliefs; it essentially sets aside logic, caring only for
probability."; "obscuring the idea that realistic agents must be
probabilistically inconsistent because it is not realistic to know all the
consequences of your own beliefs."
